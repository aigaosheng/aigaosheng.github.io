# AI Governance Brief: FSB Finalises Responsible AI Guidance for Financial Institutions

**Today:** Financial regulators are moving towards more explicit governance expectations for AI, with the Financial Stability Board (FSB) finalising guidance for financial institutions and Hong Kong’s Monetary Authority (HKMA) introducing a framework focused on accountability, resilience and trust.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **FSB Finalises Responsible AI Practices for Financial Institutions**

*Financial Stability Board · October 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The FSB has finalised a set of 12 practices to help financial institutions govern AI adoption, manage lifecycle risks and strengthen organisational accountability.

The final report incorporates feedback on a consultation launched in June 2026. Its recommendations address organisation-wide governance and risk management across the AI lifecycle, alongside coordination and information-sharing between financial institutions, supervisors and other stakeholders.

The FSB encourages boards and senior management to use the practices when developing or updating AI governance, risk management and control frameworks.

**Why it matters:** The guidance gives financial institutions an international reference point for strengthening AI oversight as deployments become more sophisticated. Banks, insurers and payment providers should assess whether existing governance arrangements adequately cover model risk, operational resilience, accountability and oversight of increasingly autonomous systems.

### 2. 🏦 **Hong Kong’s HKMA Introduces ART Framework for Banking AI**

*Hong Kong Monetary Authority · October 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The HKMA has introduced its Accountability, Resilience and Trust (ART) Framework to support responsible AI adoption across Hong Kong’s banking sector.

The framework includes updated supervisory guidance informed by the second cohort of the Generative AI Sandbox. The HKMA is also developing an AI training-data sharing platform with Hong Kong Cyberport and the University of Hong Kong, with data quality assurance as a core design principle.

Further initiatives include additional agentic AI testing through the GenAI Sandbox++ and a forthcoming discussion paper on agentic AI governance. Potential measures include accountability tied to an agent’s intended purpose, a registry of material AI agents and mechanisms for effective intervention. More than 60% of use cases explored in the second sandbox cohort involved agentic AI.

**Why it matters:** The framework signals a shift towards governance that addresses not only AI models but also autonomous systems acting across workflows. Financial institutions operating in Hong Kong should monitor forthcoming supervisory expectations and prepare to demonstrate clear accountability, operational controls and effective human oversight.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/10/20261015-3/)

### 3. 🏦 **EC-Council Offers AI Governance Framework to Regulators Worldwide**

*EC-Council via GlobeNewswire · October 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** EC-Council has released the second version of its Adopt, Defend, Govern (ADG) framework and offered public authorities free access to its framework and regulatory crosswalks.

The updated framework maps more than 180 controls to over 90 regulations, standards and global frameworks. EC-Council has also offered to help governments, regulators and standards bodies align the framework with their own laws, draft rules and national standards.

The initiative comes amid diverging approaches to AI oversight internationally, including debates about voluntary industry commitments, national regulation and multilateral governance.

**Why it matters:** Crosswalks between governance controls and multiple regulatory frameworks can help organisations structure compliance assessments across jurisdictions. However, a private framework does not replace applicable law or supervisory guidance; financial institutions should validate any mapping against their own regulatory obligations and risk profiles.

## Key Takeaways

- **Board-level accountability is becoming central to AI governance.** Financial institutions should treat AI oversight as an enterprise risk and control responsibility, rather than a technology-team function.
- **Agentic AI is raising the bar for operational controls.** Governance frameworks increasingly need to address autonomous actions, defined operating boundaries, intervention mechanisms and ongoing monitoring.
- **Regulatory alignment remains a cross-border challenge.** International guidance and governance frameworks can support consistent controls, but institutions must still map requirements to each relevant jurisdiction.

## What to Watch

Watch for the HKMA’s forthcoming discussion paper on agentic AI governance and further supervisory developments following the FSB’s final guidance. For Singapore-based institutions, the practical priority is to assess existing AI governance against emerging international expectations while continuing to follow applicable MAS requirements.
