# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Banks Slash Zhipu, MiniMax Price Targets

**Today:** China's two listed large-model pioneers are being repriced on margins and ARR multiples rather than IPO scarcity, a signal enterprise AI buyers and investors should watch as more Chinese AI firms eye Hong Kong's Chapter 18C listing route.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Banks Cut Zhipu, MiniMax Targets As AI Valuation Reset Deepens**

*21st Century Business Herald (21jingji.com) · November 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Global banks have cut price targets on Zhipu and MiniMax by 33–64% as the market moves past the scarcity premium that drove their Hong Kong IPO pops, now pricing both on margin quality and profitability timelines instead.

Zhipu's gross margin compressed from roughly 50% to 26.4% amid heavy cloud-deployment discounting and token-price competition, while the stock closed November 13 at HK$408 — still up 251% from its IPO price but down about 86% from its June peak. MiniMax closed at HK$152.4, below its own IPO price, after two Zhipu secondary placements this year left early investors sitting on losses. Analysts are increasingly benchmarking both against Anthropic's 22–29x ARR multiple rather than the triple-digit first-day pops seen at listing.

**Why it matters:** For Singapore and regional investors eyeing Hong Kong's 18C pathway for AI listings, the reset shows scarcity value fades quickly once lock-ups expire and peers list — durable valuations will depend on margin trajectory and enterprise revenue mix, not headline ARR growth alone.

### 2. 📊 **Wuhan Forum Debates Patient Capital For AI-Era Hard Tech**

*Sina Finance (via China Business News) · November 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's investment community is debating how to channel long-duration "patient capital" into AI and hard-tech ventures as a planned ¥1 trillion national venture guidance fund takes shape.

At the World Optics Valley science-insurance investment conference in Wuhan on November 13, Oriental Wealth Capital chairman Chen Wei argued individual AI stocks may be overvalued but the sector has not reached a full bubble, citing genuine demand for compute and expanding applications. Xinke Capital's Dong Qianghua warned that hard-tech fields such as semiconductors, photonics and computing need 7–15 years to reach commercialization — far longer than the typical five-to-seven-year life of most VC funds — creating a structural mismatch that risks premature exits, while a National Financial Regulatory Administration official pledged to expand tech-insurance coverage and push insurers toward early-stage, long-term hard-tech bets.

**Why it matters:** The mismatch between VC fund tenures and AI/hard-tech commercialization timelines is a structural risk for anyone assessing China's AI funding pipeline; lengthening fund life and broadening M&A and IPO exit channels could determine who can afford to keep backing China's next generation of AI chip and infrastructure startups.

## Key Takeaways

- Hong Kong's 18C listing window for Chinese AI firms is moving past its scarcity-premium phase; new entrants will be priced on margins and ARR multiples, not novelty.
- Beijing is addressing a VC-to-AI timeline mismatch with a ¥1 trillion guidance fund and insurance capital rather than relying on retail-driven IPO pops.
- Capital discipline is tightening across China's AI stack — from public-market multiples to private fund structures — even as overall funding volumes stay elevated.

## What to Watch

Whether Zhipu's and MiniMax's next earnings disclosures validate the ARR growth needed to justify their reset multiples, and how quickly the ¥1 trillion state guidance fund begins deploying capital into AI and hard-tech startups.
