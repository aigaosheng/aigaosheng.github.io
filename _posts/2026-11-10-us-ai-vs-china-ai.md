# US AI vs China AI Brief: Huang Affair Linked to $7.5B China Chip Shipments

**Today:** Fresh disclosures tied to the Nvidia-Megaspeed affair are turning a personal scandal into a quantified export-control and securities-litigation problem, just as a separate report shows a top Chinese AI lab accessing restricted Nvidia chips.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Report Ties Jensen Huang's Alleged Spy Affair to $7.5 Billion in China Chip Shipments**

*The Standard (Hong Kong), citing The Information · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new report puts a specific dollar figure and chip count on the Nvidia-Megaspeed scandal for the first time, moving it from a personnel matter into a quantified export-control exposure.

The Information reported that Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive alleged to be a Chinese intelligence asset, helped a network of companies spanning China and Singapore secure roughly 240,000 H100, H200 and A100 chips for Chinese firms, worth at least $7.5 billion. Shanghai Century Litong, founded in June 2023, reportedly won preferred Nvidia cloud-partner status by October, giving it priority chip access even as export-restricted orders stalled elsewhere. Nvidia's board and the FBI are investigating; China's foreign ministry has dismissed the espionage claims as unsubstantiated.

**Why it matters:** The scale of the figures gives congressional sponsors of the Chip Security and AI Overwatch Acts concrete numbers to cite, raising the odds of binding chip-tracking legislation that would affect every Nvidia distributor and cloud partner operating through Singapore and Southeast Asia.

### 2. 🌐 **Documents Show Moonshot AI Leasing Nvidia's Top B300 Chips Inside China**

*Bloomberg · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Filings reviewed by Bloomberg indicate China's Moonshot AI, maker of the Kimi assistant, signed an August contract to lease 32 Nvidia B300-equipped servers worth 432 million yuan, chips the US has barred from sale to China since 2022.

The filings, submitted to Beijing regulators, valued each server at roughly 13.5 million yuan, about 350% above prevailing US pricing. Moonshot disputed the accuracy of the documents and said it has no relationship with Lingmeixin (Beijing) Technology, the entity the filings list as the servers' owner; references to Moonshot were later removed from the underlying regulatory database.

**Why it matters:** Whether or not Moonshot's denial holds up, the episode illustrates how leading Chinese labs keep reaching restricted Nvidia silicon through leasing structures, a live concern for Singapore and Malaysia-based cloud operators now facing tighter Nvidia reseller terms.

### 3. 📊 **Nvidia Shareholders Sue Huang Over Non-Disclosure of China-Linked Affair**

*Seoul Economic Daily · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A federal securities class action filed against Nvidia and Jensen Huang alleges the company concealed the affair and its national-security risk exposure from investors before a one-day, roughly $190 billion market-value drop.

Rosen Law Firm filed the suit on November 9 in the US District Court for the Northern District of California on behalf of investors who bought Nvidia shares between October 9, 2025, and November 8, 2026, citing Section 10(b) and 20(a) violations. Nvidia stock fell 4.27% on November 9 after Huang admitted the affair to employees; the board's internal investigation has so far found no evidence that national security or trade secrets were compromised.

**Why it matters:** A securities suit adds a durable legal track separate from the congressional and FBI inquiries, meaning the scandal's cost to Nvidia now includes litigation risk and disclosure obligations that could reach any supplier or partner named in discovery.

## Key Takeaways

- The Megaspeed affair has moved from an unquantified security concern to a dollar-and-unit-specific export-control case, strengthening the legislative push behind the Chip Security and AI Overwatch Acts.
- Even as Washington tightens enforcement, reports of fresh leasing routes into restricted Nvidia silicon (Moonshot) show Chinese labs continuing to probe for access, keeping compliance risk live for Southeast Asian cloud intermediaries.
- Nvidia now faces simultaneous exposure on three fronts — congressional, criminal/FBI, and shareholder litigation — any one of which could force new disclosure standards for chip distribution through Singapore-linked entities.

House Republicans have asked Jensen Huang for a closed briefing on the Megaspeed matter, with a response requested by November 11; watch whether that briefing proceeds and whether it precedes the expected Trump-Xi meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen later in November.
