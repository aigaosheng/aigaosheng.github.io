# US AI vs China AI Brief: Rubio Memo Deepens Nvidia Chip Smuggling Scrutiny

**Today:** Two new reports detail how Jensen Huang's ouster is turning into a broader probe of the Southeast Asian loophole used to route restricted Nvidia chips into China, raising compliance stakes for cloud and data-center operators across the region, including Singapore.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Washington Post Ties Rubio Memo To Huang's Exit, Details Chip Loophole**

*The Washington Post · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Secretary of State Marco Rubio's memo to President Trump, built on a CIA report about Jensen Huang's ties to a woman suspected of spying for China, helped trigger Huang's November 12 resignation from Nvidia.

The Washington Post reports Rubio recast the CIA's findings in more assertive terms before sending them to Trump. The article also lays out the mechanics of the alleged loophole: large Southeast Asian data centers gave Chinese customers legitimate cloud and colocation access to high-end chips, a buildout scale that investigators say provided cover for restricted hardware to be relabeled or diverted before reaching China. A former Commerce official quoted in the piece argued the diversion needed no espionage at all, since Huang had openly pushed for China sales and a regulatory gap already existed.

**Why it matters:** The account exposes a rift inside the Trump administration over chip policy and strengthens the case Congress is building for bills such as the AI Overwatch Act and Chip Security Act; for Singapore and other regional cloud hubs, it signals that US scrutiny will extend beyond Nvidia to any data center that hosted the chips in question.

### 2. 🏦 **Megaspeed Confirms Alice Huang's 2025 Exit, Opens Internal Probe**

*CNBC · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore-based Megaspeed International, Nvidia's largest Southeast Asian chip buyer, broke its public silence on Sunday, confirming Alice Huang left the firm in August 2025 and announcing an internal investigation led by outside legal counsel.

Megaspeed said media reports about its business, supply chain and global operations are "not supported by verifiable evidence" and reiterated that it complies with US export controls and local rules. The statement follows weeks of scrutiny after Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive, was assessed by US intelligence as a Chinese intelligence asset, and comes alongside separate Department of Justice and Singapore police investigations into whether Megaspeed's Nvidia chip purchases, which have exceeded $4.8 billion since 2022, were diverted to China.

**Why it matters:** Megaspeed's cooperation will directly shape December's Congressional hearings on Nvidia and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's testimony, and feeds into the US Commerce Department's draft rule requiring AI chip export licenses for data centers in Singapore and six other countries, a compliance issue regional cloud and BaaS infrastructure providers should track closely.

## Key Takeaways

- The Nvidia-Megaspeed affair has shifted from a personal scandal into a test of whether Southeast Asian cloud and data-center hubs can remain trusted conduits for US chip exports.
- Washington's own internal process is now part of the story: a politically charged memo, not just raw intelligence, helped force out Huang, adding momentum to pending chip-export legislation.
- Megaspeed's outside-counsel review and the parallel DOJ and Singapore probes will determine how strictly new US data-center licensing rules are enforced across the region.

## What to Watch

Jensen Huang is set to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick following on December 10; the outcome of Megaspeed's inventory audit and its cooperation with Singapore and US investigators will be a key signal before then.
