# AI governance Brief — 2026-09-18

## Top Stories 

### 1. **Europe's AI industry pushes back against calls to slow frontier AI**

* **Source**: Reuters · 2026-09-18
* **Summary**: European AI companies including Mistral are challenging calls from several U.S. AI leaders to slow frontier-model development. Critics argue that broad slowdowns or safety restrictions could reinforce the position of incumbent U.S. AI companies rather than create neutral safeguards. European policymakers are simultaneously emphasizing digital sovereignty, competition, and the need for Europe to shape international AI governance.
* **Why It Matters**: The governance debate is increasingly becoming a competition-policy and geopolitical issue, not simply a safety question. Regulators face a difficult trade-off between frontier-risk controls, market concentration, and technological sovereignty.

### 2. **Anthropic says Claude is increasingly contributing to development of its successor**

* **Source**: Associated Press · 2026-09-18
* **Summary**: Anthropic reported that Claude is now involved in a significant share of the company's model research and development work under human supervision. The company said Claude contributes to roughly 90% of R&D tasks, while directly leading about 26% of its model-development work as of August. Anthropic is calling for greater transparency around progress toward increasingly autonomous AI development and third-party evaluation.
* **Why It Matters**: AI governance is moving from governing model outputs toward governing AI-assisted model development itself. This increases the importance of auditability, capability evaluations, human oversight, and transparent reporting of increasingly autonomous R&D systems.

### 3. **UNCTAD economist calls for a “macroprudential” approach to AI governance**

* **Source**: ProMarket · 2026-09-18
* **Summary**: UNCTAD economist Piergiuseppe Fortunato argues that AI governance should address systemic risks created by interactions among large populations of agents, rather than evaluating agents individually. The analysis compares potential AI feedback loops with financial-system risks, where individually rational decisions can collectively generate instability. Proposed mechanisms include multi-agent stress testing, monitoring common dependencies, and circuit breakers in sensitive domains.
* **Why It Matters**: Agentic AI creates risks that may emerge from interactions rather than from any single model. For financial services, markets, cybersecurity, energy, and logistics, governance may increasingly need system-level monitoring in addition to conventional model-risk management.
* **URL**: [https://www.promarket.org/2026/09/18/ai-governance-needs-a-macroprudential-turn/](https://www.promarket.org/2026/09/18/ai-governance-needs-a-macroprudential-turn/)
