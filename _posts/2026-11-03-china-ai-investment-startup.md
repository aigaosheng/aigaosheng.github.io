# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Baidu Denies Kunlunxin IPO Delay

**Today:** A quieter news day still produced one market-moving denial and three fresh funding rounds, underscoring how closely investors are tracking China's AI chip and embodied-AI pipelines.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Baidu Denies Kunlunxin IPO Delay Rumours**

*21st Century Business Herald · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Baidu says reports that its AI chip unit Kunlunxin is delaying its IPO are untrue, but confirmed it cannot disclose listing details while in confidential filing.

Baidu stated that "recent market rumours and related reports are untrue," adding that Kunlunxin remains in confidential filing and cannot disclose material non-public information such as listing timelines under regulatory rules. Kunlunxin, spun off from Baidu's internal chip team with Baidu retaining roughly 57.67% ownership, was last valued at about CNY 21 billion following rounds from CPE Fountain, IDG Capital, JunLian Capital and China Mobile's industry fund, and is pursuing parallel Hong Kong and mainland A-share listing tracks.

**Why it matters:** Kunlunxin is one of China's most closely watched AI-chip IPO candidates, and its confidential-filing status — rather than any confirmed delay — appears to be fuelling speculation; the episode is a reminder for Asia-based investors to treat unverified IPO timing rumours with caution.

### 2. 📊 **Moffett AI Raises Second ~CNY 1 Billion Round in Five Months**

*TechNode China · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Sparse-computing chip startup Moffett AI closed a C+ round of roughly CNY 1 billion, bringing its total raised over two rounds to about CNY 2 billion.

Investors in the new round include Renai Capital, Begonia Fund, Dongheng Petroleum, Biqu Capital, Para Capital and Zhongcai Capital. Moffett said proceeds will fund a full-stack upgrade of its sparse inference chips, next-generation chip R&D, and an industry-academia alliance on sparse computing; the company reported several-fold revenue growth in the third quarter of 2026 and plans to launch new SP200 accelerator cards by year-end.

**Why it matters:** Back-to-back large rounds for a niche inference-chip startup show investors continuing to bet on compute-efficiency plays even as headline large-model rounds dominate the funding tables — a trend enterprise AI buyers in Asia should watch for cheaper inference options.

### 3. 🤖 **Physical-AI Driving Startup Yicheng Innovation Closes CNY 200 Million Series C**

*PEDaily (投资界) · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Yicheng Innovation, which bills itself as a commercial autonomous-driving "physical AI" provider, raised a CNY 200 million Series C to scale manufacturing and its world-model technology.

Investors included Guangdong S&T Group, Shanda Investment, Anhui State Investment and Foshan Innovation Fund. The company said its embodied-AI-related order book has reached hundreds of millions of yuan, with deployments across industrial logistics, energy, materials, smart parks, transit, ports and mining, built on self-developed L4 autonomous driving and multimodal world models.

**Why it matters:** The round reflects a broader shift in China's embodied-AI capital toward industrial deployment rather than demonstration units, a pattern enterprise and logistics operators across Asia should factor into vendor evaluations.

### 4. 🤖 **AI Flight-Control Drone Startup Paiyun Zhihang Lands Angel Round**

*PEDaily (投资界) · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Long-endurance drone startup Paiyun Zhihang raised a tens-of-millions-yuan angel round to advance its AI-enabled redundant flight-control systems.

The round was backed by Beihang Tianhui and Xishan Angel Alliance. The company combines artificial intelligence with long-endurance drone engineering, targeting industrial and infrastructure applications that require sustained autonomous flight.

**Why it matters:** Early-stage capital continuing to flow into niche AI-hardware applications signals that China's venture appetite extends well beyond headline large-model and humanoid-robot rounds into specialised industrial AI niches.

## Key Takeaways

- China's AI capital markets are tracking two parallel tracks: large chip IPO candidates like Kunlunxin facing scrutiny over listing timing, and compute-efficiency chip startups like Moffett AI still raising large rounds in quick succession.
- Embodied-AI and physical-AI funding is increasingly tied to concrete order books and industrial deployments (logistics, energy, ports) rather than pure demonstration technology.
- Early-stage AI-hardware niches, such as AI flight control for drones, continue to attract angel-stage capital even as headline rounds dominate attention.

## What to Watch

Watch for Kunlunxin's next disclosure on its dual-track Hong Kong and A-share listing process, and for confirmation of Moffett AI's year-end SP200 accelerator card launch as a signal of its sparse-computing commercialisation progress.
