# AI for Science Brief: Harvard's AURORA AI Joins Cancer Trials

**Today:** Three independent announcements show AI moving from predictive modelling into active roles inside clinical and translational research workflows, a trend enterprise R&D leaders should track as a signal of where agentic AI delivers measurable scientific value first.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔬 **Cancer Grand Challenges Puts Harvard's AI Co-Scientist Into Live Research Teams**

*Cancer Research UK / BioSpace · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** An agentic AI system built at Harvard, backed by an £8 million Cancer Research UK investment, will now assist three international cancer research teams rather than remain a standalone tool.

Project AURORA's AI co-scientists reason step-by-step through research questions, drawing only on peer-reviewed sources and flagging uncertainty rather than answering independently. Three Cancer Grand Challenges teams were selected to test the system: NexTGen (paediatric CAR T-cell therapy), PROMINENT (why some cancer-mutated cells stay healthy) and MATCHMAKERS (how T-cells recognise tumours).

**Why it matters:** This is one of the first instances of a funder formally embedding an agentic AI system as a working research partner across multiple independent labs, not just a single pilot site. For enterprise R&D and pharma leaders, it is an early template for governance of AI "co-scientists" — bounded to peer-reviewed evidence, unable to make autonomous decisions — that could inform how Asian research funders and hospital systems structure similar pilots.

### 2. 🔬 **AI-Designed Personalised Cancer Vaccine Shows Durable Three-Year Responses**

*Evaxion A/S · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Evaxion's AI-Immunology platform-designed melanoma vaccine EVX-01 produced a 75% objective response rate in a Phase 2 trial, with responses holding up at three-year follow-up.

Each of the 16 trial patients received a vaccine individually designed by Evaxion's AI platform based on their own tumour biology, combined with Merck's pembrolizumab. New biomarker data presented at the SITC 2026 Annual Meeting showed 89% of AI-selected vaccine targets triggered a tumour-specific immune response, and AI-predicted immunogenicity correlated strongly (p<0.001) with actual T-cell activity.

**Why it matters:** Durable, multi-year clinical data is rarer than early-stage AI drug discovery headlines and offers a concrete read on whether AI target-selection translates into real patient outcomes. It strengthens the case pharma companies in Asia and elsewhere are making to justify continued investment in AI-designed, patient-specific biologics over fixed-formulation approaches.

### 3. 🔬 **Network Bio and Duke Pair AI Models With Biobank Data to Find Disease Targets**

*Duke University School of Medicine · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Network Bio and Duke University School of Medicine will build disease-specific AI models by pairing molecular profiling of Duke biobank specimens with de-identified clinical records.

The partnership targets earlier disease detection, better patient-therapy matching and new drug-target discovery, extending Network Bio's existing academic medical centre network after its $50 million financing launch earlier in 2026. Duke's Dr. Svati Shah said the approach could identify biomarkers and therapeutic targets faster by lowering the cost barrier to advanced molecular profiling for academic researchers.

**Why it matters:** The deal reflects a broader shift of AI-for-science value creation toward data-access partnerships with hospital biobanks rather than model architecture alone — a model that Singapore's NUHS, A*STAR and other Asian health systems sitting on large biobanks could replicate or compete with for translational research funding.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://medschool.duke.edu/news/new-partnership-will-expand-research-opportunities-duke)

## Key Takeaways

- All three stories involve AI moving from a modelling tool into an operational role inside research workflows — as a reasoning agent, a therapeutic designer validated against real patient outcomes, or a biobank-data partner.
- Funders and academic medical centres (Cancer Research UK, Duke, Harvard) are now co-designing AI governance and data-access terms directly with AI vendors, rather than treating AI as an external contractor.
- Durable multi-year clinical data, as in Evaxion's case, is becoming the bar AI drug-design platforms need to clear to justify continued capital, beyond early discovery-speed claims.

## What to Watch

Watch for Isomorphic Labs' first IND filing and first-in-human trial, targeted for year-end 2026, as the next major test of whether AI-designed molecules translate into clinical progress; and watch whether Cancer Grand Challenges publishes interim results from the three AURORA pilot teams as a signal of agentic AI's practical research value.
