# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Unitree Profit Falls in First Post-IPO Quarter

**Today:** China's AI capital cycle is splitting in two directions — public-market investors are repricing robotics on profitability, while state banks and corporate venture arms keep pushing money into early-stage physical AI.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Unitree Posts 70% Revenue Growth but 17% Profit Decline in First Report Since Listing**

*Yicai Global · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Unitree's debut quarterly report as a listed company showed fast revenue growth undercut by a sharp rise in research spending, and the shares fell on the day.

The humanoid robot maker reported third-quarter revenue of CNY 665 million, up about 70% year on year, while net profit fell roughly 17% to CNY 112.7 million as research and development spending rose around 250% to CNY 127 million, or about 19% of revenue. Over the first nine months revenue reached CNY 1.8 billion and reported net profit rose 271%, but profit excluding non-recurring items fell about 19% to CNY 349 million. Shares closed down 4.8% at CNY 662.39 after dropping as much as 7.7% intraday; the company said the humanoid robotics industry remains at an early commercialisation stage and plans to direct roughly a third of its CNY 6.1 billion in IPO proceeds to AI model development.

**Why it matters:** Unitree is the closest thing China's embodied-AI sector has to a listed benchmark, so a quarter where growth is real but margins are being spent away sets the valuation tone for the queue of robotics and AI issuers eyeing Hong Kong and the STAR Market — a queue that Asian institutional allocators are already being asked to underwrite.

### 2. 🌐 **China's Policy Banks Complete CNY 800 Billion Issuance, With a Large Share Aimed at AI**

*Global Times · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China has finished issuing CNY 800 billion (about USD 119 billion) of 2026 policy-based financial instruments, with artificial intelligence and the digital economy named among the biggest destinations.

The issuance was split across China Development Bank (CNY 365 billion), the Agricultural Development Bank of China (CNY 315 billion) and the Export-Import Bank of China (CNY 120 billion). China Development Bank directed CNY 179.01 billion to projects spanning AI, the digital economy, green and low-carbon development, biomedicine and consumption; the Export-Import Bank reported that digital economy and AI lending accounted for close to half its allocation, and the Agricultural Development Bank put roughly CNY 200 billion into AI, digital economy and related sectors. The 2026 programme is larger than the CNY 500 billion launched in 2025.

**Why it matters:** State capital is now a structural, not marginal, source of funding for Chinese AI infrastructure, which cushions the sector against the private-market repricing visible in listed names — and it reinforces why Asian corporates and lenders should read Chinese AI capex as policy-driven rather than purely demand-driven.

### 3. 📊 **WeRide Spins Out Physical AI Data Unit Jingshuo at a CNY 2.07 Billion Valuation**

*Yicai Global · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** WeRide's data-infrastructure subsidiary Jingshuo raised CNY 70 million (about USD 10.5 million) in its first external round, valuing the unit at CNY 2.07 billion (about USD 311 million).

The pre-Series A was backed by Vision Knight Capital, Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation and Sirius Capital, leaving WeRide with roughly 67.6% ownership. Jingshuo sells first-person data-collection hardware, datasets and custom data services built around its WorldEngine platform and a generative world model, and reported unaudited revenue of CNY 187 million last year alongside about CNY 1 billion in contracted and confirmed orders, according to the company.

**Why it matters:** Carving out the data layer at a separate valuation is becoming a standard way for Chinese autonomous-driving firms to monetise assets without diluting the parent, and the participation of Hong Kong's science park signals that regional public investors want a position in physical-AI data supply chains.

### 4. 📊 **Ant Group Leads CNY 100 Million Series A for Robot Electronic-Skin Maker Juqiao Industrial**

*36Kr · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Ant Group led a CNY 100 million Series A into Juqiao Industrial, a maker of fabric-based tactile sensing material for robots.

Existing shareholders Lingyi Venture Capital, Weihai Gangrong and Qingdao Hexin added to their stakes in the round. Juqiao weaves conductive fibres into textiles to create pressure-sensitive "electronic skin" for robot surfaces and consumer products; the company says its production yield exceeds 95%, that it has delivered about 10,000 tactile gloves and that it serves more than 400 customers, over 200 of them in embodied intelligence. Proceeds are earmarked for production scale-up, tactile and multimodal sensor research, and expansion into robotics, home and healthcare applications.

**Why it matters:** Ant Group's appearance as lead investor shows China's payments and fintech platforms extending corporate venture capital well beyond financial services into embodied-AI components — a pattern Southeast Asian fintech groups weighing their own CVC mandates should watch.

### 5. 🤖 **Chengdu's Koala Youran Closes B++ Round for World Models and Embodied Agents**

*PEdaily · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Koala Youran completed an undisclosed B++ round from Mingwen Weiye to fund its world-model and multimodal foundation-model work.

The Chengdu company builds a perception-to-feedback loop intended to let robots interpret tasks and plan actions on their own, and launched a general-purpose embodied agent branded Geek Mind in May 2026. It says the agent is deployed on inspection robots at large state-owned enterprise sites, where it detects anomalies and selects follow-up checks. The money will go to further model development, scaled deployment in inspection and forestry use cases, and senior engineering hires.

**Why it matters:** Deals like this show capital moving away from general chatbot plays toward narrow, revenue-adjacent industrial deployments, and the undisclosed round size is itself a signal that Chinese founders are becoming less willing to publicise terms in a softer private market.

## Key Takeaways

- The public and private sides of China's AI market are diverging: a listed bellwether was punished for spending on R&D on the same day that early-stage rounds in sensing, data and embodied agents kept closing.
- State and quasi-state capital — policy banks, a Hong Kong science park — is showing up in deal structures that used to be the preserve of private venture funds.
- Investors are paying for the picks-and-shovels layer of physical AI, specifically data pipelines and tactile hardware, rather than for another foundation-model contender.
- Disclosure is thinning: two of today's rounds came without a stated amount or valuation, which makes company-supplied operating metrics harder to benchmark.

## What to Watch

Whether Unitree's share price stabilises as the rest of China's A-share AI and robotics names complete third-quarter reporting, and whether the companies that drew policy-bank funding this cycle disclose how the money converts into deployed AI capacity.
