## Top Stories

### 1. Study Maps 'Big AI' Influence Tactics on Global Regulation
- **EurekAlert! (University of Edinburgh)** · 2026-05-18
- **Summary**: A new peer-reviewed study from an international team of researchers has mapped how major AI companies employ 'corporate capture' tactics to influence policy and oversight, drawing parallels to strategies used by Big Tobacco and Big Oil. Analyzing 100 news stories around major AI policy events (2023-2025), the research identified 249 instances of capture patterns, with 'narrative capture' being one of the most prevalent—specifically pushing the narrative that "regulation stifles innovation."
- **Why It Matters**: This provides empirical evidence for what compliance officers have long suspected: regulatory uncertainty is not accidental. For GRC professionals, understanding these influence mechanisms is crucial for anticipating future regulatory shifts that may favor industry self-regulation over binding oversight, directly impacting compliance strategies and risk assessments.
- **URL**: [Study maps how 'Big AI' influences AI laws and oversight](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1128640)

### 2. EU Reaches Deal on AI Act 'Omnibus' with Deadlines Postponed
- **Stephenson Harwood** · 2026-05-18
- **Summary**: EU legislators have reached a provisional political agreement on the "Digital Omnibus" package, significantly simplifying the EU AI Act. Key changes include postponing compliance deadlines for high-risk AI systems to December 2027 (Annex III) and August 2028 (Annex I), adding a new prohibition on AI for non-consensual intimate content, and avoiding double-regulation for machinery products. The text was confirmed on May 13 and is expected to be adopted in substantially this form.
- **Why It Matters**: The postponed deadlines give businesses additional breathing room, but the simplification does not eliminate compliance requirements. Companies should use this extended timeline to conduct thorough gap analyses and prepare for the new high-risk classifications without halting ongoing implementation projects.
- **URL**: [Neural Network - May 2026](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/neural-network-may-2026/)

### 3. EU Commission Issues Draft Guidelines on AI Act Transparency
- **Pinsent Masons** · 2026-05-19
- **Summary**: The European Commission has released long-awaited draft guidelines on Article 50 of the EU AI Act, clarifying rules for disclosing AI interactions, marking synthetic content, and handling deepfakes. The guidance introduces "average consumer" tests for obvious AI interactions but admits that currently no technical solution exists for reliably marking AI-generated outputs across all modalities. The draft is open for consultation until June 3, 2026.
- **Why It Matters**: Legal experts warn that while the guidelines provide a useful compass, their non-binding nature and reliance on abstract consumer-law concepts create litigation risks. Companies must make complex, case-by-case judgments—especially for AI-generated advertising and creative content—without clear technical solutions for compliance.
- **URL**: [EU AI Act transparency guidelines issued](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/eu-ai-act-transparency-guidelines-issued)

### 4. Colorado Overhauls AI Law, Shifting Focus to Individual Decisions
- **Holland & Knight** · 2026-05-18
- **Summary**: Colorado Governor Jared Polis signed SB 189 on May 14, 2026, substantially revising the state's landmark AI law (originally SB 205) following federal opposition and an industry lawsuit. The new law eliminates burdensome requirements like mandatory risk management programs and annual impact assessments, replacing them with a decision-by-decision accountability model focused on automated decision-making technology (ADMT). It also introduces sector-specific accommodations for HIPAA entities and a new liability framework.
- **Why It Matters**: This represents a major pivot from system-level compliance to outcome-based accountability. For employers and deployers, risk moves downstream: instead of defending systems upfront, they must now justify each individual AI-assisted adverse decision. Contract clauses indemnifying a party for its own discriminatory AI acts are now void, demanding immediate vendor contract reviews.
- **URL**: [Colorado Governor Signs SB 189](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/colorado-governor-signs-sb-189)

### 7. UK Regulators Signal Joint Focus on Agentic and Generative AI
- **Stephenson Harwood** · 2026-05-18
- **Summary**: The Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF)—bringing together the CMA, Ofcom, ICO, and FCA—published its 2026/27 workplan, setting a joint priority on deepening work around agentic and generative AI. Key initiatives include a new DRCF Research Lab, a Thematic Innovation Hub on "Authentication and Trust," and an AI-powered Digital Library prototype for navigating UK digital regulation. The workplan signals a move away from siloed digital regulation toward a more coherent cross-regulatory approach.
- **Why It Matters**: UK businesses face increasingly coordinated regulatory scrutiny across competition, data protection, and online safety domains. The focus on authentication and trust indicates that regulators are preparing for systemic risks from autonomous AI systems, not just traditional algorithmic transparency issues.

### 8. EDPS Launches AI Regulatory Sandbox for EU Institutions
- **Stephenson Harwood** · 2026-05-18
- **Summary**: The European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) has launched a pilot AI regulatory sandbox for EU institutions, bodies, and agencies, operationalizing the EU AI Act's innovation framework under Article 57(3). While Member States face an August 2026 deadline (likely postponed to 2027 under the Omnibus) to establish national sandboxes, the EDPS pilot provides early guidance on regulatory expectations, best practices, and market access facilitation—particularly for SMEs and startups.
- **Why It Matters**: The sandbox offers a controlled environment for validating AI systems before market placement, potentially serving as a template for national approaches. Participants gain early clarity on regulatory expectations and may use exit reports to demonstrate compliance through conformity assessments.
