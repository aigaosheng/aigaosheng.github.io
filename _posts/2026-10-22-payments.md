# Payments Brief: OCC Finalises GENIUS Act Stablecoin Rules

**Today:** US regulators have finalised the stablecoin rulebook, while industry data and an Asian pilot idea show stablecoin payments edging toward everyday use.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **OCC Finalises GENIUS Act Rules for Payment Stablecoin Issuers**
*PaymentExpert · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US federal rulebook for payment stablecoin issuers is now set and is scheduled to take effect on 18 January 2027.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency announced its final rule on 21 October. It covers reserve assets, redemption, risk management, capital, audits, reporting and supervision, and sets out how issuers apply, how certain state-qualified issuers can move to the federal framework and how foreign issuers will be supervised. The publisher describes the rule as including 1:1 reserve requirements and an interest ban. Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money laundering and sanctions requirements are still to come in a separate rulemaking with Treasury, and the OCC will announce when it starts accepting applications.

**Why it matters:** Asia-based payment firms that issue or distribute dollar stablecoins will need to track how foreign issuers are supervised. The pending AML and sanctions rulemaking is the main open item for compliance teams.

### 2. 📊 **Coinbase and The Block Report Puts Stablecoin Flows Above $400M an Hour**
*CoinGeek · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new industry report says stablecoin transfers now exceed $400 million an hour, but the authors are crypto-industry players, so treat the figures as advocacy data.

The report, "The Always-On Dollar" from Coinbase and The Block, says global stablecoin transfer volume has quadrupled since 2023. It says 73% of screened volume occurs outside US banking hours and counts 293 million stablecoin-holding addresses, a figure the report itself says is not directly comparable with card counts because one person can hold many wallets. CoinGeek also notes that the Trump-linked USD1 stablecoin fell from $4.45 billion to just under $4 billion in market cap over two weeks, citing Onchain Benchmark, which tied about $200 million of the drop to a single BNB Chain address.

**Why it matters:** The off-hours share supports the case for 24/7 settlement over banking-hour rails, a point that resonates with cross-border flows in Asia. Verify the underlying data before using it in internal business cases.

### 3. 💳 **Square Tech and GOAT Network Plan AI-Agent Stablecoin Payments in Korea**
*Bitcoin Magazine (press release) · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A KOSDAQ-listed company has signed a non-binding MOU to explore AI-agent stablecoin payments, starting with foreign tourists, and nothing is live yet.

Square Tech signed the MOU with GOAT Network to explore AI-agent payment services within its Digital Solutions business. The first target is a stablecoin payment programme for foreign visitors that Square Tech is developing with Lordsystem, a mobile identity verification firm. Visitors would verify identity with mobile passports and pay participating merchants in US dollar-denominated stablecoins. The companies list on-chain identity, spending limits and user authorisation as proposed safeguards, with a phased rollout subject to Korean legal and regulatory review.

**Why it matters:** It shows agentic payments and stablecoin rails being pitched together for inbound-tourist spending in Asia. The safeguards named are the design questions any such programme must answer before regulators will engage.

*PaymentExpert · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Tether's chief executive says the company has stopped 94% of stablecoin links to Iran, in response to a US government report.

In PaymentExpert's weekly Blockchain Bulletin, Paolo Ardoino responded to a US government report that described USDT as a "financial lifeline" for Iranian shadow banks. The linked item is a short video summary, so it does not show the report's evidence or how the 94% figure was calculated.

### 5. 🌐 **Analysis: Digital Euro Faces Merchant and Bank Adoption Test**
*PaymentExpert · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** This is an analysis piece, not breaking news: the ECB's 2029 digital euro still has to win over merchants and banks that have competing options.

The piece notes a June 2026 European Parliament vote and a pilot next year with more than 30 institutions, including Stripe, Revolut and Adyen. It says the digital euro would carry no merchant processing fees, against EU interchange caps of 0.2% for debit and 0.3% for credit cards. It also points to Qivalis, a consortium of more than 30 banks planning a euro stablecoin in late 2026 or early 2027. The adoption arguments come largely from a Hedera policy executive, so they reflect one stakeholder's view.

**Why it matters:** The tension between CBDCs, stablecoins and tokenised deposits described here is a design choice Asian regulators also face, though the article covers Europe only.

### 6. 💳 **Underdog Adds Stablecoin Account Funding Through zerohash**
*GlobeNewswire (press release) · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A US prediction-market and fantasy-sports operator will let users fund accounts with stablecoins and crypto, with launch expected later this year.

Underdog is partnering with zerohash, an onchain infrastructure provider, for the new funding option, which will be offered to eligible users. The company says stablecoins allow real-time settlement on weekends and evenings when fiat rails are unavailable.

## Key Takeaways
- Stablecoin regulation is moving from rulemaking to implementation in the US, with a January 2027 start date and AML and sanctions rules still outstanding.
- Stablecoin use cases are spreading to tourism, prediction markets and agent payments, but most of today's evidence is vendor- or industry-authored.
- CBDCs, stablecoins and tokenised deposits are competing designs for digital money, and sanctions exposure is a live risk on stablecoin rails.

## What to Watch
The OCC's announcement of when it will accept issuer applications, and its separate Bank Secrecy Act, AML and sanctions rulemaking, ahead of the 18 January 2027 effective date.
