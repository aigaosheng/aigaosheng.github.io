# US AI vs China AI Brief: China's AI Export Curbs Keep Core Models Onshore

**Today:** If your firm uses, hosts, licenses or buys Chinese AI models or chips, or ships hardware through Southeast Asia, today's three stories affect your vendor and supply-chain risk.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **China's Draft AI Export Curbs Would Let Foreigners Rent Chinese AI but Not Own It**
*The Standard (Reuters) · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing's draft rules separate foreign *access* to Chinese AI from foreign *possession* of it, and new detail shows they are likely to be adopted by the end of 2026.

The draft was first released on October 16. It adds 23 AI-related technologies to China's export ban-or-restrict list, including advanced models, autonomous agents, chip-design technology and methods for coordinating more than 10,000 AI chips. Overseas use through websites and cloud platforms would broadly stay open. Transfers of the underlying technology, and some overseas deployments, would need government approval.

What is new is the timeline and the legal interpretation. Public comment runs to November 15. A source told Reuters adoption is expected by year-end, with implementation guidelines later. A model is covered if it meets any of three tests: training compute of 10^26 operations, at least 2 trillion parameters, or a top-10 global ranking on certain assessments.

Law firm Fangda says registration requirements in force since October 16 already cover deals that put Chinese AI technology under foreign ownership or control, including transactions still in progress. Researchers at leading Chinese labs are seeking clarity on whether they may train or run frontier models on foreign-owned servers abroad.

**Why it matters:** API and SaaS use of Chinese models looks unaffected. Self-hosting downloaded weights, joint ventures, and acquiring Chinese-founded AI teams now carry approval risk. Asia-based firms doing vendor due diligence on Chinese models should flag this as a change in risk.

### 2. 🤖 **CXMT to Start HBM3E Production Despite Yields as Low as 20%**
*The Standard (Reuters) · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's largest DRAM maker plans to make advanced AI memory at very low yields, because US controls have left domestic AI chipmakers short of HBM.

Two people familiar with the matter say CXMT has sent HBM3E samples to several Chinese AI chip designers. One of them expects limited production within the next two months.

The sources put yields at roughly 20–30%, against about 70% at market leader SK Hynix. In testing, the chips performed closer to SK Hynix's previous-generation HBM3 than to its HBM3E. They are also expected to cost more than comparable overseas products.

The US tightened controls on certain advanced HBM exports to China in December 2024. Reuters reported last month that Huawei, Cambricon, MetaX and Iluvatar CoreX had raised AI chip prices by 20–50% as memory costs bit.

**Why it matters:** For now, memory rather than logic is the binding constraint on China's domestic AI compute. Higher-cost, lower-performance HBM implies continued pricing pressure on Chinese AI infrastructure and cloud services. The Korean incumbents' lead in HBM looks secure for at least one product generation.

### 3. 🔒 **Malaysia Seizes RM49.5 Million in AI Servers, Fifth Case Tied to One Syndicate**
*Malay Mail · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysian customs has now linked more than RM100 million in seized AI chip shipments this year to a single suspected smuggling network operating through KLIA.

KLIA Customs found 30 servers containing advanced AI chips during an October 15 inspection of a warehouse in the airport's Free Trade Zone. The shipment was attempting to transit without the required authorisation.

The Strategic Trade Secretariat of MITI (the trade ministry) said the chips are controlled as dual-use items. Malaysia's rules on the export, transit and transhipment of advanced AI chips aim to stop the country from serving as a route for chips headed to sanctioned destinations. Officials did not name the chips' origin, brand or final destination, citing the ongoing probe.

**Why it matters:** Southeast Asian free-trade zones are now active enforcement points in the US-China chip contest. Logistics firms, data-centre operators and their financiers in the region should expect closer scrutiny of AI hardware flows and stronger know-your-customer checks.

## Key Takeaways
- Export control is now two-way. Washington restricts compute, hardware and capital flowing into China, while Beijing is starting to restrict models and AI know-how flowing out.
- US curbs are working as a cost tax rather than a hard stop. China is building substitutes (CXMT's HBM) at poor yields and higher prices, and still trying to obtain foreign hardware through transit hubs.
- Southeast Asia sits in the middle: as a transit route that is being policed more tightly, and as an overseas compute base that China's new rules may make harder to use.

## What to Watch
The November 15 close of public comment on China's draft AI export catalogue, and whether final rules clarify the use of foreign-owned overseas servers. Also watch whether CXMT's limited HBM3E production begins within the two-month window sources described.
