# Payments Brief: Trump Backs 10% Card Rate Cap With Democrats

**Today:** A possible US political deal on card pricing and a new Visa push into Southeast Asian QR interoperability are the two verifiable developments published since the last edition's cut-off.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Trump Says He Would Work With Democrats to Cap Credit Card Interest at 10%**

*Associated Press, via PBS NewsHour · November 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** President Trump has said he would work with the incoming Democratic-controlled Congress on a 10% cap on credit card interest rates, reviving an idea that went nowhere under Republican control.

In a Fox News interview on Saturday, Trump said he would pursue the cap and argued that card companies have made a lot of money. He first floated a one-year 10% cap in January 2026. Banks and card issuers opposed it, warning they would cancel cards for less creditworthy customers or cut rewards, and the Republican-led Congress made little effort to legislate. AP reports that Sen. Elizabeth Warren, expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee, welcomed the idea. It also notes that Sen. Josh Hawley, a Republican, has backed caps before. AP cites a New York Fed survey putting the median card rate at about 22.2%. It also cites researchers who estimate a 10% cap would save Americans roughly $100 billion in interest a year.

**Why it matters:** A cap with support from both parties would hit issuer economics and could change how rewards and credit access are priced, which would matter to card networks and their issuing partners. For Asia-based issuers and processors with US exposure, the signal to watch is whether this moves from rhetoric to a bill. The AP report describes no new industry statement on the latest remarks.

### 2. 🌐 **Visa and National Bank of Cambodia Sign MOU on Cross-Border QR Connectivity**

*Agence Kampuchea Presse · November 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa and Cambodia's central bank have agreed to explore linking the country's national QR infrastructure to Visa's global network, so overseas cardholders can pay at KHQR merchants.

AKP reports that the memorandum was signed on November 16, 2026, and cites a Visa release dated November 21. The National Bank of Cambodia says it wants to widen acceptance of KHQR scans for transactions started on overseas networks and partner institutions connected to Visa. Visa demonstrated its QR Connector, which is meant to let travellers pay at local QR merchants with familiar payment methods while merchants keep their existing QR setup. The article builds on a 2025 integration of Visa cards into the Bakong Tourists App. It also cites figures that Bakong processed about 1.33 billion transactions in 2025 and that Cambodia has five bilateral QR links across ASEAN. These are as reported by AKP and were not independently checked.

**Why it matters:** Card networks are positioning themselves as connectors between domestic QR schemes rather than competing with them, which affects how interoperable payments in ASEAN develop. The MOU is exploratory, with no launch timeline or commercial terms reported.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://akp.gov.kh/post/detail/387734)

## Key Takeaways

- Card pricing is returning to the US policy agenda, and a shift in Congress's political make-up is what makes a 10% cap look more plausible than it did in January.
- Networks are working with central banks and national QR schemes in Asia to connect domestic rails, as the Visa and Cambodia MOU shows.
- Both stories are at an early stage, a stated intention in one case and an exploratory MOU in the other, so execution details remain open.

## What to Watch

Watch for any bill text, committee scheduling or issuer response once the new Congress is seated. Also watch for follow-on detail on the Visa and National Bank of Cambodia QR work, such as pilots or merchant coverage.
