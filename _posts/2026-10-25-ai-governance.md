# AI Governance Brief: Texas Sues OpenAI Over ChatGPT Safety Claims

**Today:** Texas takes OpenAI to court over ChatGPT safety, Pennsylvania adds an AI ad-label law, and US bank supervisors ask lenders how they would stop an AI agent.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Texas Sues OpenAI and Altman, Seeking Court-Approved Review of New Models**
*Dallas Express · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Texas is asking a state court to put OpenAI's new models behind an independent, court-approved review, using consumer-protection and child-safety law.

Attorney General Ken Paxton filed a 67-page petition in Collin County on Friday against six OpenAI entities and CEO Sam Altman personally. It alleges ChatGPT was sold as safe and reliable without adequate warnings, in breach of the Texas consumer-protection statute and the SCOPE Act on children's online safety. Texas seeks more than $1 million in relief, including $10,000 per violation, restrictions on minors' access, and an independent evaluator who would review safeguards and approve new models before they reach Texas users. These are requests, not orders. OpenAI says it has worked with experts on safeguards, supports industrywide safety standards and will work with Texas and other states.

**Why it matters:** Even as a request, pre-release approval would turn model launches into a legal risk for every frontier vendor. Enterprises in Asia that build on OpenAI should check vendor-risk and continuity terms in their contracts.

### 2. 🏦 **Pennsylvania Enacts Label Requirement for Misleading AI-Generated Ads**
*WebProNews · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Pennsylvania will require a label on AI-generated or heavily AI-edited ads that could mislead buyers, effective Dec. 21, and the law has no knowledge requirement.

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed Senate Bill 806 (Act 99 of 2026) on Thursday. A label is required when synthetic content creates a false impression of what is being sold. It must say the ad contains content modified or generated using AI, appear when the content is first shown, and run in the same medium. Fictional content, parody, satire, commentary and text-only ads are exempt. A June amendment removed the "knowingly, recklessly or willfully" standard, and the trade group NetChoice objected that this comes close to strict liability. The attorney general and the state's 67 district attorneys enforce the law, courts can add fines of up to $3,000, and there is no private right of action.

**Why it matters:** Per the article, New York's synthetic-performer law uses a different test: actual knowledge, with audio ads exempt. Fintech and payments brands running AI-generated creative in US campaigns now face a state-by-state patchwork. Agency and vendor-produced assets may need labeling checks.

### 3. 🏦 **US Bank Regulators Press Lenders on Auditing and Stopping AI Agents**
*ComplexDiscovery · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US supervisors are informally asking banks whether they can audit what AI agents do and shut them down, while formal model-risk guidance still excludes agentic AI.

Reuters first reported on Oct. 13 that the Fed, OCC and FDIC are putting questions to banks of different sizes. The questions cover limits on AI systems, who monitors and approves them, audit trails for agent decisions, and shutdown procedures. Sources described this as information gathering, not new expectations. The new element is that a promised request for information on model risk and AI had not appeared in the Federal Register as of Oct. 24. April's revised model-risk guidance, SR 26-2, is nonbinding and carves out generative and agentic AI. In a Wolters Kluwer survey of 230 US banking professionals, 34% named kill-switch protocols as their least-prepared incident-response area.

**Why it matters:** Agent inventories, authority limits, tested shutdown and retained logs are practical readiness checks for any regulated bank or payments firm deploying agents, in any jurisdiction. The article also notes that agent logs can be sought in litigation.

## Key Takeaways
- US action is coming from states and supervisors rather than federal statute: Texas through litigation, Pennsylvania through legislation, and bank regulators through informal inquiry.
- Accountability is shifting toward provable control: labels on content, independent review before release, and audit trails and shutdown capability for agents.
- Liability standards are tightening. Pennsylvania dropped its knowledge requirement, and Texas names a chief executive personally.

## What to Watch
Comments on the US agencies' proposed third-party risk management guidance are due Nov. 16, and the promised AI request for information on model risk is still pending. Pennsylvania's ad-label rule takes effect Dec. 21.
