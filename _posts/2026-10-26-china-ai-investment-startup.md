# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: DeepSeek Draws $15 Billion From Chinese Backers

**Today:** China's AI money is moving in opposite directions: private capital is piling into DeepSeek, while public investors are marking down the listed model makers.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **DeepSeek Reportedly Raises About $15 Billion, With Post-Money Value Near $90 Billion**
*The Tech Capital · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DeepSeek has reportedly pulled in roughly 100 billion yuan (US$14.98 billion), about double its original target, which puts it among China's largest private AI financings.

Tencent and CATL are among more than 15 reported investors, and the deal could value the Hangzhou-based developer at about US$90 billion after the new money. The round comes ahead of a possible stock market listing and only days after new US federal restrictions targeting the company. The figures are reported and have not been confirmed by DeepSeek.

**Why it matters:** The round sets a private valuation marker for Chinese frontier labs, and it shows domestic strategic and industrial capital can fund a top lab without US investors. For Asia-based enterprises using DeepSeek's open-weight models, a well-funded vendor lowers continuity risk. Because US measures against the company are growing, the jurisdictional and compliance exposure still needs separate review.

### 2. 📊 **MiniMax Falls About 10% and Zhipu Over 5% in Hong Kong**
*Investing.com · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's two listed large-model developers fell sharply on Monday even though the Hang Seng index gained, which suggests investors are repricing the sector on fundamentals.

MiniMax dropped 9.8% to HK$169.2 intraday. Investors questioned the quality of its October M3.1 Flash release, which cut usage costs by 70% but showed a high hallucination rate. Zhipu AI also fell more than 5%. Earlier in the week MiniMax had briefly traded below its Hong Kong IPO price amid a deepening price war and a broader tech sell-off.

**Why it matters:** Public markets are now discounting Chinese model makers that compete mainly on price. That could weigh on IPO valuations for private peers lining up Hong Kong listings. Enterprise buyers should expect aggressive pricing to continue, and should weigh that against model quality and the vendor's financial durability.

## Key Takeaways
- Private and public valuations are diverging. Strategic investors are paying up for the leading lab, while listed peers are being marked down on quality and pricing worries.
- Price cuts are no longer enough to support a valuation. Investors now want evidence of model quality and a path to sustainable margins.
- Domestic corporates and state-linked capital, rather than global VCs, are now the main source of funding for China's frontier AI.

## What to Watch
Watch for formal confirmation that DeepSeek's round has closed and for any listing filing, which would publish its first audited numbers. Also watch whether MiniMax holds above its HK$165 IPO price as the model price war continues.
