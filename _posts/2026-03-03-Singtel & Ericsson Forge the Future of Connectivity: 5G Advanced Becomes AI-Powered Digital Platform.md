# **Singtel & Ericsson Forge the Future of Connectivity: 5G Advanced Becomes AI-Powered Digital Platform**

In a bold step that highlights how telecommunications is shifting from mere connectivity toward intelligent digital services, **Singtel** and **Ericsson** have announced a strategic collaboration to evolve **5G Advanced** into an **AI-powered digital platform** — transforming the way networks serve consumers, enterprises and the broader digital economy. ([Tech in Asia][1])

At the heart of the partnership is the vision to modernise next-generation mobile infrastructure. Rather than simply providing faster internet, the new platform promises **programmability, automation, and on-demand optimisation** — traits that pave the way for real-time artificial intelligence (AI) workloads, immersive applications like augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR), and a new class of enterprise services that thrive on low latency and dynamic network behaviour. ([TelecomLead][2])

This initiative aligns with broader trends in telecom — including global efforts to embed AI throughout network operations and edge computing infrastructures — which analysts see as critical for unlocking monetisation opportunities beyond consumer mobile subscriptions. Telecom operators worldwide are racing to shift from pure connectivity providers to **digital services platforms**, a necessary evolution as traditional 5G monetisation challenges persist. ([Developing Telecoms][3])

## **Why This Matters**

1. **AI & Programmability**: Evolving 5G Advanced into a programmable, AI-driven platform means networks can automatically allocate resources, optimise performance and adapt in real time to traffic demands. This isn’t static connectivity — it’s an intelligent system that can autonomously manage itself and tailor service quality based on business needs and performance objectives. ([ericsson.com][4])

2. **Enterprise-Ready Infrastructure**: The enhanced platform is designed to support enterprise use cases — from large-scale IoT deployments and industrial automation, to immersive AR/VR experiences and mission-critical operations. This positions carriers like Singtel as strategic digital partners for industries beyond mobile broadband. ([Telecompaper][5])

3. **Ecosystem Momentum**: The initiative also builds on industry developments such as collaborations among infrastructure players — for example, Ericsson’s broader partnerships aimed at accelerating AI-native networks and the path toward future **6G** networks — further signalling where global telecom is heading. ([Newsroom][6])

## **What to Watch Next**

* **Commercial rollouts and developer toolkits** for programmable network features.
* **Potential enterprise services and new business models** leveraging intent-driven networking.
* **Interplay with broader AI and edge compute developments**, including partnerships like those between infrastructure vendors and AI/compute leaders.

## **Glossary**

**5G Advanced** – The next evolutionary phase of 5G defined by 3GPP standards, introducing enhanced performance, efficiency and new capabilities such as AI integrations and improved network automation. ([ericsson.com][4])
**Programmable Network** – A network that operators can dynamically configure and optimise through software, enabling flexible service delivery and resource allocation. ([ericsson.com][4])
**AI-Powered Platform** – A system where artificial intelligence is used to automatically manage, optimise, and adapt network behaviour in real time to achieve performance and efficiency goals. ([ericsson.com][4])
**Latency** – The time it takes for data to travel from source to destination; low latency is critical for real-time applications like autonomous systems and AR/VR.
**Edge Computing** – Distributed computing at the edge of the network closer to end users, reducing latency and enabling real-time data processing for advanced applications.
