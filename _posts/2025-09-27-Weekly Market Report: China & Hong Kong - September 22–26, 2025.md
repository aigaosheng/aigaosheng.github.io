**Weekly Market Report: China & Hong Kong (September 22–26, 2025)**

---

### Executive Summary

The markets in China and Hong Kong experienced a pullback this week, ending a streak of gains. The Hang Seng Index declined 1.6%, marking its first weekly loss in September, while mainland indices also retreated. Investor sentiment turned cautious amid concerns over stretched valuations and renewed tariff threats from the U.S. Notably, biotech stocks like Wuxi Biologics and Wuxi AppTec fell sharply following the announcement of a 100% tariff on patented pharmaceuticals. Tech stocks such as Xiaomi and Alibaba also faced pressure. Social media discussions reflected a mix of anxiety and opportunistic optimism, with many investors debating the timing of a potential market bottom.

---

### Detailed Analysis

#### Market Movements

* **Hong Kong**: The Hang Seng Index closed at 26,128.20, down 1.4% on Friday and 1.6% for the week. The Hang Seng Tech Index fell 2.9%. Xiaomi's stock dropped 8.1% after a strong initial surge earlier in the week. Biotech firms Wuxi Biologics and Wuxi AppTec saw declines of 2.4% and 3.3%, respectively, due to new U.S. tariffs on patented pharmaceuticals. ([South China Morning Post][1])

* **Mainland China**: The CSI 300 Index declined 1% and the Shanghai Composite Index fell 0.7%. Investors are awaiting policy signals from the upcoming Communist Party plenum in October. ([South China Morning Post][1])

#### Sector Performance

* **Biotech**: The sector was hit hard by the U.S. tariff announcement, with Wuxi Biologics and Wuxi AppTec leading the declines.

* **Technology**: Xiaomi's stock tumbled 8.1% despite a new product launch, indicating investor skepticism. Alibaba and Tencent also saw declines, reflecting broader market concerns.

* **Consumer Goods**: Li Ning's stock rose 8.8% after reporting strong first-half net income, outperforming expectations. ([South China Morning Post][2])

#### Social Media Sentiment

* **Weibo & Zhihu**: Discussions centered on the impact of U.S. tariffs and the timing of the market bottom. Many investors expressed caution, while others viewed the dip as a buying opportunity.

* **X/Twitter**: Analysts and investors highlighted the disconnect between market enthusiasm and underlying economic fundamentals. Some noted that the recent rally felt "stretched" and that a correction was overdue. ([South China Morning Post][1])

---

### Social Media Sentiment Summary

Investor sentiment this week was mixed. While some expressed concern over valuation levels and external risks like U.S. tariffs, others remained optimistic, viewing the market pullback as a potential entry point. The prevailing mood was one of cautious optimism, with many awaiting clearer policy guidance from the upcoming Communist Party plenum.
