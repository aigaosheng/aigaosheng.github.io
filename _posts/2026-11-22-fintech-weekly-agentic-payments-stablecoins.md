## 2. In-Depth Highlight

### Singapore's National AI Missions: Moving Financial AI from Experimentation to Infrastructure

[Singapore sets national AI missions for financial services](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48781/singapore-sets-national-ai-missions-for-financial-services)

Singapore's announcement of three national AI missions represents an important development in the relationship between financial services, artificial intelligence and public policy. Led by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) and supported by the Future of Finance Institute (FFI), the initiative focuses on financial crime prevention, improving access to business financing and enabling safe agentic transactions.

The participation of DBS, OCBC, Standard Chartered and UOB is particularly significant. Rather than requiring each institution to independently develop AI capabilities, the initiative aims to establish shared resources, including cross-institution synthetic datasets for scam detection. This could help address a persistent challenge in financial crime prevention: individual institutions often have incomplete visibility of fraudulent activity across the wider financial ecosystem.

Another important component is the proposed agentic money sandbox. Financial institutions will be able to experiment with AI agents conducting transactions using digital money, subject to defined permissions, transaction limits and safeguards. Potential applications include agent-initiated payments and treasury management.

For financial institutions, this creates an opportunity to move beyond conventional AI assistants towards autonomous financial workflows. However, it also introduces questions around transaction authorisation, accountability, identity verification, auditability and liability when an AI agent makes an incorrect decision.

For Singapore, the initiative could strengthen its competitive position as a regional centre for financial innovation. Its success will depend not simply on AI model capabilities, but on whether the supporting governance, payment infrastructure and institutional collaboration can make autonomous financial services safe enough for real-world deployment.

## 3. Market & Industry Insight

### Three Forces Converging to Reshape Financial Infrastructure

First, agentic commerce is moving towards practical implementation. OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet and Visa's expanded Agentic Ready programme illustrate two complementary approaches: consumer-facing AI interfaces and payment-network infrastructure. The next competitive battleground will involve securely connecting AI agents with merchants, payment credentials, identity systems and transaction authorisation.

The critical question is no longer whether an AI agent can initiate a purchase. It is whether financial institutions can establish reliable mechanisms to verify the agent, authenticate the user's intent, enforce spending limits and resolve disputes. Payment providers that establish these capabilities early could gain a meaningful advantage.

Second, regulated stablecoins are increasingly being explored as financial infrastructure rather than speculative investment instruments. The DBS-Circle partnership and World Bank-Citi-Circle initiative illustrate two different applications: institutional liquidity management and international development payments. The potential benefits include continuous settlement, improved transparency and reduced dependence on traditional banking hours. However, regulatory compliance, liquidity management, redemption guarantees and operational resilience remain essential.

Third, enterprise finance is becoming an increasingly attractive market for AI automation. Payhawk's funding demonstrates investor interest in combining corporate payments, expense management and AI-driven workflows. The opportunity extends beyond automating individual tasks towards integrating entire financial processes, from invoice processing and reconciliation to payment execution and financial reporting.

For Asia-Pacific fintech businesses, these developments suggest that the next generation of financial infrastructure will be defined by the convergence of AI, programmable money and trusted payment networks.

## 4. Company & Startup Spotlight

### Payhawk — Building an AI-Native Finance Operating System

[Goldman Sachs leads $280m Series C for Payhawk](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48775/goldman-sachs-leads-280m-series-c-for-payhawk)

Payhawk's $280 million Series C, led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives, values the company at $2 billion. Operating across 33 countries, the company intends to combine AI agents, payment infrastructure and enterprise governance into a unified finance platform.

The strategic significance is its focus on controlled financial execution rather than simply generating AI recommendations. This is an important distinction for businesses considering autonomous finance operations.

### Lockwood — Rethinking Digital Banking Around AI Agents

[Starling founder Anne Boden unveils Lockwood](https://www.fintechfutures.com/fintech-start-ups/starling-founder-anne-boden-unveils-new-banking-venture-lockwood)

Founded by former Starling CEO Anne Boden, Lockwood aims to give customers AI assistants capable of paying bills, cancelling subscriptions and managing routine financial activities within predefined limits.

Its planned EMI application in 2027 and banking launch in 2028 make it an early-stage proposition rather than an established competitor. Nevertheless, its approach raises an important strategic question: could the future primary interface for banking become an AI agent instead of a conventional mobile banking application?
