# Payments Weekly | 18 October 2026: Mastercard's Stablecoin Platform, Visa's China Clearing Approval and Ramp's $60B Valuation

---

## 1. Top Headlines

- [Mastercard and BVNK bring fiat and stablecoin payments together in global money platform](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48590/mastercard-and-bvnk-bring-fiat-and-stablecoin-payments-together-in-global-money-platform) (**Finextra**): Mastercard's first major launch since buying BVNK gives businesses one API for accounts that hold fiat and stablecoins side by side. It signals that card networks now treat stablecoins as core plumbing, not a side bet.

- [Visa lands PBOC approval to begin China card clearing business preparations](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/visa-lands-pboc-approval-to-begin-china-card-clearing-business-preparations) (**FinTech Futures**): Visa can start building a domestic clearing arm in China and has one year before it applies for a full licence. American Express and Mastercard already hold licences, so Visa is closing a long-standing gap.

- [Ramp hits $60bn valuation](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48591/ramp-hits-60bn-valuation) (**Finextra**): The corporate card and spend platform raised $1.85bn and reports $2bn in annualised revenue with positive cash flow. Investors are still paying up for payments businesses with a clear AI story.

- [Qivalis inches closer to stablecoin launch with EMI authorisation](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/qivalis-stablecoin-launch-emi-authorisation) (**FinTech Futures**): The bank-backed Dutch issuer can now issue euro e-money tokens under MiCA and plans to start within a month. Backing from 37 banks gives a euro stablecoin real distribution potential.

- [AI-native payments platform Movere launches](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48593/ai-native-payments-platform-movere-launches) (**Finextra**): The London startup says merchants can go live in as little as 15 minutes through AI agents using Model Context Protocol onboarding. Merchants also set their own routing rules, such as lowest cost or best approval odds.

- [Pepkor's $1.3bn fintech deal comes under shareholder scrutiny](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48569/pepkors-13bn-fintech-deal-comes-under-shareholder-scrutiny) (**Finextra**): Directors and investors object that shareholders had no vote on merging Flash with Shop2Shop, two platforms serving South Africa's informal traders. Exchange rules did not require one, but the row shows governance risk in payments M&A.

- [OCC fines American Express $350m](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/occ-fines-american-express-350m) (**FinTech Futures**): The US regulator alleges systemic failures in suspicious activity monitoring, including about $13bn of suspected trade-based money laundering that went unreported over a decade. It is a pointed reminder of what weak AML controls cost card issuers.

- [FCA grants Pliant UK EMI regulatory approval](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/fca-grants-pliant-uk-emi-regulatory-approval) (**FinTech Futures**): The German B2B fintech can now issue payment products to UK customers through its own regulated subsidiary. That means less reliance on partners in a key market.

- [National Bank of Bahrain partners TerraPay to expand international payments network](https://www.fintechfutures.com/cross-border-payments/nbb-partners-terrapay) (**FinTech Futures**): NBB will use its existing Swift link to reach TerraPay's payout network of bank accounts, wallets and local schemes. TerraPay has signed several banks this year, which points to demand for outsourced cross-border reach.

## 2. In-Depth Highlight

Mastercard has launched its Global Money Platform, the first major product from its $1.8bn acquisition of stablecoin infrastructure firm BVNK, [according to Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48590/mastercard-and-bvnk-bring-fiat-and-stablecoin-payments-together-in-global-money-platform). At its centre is a global money account where fiat and stablecoins sit side by side, with conversion between the two handled through a single API. Customers can spend stablecoin balances across Mastercard's acceptance network, and card issuing is available in more than 130 countries. Businesses can also use the platform to manage their own treasury, payments and settlement activity. The point is consolidation: banks, fintechs and enterprises can adopt one layer instead of stitching together separate providers for stablecoin custody, FX and card issuing. The open questions for regulated players are how supervisors will treat stablecoin balances held next to conventional money, and how quickly rivals respond.

## 3. Market & Industry Insight

Stablecoins are moving from the edge of payments into the infrastructure itself. Mastercard's move shows the card networks bundling digital dollars with conventional rails. Europe's answer is Qivalis, which now holds its [e-money licence from the Dutch central bank](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/qivalis-stablecoin-launch-emi-authorisation) and has the backing of 37 banks across 15 countries. The competitive question is shifting from whether stablecoins will be used in payments to which issuers and networks will own distribution.

Meanwhile, the industry's wallet is still open. In the [Finextra and ACI Worldwide Payments 2030 report](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48597/finextra--aci-worldwide-launch-global-survey-report-payments-2030-the-payoff-year), 43% of respondents are accelerating payments investment, against 22% pulling back, and 95% feel confident about hitting their 2030 goals. The survey covered [152 industry leaders](https://www.finextra.com/researcharticle/391/payments-2030-the-payoff-year) in May. Some 77% already run, or are moving towards, a unified payments platform, mainly to get better data and insight rather than cut costs. AI is also showing measurable value, most often in exceptions and investigations (43%) and ISO 20022 data enrichment (34%). Note that this is sponsored research, so treat the sentiment figures as directional.

## 4. Company & Startup Spotlight

**Ramp.** [Ramp closed a $1.85bn round led by Dragoneer](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48591/ramp-hits-60bn-valuation) at a $60bn valuation, up from $44bn at its June Series F. It began as a corporate card and now sells spend management and finance automation to more than 85,000 clients, including Visa, Uber and Shopify. Finextra reports $2bn in annualised revenue and positive cash flow, plus new AI tools such as a workspace called Glass and a dashboard for tracking AI token spending. It has also gained UK and Swedish payment licences through its [Billhop acquisition](https://www.fintechfutures.com/venture-capital-funding/ramp-reportedly-hits-60bn-valuation-in-1.85bn-funding-round). Readers should care because Ramp shows how issuing, payments and software can be bundled into one fast-growing platform.

**Pliant.** The Berlin-based B2B payments company has [secured a UK EMI licence](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/fca-grants-pliant-uk-emi-regulatory-approval) for its UK subsidiary. It already holds a Finnish e-money licence from August 2023 and, with its Mastercard partnership, issues commercial cards in 13 currencies across 32 countries. Direct authorisation lets it run UK programmes through its own regulated entity.

## 5. Regulatory & Policy Watch

- China's central bank has [cleared Visa to begin preparing](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/visa-lands-pboc-approval-to-begin-china-card-clearing-business-preparations) a domestic card clearing institution, starting a one-year window before a full licence application.

- The Dutch central bank has [authorised Qivalis as an e-money institution](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/qivalis-stablecoin-launch-emi-authorisation), clearing the way to issue euro tokens under the EU's MiCA crypto rules.

- The OCC's [$350m penalty and cease-and-desist order](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/occ-fines-american-express-350m) against American Express National Bank cites alleged BSA/AML programme deficiencies. The allegations have not been tested in court.
