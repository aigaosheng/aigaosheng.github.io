# AI Fintech Brief — 2026-09-25

**Today:** AI fintech is moving from experimentation toward governed infrastructure, with agentic commerce, bank-specific AI economics, fraud automation and regulatory controls emerging as the key themes.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Agentic commerce is becoming an infrastructure problem, not just an AI problem**

*PYMNTS · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The next bottleneck for agentic commerce is enabling AI agents to transact safely across merchants, banks and payment networks rather than simply making agents capable of shopping.

PYMNTS reports that Mastercard sees agentic commerce increasingly as a new interface for interacting with commerce rather than merely another sales channel. The infrastructure challenge now includes authorization, liability, payment execution and interoperability across the ecosystem.

Mastercard's fintech programs chief Sabrina Tharani expects early mainstream adoption to center on low-value, high-frequency purchases where consumers prioritize convenience and speed.

**Why it matters:** Payment infrastructure is becoming part of the AI-agent stack. Whoever controls authorization, identity, risk and settlement may become strategically important even when the AI agent itself is owned by another company.

### 2. 🤖 **DBS is optimizing AI economics by matching models to individual banking tasks**

*The Edge Singapore · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DBS is treating model selection as an operating-cost and governance decision, using smaller self-hosted models for routine workloads instead of defaulting to expensive frontier models.

DBS chief data and transformation officer Nimish Panchmatia said the bank is matching each task with the most cost-efficient technology capable of completing it safely. Routine workloads can be assigned to smaller open-source models hosted internally, while caching can reduce repeated model-processing costs.

The approach reflects a broader shift from measuring AI adoption by model size or token consumption toward measuring business value, unit economics and task-level performance.

**Why it matters:** For financial institutions, the emerging AI architecture is likely to be heterogeneous rather than model-centric: frontier models for complex reasoning, smaller models for routine tasks, and governance and data infrastructure connecting the two.

### 3. 🏦 **RBI deputy governor calls for AI governance before financial-services scale**

*The Financial Express · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's central bank is emphasizing governance, cybersecurity and resilience as prerequisites for scaling AI and digital financial services.

RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain said AI is expected to fundamentally change financial services, but argued that governance must precede scale. He also framed technology spending as a form of risk investment linked to operational continuity, confidence and financial stability.

The comments came at the SBI Banking & Economics Conclave and place AI deployment within the broader resilience requirements of increasingly digital banking and payment systems.

**Why it matters:** The regulatory direction is increasingly moving beyond model accuracy toward enterprise controls covering cybersecurity, resilience, accountability and operational risk—especially important as financial institutions move from copilots toward autonomous systems.

### 4. 🔒 **Fraud teams are shifting spending from people toward technology**

*PYMNTS · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** More than half of surveyed e-commerce merchants expect fraud-management staffing to remain flat or decline while nearly two-thirds plan to increase investment in fraud technology.

PYMNTS reports that 51% of global e-commerce merchants expect fraud-management staff spending to remain flat or decline, while 63% plan to increase investment in fraud-management technology. The report highlights growing demand for orchestration across identity checks, behavioral analytics, device intelligence, transaction scoring and payment routing.

The findings point toward a more integrated fraud stack rather than isolated point solutions.

**Why it matters:** AI-native fraud prevention is increasingly being evaluated as an operating-efficiency platform as well as a security capability. For payment companies, the opportunity is to combine real-time signals, automated investigation and decision orchestration while keeping human oversight for higher-risk cases.

### 5. 🤖 **Fintech infrastructure is being reshaped around AI as an economic infrastructure layer**

*FinTech Futures · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** AI is increasingly being viewed as infrastructure capable of changing productivity, labor markets and ultimately the financial strength of economies.

FinTech Futures' Sibos analysis compares the economic implications of AI with those of railways, emphasizing that the technology's impact will depend not only on adoption but also on how its benefits and transition costs are distributed. The article highlights Moody's efforts to develop frameworks for assessing AI's potential effects at sovereign level.

For financial institutions, this moves the AI discussion beyond individual use cases toward infrastructure, productivity and macroeconomic implications.

**Why it matters:** AI investment decisions may increasingly be treated like infrastructure allocation decisions, requiring institutions to consider productivity gains, capital requirements, workforce effects and long-term competitive positioning alongside conventional technology metrics.
