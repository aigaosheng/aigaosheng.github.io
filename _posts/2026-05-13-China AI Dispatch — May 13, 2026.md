**2. China Unveils First National Regulatory Framework for AI Agents**
- **Source**: BRICS Competition · May 13, 2026
- **Summary**: Three central Chinese agencies (CAC, NDRC, MIIT) jointly issued guidelines establishing a tiered governance system for AI agents. The framework prioritizes 19 specific application scenarios—from healthcare and finance to public transit—while implementing risk-based oversight. High-risk applications face stricter rules, while lower-risk tools rely on self-compliance.
- **Why It Matters**: This provides the regulatory certainty needed for enterprise adoption of agentic AI. The government's blessing of specific use-cases (like e-government and judiciary) acts as a market signal, directing capital and development towards state-prioritized verticals. 
- **URL**: [http://bricscompetition.org/news/china-introduces-national-ai-agent-tiered-oversight-system](http://bricscompetition.org/news/china-introduces-national-ai-agent-tiered-oversight-system)

**3. DeepSeek Optimizes Models for Huawei Chips, Accelerating "Decoupling"**
- **Source**: The Business Times · May 12, 2026
- **Summary**: DeepSeek announced its latest AI model has been optimized to run on Huawei chips for inference. While training still relies on Nvidia hardware, the move represents a significant step in building a domestic AI stack. The announcement is strategically timed ahead of the Trump-Xi summit, signaling that US export controls have not frozen China's AI progress.
- **Why It Matters**: This bifurcates the global AI supply chain. As Chinese models shift to domestic chips for inference, US semiconductor leverage diminishes. This forces global hardware startups to choose which stack to optimize for, potentially creating two incompatible AI ecosystems.

**4. China Fast-Tracks Comprehensive AI Law on 2026 Legislative Agenda**
- **Source**: MLex / Law360 · May 12, 2026
- **Summary**: China’s State Council has officially added comprehensive AI legislation to its 2026 legislative agenda. The plan targets governance of data, algorithms, computing power, and data property rights, alongside revisions to cybersecurity and technology import/export regulations.
- **Why It Matters**: The shift from guidelines to enforceable law will redefine compliance burdens for multinationals operating in China. The focus on "data property rights" and "supply-chain security" suggests future regulations will legally mandate domestic data processing and hardware sourcing.
