# AI Governance Brief: China Moves To Legislate Full AI Lifecycle

**Today:** Two separate developments show regulators moving from AI principles to enforceable detail, in China's new lifecycle rulebook and an EU court opinion that could reshape how AI chatbots use news content.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **China to Legislate Full AI Lifecycle, Naming DeepSeek and Alibaba**

*The Star (Bloomberg) · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's state planner is accelerating a comprehensive AI law covering data, computing power, algorithms and applications from development through deployment.

The National Development and Reform Commission said it will set safety standards and ethical guidelines spanning the full AI lifecycle, with rules reaching leading domestic developers such as DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and Alibaba Group. NDRC official Wang Ruomeng said developers and operators will be required to strengthen risk management and promptly disclose safety incidents and vulnerabilities, with a transition period and sector-specific regulatory sandboxes to ease compliance. The framework will also direct authorities to monitor AI's impact on employment and provide social safety nets for displaced workers.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the most comprehensive national AI frameworks proposed globally and signals Beijing is prepared to regulate its own frontier-model champions as closely as it has proposed restricting foreign access to Chinese AI exports. For fintechs and BaaS providers with China-linked AI vendors or data flows, incident-disclosure and risk-management obligations modeled here could foreshadow requirements regional regulators adopt next.

### 2. 🏦 **EU Court Adviser: Google Gemini May Infringe Press Copyright**

*Reuters (via Investing.com) · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** An Advocate General at the EU's top court said AI chatbots that reuse press publishers' content to generate answers may infringe copyright, even though training large language models on that content remains permitted.

In a case brought by Hungarian news site operator Like Company against Google, Advocate General Maciej Szpunar said that when a chatbot retrieves a publication and reuses it to generate a user-facing answer, the publisher's neighbouring right "may be infringed" under EU law "in certain circumstances." He distinguished this from text-and-data-mining exceptions that keep model training itself lawful, noting copyright protects a publication's specific expression rather than the underlying facts. The Court of Justice typically follows its Advocates General's opinions, with a binding ruling expected at a later date not yet specified.

**Why it matters:** A final ruling against Google would pressure AI search and chatbot products to license or attribute news content more explicitly across the EU, a precedent other jurisdictions' courts and regulators are likely to watch, including in Asia where several countries are still drafting their own AI and copyright frameworks.

## Key Takeaways

- Major AI jurisdictions are shifting from broad principles to specific, enforceable obligations: incident disclosure, lifecycle risk management and content-use liability.
- Regulatory attention is converging on how AI systems *use* third-party content and data, whether in training (generally permitted) or in generated outputs (increasingly contested).
- Asian regulators continue to track Western and Chinese precedents closely; outcomes in Beijing and Luxembourg will likely inform future guidance in Singapore, Hong Kong and elsewhere in the region.

## What to Watch

The EU Court of Justice has not set a date for its binding ruling in the Google Gemini copyright case; watch for the judgment following the Advocate General's opinion. Separately, China's draft AI lifecycle legislation and its related AI-technology export-control proposals remain open for public comment, with adoption targeted by the end of 2026.
