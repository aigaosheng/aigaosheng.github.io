### 1. **China’s Supreme Court Sets New Liability Rules for AI-Generated Content**

* **Source**: South China Morning Post · 8 September 2026
* **Summary**: China’s Supreme People’s Court has issued judicial guidance covering civil disputes involving AI, including deepfakes, voice cloning, hallucinations, privacy and consumer-related harms. The 24-article opinion applies existing legal frameworks rather than introducing a standalone AI law, assigning potential responsibility across developers, providers and users.
* **Why It Matters**: China is moving AI governance deeper into the judicial system, creating concrete liability expectations for AI companies and deployers. The approach also demonstrates how existing civil, copyright and data-protection laws can be adapted to regulate AI without waiting for a dedicated AI statute.

## Executive Takeaway

**AI governance is moving from principles to operational accountability.** Today’s developments show three distinct but converging trends: courts are assigning concrete liability for AI-generated harms, governments are developing practical risk-management and assurance mechanisms, and policymakers are beginning to govern AI infrastructure and emerging technology ecosystems.

The most important strategic shift is the growing focus on **agentic and autonomous AI**. Governance is increasingly concerned not only with what a model generates, but with **what an AI system is permitted to do, what resources it can access, who is accountable for its actions, and how those actions can be independently monitored and challenged**.
