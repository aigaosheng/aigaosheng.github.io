# AI for Society Brief: Most Tweens And Teens Now Use AI Chatbots

**Today:** Two new data points show AI's social footprint widening in opposite directions — mass youth adoption of chatbots is outpacing safety guardrails, while youth-built AI tools are starting to deliver measurable accessibility gains in Singapore.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **Nearly Two-Thirds of US Tweens and Teens Use AI Chatbots, Study Finds**

*NPR · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** New research finds AI chatbot use is now nearly universal among US 9-to-17-year-olds, and companionship-focused use is linked to greater loneliness over time.

A joint study by Common Sense Media and the JED Foundation, surveying roughly 5,500 children aged 9 to 17, found about 90% have used an AI chatbot, with nearly half of 9-to-12-year-olds doing so despite most platforms setting a minimum age of 13. By age 13, close to three-quarters of kids have used a chatbot. Fifty-eight percent use them for schoolwork help, and a similar share turn to them when sad, stressed, lonely or overwhelmed; researchers found middle schoolers who used chatbots for companionship grew lonelier over time.

**Why it matters:** The findings widen the gap between consumer adoption and age-verification or safety design at chatbot makers, a pressure point for any enterprise building consumer-facing conversational AI, including in Singapore and the wider region where youth chatbot use is also rising fast alongside school AI rollouts.

### 2. 👥 **Singapore Student Team Wins Huawei AI Prize for Accessibility App**

*The Tech Revolutionist · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A student-built, voice-first AI assistant that helps blind and motor-impaired users operate smartphones took the top prize at Huawei's largest-ever youth AI competition in Singapore.

Team Sunday, students from the Singapore Institute of Technology and Singapore University of Technology and Design, won S$16,000 at the fifth Huawei Tech4City Competition, developed with the Singapore Association of the Visually Handicapped. In testing with 73 users, the app cut task-completion time from about 20 minutes to 5 and let users finish 45% more tasks without human help, using voice commands, descriptive narration, vibration cues and a guardrail blocking in-app payments. The 2026 edition drew 240 entries from more than 15 institutions, double the prior year, under the theme "Fueled by AI, Powered for Public Good."

**Why it matters:** It is a concrete, measured example of generative AI closing an accessibility gap rather than just promising to, and underscores Singapore's role as a testbed where corporate-backed youth innovation programmes turn into deployable public-good tools.

## Key Takeaways

- Youth AI chatbot adoption is running years ahead of platform age limits and safety guardrails, with companionship use showing a measurable link to loneliness.
- Singapore is emerging as a proving ground for applied accessibility AI, with youth-led teams delivering quantified gains for underserved users rather than concept demos.
- Both stories point to the same gap: AI's social benefits and risks are being documented by researchers and competitions faster than platforms and institutions are building formal safeguards or scaling the fixes.

## What to Watch

Watch for any platform or school-policy response to the Common Sense Media/JED Foundation findings, and whether Team Sunday's "Sunday" accessibility app proceeds to its planned public launch on the Google Play Store.
