# Quantum Brief: Alice & Bob Targets 200 Logical Qubits

**Today:** Hardware vendors are tightening their fault-tolerance timelines and packaging upgrade paths, while telecom and software players push quantum-linked security and tooling toward real deployments, which gives enterprise and financial-sector technology leaders more concrete planning inputs.

## Top Stories

### 1. ⚛️ **Alice & Bob Accelerates Roadmap to 200 Logical Qubits by 2030**

*Alice & Bob · November 18, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Alice & Bob says its cat-qubit architecture could deliver 200 logical qubits as soon as 2030, twice its earlier target, using far fewer physical qubits than conventional approaches.

The Paris and Boston company published an updated modular roadmap that centres on a machine called Carbon, which it plans to build from 3,600 physical cat qubits. Milestones run through 2034, and the company credits a new modular architecture, recent experimental results and expanded manufacturing capacity. The company also repeated its earlier claim that RSA-2048 could be broken with 40 times fewer qubits than previously estimated.

**Why it matters:** These are company targets, not delivered hardware, but shrinking qubit overheads feed directly into how banks and payment operators judge the timing of cryptographic migration. Asian institutions with long-lived data should treat vendor roadmaps like this as inputs to, not replacements for, their own quantum-risk assessments.

### 2. ⚛️ **IQM Unveils Halocene Upgrade Path Beyond 1,000 Qubits**

*IQM Quantum Computers · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IQM is offering customers of its Halocene error-correction line a route from today's 150-qubit system to a future machine with more than 1,000 qubits, without downtime between generations.

Announced at the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago, the programme has IQM install each new Halocene system while the existing one keeps running, then remove the older unit once the new one is live. IQM's HPC integration service, which connects systems to a centre's existing schedulers and GPU platforms, is included with every upgrade. The company cited Finland's LUMI AI Factory, which has chosen Halocene for its LUMI-IQ system, as a customer.

**Why it matters:** Concern that quantum hardware bought today will be obsolete within a few years is a real procurement barrier, and a defined upgrade path addresses it directly. The emphasis on hybrid operation alongside AI and HPC systems also shows where vendors expect near-term enterprise value.

### 3. 🔒 **Telia, Nokia and QMill Demo Quantum-Powered Encryption in 5G**

*The Quantum Insider (company press release) · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Telia Finland, Nokia and QMill say they have run a proof of concept that adds a quantum-generated layer on top of standard post-quantum cryptography in a live 5G environment using commercial equipment.

QMill's solution was built into Nokia's 5G base-station software and demonstrated in Telia's 5G innovation environment. It pairs a new quantum-powered key exchange with user-verifiable quantum random numbers, and was used to secure the link between a base station and the network's security gateway. The companies say it went from concept to working proof of concept in under 90 days and are now exploring use cases toward commercialisation. The "first" framing is the companies' own claim.

**Why it matters:** Telecom networks carry much of the traffic behind payments and banking, so layered quantum-safe designs there matter to financial-sector risk teams. For now this remains a demonstration, and standards-based post-quantum cryptography is still positioned as the foundation.

### 4. 📊 **Planckian Wins €10 Million EIC Package for Superconducting Chips**

*Quantum Computing Report · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Italian hardware developer Planckian has been selected for a €10 million blended financing package from the European Innovation Council to scale its shared-control superconducting processors.

The package combines a €2.5 million grant for a 24-month development programme with a planned €7.5 million equity investment from the EIC Fund, timed with its upcoming Series A and subject to due diligence and grant agreement finalisation. Planckian was one of 35 companies chosen from 453 full applications. Its approach lets qubits share control lines, targeting the wiring, thermal and cost bottlenecks of conventional superconducting designs.

**Why it matters:** Public blended finance is becoming a meaningful route for deep-tech hardware startups ahead of venture rounds, and wiring bottlenecks are one of the practical limits on scaling. Investors in Asia comparing hardware approaches may find the control-line reduction a useful differentiator to watch.

### 5. 🤖 **Zapata Quantum Opens ForShor.ai Challenge for Verified AI Quantum Research**

*The Quantum Insider (company press release) · November 18, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Zapata Quantum and the University of Maryland have launched an open challenge that uses a formally verified version of Shor's algorithm to test whether AI-generated quantum research can be proven correct and explained.

Participants can submit AI-generated solutions to a leaderboard, and contributors may become co-authors of a later research paper. Peter Shor, the algorithm's creator, recently joined Zapata's scientific advisory board and endorsed the approach. Zapata says the framework is meant to extend beyond Shor's algorithm to other quantum algorithms.

**Why it matters:** As AI tools begin to propose quantum algorithms, verification becomes the bottleneck for trusting results, a concern for any enterprise relying on them. This is an early, company-led initiative, so its influence on wider practice remains to be shown.

### 6. ⚛️ **SAXON Q Unveils Quantum Watchdog Fault-Detection System**

*The Quantum Insider (company press release) · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** SAXON Q has introduced a patented safety system that monitors a quantum computer at runtime and puts it into a safe mode if it malfunctions.

The watchdog runs on its own classical processor and periodically swaps a user's calculation for a test problem with a known answer, flagging failure if errors exceed expected levels. It also tracks power draw, temperature and magnetic field, and uses comparison or majority voting across multiple processors. Each system is custom-built, and the company positions it for settings such as robotics, health care, automotive and defence.

**Why it matters:** Runtime integrity monitoring is a gap as quantum machines move from labs toward regulated, safety-relevant uses. Financial and critical-infrastructure users will likely ask similar assurance questions of any quantum service they adopt.

### 7. 💳 **QUDORA Adds NVIDIA CUDA-Q to Its Quantum SDK**

*The Quantum Insider (company press release) · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** German ion-trap developer QUDORA now lets developers write hybrid quantum-classical programs with NVIDIA CUDA-Q and test them on its noise-aware emulator ahead of hardware availability.

The integration runs on Qamelion, QUDORA's emulator, which models its upcoming trapped-ion hardware under realistic noise. It is available now through the QUDORA SDK and QUDORA Cloud. The company notes that performance on physical hardware will still need to be evaluated once systems are available.

**Why it matters:** CUDA-Q is becoming a common bridge between GPU-centred AI infrastructure and quantum hardware, lowering switching costs for teams prototyping today. It also lets enterprise teams build skills before committing to a specific hardware platform.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/17/qudora-integrates-nvidia-cuda-q-quantum-software-development-kit/)

## Key Takeaways

- Vendors are competing on timeline credibility and upgradeability, with Alice & Bob and IQM both framing their roadmaps around reducing qubit overheads and protecting customer investment.
- Hybrid quantum-GPU-HPC integration is the common thread in this cycle, from IQM's HPC service to QUDORA's CUDA-Q support, signalling that near-term value is expected alongside AI infrastructure.
- Security work is shifting from migration advice to layered designs and assurance, as seen in the Telia, Nokia and QMill demo and SAXON Q's runtime monitoring; most claims remain company-reported proofs of concept.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Alice & Bob's roadmap milestones, such as its planned 400-cat-qubit processor, show up in independent technical results, and whether Telia, Nokia and QMill name concrete commercial use cases. Also track Planckian's Series A and the finalisation of its EIC grant agreement.
