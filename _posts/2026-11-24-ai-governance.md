# AI Governance Brief: Australia Maps AI Governance Reforms

**Today:** Australia’s parliamentary recommendations and new regulator guidance in the UK show how existing and proposed rules are starting to address AI agents and broader AI risks.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Australia Committee Proposes 46 AI Reforms**

*ABC News · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** An Australian parliamentary committee has recommended reforms spanning AI oversight, infrastructure, safety and copyright, but the recommendations are not law.

The Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence released a report with 46 recommendations, including a proposed payment mechanism for Australian creators whose work trains AI models. It also called for data centre licensing and monitoring and reporting obligations for AI-related incidents. The committee did not agree on what energy sources data centres should use.

**Why it matters:** The proposals point to a broader Australian policy agenda for AI developers and infrastructure operators, while leaving the government to decide which recommendations to pursue. For businesses, the report is a signal of possible future legislation, not a change in current obligations.

### 2. 🏦 **Australia Agrees Digital Duty Of Care Changes**

*AP News · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Australia’s government has agreed to amendments to its proposed Digital Duty of Care Bill, which covers services including AI chatbots.

The amendments include a private right of action and maximum penalties of up to 5% of a company’s global revenue, according to AP. The bill would require online services to reduce foreseeable harms; it had not yet passed Parliament at the time of publication. The government expected further Senate and House votes later in the week.

**Why it matters:** If enacted, the bill would create additional online safety obligations for services that include AI chatbots. Companies should track the final text and assess how its scope and enforcement provisions apply to their products.

### 3. 🏦 **FCA Publishes Guidance For AI Agent Users**

*Financial Conduct Authority · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The UK FCA has advised consumers to understand an AI agent’s permissions, data access and protections before allowing it to act on their behalf.

The regulator’s new guidance highlights the potential for agents to find, compare and manage financial products, as well as make transactions. It advises users to consider how much decision-making authority to grant, what information to share and what recourse may be available if something goes wrong.

**Why it matters:** The guidance puts consumer control and clarity in focus as agents enter financial journeys. Firms building agent-enabled services can expect customers and regulators to scrutinise permissions, disclosures and support when automated actions fail.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/using-ai-agents)

### 4. 🏦 **ICO Sets Privacy Expectations For Consumer AI Agents**

*Information Commissioner’s Office · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The UK’s ICO says organisations developing, deploying or integrating consumer-facing AI agents must meet existing data protection obligations and build privacy protections into their services.

The regulator points to risks from changing data permissions and unclear information about the actions an agent can take. It says its call for evidence will inform guidance due in spring 2027, and that it is engaging with firms through the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum. The ICO says it will act where organisations rush products to market without adequate privacy protections and user controls.

**Why it matters:** Agent deployments that access personal data or third-party accounts need clear permissions, meaningful transparency and ongoing risk controls. Singapore and other markets may also watch how the UK applies existing privacy law to autonomous consumer services.

### 5. 🏦 **South African MPs Weigh A Dedicated AI Act**

*TechCentral · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A position paper presented to South Africa’s communications committee proposes a dedicated AI Act, while the government’s national AI policy redraft remains delayed.

The paper proposes risk-based regulation, governance duties for AI builders and deployers, human oversight of significant decisions, and independent audits for high-impact systems. It also suggests coordinating existing regulators through an AI Regulators Forum. These are proposals for committee consideration, not an agreed government position.

**Why it matters:** The proposals could shape future obligations around AI accountability and auditability in South Africa. Businesses operating across African markets should monitor whether policymakers advance the paper into formal legislation or policy.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://techcentral.co.za/officials-mps-dark-south-africa-ai-policy/288639/)

## Key Takeaways

- Australia’s committee report sets out a wide range of possible AI reforms, but the government must decide what becomes policy or law.
- Regulators are applying existing obligations to agentic systems, with permissions, user control and accountability emerging as practical priorities.
- South Africa’s proposed AI Act remains at an early discussion stage, with no agreed framework yet.

## What to Watch

Track the Australian Senate and House votes on the Digital Duty of Care Bill this week, and whether the government follows the committee report with draft AI standards early next year. In South Africa, watch whether the revised national AI policy advances from the ministerial submission target of November 30 toward Cabinet consideration in March 2027.
