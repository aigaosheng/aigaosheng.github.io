# AI finance in Singapore Brief — 2026-09-21

## Top Stories 

### 1. **MAS: AI adoption is moving from pilots to scaled deployment across Singapore finance**

* **Source**: Bank for International Settlements · 21 September 2026
* **Summary**: Monetary Authority of Singapore Managing Director Chia Der Jiun said AI adoption in Singapore's financial sector has moved beyond pilots and is being deployed at scale. He identified fraud detection, credit underwriting, risk management, regulatory compliance, marketing, customer service and document processing as established use cases. MAS's focus for large institutions is increasingly on safety, guardrails and accountability rather than simply encouraging experimentation.
* **Why It Matters**: Singapore's financial-sector AI agenda is shifting from proof-of-concept activity toward operational deployment and governance. For banks and fintechs, the strategic challenge is increasingly how to scale AI safely while extending productivity gains beyond the largest institutions.

### 2. **Singapore investors are using AI extensively, but retain a human decision layer**

* **Source**: The Business Times · 21 September 2026
* **Summary**: A Business Times discussion with HSBC Singapore's Ishan Sarkar highlighted a large gap between AI usage and willingness to delegate investment decisions. Seventy-six per cent of Singapore investors surveyed by HSBC use AI for investment-related analysis, while only 8 per cent said AI was the most influential source in their most recent major investment decision. The findings point toward AI-assisted rather than fully autonomous wealth management.
* **Why It Matters**: The Singapore wealth-management opportunity is increasingly about augmenting advisers rather than replacing them. AI can compress research and analysis workflows, while trust, suitability, judgement and accountability remain important components of the client decision process.
* **URL**: [https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/podcasts/ask-vs-answer-human-edge-ai-cant-replicate/](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/podcasts/ask-vs-answer-human-edge-ai-cant-replicate/)

### 3. **AI-driven financial crime defence attracts $25 million funding round**

* **Source**: FinTech Global · 21 September 2026
* **Summary**: Financial-crime technology company Footprint raised US$25 million in Series B funding led by QED Investors, with participation from MUFG and other investors. The company is developing an agentic AI platform designed to automate financial-crime compliance investigations and standard operating procedures. Its Percy platform is positioned as an AI operating system for financial institutions, supported by governed infrastructure for risk and compliance workloads.
* **Why It Matters**: The funding illustrates where AI is moving next in financial services: from detection models and fixed rules toward agentic investigation workflows. For Singapore's regulated banks and payment firms, this direction is particularly relevant to AML, transaction monitoring, KYC/KYB and analyst productivity.
* **URL**: [https://fintech.global/2026/09/21/footprint-bags-25m-to-arm-banks-against-ai-driven-crime/](https://fintech.global/2026/09/21/footprint-bags-25m-to-arm-banks-against-ai-driven-crime/)

### 4. **Singapore stablecoin payments firm dtcpay expands Series A to $25 million**

* **Source**: Blockhead · 21 September 2026
* **Summary**: Singapore-based digital-payments company dtcpay expanded its Series A financing to US$25 million after Japan's SBI Group joined as a strategic investor. Combined with AI-enabled compliance and payment operations, programmable digital money is becoming part of a broader technology stack for next-generation financial services.

### 5. **Singapore-founded Aolani partners with NVIDIA to expand AI compute infrastructure**

* **Source**: ACCESS Newswire · 21 September 2026
* **Summary**: Singapore-founded AI cloud company Aolani announced a strategic compute collaboration with NVIDIA covering AI infrastructure across Southeast Asia. The company said the collaboration will bring its total planned AI-factory capacity above 100 MW, with 22,000 GPUs expected across facilities in Malaysia and the Philippines in early 2027. The company also described financing mechanisms intended to broaden access to AI compute for enterprises, sovereigns and AI-native companies.
* **Why It Matters**: Although not a financial-services product, the development is relevant to Singapore's AI-finance ecosystem because access to scalable compute is becoming an economic and infrastructure constraint for regulated institutions and fintechs deploying larger AI workloads. Singapore-based AI infrastructure providers can also play a regional role as financial institutions expand AI beyond experimentation.

## Executive Takeaways

* **AI in Singapore finance is entering the deployment phase.** MAS says major financial institutions are already deploying AI at scale across fraud, credit, risk, compliance and customer operations.
* **Human-in-the-loop remains central in wealth management.** High AI usage among Singapore investors has not translated into broad willingness to delegate major investment decisions to AI.
* **Agentic compliance is becoming investable infrastructure.** New funding for AI-native financial-crime platforms signals growing investor confidence in autonomous investigation and compliance workflows.
* **Digital-asset infrastructure continues to attract strategic capital.** dtcpay's expanded Series A highlights continued institutional interest in Singapore-based stablecoin payment infrastructure.
* **Compute is becoming part of the financial-AI stack.** Singapore's emerging role in regional AI infrastructure could support the next wave of enterprise and financial-services AI deployment.
