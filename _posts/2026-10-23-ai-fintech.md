# AI Fintech Brief: US Banks Block Outside AI Agents From Accounts

**Today:** Big US banks are drawing a hard line on third-party AI agents, while vendors, insurers and lenders race to build the governed rails and credit models agents will need.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **JPMorgan, Citi, Bank of America and Schwab Restrict Outside AI Agents From Customer Accounts**
*FinTech Weekly · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Large US banks are not rejecting AI; they are refusing uncontrolled agents that log in with customer credentials and can move money.
An analysis piece reports that, within a single week in mid-October, JPMorgan Chase, Citigroup, Bank of America and Charles Schwab moved to limit external AI agents. Read-only access through approved connectors remains possible, and Schwab plans to offer Model Context Protocol servers so a client's chosen AI model can check balances and run research without moving funds. The banks are also building their own assistants (Schwab's Charley, JPMorgan's Smart Cash pilot), while the article contrasts this with a July pilot in Germany by Worldline, ING and Visa in which an agent took part in a purchase without replacing customer authorisation. The article also notes that deposit retention is an unstated factor, citing about US$1.6 trillion in non-interest-bearing deposits across JPMorgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo.

**Why it matters:** The open questions are who authorises an agent's action and who bears the loss when it errs. Asia-based banks and BaaS providers designing agent access can expect the same split: wide read access, narrow and auditable permission to transact.

### 2. 🤖 **Sutherland and FlowX.AI Put 220+ AI Agents on Top of Legacy Bank Cores**
*PYMNTS · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Rather than replacing decades-old core systems, banks are being offered prebuilt agents that sit on top of them.
PYMNTS reports on the Sutherland and FlowX.AI partnership, announced October 19, under which Sutherland will deploy and run FlowX.AI's agents against banks' existing applications and data. Banking targets include onboarding, account opening, lending, mortgage underwriting, anti-money laundering checks and customer retention. FlowX.AI says one European commercial lender removed 80% of manual handoffs in credit workflows and another institution cut lending operating costs by 40%; neither is named, so the figures are unverified company claims. The piece also notes that Kastle raised a US$24 million Series A in September to build agents over existing cores, while Maximum is betting on replacement with a US$30 million seed round.

**Why it matters:** For mid-sized banks across Asia, an "action layer" over legacy systems is a lower-risk entry point than core replacement. The Kastle example of an agent that can process a collections payment but not reverse it shows how narrow permissions can be built in from the start.

### 3. 📊 **Marrow Raises £1.5 Million to Let AI Agents Quote and Buy Insurance on Insurers' Terms**
*Startupmag · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A UK start-up is betting that regulated insurance sales by AI agents need a protocol layer controlled by the insurer.
Marrow closed a pre-seed round led by Insurtech Gateway, with Seed X, Leo Capital, Portfolio Ventures and angels participating. Its platform lets insurers and brokers map pricing and policy systems once, then exposes products to any agent while recording what was shown to the customer. It entered production with Aviva in March, and its open AMI Standards, published in September, are under review by insurers and brokers across five lines of business. The article also cites Marrow-supplied data that 18.7% of UK home insurance shoppers used an AI tool, against 14.5% who used a broker.

**Why it matters:** Insurance is an early test of agent-led distribution, and insurers are weighing whether to block agents or govern them. A shared, open standard would reduce one-off integrations for insurers and distributors in any market.

### 4. 🤖 **Study Argues AI Credit Will Shift From Underwriting the Borrower to Underwriting the Purchase**
*PYMNTS · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A PYMNTS Intelligence and Thredd report argues that real-time data could let lenders price each transaction rather than a fixed borrower profile.
The latest "Future of AI Credit" report says creditworthiness is not constant: the same customer might suit a six-month instalment plan for a US$2,000 purchase but not an open-ended revolving balance. Credit would become a feature deployed when a transaction needs it, with structure, duration and potentially price varying by purchase. The report frames the opportunity as finding creditworthy borrowers inside categories the old system labels uncertain, rather than only rejecting risk faster. It is a conceptual research piece and gives no deployment figures.

**Why it matters:** For card issuers and BNPL providers in Asia, transaction-level decisions would change product design, disclosure and fairness testing. These are areas where regulators are likely to look closely, so lenders should treat it as a design direction rather than a ready product.

## Key Takeaways
- The agent debate has moved from capability to permission: who authorises, with what limits, and who is liable.
- Vendors and start-ups are selling governed agent layers over existing systems (bank cores, insurer pricing engines) rather than asking institutions to rebuild.
- Several headline results (80% fewer handoffs, 40% lower lending costs) come from unnamed clients or vendors and should be treated as claims, not benchmarks.

## What to Watch
Whether banks publish permissioned access routes for outside agents, such as Schwab's planned MCP servers, and whether Marrow's working group takes the AMI Standards to a first full version.
