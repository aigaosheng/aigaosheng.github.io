# Quantum Brief: Toshiba Launches Quantum-Safe Network Platform

**Today:** Commercial quantum communications reached multi-site scalability with Toshiba’s network platform launch, while critical milestones in mid-circuit error control and post-quantum payments illustrate the twin acceleration of quantum compute and cryptographic defense.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Toshiba Launches Scalable Quantum-Safe Networking Platform**

*The Quantum Insider · October 21, 2026*
**Bottom line:** Toshiba has launched an end-to-end commercial networking platform designed to scale quantum-secured communication across metro and enterprise fiber infrastructure.
Toshiba Corporation announced the rollout of its Quantum-Safe Networking (QSN) Platform, transitioning quantum key distribution (QKD) and post-quantum security from isolated point-to-point pilots into enterprise-wide deployments. The system integrates optical switching, shared-fiber architectures, and compact transceivers coordinated by an automated, software-defined QSN Controller, allowing a single operational hub to serve hundreds of enterprise endpoints. Andrew Shields, Vice President at Toshiba Europe and Head of its Quantum Technology Division, noted that the platform directly targets the industry's need to broaden quantum-safe protection efficiently across diverse corporate site typologies.
**Why it matters:** Regional financial institutions and telcos across Asia can now integrate quantum-safe key distribution across existing multi-tenant optical networks without paying for costly dedicated dark fiber for every node.

### 2. ⚛️ **Rutgers and IBM Achieve Mid-Circuit Control Across 100 Qubits**

*The Quantum Insider · October 21, 2026*
**Bottom line:** Researchers demonstrated dynamic, real-time quantum feedback across 100 qubits on commercial IBM hardware, proving that runtime resets can suppress quantum chaos.
A collaborative research initiative led by Rutgers University and IBM successfully executed real-time quantum feedback across a connected chain of up to 100 qubits using a 156-qubit IBM Quantum Heron processor. The experiment carried out nearly 5,000 two-qubit operations and roughly 5,000 measurements and resets while the processor continued running, verifying theoretical models that predict a sharp transition from chaos to stability when active resets outpace quantum scrambling. IBM researchers highlighted that the milestone proves current superconducting hardware can orchestrate repeated, mid-circuit stabilization at scale.
**Why it matters:** Real-time feedback and mid-circuit reset capabilities are the core operational requirements for active quantum error correction, bringing fault-tolerant enterprise simulation closer to practical reality.

### 3. 🤖 **Nvidia Simulates 104-Qubit Fault-Tolerant Processor on JUPITER Supercomputer**

*The Next Web · October 21, 2026*
**Bottom line:** Nvidia completed the world’s largest full simulation of an error-corrected quantum computer using Germany’s flagship exascale system.
Leveraging 8,192 GPUs on JUPITER—Europe’s premier exascale supercomputer located at the Jülich Supercomputing Centre—Nvidia executed a complete simulation of a 104-qubit fault-tolerant quantum computer. The simulation accurately modeled physical hardware noise and realistic circuit degradation, providing hardware engineers with granular data on how noise disrupts logical operations before fabricating physical chips. The computational milestone, unveiled ahead of Nvidia’s GTC Berlin conference, demonstrates how high-density classical GPU clusters are accelerating the physical roadmaps of quantum manufacturers.
**Why it matters:** High-performance emulation allows enterprise software developers to test quantum algorithms on realistic, noise-modeled logical qubits years before physical fault-tolerant machines are commercially deployed.

### 4. 🔒 **Black Kite Introduces Automated Vendor Auditing for Post-Quantum Readiness**

*PR Newswire · October 21, 2026*
**Bottom line:** Cyber risk intelligence vendor Black Kite has rolled out continuous third-party assessment tools to track enterprise supply chain exposure to quantum deadlines.
Black Kite launched "Quantum Readiness," an automated capability within its vendor risk management platform that evaluates external software supply chains against looming cryptographic sunset deadlines. The system measures vendor migration progress against mandatory milestones, including US Executive Order 14412, OMB M-26-15 deadlines (which require post-quantum key establishment by 2030 and digital signatures by 2031), and European NIS2 cryptographic obligations. The tool allows security teams to systematically identify which suppliers still rely on classical public-key cryptography and monitor remediation progress across critical external vendors.
**Why it matters:** Regulated financial institutions in Singapore and across Asia face increasing audit pressure to prove that their third-party software supply chains are moving toward quantum-safe cryptography ahead of regulatory cutoff dates.

### 5. 💳 **Splendor Labs Demonstrates Direct Post-Quantum NFC Payments on Mobile Devices**

*EIN Presswire · October 20, 2026*
**Bottom line:** Swiss startup Splendor Labs completed live demonstrations of direct, cardless point-of-sale payments protected by NIST-approved quantum-resistant algorithms.
Splendor Labs unveiled a peer-to-peer payment architecture that circumvents legacy credit card networks by turning consumer smartphones into points of sale using physical NFC security cards. Transactions settle directly over the Splendor Quantum Chain using a cryptographic stack that incorporates ML-DSA-65, ML-KEM-768, dual ML-DSA/SLH-DSA validation, and Random Linear Network Coding. The workflow combines QR code generation with physical card taps to deliver cryptographically authenticated authorizations for consumer checkout and autonomous agent transactions.
**Why it matters:** The architecture demonstrates how post-quantum digital signatures can be embedded directly into physical contactless payment rails, safeguarding retail and agentic commerce against future quantum decryption.

### 6. 🔒 **NowSecure Audit Finds 97% of Mobile Apps Lack Quantum-Safe Encryption**

*The Quantum Insider · October 20, 2026*
**Bottom line:** An extensive binary evaluation of over 136,000 consumer and enterprise mobile apps revealed near-total absence of post-quantum cryptography.
A security analysis conducted by NowSecure across 136,711 iOS and Android applications found that 96.9% contained zero detectable post-quantum cryptographic libraries or code. Alarmingly, the inspection revealed that more than 3,100 mobile apps continue to rely on long-deprecated classical algorithms including DES and RC4, exposing user transactions and intellectual property to immediate interception and harvest-now, decrypt-later risks. NowSecure emphasized that while enterprise servers often receive priority in migration budgets, client-facing mobile applications remain a critical, uninventoried attack surface.
**Why it matters:** Fintech mobile apps and digital wallets that neglect client-side cryptographic inventories face severe data exposure and compliance liabilities as regional regulators ramp up post-quantum transition benchmarks.
