# Quantum Brief: UK FCA Launches Quantum Group To Fight Fraud

**Today:** Regulators and quantum vendors both moved on the finance-quantum intersection, with the UK's FCA opening a formal channel to test quantum machine learning against fraud while IQM's results show paying customers, including one in Japan, are starting to show up.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **UK FCA To Trial Quantum Machine Learning Against Fraud and Money Laundering**

*The Quantum Insider · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The UK Financial Conduct Authority will launch a Quantum Experimentation Group in Q1 2027 to test whether quantum machine learning can improve detection of money laundering and fraud, using synthetic FCA data.

The FCA, which says this is the first initiative of its kind by a financial regulator, is opening registration to financial firms, quantum technology providers and national research labs, with applications due by December 31, 2026. The programme builds on the FCA's existing research into quantum's long-term role in financial services, separate from its earlier quantum-safe migration guidance for firms.

**Why it matters:** A regulator actively testing quantum-enhanced AML tooling signals growing confidence that quantum machine learning could move from theory to pilot use in financial crime detection within the next two to three years; Asian regulators, including MAS, have run comparable quantum-finance sandboxes and will likely watch the FCA's results closely.

### 2. 📊 **IQM Posts 51% Revenue Growth and €102.5 Million Order Backlog**

*The Quantum Insider · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Finland's IQM Quantum Computers grew Q3 2026 revenue 51% year-on-year to €8.9 million and booked €42.2 million in new orders, lifting its total order backlog to €102.5 million, up 52% since the start of the year.

The quarter's largest win was a €33 million contract tied to CSC's LUMI AI Factory for IQM's Halocene system, alongside the company's first South American sale and a second unit sold to Japan's TOYO Corporation. IQM reaffirmed full-year guidance of €65-75 million in order intake and €42-47 million in revenue, while posting a wider operating loss tied largely to one-time listing costs.

**Why it matters:** Recurring commercial orders from named customers, rather than grants or pilots, are the clearest signal yet that quantum hardware vendors are reaching paying enterprise and research buyers; the repeat Japan sale underscores Asia-Pacific as an active early market for on-premise quantum systems.

### 3. 🔒 **Orange Joins Quantum-Safe eSIM Provisioning Programme With Acceleronix and IDEMIA**

*The Quantum Insider · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Orange has joined an In-Factory Profile Provisioning programme from Acceleronix and IDEMIA Secure Transactions that loads eSIM operator profiles onto IoT devices during manufacturing using quantum-safe credential binding.

The scheme, built on the GSMA SGP.41 framework, lets device makers use a single hardware design across markets instead of region-specific SIM variants, with devices connectable immediately after power-on. IDEMIA and Acceleronix plan a live demonstration at electronica 2026 in Munich from November 10-13, and trial kits are available to manufacturers now.

**Why it matters:** Embedding quantum-safe cryptography into IoT and connected-device supply chains ahead of large-scale quantum threats is a practical, low-profile step toward crypto-agility that payments and telecom infrastructure providers in Asia will need to track as connected-device volumes grow.

### 4. 🔒 **Bitcoin Cash Developers Propose Quantum-Resistant Signature Path**

*The Quantum Insider · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Bitcoin Cash's developer community formalised CHIP-2026-06, a proposal adding an OP_SIGHASH opcode that would let the network support post-quantum and hybrid classical-quantum signature schemes without rewriting existing wallet infrastructure.

The design lets public keys select a signature algorithm from a registry, starting with the existing secp256k1 curve and later adding hash-based post-quantum schemes such as SPHINCS+ and Falcon-512; hybrid keys would require both classical and quantum-resistant checks to pass during a migration period. Proposers are targeting lock-in on November 15, 2026, with mainnet activation set for May 15, 2027.

**Why it matters:** The proposal is an early, concrete example of a cryptocurrency network engineering a gradual migration path against future quantum attacks on ECDSA signatures, a risk that also applies to custodial wallets and blockchain-based payment rails used across Asia.

### 5. ⚛️ **IonQ and QuantumCT Expand Trapped-Ion Quantum Access in Connecticut**

*The Quantum Insider · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IonQ and the state-backed QuantumCT initiative have agreed to give Connecticut universities, research labs, startups and enterprises access to more than 150 hours of cloud runtime on IonQ's trapped-ion quantum computers.

The partnership, anchored by the University of Connecticut and Yale University, targets quantum algorithms, optimisation, materials science and workforce development, and is framed by both parties as moving the state's quantum community from exploration toward applied use cases.

**Why it matters:** Regional, subsidised access programmes like this are becoming a standard way for quantum hardware vendors to seed future enterprise demand; Singapore's and Asia's own national quantum access schemes follow a similar playbook of pairing hardware vendors with universities and grant-backed pilots.

## Key Takeaways

- Financial regulators are moving from quantum-safe migration mandates toward actively testing quantum-enhanced tools, with the FCA's AML/fraud pilot a notable first for a major regulator.
- Quantum hardware vendors are starting to show real, named commercial traction rather than purely grant- or pilot-funded revenue, with IQM's Japan repeat sale a signal for Asia-Pacific demand.
- Crypto-agility work is broadening beyond banks and governments to include device manufacturing supply chains (eSIM) and blockchain protocols (Bitcoin Cash), reflecting a wider shift toward embedding post-quantum readiness at the infrastructure level.
