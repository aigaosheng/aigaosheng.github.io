### US AI Brief — May 7, 2026

### Top Stories

- **Headline:** White House Considers Pre-Release Review of New AI Models as Policy Shifts
- **Source:** Ming Pao (citing The New York Times) · May 6, 2026
- **Summary:** The U.S. government is reportedly considering a major policy shift that would establish a pre-release review and regulatory mechanism for new AI models, moving away from the Trump administration’s previous hands-off approach. This potential executive order would create a working group of tech executives and officials, modeled partly on the UK’s AI safety framework. The shift is driven by growing concerns over AI’s impact on jobs, privacy, and national security, particularly following Anthropic’s decision to delay public access to its powerful “Mythos” model due to cybersecurity risks.
- **Why It Matters:** This marks a potential reversal of the administration’s pro-innovation stance and could introduce significant regulatory hurdles for AI developers. Formalizing government pre-screening would fundamentally change the go-to-market strategy for U.S. AI labs and set a precedent for how frontier models are governed.

- **Headline:** Google, Microsoft, and xAI Agree to Give US Government Early Access to AI Models
- **Source:** CNBC TV18 (citing Bloomberg) · May 6, 2026
- **Summary:** Alphabet’s Google, Microsoft, and xAI have formally agreed to provide the U.S. Commerce Department’s Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) with early access to their new AI models for pre-release security and capability assessments. OpenAI and Anthropic have renegotiated their existing partnerships under this new framework, with CAISI having already completed over 40 evaluations, including on models that remain unreleased. The agreements come as the White House weighs a broader executive order on AI oversight and as Anthropic’s “Mythos” model continues to raise national security concerns.
- **Why It Matters:** This voluntary yet strategic alignment between the government and AI leaders suggests an emerging public-private framework for AI safety. It pre-emptively shapes the regulatory landscape, giving early-mover advantages to compliant incumbents while potentially creating new compliance benchmarks for all frontier model developers.

- **Headline:** US to Assess New AI Models Before Their Release in Formal Policy Shift
- **Source:** The Star (via AFP) · May 6, 2026
- **Summary:** The U.S. government has formally announced that it will have access to tech giants' new AI models for evaluation before they are released to the public, confirming a major policy pivot. The agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI were renegotiated from the Biden era and are now overseen by CAISI. The immediate catalyst for the shift was Anthropic’s “Mythos” model, which demonstrated a powerful ability to identify software vulnerabilities, leading the company to withhold public release due to cybersecurity concerns.
- **Why It Matters:** This policy shift codifies government oversight as a standard part of the AI development lifecycle. By directly linking national security to pre-deployment evaluations, the administration is creating a de facto licensing requirement for cutting-edge AI capabilities.

- **Source:** The Wall Street Journal · May 6, 2026

- **Headline:** Chip Maker Infineon Lifts Guidance as AI Boom Gains Strength
- **Source:** The Wall Street Journal · May 6, 2026
- **Summary:** German chip maker Infineon Technologies raised its sales and margin forecasts for the fiscal year, citing high demand for its power supply solutions used in AI data centers. The company now expects revenue to grow significantly from the previous fiscal year, with its closely watched segment result margin expected to climb to around 20%. This follows a prior forecast of only a moderate increase and a margin in the high-teens percentage range.
- **Why It Matters:** The AI boom’s impact is rippling beyond U.S. borders, boosting semiconductor demand across the global supply chain. Infineon’s revised guidance confirms that AI infrastructure build-out is a durable and powerful tailwind for component manufacturers, even those not producing leading-edge compute chips.
