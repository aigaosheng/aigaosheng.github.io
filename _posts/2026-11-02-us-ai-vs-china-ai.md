# US AI vs China AI Brief: Trump Hedges on Nvidia's Huang Amid Spy Row

**Today:** A new detail-rich twist in the Nvidia-Megaspeed espionage story signals that chip-export politics in Washington are entering unusually personal territory, with direct implications for firms that route AI compute through Singapore and the region.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Trump Hedges on Keeping Jensen Huang as Tech Adviser**

*The Tribune (India) · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** President Trump publicly hedged for the first time on whether Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang will remain one of his technology advisers, after a New York Times report named a specific Chinese national as the alleged intelligence asset who cultivated ties with him.

Asked directly if Huang would stay on his advisory council, Trump said "I would like to say yes... unless we find out something to the contrary," a notably more conditional tone than his earlier blanket defense of Huang. The report identifies Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a senior executive at Singapore-based data-center operator Megaspeed, as the individual US intelligence concluded had been passing information to Beijing while maintaining a close relationship with the Nvidia chief; it also references a 25% government stake arrangement tied to certain Nvidia processors approved for China sales. Nvidia has said its Megaspeed dealings were "normal" and that related diversion allegations were "investigated and debunked."

**Why it matters:** Huang sits on the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology and has personally lobbied the administration to ease China chip curbs, so any erosion of Trump's confidence in him could shift US export-control politics. For Singapore and the wider region, the episode keeps Megaspeed-style compute-leasing arrangements under a harsher compliance spotlight just as Southeast Asian data centers face growing scrutiny as a conduit for Chinese AI firms' chip access.

## Key Takeaways

- The Nvidia-Megaspeed affair has moved from a diversion-compliance question to a direct test of Trump's trust in his own AI policy advisers, raising the odds of tighter chip-export enforcement.
- Singapore-domiciled compute resellers remain a focal point for US scrutiny of how Chinese AI firms access restricted hardware, a reputational risk for the region's fast-growing data-center sector.
- Congressional China hawks are likely to use the episode to push stalled chip-export legislation, adding uncertainty for enterprises planning China-adjacent AI infrastructure investment.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Trump's hedge hardens into any formal distancing from Huang's advisory role, and track the US-China "Super Intelligence" dialogue expected before end-November alongside the November 9 expiry of the BIS affiliates-rule suspension, both of which could reshape chip-export enforcement in the weeks ahead.
