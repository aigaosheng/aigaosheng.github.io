# **Australia’s Financial Watchdog Puts Fintech Unicorn Airwallex Under the Microscope**

Australia’s financial crime regulator has ordered a high-stakes audit of **Airwallex**, a global fintech powerhouse, amid growing concerns that the company may not fully comply with key **anti-money laundering (AML)** and **counter-terrorism financing (CTF)** laws. The move has sent ripples across the fintech world, highlighting regulatory pressures facing fast-growing payments platforms expanding internationally. ([AUSTRAC][1])

## **Regulator Tightens the Screws on Compliance**

On **January 22, 2026**, the **Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre (AUSTRAC)** issued a formal directive requiring Airwallex to appoint an **external auditor** to evaluate its AML/CTF compliance framework. The audit will assess whether Airwallex has robust controls in place for:

* Monitoring financial transactions and identifying risks,
* Conducting ongoing customer due diligence,
* Reporting suspicious matters in a timely and accurate way. ([AUSTRAC][1])

AUSTRAC’s concerns stem from indications that the company’s **transaction monitoring systems** may not be aligned with the full spectrum of risks it faces as a global payments platform, particularly in handling **cross-border fund transfers**. The regulator also questioned whether Airwallex has a clear understanding of who its customers are and what reporting obligations apply. ([AUSTRAC][1])

“**Strong compliance systems and timely reporting of suspicious activity are essential to disrupting criminal activities and illicit proceeds of crime**,” said AUSTRAC CEO Brendan Thomas, underlining the seriousness of the review. ([AUSTRAC][1])

## **Why This Matters for Fintech and Global Payments**

Airwallex has grown rapidly since its founding in Australia, scaling into one of the world’s most valuable fintech firms with operations spanning **San Francisco, Singapore, and beyond**. The company serves hundreds of thousands of businesses with digital payment infrastructure, FX tools, and cross-border transaction services. ([The Business Times][2])

Regulators are increasingly focused on whether fintech firms’ compliance frameworks scale as fast as their business. In Airwallex’s case, AUSTRAC’s audit marks a significant test of confidence in the company’s systems at a time when fintechs are pushing deeper into international markets and handling **high-volume global money flows**.

If shortcomings are confirmed, AUSTRAC may escalate its enforcement actions, potentially including financial penalties or operational constraints — underscoring that rapid innovation must be met with equal rigor in compliance. ([AUSTRAC][1])

## **Airwallex Responds With Assurance and Cooperation**

Airwallex has publicly stated it will **fully cooperate** with the audit and reaffirmed its commitment to maintaining strong AML/CTF controls. The company noted that an independent auditor validated parts of its compliance framework in 2025 following a prior regulatory review. ([Airwallex][3])

In its official statement, Airwallex emphasized its zero-tolerance stance on financial crime and expressed confidence that the upcoming evaluation will demonstrate the strength of its compliance systems. ([Airwallex][3])

## **Broader Implications for Fintech Ecosystems**

This regulatory action comes at a critical juncture for fintech:

* Regulators globally are sharpening scrutiny over AML/CTF controls and transparency obligations.
* Cross-border payment platforms face unique challenges in monitoring complex, high-velocity transactions that span jurisdictions.
* Businesses that integrate with platforms like Airwallex may see increased compliance expectations and reporting demands. ([Finance Magnates][4])

For customers and partners, the audit underscores the importance of aligning operational due diligence with evolving financial crime prevention standards.

## **Glossary**

**Anti-Money Laundering (AML)**
A set of laws and procedures aimed at preventing criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate by moving them through financial systems.

**Counter-Terrorism Financing (CTF)**
Regulations designed to detect and stop funds being used to support terrorist activities.

**Transaction Monitoring**
The automated or manual review of financial transactions to identify patterns that could indicate suspicious activity.

**Customer Due Diligence (CDD)**
The process of verifying a customer’s identity and assessing risk to prevent fraud and financial crime.

**Suspicious Matter Reporting**
A legal requirement for financial institutions to report transactions or behaviors that may suggest illicit activity.
