# Payments Brief: Visa Expands Stablecoin Settlement With Western Union

**Today:** Card networks and banks are racing to wire stablecoins directly into regulated settlement rails, while the first consumer-facing AI payment agents go live, giving executives fresh evidence of how fast agentic and digital-dollar payments are converging.

## Top Stories

### 1. 💳 **Visa Adds Western Union's USDPT Stablecoin to VisaNet Settlement**

*FinanceFeeds / The Block · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa has integrated Western Union's USDPT stablecoin as a native settlement asset on VisaNet through a partnership with Anchorage Digital.

Anchorage Digital Bank, which issues and custodies USDPT on Solana, confirmed that Visa and eligible partners can now use the token to settle VisaNet obligations. The move extends Visa's stablecoin settlement network, which the company says reached a $20 billion annualised run rate in September 2026, up more than 15-fold year over year, following earlier additions of Monad, Canton and Base. Western Union launched USDPT in May 2026 and has already deployed it in the Philippines and Bolivia to support 24/7 cross-border settlement.

**Why it matters:** Each new issuer Visa onboards strengthens stablecoins as plumbing for regulated card-network settlement rather than a parallel crypto system, a shift that matters for remittance-heavy corridors into Southeast Asia such as the Philippines.

### 2. 💳 **PNC Bank Launches Corporate USDC Payments With Coinbase**

*The Daily Hodl · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** PNC Bank has gone live with incoming USDC stablecoin payments for eligible corporate and institutional clients, built on Coinbase's payment infrastructure on Ethereum.

Funds received in USDC convert automatically one-to-one into US dollars and land in a designated PNC account, with the service starting on a limited pilot basis. PNC framed the launch as giving business clients faster access to funds and more efficient cross-border transactions, building on its existing relationship with Coinbase.

**Why it matters:** A top-10 US bank offering stablecoin receivables natively signals that corporate treasury adoption is moving from pilot programmes at crypto-native firms into mainstream commercial banking, a template other regional banks serving Asia-based exporters may follow.

### 3. 💳 **Singapore's Triple-A Enables Stablecoin Payments for Fintech Association Fees**

*The Paypers · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** MAS-licensed Triple-A is now processing Singapore FinTech Association membership fee payments in stablecoins, converting funds to SGD for settlement.

Members can pay in supported stablecoins through a digital wallet, with Triple-A handling conversion so SFA itself never holds digital assets. SFA president Holly Fang said the arrangement supports wider stablecoin adoption and reinforces Singapore's role as a payments hub, pointing to a DBS/EY survey of 422 finance leaders in which 63% said they already use stablecoins in business operations.

**Why it matters:** As a real-world use case run through MAS-licensed infrastructure, this gives Singapore institutions a low-risk template for accepting stablecoins without altering treasury operations, ahead of Singapore FinTech Festival and Singapore Blockchain Week this month.

### 4. 🌐 **RCBC Adds Stablecoin Transfers to US Virtual Account for Filipino Freelancers**

*Fintech News Philippines · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** RCBC and US payments firm Meridian now let Filipino freelancers and remote workers receive USDC and USDT transfers from US clients directly into RCBC's Pulz US Virtual Account.

Incoming stablecoin transfers on Ethereum, Polygon and Base are automatically converted to US dollars before crediting the account, which carries a $10,000 monthly receiving limit and requires no US address or social security number. The bank said the key benefit is 24/7 settlement, including weekends and holidays, compared with banking-hours-bound ACH and Fedwire transfers.

**Why it matters:** The feature targets a core Philippine export industry, outsourced freelance labour, and reflects BSP-supervised banks embedding stablecoin rails to compete with informal remittance channels.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://fintechnews.ph/74408/crypto/rcbc-pulz-us-virtual-account-stablecoin-transfers/)

### 5. 🤖 **Wirex Launches Navoka, an AI Agent With Its Own Bank Account and Card**

*PR Newswire · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Wirex has launched Navoka, an invite-only AI payment agent that holds its own IBAN, debit card and stablecoin wallet, kept fully separate from a user's personal accounts.

Users set spending limits, merchant restrictions and country controls that Navoka cannot override, and a "kill switch" lets them freeze the card or disconnect linked AI assistants instantly. The product launched at Full Stack London on Telegram, with WhatsApp and iMessage planned, citing Juniper Research projections of 1.3 billion agentic-commerce users by 2031 against survey findings that only 23% of US consumers currently trust GenAI with payments.

**Why it matters:** Account-level segregation and hard spending limits address the trust gap that has slowed agentic commerce elsewhere, offering a design pattern banks and card networks serving Asia's wallet-heavy markets will likely need to match.

## Key Takeaways

- Card networks and banks are converging on stablecoins as settlement infrastructure rather than treating them as a separate asset class, with Visa, PNC and Anchorage Digital all expanding native integrations this week.
- Southeast Asia is a proving ground for stablecoin use cases that solve concrete problems, remittance speed for Filipino freelancers via RCBC, and low-friction B2B settlement for Singapore's fintech sector via Triple-A, rather than speculative trading.
- Agentic commerce is moving from card-network pilots to standalone consumer products, with Wirex's Navoka betting that strict account segregation and spending controls, not broader AI capability, will unlock consumer trust.

## What to Watch

Watch for adoption data and partner lists as Singapore FinTech Festival (November 18-20) and Singapore Blockchain Week (November 13-17) unfold, both likely venues for further stablecoin and tokenised-deposit announcements from MAS-linked institutions.
