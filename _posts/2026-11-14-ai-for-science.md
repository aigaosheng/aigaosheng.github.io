# AI for Science Brief: Google Antigravity Agents Formalize Weil Conjectures

**Today:** A multi-agent system from Google Research just automated a feat of pure mathematics that took humanity five decades, while a Chinese CRO and an India-Italy science pact show how AI-for-science infrastructure and funding are scaling in parallel across Asia.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔬 **Google's Multi-Agent System Formalizes Weil Conjectures, Reads Molecule Spectra**

*Google Research · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Google's Antigravity "/teamwork" feature coordinated thousands of AI subagents to machine-verify a landmark 1974 mathematical proof and to identify molecular structures from lab spectra with near-perfect accuracy, pointing to agentic AI as a genuine research collaborator rather than just a coding assistant.

Google Research reported that /teamwork produced an end-to-end, machine-checked formalization of the Weil conjectures — originally proved by Pierre Deligne — generating roughly 1.8 million lines of verified Lean code across 28,800 subagent calls. A separate run organized 30 specialized agents around a physics-informed spectral simulator to determine molecular structures from nuclear magnetic resonance data, hitting 95% top-one and 100% top-three accuracy on complex benchmark molecules, nearly double a single agent's performance. Google says it is opening six months of free /teamwork access to 10,000 academic researchers.

**Why it matters:** This moves agentic AI beyond benchmark chasing into formally verifiable mathematics and chemistry workflows that research-intensive sectors — pharma, materials, finance quant teams — will want to evaluate for compressing discovery and proof-checking cycles; Asian universities and research institutes eligible for the academic access program stand to gain early exposure to the tooling.

### 2. 🔬 **Sino Biological Launches High-Throughput Wet-Lab Service for AI-Designed Proteins**

*PR Newswire · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Sino Biological's new 4RISE service closes the loop between AI-generated protein designs and physical validation, promising three-day turnaround from synthesis to binding data.

The company said its XPressMAX platform synthesizes proteins — including miniproteins, VHHs, scFvs, Fabs, enzymes and cytokines — in about three hours with over 99% success rates, then runs binding assays using Carterra SPR, Biacore SPR and Octet BLI instruments. Structured binding data is delivered via a real-time API within three days, intended to support iterative AI design-test-learn cycles for drug discovery teams.

**Why it matters:** As generative AI models churn out ever more candidate protein designs, wet-lab validation capacity — not model quality — is becoming the bottleneck; commercial CROs offering fast, API-connected validation could become a standard layer in biopharma AI pipelines, including for Asia-based biotechs that lack in-house high-throughput labs.

### 3. 🌐 **India and Italy Plan Joint AI, Biotech and Energy Research Push**

*The Sentinel · November 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India and Italy agreed to deepen scientific cooperation with a proposed 2027 funding call spanning AI and high-performance computing, clean energy, and advanced biotechnology including gene therapy and mRNA research.

The plan follows talks between India's Union Minister Jitendra Singh and Italy's Minister of University and Research Anna Maria Bernini, building on the two countries' 2025 science memorandum of understanding. India separately committed more than 7 million euros as a co-partner in Italy's Elettra 2.0 synchrotron beamline programme, which supports materials and life-sciences research.

**Why it matters:** Bilateral science-funding pacts are becoming a standard channel for AI-for-science capacity building, a pattern Singapore has also used via grants to NUS and NTU; the proposed 2027 call is still a government commitment rather than a funded program, so concrete project awards warrant tracking before drawing conclusions about impact.

## Key Takeaways

- Frontier labs are shifting from single-model chatbots to multi-agent "co-scientist" systems that formalize mathematical proofs and solve structure-elucidation problems once reserved for expert teams, raising the bar for what counts as an AI-for-science tool versus a general-purpose LLM.
- Commercial wet-lab validation infrastructure is maturing alongside AI-first drug design platforms, signaling that the next competitive bottleneck in biopharma AI is experimental throughput, not model capability.
- Government science-funding diplomacy is increasingly framed around AI explicitly, a trend enterprises operating across Asia should watch for signs of where public research capacity — and potential partnership or grant opportunities — will concentrate next.
