# Quantum Brief — 2026-10-07

**Today:** Quantum's commercial stack is becoming more concrete: semiconductor manufacturing is moving into quantum hardware, photonic startups are raising capital, and Europol is urging immediate post-quantum security preparation.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Infineon and ZuriQ expand partnership to scale trapped-ion quantum chips**

*Infineon Technologies · October 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Infineon and Swiss quantum startup ZuriQ are expanding their collaboration to combine trapped-ion quantum architecture with semiconductor manufacturing and advanced packaging expertise.

The partnership targets scalable quantum processing units based on ZuriQ's Penning micro-trap architecture. The companies are building on ZuriQ's recent demonstration of a two-dimensional array of individually controlled ions, with the goal of moving the technology toward commercially viable systems.

**Why it matters:** The announcement highlights a critical shift in quantum computing from laboratory experimentation toward semiconductor-style manufacturing, packaging, and integration. Access to established industrial fabrication capabilities could become as important as qubit architecture itself as the industry pursues scale.

### 2. 📊 **Quanfluence raises $10 million to build a full-stack photonic quantum computer**

*Quanfluence · October 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's Quanfluence has raised $10 million to integrate photonic chips, control electronics, optics, and software into a full-stack quantum computing system.

The Bengaluru-based company said the round was led by Chiratae Ventures, with participation from returning investor Pi Ventures and Rainmatter by Zerodha. Quanfluence plans early processors by 2027 and larger scalable machines by 2029.

**Why it matters:** The financing reflects growing investor interest in photonic architectures and vertically integrated quantum systems. Building the full stack internally could give emerging quantum companies tighter control over performance and system-level optimization as the market moves beyond individual component demonstrations.

### 3. 🔒 **Europol urges organizations to prepare now for quantum threats**

*Europol · October 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Europol is urging governments, businesses, and the cryptocurrency industry to begin migrating toward quantum-resistant cryptography before sufficiently capable quantum computers arrive.

Two new reports examine risks to cryptocurrency wallets and encrypted information, including the possibility that attackers could collect encrypted data today and decrypt it later. Europol identifies wallet cryptographic keys as a primary exposure point for digital assets and recommends phased migration to post-quantum cryptography alongside stronger key management and crypto-agility.

**Why it matters:** The message moves quantum security from a theoretical future concern toward a current infrastructure-planning priority. Organizations with long-lived sensitive data and blockchain-based assets face particularly strong incentives to inventory cryptographic dependencies and begin migration before quantum-resistant upgrades become urgent.
