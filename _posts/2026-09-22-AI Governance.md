### 1. **Audit Committees Elevate AI Governance as a Board-Level Priority**

* **Source**: Deloitte / Center for Audit Quality · September 22, 2026
* **Summary**: Deloitte and the Center for Audit Quality's latest Audit Committee Practices Report finds that 75% of respondents now identify AI governance as a top-three priority, up from 35% a year earlier. However, only 56% say they are confident in their committees' ability to effectively oversee AI governance.
* **Why It Matters**: AI governance is moving from technology and compliance functions into formal board and audit-committee oversight. The capability gap suggests that governance structures may be expanding faster than the expertise and visibility required to operate them effectively.

### 2. **Regnology Study Finds a Large Gap Between AI Pilots and Production Regulatory Reporting**

* **Source**: FF News · September 22, 2026
* **Summary**: A Regnology and Oliver Wyman study reports that 87% of financial institutions are exploring or piloting AI, while only 16% have moved these technologies into production. The research highlights traceability, reconstruction of outputs, data quality, and human oversight as critical requirements for agentic AI in regulatory reporting.
* **Why It Matters**: In regulated financial workflows, governance is becoming a production-enablement layer rather than simply a compliance checkpoint. The ability to prove how an AI system reached an output may become as important as the automation itself.

### 3. **Akamai Calls for Behavioral Governance of Autonomous AI Agents**

* **Source**: GlobeNewswire / Akamai · September 22, 2026
* **Summary**: Akamai's latest State of the Internet security report argues that enterprise AI risk is shifting from traditional human identity management toward governing autonomous, nonhuman entities. The report highlights gaps around AI agents, Model Context Protocol (MCP) exposure, browser-based AI tools, and sensitive information entering chatbot interactions.
* **Why It Matters**: Agentic AI changes governance from controlling what users can access to controlling what autonomous systems can do. Identity, permissions, runtime behavior, tool access and API actions increasingly need to be governed as a single control surface.
