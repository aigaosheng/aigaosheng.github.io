# US AI vs China AI Brief: China's Spy Agency Pursues AI Chips

**Today:** Two verified developments show how the US-China chip contest is moving beyond licensing rules, into state intelligence operations and US voter opinion, which matters to enterprise AI, fintech and payments teams that depend on cross-border compute and Asian data-centre supply chains.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **China's State Security Ministry Reportedly Runs Covert Campaign to Obtain Advanced AI Chips**

*Seoul Economic Daily · November 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A report relayed by Seoul Economic Daily, which attributes it to The New York Times, says Beijing's intelligence apparatus has made acquiring restricted AI chips a dedicated, minister-led priority.

Seoul Economic Daily says the New York Times, citing current and former foreign intelligence officials, reported that China's Ministry of State Security has worked for at least two years to get around US semiconductor export controls. The effort is said to be run by State Security Minister Chen Yixin through a working team that reports to him. Its tasks reportedly include helping Chinese companies procure and import restricted chips and collecting intelligence on the global chip supply chain. The article notes that Chen publicly framed the work as a national-security struggle in an April Communist Party journal article. The claims rest on anonymous sourcing, and Seoul Economic Daily's piece is a secondary account of the NYT report, which this brief could not open directly.

**Why it matters:** If accurate, the report suggests export-control enforcement now has to contend with state-backed procurement networks rather than only commercial resellers. That raises diligence expectations for Singapore and wider Asian cloud, data-centre and distribution partners that handle advanced accelerators.

### 2. 👥 **Poll Finds Most US Voters Favour Pausing AI Chip Sales to China During Investigations**

*Daily Caller · November 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A commissioned YouGov Blue survey reports strong voter support for pausing advanced AI chip sales to China, which adds political pressure on chip export policy.

The Daily Caller reports a poll of 1,000 voters, fielded November 13 to 17 with a margin of error of 3.7 percentage points, commissioned by the advocacy group Demand Progress Education Fund. According to the article, 64% back pausing advanced AI chip sales to China while officials investigate, and roughly three-quarters say the federal government should examine whether such chips reached China. It also reports that 70% found the argument that chip sales could aid China's military convincing, and that almost two-thirds think Nvidia holds too much of the AI chip market. Because the sponsor is an advocacy organisation, the results should be read as one stakeholder's survey rather than neutral polling.

**Why it matters:** Public sentiment of this kind can strengthen the hand of lawmakers pressing for tighter chip rules, adding policy risk for vendors and customers whose roadmaps depend on China-linked demand or Southeast Asian capacity. Asia-based operators should expect continued volatility in US licensing posture.

## Key Takeaways

- Export-control contests are shifting from rule-writing to enforcement against organised procurement, including reported state intelligence involvement in China.
- US political opinion, at least in this sponsor-commissioned survey, leans toward restricting chip sales, which keeps policy risk elevated for vendors with China exposure.
- Reporting on both stories rests on anonymous sourcing or advocacy-sponsored research, so executives should treat the findings as signals rather than confirmed facts.

## What to Watch

Watch for any direct confirmation or Chinese government response to the reported State Security campaign, and for the closed-door House interviews with former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang that have been scheduled for December 1.
