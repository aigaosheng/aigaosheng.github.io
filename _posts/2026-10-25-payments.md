# Payments Brief: Bank of Korea Plans Stablecoin Payment Rules

**Today:** Two Asia-relevant stablecoin signals: Seoul's forex-law route to regulation, and Visa's cautious outlook for the Philippines.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Bank of Korea Plans to Regulate Stablecoins as Payment Means Under Forex Law**
*Forex Crunch · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea is set to bring stablecoins under its foreign exchange law before a dedicated stablecoin law passes.

Per Forex Crunch, which cites Bitcoin Magazine's earlier report, the Bank of Korea will work with the government to draft an amendment to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act before year-end. The act treats a "means of payment" as an instrument whose cross-border movement must be reported, so dollar stablecoins used by Korean residents would fall into the same reporting regime as bank wires. The article says no draft text or passage date exists. A separate Digital Asset Basic Act, which would cover issuance and licensing, is still being drafted. This is secondary reporting of a plan, not an enacted rule.

**Why it matters:** For payment firms and remittance providers serving Korea corridors, the FX-law route points to reporting obligations on cross-border stablecoin flows well before any licensing regime exists. Compliance teams should expect the central bank, not only the financial regulator, to shape the rules.

### 2. 🌐 **Visa Has No Firm Timeline for Stablecoin Transfers in the Philippines**
*The Philippine Star · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa says stablecoin adoption in the Philippines depends on local readiness, even as it commercializes stablecoin prefunding elsewhere.

Visa Direct's Southeast Asia head said at a media briefing that uptake depends on financial institutions, regulators and consumers being ready, and that she could not give a date. Visa said on October 15 that its stablecoin prefunding service had moved from pilot to commercial deployment, with Inyo Global as the first partner. A separate partner-led Visa Direct service is targeted for the Philippines within six months, but it uses conventional currencies. Visa's Money Travels 2026 survey found that 38% of Filipino respondents trust stablecoin remittances, versus 79% for conventional digital transfers.

**Why it matters:** Infrastructure is running ahead of consumer trust in a major remittance market. Providers targeting Philippine corridors should expect fiat rails to carry the volume near term, and trust and regulator engagement to gate any stablecoin push.

## Key Takeaways
- Asian authorities are folding stablecoins into existing legal frameworks, such as Korea's FX law, rather than waiting for standalone regimes.
- Supply-side readiness is not the bottleneck: networks are deploying stablecoin settlement, while consumer trust and local rules decide the pace.

## What to Watch
Whether Korean authorities publish draft text of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act amendment before year-end, and whether Visa's targeted Philippine Visa Direct rollout lands within its six-month window.
