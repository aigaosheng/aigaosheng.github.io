# US AI vs China AI Brief: US Commission Says PLA Seeks Nvidia Chips

**Today:** A congressional commission's new findings on China's military demand for Nvidia chips sharpen the case for tighter compute controls, which matters to enterprises and cloud operators in Asia that host or rent advanced AI capacity.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **US Commission Says China's Military Is Pursuing Nvidia's Most Advanced AI Chips**

*CNBC · November 18, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A US congressional commission says China's military is actively trying to obtain Nvidia's most advanced chips for AI-enabled military use, despite export controls and a substantial compute gap with the US.

According to CNBC, the finding comes from the 2026 annual report to Congress of the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission. The report says Beijing is working to evade US export controls for the chips behind its military modernisation drive. It also says China has not yet overcome the hardest bottlenecks, including leading-edge chip fabrication, memory, manufacturing equipment and Nvidia's CUDA software ecosystem, and is unlikely to do so soon. CNBC adds that Chinese firms have reportedly reached Nvidia hardware through data centres in third countries, including Malaysia, and links the report to a recent Commerce Department rule aimed at that remote-access route. This summary relies on CNBC's account, as the commission's full report text was not independently reviewed.

**Why it matters:** The commission's framing supports the argument that chips and cloud access, not only chip shipments, are now the main enforcement front in US-China AI competition. For Singapore and Southeast Asian data centre operators and their enterprise customers, that points to closer scrutiny of who rents compute and from where.

## Key Takeaways

- The compute gap is the central premise of US policy. The commission describes it as substantial and attributes it largely to export controls, while saying China is trying to circumvent them.
- Enforcement is shifting from hardware shipments to access routes. Third-country data centres, including those in Malaysia, are now explicitly part of the US concern.

## What to Watch

Watch for the full text of the commission's 2026 annual report and its recommendations on chips and AI, and for any further Commerce guidance on remote access to advanced chips. Also monitor whether Chinese authorities respond with concrete countermeasures.
