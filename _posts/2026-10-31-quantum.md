# Quantum Brief: QNu Labs And Netpoleon Target Quantum-Safe Banking

**Today:** Quantum-safe security is shifting from pilot projects to channel-led enterprise sales in Asia, while funding advocates in Washington and investors in London debate whether capital is keeping pace with the hardware roadmap.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **QNu Labs And Netpoleon Partner On Quantum-Safe Security For Indian Banks**

*The Quantum Insider · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** QNu Labs and distributor Netpoleon India are teaming up to push quantum-safe cybersecurity tools into Indian banking, government, defence and telecom customers through Netpoleon's channel-partner network.

The partnership pairs QNu Labs' QShield 2.0 platform, which handles cryptographic risk assessment and governance, with Netpoleon's systems-integrator distribution reach. The companies say the goal is to help enterprises identify cryptographic vulnerabilities and build "crypto-agility" as quantum-era standards evolve, building on QNu Labs' earlier quantum key distribution deployments in Indian defence networks.

**Why it matters:** For Singapore and regional banks already working toward CSA and MAS-style quantum-safe migration timelines, a distributor-backed model for reaching mid-sized financial institutions points to how post-quantum readiness may scale across South and Southeast Asia faster than direct vendor sales alone.

### 2. 🏦 **Quantum Industry Coalition Presses Congress On National Quantum Initiative Funding**

*The Quantum Insider · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A coalition of quantum companies has written to US congressional leaders urging passage of the National Quantum Initiative Reauthorization Act through the 2027 defense authorization bill, with funding above current agency spending levels.

The letter asks lawmakers to sustain funding for quantum testbeds and technology centers, back workforce development from K-12 through university level, and harmonise research-security definitions with the CHIPS and Science Act, including coverage of quantum-classical hybrid systems. The original 2018 National Quantum Initiative Act lapsed in September 2023.

**Why it matters:** Continued uncertainty over US federal quantum funding affects the pace of hardware roadmaps that Asian banks, telcos and governments are already citing in their own post-quantum migration deadlines; delays upstream can push those timelines further out.

### 3. 📊 **UK Deep-Tech Leaders Flag Capital And Risk-Appetite Gaps At City Quantum Summit**

*The Quantum Insider · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Speakers at the City Quantum & AI Summit in London said UK quantum and deep-tech scale-ups face a "plumbing problem" connecting domestic capital, including pension funds, to venture-backed hardware firms.

Panellists pointed to UK defence procurement cycles that can take over 1,000 days to close contracts above £200 million as a cultural, not just regulatory, barrier to faster quantum adoption. They cited the British Business Bank's £100 million anchor investment in Oxford Quantum Circuits' £260 million Series C as a model for signalling confidence to private investors, alongside a proposed £1 billion-plus UK Scale-up Fund announced in July 2026.

**Why it matters:** The financing bottleneck debate is a preview of questions Asian sovereign funds and corporates will face as they weigh direct quantum hardware investment against buying capacity from established players.

### 4. ⚛️ **Nord Quantique Says Error-Correction Gain Has More Than Doubled Since 2024**

*The Quantum Insider · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nord Quantique says its bosonic-code "one cavity, one logical qubit" architecture has pushed quantum error-correction gain past 2x, more than double the 1.1x figure the company reported in 2024.

In an interview, the company's co-founder said a four-qubit system with integrated error correction is entering operation in 2026, with a company target of reaching error rates of 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁹ by 2032. Nord Quantique says it is leveraging more than C$1 billion in existing Quebec public infrastructure, including fabrication facilities and dilution refrigerators, to limit private capital needs, and is in discussions on its first system sale.

**Why it matters:** These are company-reported figures rather than independently verified benchmarks, but the approach illustrates an alternative error-correction path to the many-physical-qubits-per-logical-qubit model used by superconducting rivals, relevant to enterprises evaluating which hardware bets to track.

### 5. ⚛️ **Infleqtion Tests Optical Atomic Clock Aboard US Navy Vessel**

*The Quantum Insider · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Infleqtion ran its Tiqker optical atomic clock continuously aboard the US Navy support vessel RV Merlin for over two weeks during the Maritime Big Play Pacific exercise off Southern California.

The company says the test exposed the system to large temperature swings and sustained vibration to evaluate precision timing under real operating conditions, building on earlier UK Royal Navy submarine and flight trials. Tiqker is designed to give independent timing when GPS is degraded, jammed or spoofed.

**Why it matters:** Quantum timing and sensing is advancing in parallel with quantum computing and is increasingly dual-use; Asian defence and critical-infrastructure operators evaluating GPS-independent timing should watch this maturing alongside post-quantum cryptography migration.

## Key Takeaways

- Post-quantum security is moving from vendor pilots to channel-partner distribution in Asia, a sign the market is maturing beyond direct enterprise sales.
- Funding and capital-access questions, not just technical milestones, are becoming the binding constraint on quantum timelines in both the US and UK.
- Hardware architecture debates, such as Nord Quantique's bosonic-code claims, remain company-reported and should be weighed against independent benchmarks before informing procurement decisions.

## What to Watch

Watch whether the National Quantum Initiative Reauthorization Act is included in the 2027 defense authorization bill, and whether more Asian banks follow India and Singapore in publishing concrete post-quantum migration deadlines and vendor-readiness requirements.
