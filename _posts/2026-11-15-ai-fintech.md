# AI Fintech Brief: Security Bank Scales AI Agents Across Workforce

**Today:** With much of the week's AI-in-banking coverage either already reported or outside this edition's scope, the standout verified development is a concrete, numbers-backed case of agentic AI moving from pilot to workforce-wide deployment at a mid-sized Asian bank.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Security Bank Trains Staff, Deploys 3,100 AI Agents**

*The Philippine Star (Philstar) · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Security Bank has pushed agentic AI past the pilot stage, with staff building thousands of task-specific agents while keeping credit, fraud and complaints decisions with humans.

The Philippine lender trained more than 3,000 employees in agentic AI capabilities, and staff across business, operations, finance and risk roles have since built 3,100 "low-level" AI agents to handle routine tasks. Separately, 1,210 employees with Microsoft Copilot Premium licences show 98% daily engagement. Chief digital and technology officer Patrick Meneses said accountability for decisions affecting customers, including credit evaluation and fraud detection, stays with people rather than algorithms.

**Why it matters:** The scale of self-built, staff-driven agents at a non-tier-one bank suggests agentic AI tooling has become accessible enough for mid-sized Asian lenders to deploy broadly, not just global banks with large AI budgets. It also offers a template other ASEAN banks may point to as regulators, including the Philippines' BSP, scrutinise accountability for AI-assisted decisions.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/11/15/2570124/security-bank-trains-more-staff-develops-ai-agents)

## Key Takeaways

- Agentic AI adoption in Asian banking is increasingly driven by internal staff building narrow, task-specific agents rather than only vendor-led platform rollouts.
- Banks publicising AI scale are pairing it with explicit human-accountability statements on credit, fraud and complaints, a likely response to regulatory sensitivity around delegated decision-making.
- Coverage of bank AI agents this week skewed heavily toward governance, access restrictions and fraud-detection angles rather than new banking, lending, insurance or wealth deployments, suggesting a near-term lull in net-new product news in this specific slice of the market.

## What to Watch

The Singapore FinTech Festival (November 18-20, 2026) is likely to produce a wave of new bank and insurer AI announcements across Asia; watch for whether other ASEAN banks disclose comparable agent-count or adoption metrics in the days around the event.
