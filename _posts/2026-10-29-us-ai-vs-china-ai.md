# US AI vs China AI Brief: Nvidia CEO Tied To Alleged Beijing Asset

**Today:** The chip-control fight has moved from customs paperwork to counter-intelligence, and Singapore sits at the centre of the allegations.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **US Officials Concluded Megaspeed Executive Was Gathering Intelligence for Beijing**

*The New York Times · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** American officials determined that a senior executive at Singapore-based data centre firm Megaspeed, who cultivated a close relationship with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, was collecting intelligence for Beijing.

The New York Times reports that US surveillance of Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a Chinese citizen and Megaspeed executive already suspected of helping procure restricted semiconductors for China, revealed ties to both the Nvidia chief and Chinese authorities. According to the report, intelligence officials briefed President Donald Trump roughly a year ago and he opted not to act, while Jensen Huang received no formal warning and surveillance continues. Nvidia told the paper it had rejected claims that Megaspeed was supplying chips to China and that its dealings with the firm matched those with other regional customers; Megaspeed said its current leadership is treating the matter seriously, has engaged outside counsel and has told the US government it will cooperate.

**Why it matters:** Singapore and Malaysia have spent the past two years tightening transshipment controls precisely to avoid being cast as a leak in the US export-control perimeter, and an espionage framing raises the compliance bar for every regional data centre, cloud reseller and GPU-leasing counterparty. Firms with Asian compute supply chains should expect sharper counterparty due diligence and end-use questions from both US regulators and their own banks.

### 2. 🤖 **Huawei Extends Its Tau Chip Design Approach to Telecom Base Stations**

*Digital Today · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Huawei has applied its "Tau scaling law" design principle to next-generation wireless base station hardware, widening a workaround strategy built around architecture rather than smaller transistors.

Huawei says its UBBPi baseband products deliver a 1.3-fold improvement in average perceived network quality alongside a 40% cut in energy use — company figures, not independently verified. The Tau scaling law, unveiled in May 2026 with an accompanying LogicFolding architecture, targets performance equivalent to a 1.4-nanometre process by 2031 without the advanced lithography tools that export controls keep out of China. Huawei has already shipped the approach in the Kirin 9050 Pro smartphone chip and plans to extend logic folding to its Ascend 990 AI accelerator around 2030.

**Why it matters:** If architectural efficiency can partly substitute for process-node access, the practical ceiling that export controls impose on Chinese AI infrastructure is softer than the node-gap numbers suggest. For Asian buyers of network and AI hardware, it also means a widening split between US- and China-aligned stacks that will eventually show up in procurement, certification and data-residency decisions.

### 3. 🌐 **Former CIA Deputy Director Urges US to Spy on Chinese Companies**

*South China Morning Post · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** David Cohen, CIA deputy director under Presidents Obama and Biden, has argued that Washington should lift its ban on economic espionage to help US firms keep their technology lead over China.

Writing in Foreign Affairs and reported by the South China Morning Post, Cohen said the US lead in several critical advanced technologies is being contested or erased by China, and that a 2014 Obama policy directive barring intelligence collection aimed at giving US companies a commercial advantage no longer serves American interests. He called on the United States to formally end that prohibition — a break from decades of declared policy that distinguished US intelligence practice from the commercial spying Washington has long attributed to Beijing.

**Why it matters:** This is advocacy, not policy, but it signals how far the mainstream US national-security debate has drifted toward treating commercial technology as a state asset. Multinationals operating across both blocs — including banks and payment firms running AI workloads in Asia — should assume rising scrutiny of cross-border technical collaboration whichever way the policy lands.

## Key Takeaways

- The enforcement centre of gravity has shifted from licensing rules to intelligence and investigations, with Singapore- and Malaysia-linked entities repeatedly named in diversion allegations.
- Huawei's response to controls is increasingly architectural rather than lithographic, which makes China's progress harder to measure using node size alone.
- Both capitals are moving toward treating commercial AI technology as strategic state property, raising the compliance cost of operating across both ecosystems.

## What to Watch

Watch for any Commerce Department or Singapore regulatory follow-up on the Megaspeed allegations and for Nvidia's next formal statement on its Southeast Asian customer base, plus whether Huawei publishes third-party verification of its Tau-based baseband performance claims.
