# Payments Brief: Wall Street Banks Fund Project Faro Stablecoin

**Today:** A 21-bank consortium has put real money behind a dollar stablecoin to rival Tether and Circle, just as Visa and Rain extend blockchain and AI-agent rails deeper into everyday card settlement.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Goldman, Citi and 19 Other Banks Fund Project Faro Stablecoin**

*Blockhead · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A consortium of 21 major banks has committed roughly USD 270 million to launch a bank-issued dollar stablecoin targeting a first-half 2027 debut.

Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Deutsche Bank, Banco Santander, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, UBS and MUFG are among the backers of Project Faro, with the funding covering five years of operating costs for a US-incorporated issuer. A euro-denominated token is planned as a priority follow-on, and the venture is still searching for a CEO and final branding. Bloomberg reported the funding figure; the banks' own September announcement had not disclosed one.

**Why it matters:** Tether and Circle control roughly 85% of the near-$290 billion stablecoin market, and this is one of the clearest signs yet that traditional banks intend to contest that share using their own regulatory standing and client networks. For BaaS and payments players in Asia, a credible bank-backed dollar stablecoin would reshape the competitive and compliance landscape for cross-border settlement partnerships.

### 2. 💳 **Visa Adds Monad Network to Stablecoin Settlement Options**

*GlobeNewswire · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa has added the Monad blockchain as a new settlement option for card issuers and acquirers settling obligations in stablecoins.

Monad is fully EVM-compatible and offers sub-second finality versus the traditional five-business-day bank settlement cycle, letting institutions extend existing Ethereum-based custody and compliance tooling rather than rebuild it. As of September 2026, Visa reported a USD 25 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate, roughly 10 times higher year-over-year, across more than 225 live stablecoin-linked card programs.

**Why it matters:** Faster, always-on settlement is invisible to merchants and cardholders but materially cuts working-capital costs for issuers and acquirers. The scale of Visa's existing stablecoin volume shows this is no longer experimental infrastructure, which should inform how regional card programs evaluate settlement partners.

### 3. 🤖 **Rain Launches Agent Vaults for AI-Driven Card Payments**

*PR Newswire · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Payments infrastructure firm Rain has launched Agent Vaults, letting AI agents spend from an owner-funded balance using single-use virtual Visa or Mastercard credentials.

Each purchase generates a scoped card with fixed amount, merchant and expiry that retires after one transaction, and agents can also send funds to bank accounts in more than 80 countries. Rain is a Visa and Mastercard principal member, and the vaults carry Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay credentials to signal the account owner's consent to merchants and issuers.

**Why it matters:** This addresses a real gap in agentic commerce — giving an AI agent raw card numbers or a third-party wallet both carry control and branding problems. Card issuers and program managers should watch how scoped, single-use credentials become the default control model before agent-initiated payments scale.

### 4. 🌐 **Boost and Medical Tourism Association Cut Cross-Border Card Fees**

*PR Newswire · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Boost Payment Solutions has partnered with the Medical Tourism Association to let healthcare providers in more than 180 countries accept US-issued commercial cards without cross-border assessment fees.

The Boost 100XB platform lets payers use their existing US-issued commercial card, with transactions flowing exclusively over the Mastercard network and no foreign bank identification number required from providers. Boost cited FXC Intelligence data projecting cross-border B2B payments will grow from USD 34.8 trillion in 2025 to USD 51.2 trillion by 2033.

**Why it matters:** Removing cross-border card fees is a concrete, near-term lever for expanding card acceptance among overseas providers who otherwise default to wire transfers, a model with direct relevance to any B2B cross-border payments or healthcare-adjacent corridor in Asia.

## Key Takeaways

- Banks are no longer watching stablecoins from the sidelines: Project Faro shows incumbents are willing to fund multi-year infrastructure to compete with Tether and Circle directly.
- Card networks are quietly industrializing blockchain settlement — Visa's $25 billion run rate shows stablecoin rails are now a scale business line, not a pilot.
- Agentic commerce is converging on scoped, single-use tokenized credentials (Rain, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay) rather than shared card numbers, setting an early control standard issuers will need to support.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Project Faro names a CEO and discloses its blockchain and reserve structure, and whether Visa or Mastercard disclose additional stablecoin settlement network partners following the Monad addition.
