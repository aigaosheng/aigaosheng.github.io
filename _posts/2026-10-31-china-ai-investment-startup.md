# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Kuaishou's Kling AI Closes Record $2.9B Round

**Today:** A record-setting single-round raise for Kuaishou's AI video unit and a near-halving of embodied AI deal flow show China's AI capital splitting sharply between proven revenue leaders and everyone else.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Kuaishou's Kling AI Completes RMB 19.8 Billion Video-AI Funding Round**

*10jqka (Straight Flush Financial) · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Kling AI, the video-generation unit Kuaishou spun off as an independent entity, has closed what is being described as the largest single funding round ever for a global AI video company, raising roughly RMB 19.8 billion (about $2.9 billion) at a post-money valuation exceeding RMB 120 billion (around $17.9 billion).

Investors paying into the final closing include Shanghai Guofang Digital Technology, China's national AI industry investment fund, Alibaba Cloud, Tencent and Baidu, alongside a mix of state, PE/VC and media-industry capital; two investors that missed payment deadlines had their allocations reassigned to replacement backers. Kuaishou's holding vehicle, Lucky Labs, retains 66.68% of Kling's equity and 52.45% of voting rights after the deal. Kling reports more than 100 million users across 224 countries and roughly 50,000 enterprise clients, with annualized revenue approaching $500 million.

**Why it matters:** The round shows Chinese mega-cap platforms and state funds concentrating capital in AI units with demonstrated monetization rather than early-stage bets, and Kling's valuation now tops Kuaishou's own Hong Kong market capitalization. For enterprise AI and content-tooling buyers in Singapore and the region, a better-capitalized Kling is likely to compete more aggressively on price and distribution against Western video-generation rivals.

### 2. 📊 **China's Embodied AI Funding Nearly Halves in October**

*21st Century Business Herald (21财经) · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Funding deals in China's embodied AI and humanoid robotics sector fell to 33 in October, worth a combined RMB 10.1 billion (about $1.4 billion), down from 60 deals worth RMB 11 billion in September, according to IT Orange data cited by the outlet.

The report frames the pullback as a shift from "imagination pricing," where capital chased prototypes and concept demonstrations, toward "delivery pricing" based on confirmed orders and repeat enterprise customers. October's total is well below the sector's June 2026 peak of 55 deals worth RMB 30.9 billion, with the slowdown coinciding with weak post-listing share performance among already-public robotics names and tighter Hong Kong exchange scrutiny of new listings.

**Why it matters:** The cooldown signals China's hottest AI-hardware vertical is entering a maturity phase where commercial traction, not hype, will gate future rounds and IPOs. Singapore and regional investors or corporates evaluating co-investment or supply partnerships with Chinese embodied AI firms should expect slower valuations and tighter diligence through the rest of the year.

## Key Takeaways

- Capital is concentrating in proven revenue leaders: Kling's $2.9bn raise, backed by Alibaba, Tencent and state funds, mirrors the pattern behind DeepSeek's recent mega-round, while early-stage and embodied AI deal flow cools.
- Valuation discipline is returning across China's AI sector, with embodied AI deal counts down nearly 50% month-on-month as investors demand order books over prototypes.
- State and municipal AI investment funds remain the common anchor across the sector's largest rounds, reinforcing Beijing's preference for strategically important AI assets to carry state-linked capital ahead of any public listing.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Kuaishou pursues a separate listing path or further stake sales for Kling now that its implied valuation exceeds its parent's Hong Kong market cap, and whether November's embodied AI funding tally stabilizes or extends October's near-50% drop as commercialization data from firms like Unitree and AgiBot feeds into investor decisions.
