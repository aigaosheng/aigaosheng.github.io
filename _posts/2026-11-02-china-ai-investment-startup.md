# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: DeepSeek Funding Breakdown Reveals State Capital Surge

**Today:** Fresh detail on DeepSeek's mega-round, a new embodied-AI raise and a wave of young-founder megadeals show how Chinese capital is both deepening state involvement in national AI champions and sharpening commercial discipline elsewhere in the sector.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **DeepSeek's ¥105.5 Billion Round Investor List Surfaces**

*PEdaily (投资界) · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Newly disclosed investor-level detail on DeepSeek's latest funding round shows state-linked capital's share nearly tripling, reinforcing Beijing's deepening strategic stake in the country's leading AI lab ahead of a planned listing.

Citing reporting from The Information, PEdaily detailed how the round — previously reported in October at roughly ¥100 billion ($15 billion) — closed at ¥105.5 billion, valuing DeepSeek at about ¥615.5 billion. Tencent and CATL each roughly doubled their prior stakes, new backers including Xiaohongshu and Meituan joined, and state-linked entities such as China Mobile, China Telecom and CITIC Securities took part; the article reports state-owned capital's share of the round jumped from about 2% in the prior round to 27%, with external investors said to have waived voting rights under a five-year lockup.

**Why it matters:** A rising state-capital share in China's top frontier-AI lab signals tighter government alignment as DeepSeek prepares for a possible Shanghai STAR Market listing — a governance and ownership shift that enterprise and fintech partners relying on Chinese AI infrastructure should track closely.

### 2. 🤖 **RoboScience Closes A2 Round for Embodied-AI Platform**

*PEdaily (投资界) · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Embodied-AI startup RoboScience raised several hundred million yuan in a fresh A2 round, extending China's robotics financing wave to a company less than two years old.

The round was co-led by Amber Capital, Hongruida Investment and Shanghai Semiconductor Industry Investment, with Qihoo 360's strategic investment arm and several listed-company platforms also participating. Founded in December 2024 by two Stanford AI Lab alumni, RoboScience has built a physics simulation engine, a general-purpose embodied model called Visics, and a wheeled humanoid robot, and plans to use the new capital for model iteration and scaled deployment in logistics, retail and industrial settings.

**Why it matters:** The raise adds to a embodied-AI financing boom that industry trackers say grew roughly fivefold year-on-year in H1 2026, a trend increasingly feeding automation use cases relevant to logistics and back-office operations across Asian enterprises.

### 3. 📊 **Chinese Capital Demands Shipped Robots, Not Just Demos**

*21st Century Business Herald (21财经) · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Chinese investors are increasingly conditioning embodied-AI funding on near-term commercial proof — factory orders and shipped units — in contrast to the platform-potential bets common among US investors in the sector.

The analysis contrasts US backers pricing robotics "brain" platforms on potential, citing Physical Intelligence's roughly $20 billion valuation despite minimal revenue, with Chinese investors in startups such as Ant Lingbo (蚂蚁灵波) and Xinghai Tu demanding evidence of deployment and customer ROI before committing capital. A Huagai Capital investment director was quoted saying valuation is shifting "from demo to orders," with firms lacking orders facing survival pressure.

**Why it matters:** A more order-driven funding discipline could make Chinese embodied-AI valuations more durable than platform-style bets elsewhere, a useful benchmark for investors and enterprise buyers comparing vendor stability across markets.

### 4. 📊 **Young Founders Pull In Outsized Rounds Amid FOMO Warnings**

*China Securities Journal, via 21st Century Business Herald · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A cohort of China's youngest AI founders is raising unusually large, fast rounds at billion-yuan-plus valuations, even as some investors caution against FOMO-driven due diligence.

The report names founders including Tsinghua PhD Liu Songming's LiberAI (over ¥10 billion raised across six rounds), Stanford dropout Hong Letong's Axiom ($200 million A-round at an ¥11 billion valuation), and 22-year-old Peking University graduate Chen Boyuan's Inverse Matrix (over $100 million at a valuation above ¥5 billion). One investor described completing a funding decision within 30 minutes, while another warned that diligence thins "the younger someone is."

**Why it matters:** The velocity and scale of these rounds highlight both genuine technical enthusiasm and rising governance risk in China's AI venture market, a signal for institutional LPs sizing exposure to the sector.

## Key Takeaways

- State-linked capital is taking a visibly larger role in China's flagship AI labs, with DeepSeek's state-ownership share nearly tripling in its latest round.
- Embodied AI and robotics remain the most active funding category, but investors are increasingly tying checks to delivery and order metrics rather than platform potential.
- A new generation of very young founders is raising rapid, outsized rounds, drawing both enthusiastic capital and explicit diligence concerns from investors.

## What to Watch

Watch for DeepSeek's next steps toward a Shanghai STAR Market filing with CITIC Securities, and whether more embodied-AI rounds in the coming weeks explicitly tie financing terms to delivery or order volumes rather than demo milestones.
