# **US & China AI Brief: US Expands Controls on Overseas AI Compute**

**Today:** A new US export-control rule takes effect, extending restrictions beyond physical chip shipments to remote access to advanced computing—and directly affecting Singapore's semiconductor supply chain.

## **Top Stories**

### **1. 🏦 US Expands Controls on Remote Access to Advanced Chips**

*US Bureau of Industry and Security · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US has made it harder for China-linked companies to access advanced US computing power through overseas data centres, while adding new licensing requirements for advanced-chip transfers to Singapore and four other countries.

The Bureau of Industry and Security's interim final rule, published in the Federal Register and effective November 19, requires licences for certain advanced semiconductor exports, re-exports and domestic transfers when overseas data centres provide significant computing capacity to companies headquartered in China or other restricted jurisdictions. It also introduces licensing requirements for covered advanced chips destined for Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, subject to specified exceptions. The rule formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, which BIS says was never enforced.

**Why it matters:** Singapore is explicitly named in the new controls, creating direct compliance implications for chip distributors, data-centre operators, cloud providers and AI infrastructure investors. Companies should review end-user ownership, beneficial control, compute access arrangements and licensing responsibilities—not just the physical destination of hardware.

### **2. 🌐 TSMC and Samsung Suspend Some China Chip Shipments**

*Financial Times · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** TSMC and Samsung have paused some chip shipments to Chinese customers while seeking licences under the latest US export controls.

The new requirements affect advanced chips manufactured using US technology, including products shipped through independent packaging and testing companies. The Financial Times reports that affected customers include Baidu's Kunlun unit and ByteDance, while TSMC supplies smartphone-chip customers including Xiaomi, Oppo and Vivo. The licensing changes could also affect advanced-process chips intended for consumer products.

**Why it matters:** The impact of US controls is extending beyond AI accelerators into the wider semiconductor supply chain. Chinese technology companies may face longer procurement cycles, while Asian chipmakers and customers must reassess licensing exposure, shipment schedules and supply alternatives.

## **Key Takeaways**

- **Remote compute is now part of the control perimeter:** Export compliance increasingly concerns who can access computing capacity, not simply where chips are shipped.
- **Singapore faces direct implementation implications:** Advanced-chip transfers to Singapore are covered by new licensing requirements, making customer due diligence and ownership checks more consequential.
- **Supply-chain effects are spreading:** TSMC and Samsung's reported shipment suspensions illustrate how regulatory changes can affect Chinese customers across AI and consumer electronics.

## **What to Watch**

Watch for BIS guidance on licensing procedures, implementation details and the January 19, 2027 comment deadline. For Singapore, the immediate signal will be how chip distributors, data-centre operators and cloud providers adapt customer screening and licensing workflows.

## **MY TAKE**

The strategic contest is increasingly about control of the full AI compute stack: chips, manufacturing, packaging, data centres and access to cloud capacity. The new US rule raises compliance costs for international infrastructure providers, including those in Southeast Asia. China will have stronger incentives to accelerate domestic semiconductor capabilities and diversify supply chains. For Asian businesses, the priority is to assess exposure across the entire compute supply chain rather than treating export controls as a procurement issue alone.
