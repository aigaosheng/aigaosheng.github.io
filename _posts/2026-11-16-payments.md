# Payments Brief: Toss Plans Stablecoin Wallet for 30 Million Users

**Today:** A major Asian super-app is moving stablecoins into mainstream consumer wallets, while Brazil's central bank signals how tightly regulators may fence stablecoin-based cross-border transfers.

## Top Stories

### 1. 💳 **Toss Prepares Non-Custodial ETH, USDT and USDC Wallet for 30 Million Users**

*crypto.news · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea's Toss is reportedly about to embed a stablecoin-capable crypto wallet in its app, putting stablecoins within reach of one of Asia's largest consumer user bases.

Per crypto.news, which cites Edaily and Electronic Times, Toss (Viva Republica) plans to launch "Toss Coin Wallet" for its roughly 30 million registered users, with a rollout reported for the week of November 16. The wallet supports ETH, USDT and USDC, is non-custodial, and lets users send to phone contacts instead of blockchain addresses. The first release covers sending, receiving and balance checks only; there is no exchange, won on-ramp or merchant stablecoin acceptance yet. Separately, Toss Bank reportedly began a first-stage Solana/USDC overseas remittance trial, and Toss is preparing to seek virtual asset service provider registration.

**Why it matters:** The launch shows how consumer fintechs in Asia can distribute stablecoins without building new rails. The harder question is whether Toss can later connect the wallet to its network of more than 500,000 merchants, which depends on Korean regulatory approval.

### 2. 🏦 **Brazil Central Bank Official Warns Some Stablecoin Transfers May Amount to Money Laundering**

*crypto.news · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Brazil's central bank is drawing a line between stablecoins used as FX plumbing and stablecoins sold directly to customers for cross-border transfers, and further restrictions may follow.

Per crypto.news, citing Livecoins, Gerson Romantini, the central bank's head of foreign exchange, told an industry meeting that some "stablecoin sandwich" structures resemble the informal "dólar cabo" market. He described one model as permitted, where a regulated institution uses virtual assets as infrastructure for a conventional FX service, and another as prohibited, where the institution sells stablecoins directly to customers. The article notes that Resolution BCB 561, effective October 1, bars virtual assets in settlements between regulated eFX providers and overseas counterparties. It also reports, based on an unnamed source and without confirmation from the central bank, that a November 19 meeting could reserve some stablecoin services for banks.

**Why it matters:** Brazil is a leading indicator for how emerging-market regulators may treat stablecoin remittance flows that sidestep FX controls, which is relevant to Asian corridors with similar capital-flow sensitivities. Fintechs and payment providers should expect licensing and structuring scrutiny.

## Key Takeaways

- Distribution is shifting to consumer super-apps: stablecoin access is arriving through existing wallets and contact-based transfers rather than standalone crypto products.
- Regulators are focusing on how stablecoins are sold and settled, not just on whether they exist; product structure may decide what is permitted.
- Cross-border remittance is the common use case in both stories, from Toss Bank's USDC trial to Brazil's FX concerns.

## What to Watch

Whether Toss Coin Wallet goes live this week as reported, and whether the Brazilian central bank's reported November 19 meeting produces any confirmed restrictions on fintech stablecoin services.
