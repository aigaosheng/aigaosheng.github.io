# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Dongshan Precision Slips in Hong Kong Debut

**Today:** A light deal day. One soft AI supply-chain IPO and two hardware-led private rounds show that China AI investors now want revenue and order books, not narratives.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Dongshan Precision Slips in Hong Kong Debut as AI IPO Appetite Cools**
*The Standard (Reuters) · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A HK$9.88 billion listing by an AI-server component supplier fell on day one, which suggests Hong Kong buyers are pricing China AI deals more cautiously.

Suzhou Dongshan Precision Manufacturing opened at HK$82.40, below its HK$86.80 offer price, and traded about 2.1% lower on Wednesday. The company had priced about 7.5% below its HK$93.80 ceiling and did not use its option to upsize. Retail investors took up only 0.52 times their allocation, while the international book was 3.17 times covered. Cornerstone investors, including JPMorgan Asset Management, Alibaba Investment, Aurora SF and CPE Bodhi, took half the offering under a six-month lock-up. The debut came after robot maker Changzhou Microintelligence and a tourism group delayed their Hong Kong share sales, citing market conditions. In the view of one uSMART Securities analyst, the local AI supply-chain theme has passed its peak enthusiasm.

**Why it matters:** Weak retail demand, pricing below the range and a delayed robotics IPO are useful signals for the pipeline of Chinese AI and robotics listings now preparing to come to Hong Kong. Issuers may need to accept lower valuations to get deals done.

### 2. 📊 **HOVERAir Maker Zero Zero Robotics Reportedly Reaches US$1 Billion Valuation**
*TechNode · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Chinese maker of AI-driven self-flying cameras has reportedly become a unicorn, backed by revenue estimates rather than only technology claims.

Citing Leiphone, TechNode reports that Zero Zero Robotics reached a US$1 billion valuation in its latest round. People close to the company estimate fourth-quarter revenue at RMB 600 million to RMB 700 million. This year the company disclosed Series D and D-plus rounds, with investors including Boyu Capital, Xiang He Capital, Delian Capital and an Ant Group affiliate. Stanford PhD graduates founded the company in 2014. It makes portable cameras that launch from a user's palm and follow subjects automatically.

**Why it matters:** Consumer AI hardware with real sales is still raising growth capital while pure model developers face tougher public-market scrutiny. An Ant Group-linked investor in the round shows fintech groups continuing to make strategic bets on AI devices.

### 3. 📊 **Embodied-AI Startup Zhongke Huiling Raises Series A Extension on Factory Orders**
*Dealroom · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A CAS-backed robotics startup raised several hundred million yuan by pointing to a concrete industrial order book, not demos.

Beijing Zhongke Huiling Robot Technology announced the Series A extension on October 26. Investors included Zhilai Investment, Yonghua Investment, Maotian Investment, Yuanhe Chenkun, Guoke Kezhi and Luning Investment. Founded in 2023 with backing from the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Institute of Automation, the company builds robot bodies, an embodied "brain" and dexterous hands for manufacturing, mining, energy and commercial services. It says it holds more than US$55M in shoe-making robot orders for 2026, with production planned in the thousands of units. A lead investor said the firm now prioritizes real-world commercial deployment.

**Why it matters:** Embodied-AI funding in China is going to companies that can show production volumes and paying industrial customers. Investors should expect deployment metrics to become the main valuation test in this sector.

## Key Takeaways
- Hong Kong's market for China AI-linked listings is now selective. Deals are pricing below range, retail demand is weak, and at least one robot maker has delayed its offering.
- Private rounds are going to AI hardware companies that disclose revenue or order books, such as Zero Zero's quarterly revenue estimates and Zhongke Huiling's footwear-robot orders.
- Strategic and fintech-linked capital, including an Ant Group affiliate and Alibaba Investment as a cornerstone investor, remains an active source of support for China AI deals.

## What to Watch
Watch how Dongshan trades through its first week and whether Changzhou Microintelligence sets a new date for its delayed Hong Kong IPO. Both will show whether the window for China AI and robotics listings is narrowing or only repricing.
