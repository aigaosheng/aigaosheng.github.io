# **Google’s Opal Levels Up: AI Agents Now Turn Text Prompts Into Interactive Workflows**

In a move that blurs the line between natural language and automated computing, **Google has upgraded its Opal platform with a brand-new AI agent** that empowers users to build *agentic workflows* simply by typing what they want. This isn’t your typical “run this prompt and get a response” feature — Opal now can plan, select tools, ask follow-up questions, and execute multi-step tasks on your behalf. ([blog.google][1])

## **From Static Prompts to Dynamic Intelligence**

First launched in **mid-2025** as an experimental no-code “vibe-coding” tool, Opal lets anyone — from creators to product teams — build **mini-AI apps (or “mini-apps”)** through plain English prompts and visual workflows. Traditionally, Opal required users to define each logical step and tool in advance. With the new upgrade, that changes dramatically. ([Yahoo! Tech][2])

Instead of manually picking models or sequencing steps, **the upgraded Opal agent interprets your high-level goal**, then…

* **Plans the workflow** — breaking your objective into executable steps.
* **Chooses tools and models** automatically (like Google Sheets, search, or other Gemini-powered services).
* **Engages interactively** — asking questions or offering choices when needed to refine the task.
* **Maintains memory across sessions**, letting your apps remember context like ongoing lists or preferences. ([Dataconomy][3])

Whether you’re building a shopping list manager, internal briefing generator, or interactive storytelling tool, Opal’s agent steps make workflows feel less like rigid scripts and more like *collaborative assistants* that help refine and execute your vision. ([blog.google][1])

## **Why This Matters**

This upgrade reflects a growing shift in the AI world toward **“agentic AI.”** Instead of just responding to static prompts, these systems can **reason, choose the right tools, and take action autonomously** — all while maintaining conversational feedback loops with users.

In practical terms, you no longer need to be a developer to build dynamic, multi-step automations that adapt in real time. That puts Opal in direct competition with platforms like **Microsoft Power Automate** and **Zapier**, but with intelligence that can ask clarifying questions and make workflow decisions on the fly. ([MLQ][4])

The update also underscores Google’s strategy of integrating its AI ecosystem tightly — with Gemini models powering the logic, Sheets providing memory, and Opal tying it all together in a no-code canvas. ([Yahoo! Tech][2])

## **The Bigger Picture: Agents Everywhere**

This isn’t just a one-off feature — it’s part of a broader industry trend. Tech companies (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.) are racing to make AI agents that don’t just answer questions but **take action, plan tasks, and automate outcomes**. That evolution has ripple effects for productivity tools, enterprise automation, and the future of work itself. ([blog.google][1])

## **Glossary**

* **Agentic Workflow** – An AI process where the system not only responds to inputs but plans, executes, and adapts tasks autonomously.
* **No-Code / Vibe Coding** – Software creation techniques that don’t require writing code; instead, users express intent via natural language or visual interfaces.
* **Gemini 3 Flash Model** – Google’s powerful AI model that underpins Opal’s agent logic.
* **Dynamic Routing** – The AI’s ability to choose different execution paths based on task requirements and real-time input.
