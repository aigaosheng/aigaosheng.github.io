# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Alibaba Files T-Head Chip Unit for IPO

**Today:** Alibaba's move to list its AI chip unit in Hong Kong is the clearest signal yet that China's capital markets are being asked to independently price AI hardware bets, even as early-stage money keeps flowing into niche AI tooling and infrastructure startups.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Alibaba Files to Spin Off AI Chip Unit T-Head for Hong Kong IPO**

*South China Morning Post · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Alibaba is seeking to raise roughly US$2–3 billion by taking its AI chip design unit T-Head public in Hong Kong, testing investor appetite for standalone China AI-chip valuations.

T-Head designs semiconductors for AI workloads, including its Zhenwu M890 and V900 processors; the V900, launched in September 2026, is positioned as China's most powerful domestic AI chip, with mass production targeted for the first quarter of 2027. Proceeds from the listing could exceed the combined amounts raised in recent Hong Kong listings by fellow chip designers Biren Technology and Iluvatar CoreX. Analysts note the business was previously folded into Alibaba Cloud and difficult to value on its own.

**Why it matters:** A successful listing would give public investors direct exposure to China's AI-chip buildout and could prompt other large tech groups to carve out and separately value their AI hardware units, a pattern Singapore and regional investors tracking Hong Kong's tech IPO pipeline should watch closely.

### 2. 🤖 **AI-Native Chip Design Startup Panfuncs Raises Near-RMB 100M Angel Round**

*PEdaily · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Shanghai-based Panfuncs has raised close to RMB 100 million in its latest round, backing a platform that uses multi-agent AI to automate chip design from natural-language briefs through to tape-out-ready files.

The round was led by the state-backed Shanghai Future Industry Fund, with Shanghai Information Investment, Shanghai Science & Innovation Group and several other investors participating. Founded in March 2026 by Tsinghua-trained engineers, the company has completed four funding rounds in under a year and says a customer chip designed on its platform has already returned from fabrication with a successful first silicon run and entered mass production.

**Why it matters:** Investors are betting that AI can compress chip design cycles industry-wide, a capability that matters for Asia's broader semiconductor ecosystem as China pushes to reduce reliance on foreign design tools and talent.

### 3. 📊 **SiC Power Semiconductor Maker Lipus Closes Series B for AI Data Centre Push**

*PEdaily · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Wuxi-based Lipus Semiconductor has closed a roughly RMB 100 million Series B round led by Shanghai Xinwei Capital to expand silicon carbide and IGBT power modules for AI data centres.

The company's power module solutions also serve electric vehicles, renewable energy and grid applications, but it is directing new capital toward qualifying AI data centre (AIDC) product samples for reliability, with volume production targeted for 2027 from a Yangzhou facility planned to reach 1.5 million SiC modules annually by April 2027.

**Why it matters:** The round underscores how China's AI infrastructure boom is pulling capital into power electronics and other unglamorous hardware layers that underpin data centre build-outs, a supply chain Singapore-based data centre operators and investors also depend on.

### 4. 🤖 **Underwater AI Robotics Startup Bozhan Secures First Institutional Round**

*PEdaily · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Shanghai-based Bozhan Robotics has closed its first institutional funding round, worth tens of millions of yuan, to commercialise autonomous hull-cleaning robots for ships.

The round was led by Daotong Investment with Shida Marine participating. Bozhan's Metis-series robots combine visual recognition with AI-based path planning and fuse visual, inertial and odometer data to achieve centimetre-level underwater positioning, allowing the robots to clean ship hulls autonomously rather than under continuous remote control.

**Why it matters:** The deal shows investor interest extending beyond land-based humanoid and industrial robots into maritime applications, relevant for Singapore and other Asian port and shipping hubs exploring automation of hull maintenance and inspection.

## Key Takeaways

- Capital markets are now being asked to price standalone AI-chip businesses, with Alibaba's T-Head following Biren Technology and Iluvatar CoreX toward Hong Kong listings.
- Early-stage funding continues to target narrow AI tooling niches, such as chip-design automation and autonomous underwater robotics, at sub-RMB100 million ticket sizes.
- State-linked funds, including Shanghai Future Industry Fund and Shanghai Xinwei Capital, remain anchor investors in hard-tech AI rounds, reflecting the continued role of government-backed capital in China's AI build-out.

## What to Watch

Watch for T-Head's formal HKEX prospectus filing and any disclosed valuation or Alibaba retained-stake figures, plus whether Panfuncs or Lipus disclose follow-on rounds as their AI chip-design and AI data centre products move toward volume production.
