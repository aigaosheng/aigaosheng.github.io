# Payments Brief: Taiwan Drafts Bank-Led Stablecoin Issuance Rules

**Today:** Two major regulators moved on stablecoins within a day of each other, while Singapore and India both advanced payments infrastructure, giving practitioners a dense day of structural news to track.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Taiwan's FSC Proposes Bank-Controlled Ownership Rules for Stablecoin Issuers**

*Blockhead · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Taiwan's draft stablecoin framework would effectively bar non-bank tech firms from issuing stablecoins independently, requiring banks to hold controlling stakes in any issuer.

Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission released draft rules under which only banks, bank-invested companies, financial holding groups or FSC-designated entities may apply to issue stablecoins, according to Blockhead. A single bank or financial holding company must own more than 25% of an issuing entity, with combined bank ownership exceeding 50%; non-bank applicants must still route through a bank-led structure. The rules build on Taiwan's Virtual Asset Service Act, which mandates fully reserved, trust-held, fiat-pegged tokens with no yield payments, and would take effect alongside that law, estimated no earlier than Q1 2027.

**Why it matters:** The rules set a template other Asian regulators may watch closely: they tie stablecoin issuance tightly to the licensed banking system rather than opening the door to standalone fintech or Big Tech issuers, reshaping the competitive field for any firm eyeing a Taiwan dollar or USD-pegged token.

### 2. 🏦 **Nigeria's Central Bank Sets Capital Floors for Stablecoin and Virtual Asset Operators**

*The Gazette (Nigeria) · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nigeria's CBN has issued binding capital and licensing rules for stablecoin issuers and virtual asset operators, with a 180-day deadline to comply or shut down.

The Central Bank of Nigeria's "Regulations for the Operations of Non-Security Virtual Assets," issued November 5 and reported November 6, set minimum capital at ₦10 billion for naira-referenced stablecoin issuers, ₦15 billion for foreign-currency-referenced issuers, and ₦1 billion for other virtual asset service providers. Existing operators must register within 30 days, file supervisory reports within 60 days, and submit full licence applications within 180 days or cease operations; algorithmic stablecoins are banned outright and peer-to-peer platforms require BVN verification and CBN approval.

**Why it matters:** The rules formalise one of Africa's largest crypto and remittance markets under central-bank oversight, raising the bar for payment and remittance firms operating naira or FX-stablecoin rails there, and adding to a global pattern of regulators separating licensed stablecoin issuance from unregulated token activity.

### 3. 🌐 **UnionPay International and Tencent's TenPay Global Sign MOU on Cross-Border Remittances**

*Media OutReach Newswire via The Manila Times · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Two of China's largest payment networks are exploring a joint cross-border remittance offering, their first collaboration in this segment.

UnionPay International and TenPay Global signed a memorandum of understanding at Hong Kong FinTech Week to jointly explore remittance services spanning personal transfers, corporate payroll and international insurance payouts, aiming to cut last-mile costs and foreign-exchange friction for enterprise clients. The companies said they intend to combine UnionPay's network reach, which already spans 14 APEC economies through UnionPay MoneyExpress, with TenPay's platform and use-case strengths.

**Why it matters:** A tie-up between UnionPay and Tencent's payments arm would pool two of the largest Chinese payment rails behind cross-border remittances, intensifying competition for banks and money-transfer operators serving Chinese outbound and diaspora payment corridors across Asia.

### 4. 🤖 **NPCI Launches AI and Blockchain Subsidiary NPCI Tech Solutions**

*Fintech Singapore · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's National Payments Corporation is spinning up a dedicated technology arm to push AI and blockchain work across its payment infrastructure.

NPCI Tech Solutions Limited (NTSL), approved by NPCI's board and the Reserve Bank of India, becomes NPCI's fourth wholly owned subsidiary, with Vishal Kanvaty named Managing Director and CEO after nearly a decade at the parent organisation. NTSL will offer Global Capability Centre-style technology services and support existing NPCI initiatives including the eKYC Setu identity layer and the Unified Ticketing Platform, per Fintech Singapore's report.

**Why it matters:** Carving out a separate AI and blockchain entity signals NPCI intends to commercialise and scale its technology stack beyond UPI itself, a move other national payment switches in Asia may watch as a model for monetising payments infrastructure expertise.

### 5. 💳 **Cross-Border Payments Firm Pyvio Secures Singapore's Major Payment Institution Licence**

*TechNode Global · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Pyvio Network Technology has obtained MAS's Major Payment Institution licence, clearing the way for it to scale cross-border payment services across Asia-Pacific from Singapore.

The licence, effective November 1, covers account issuance, domestic and cross-border money transfer, merchant acquisition and e-money issuance, and exempts Pyvio from the transaction-volume and e-money float thresholds that apply to standard Payment Institution licensees, according to TechNode Global. The company said it plans to use its Singapore entity as a regional hub for cross-border payment services, though it has not disclosed a launch timeline or volumes.

**Why it matters:** Each new MPI licensee adds to Singapore's dense cluster of regulated cross-border payment providers, reinforcing the city-state's position as the preferred regulatory base for firms building payment rails across Southeast Asia.

### 6. 💳 **Remote Launches Fee-Free Virtual Expense Cards for Globally Distributed Employees**

*Fintech Singapore · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** HR and payroll platform Remote has added virtual expense cards that let employees hired via its Employer of Record service spend directly instead of waiting for reimbursement.

Cards typically activate within one business day, carry no platform or per-card fees, and let employers set spending caps and purchase restrictions, with transactions feeding automatically into Remote's expense system for receipt capture, per Fintech Singapore. CFO Michiel Boere said the feature ties employment, payroll and spending data together in one place.

**Why it matters:** Embedding card issuance into global payroll and EOR platforms is becoming a standard expectation, pushing competing HR-tech and payroll providers to add similar card and expense capabilities or risk losing share of distributed-workforce spend.

## Key Takeaways

- Stablecoin regulation is converging on a common pattern globally: Taiwan and Nigeria both moved this week to tie issuance tightly to licensed, well-capitalised institutions rather than open platforms.
- China's largest payment networks are consolidating around cross-border remittance partnerships rather than competing head-on, echoing the broader trend of network-to-network tie-ups over new market entry.
- Singapore and India both used today's news cycle to deepen payments infrastructure rather than launch consumer products, with a new MAS-licensed entrant and a new NPCI technology arm.

## What to Watch

Watch for Taiwan's FSC to publish explanatory documents clarifying whether the 25%/50% bank-ownership thresholds are final after its consultation period, and for further detail on how Nigeria's CBN will coordinate licensing with the SEC during the 180-day compliance window.
