# AI for Society Brief: AI Data Centers Burden Black Communities

**Today:** New reporting and research highlight who bears AI infrastructure’s local costs, how AI is reshaping early-career work, and what helps employees and consumers navigate adoption.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **AI Data Centers Burden Black Communities**

*The Associated Press · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** AP’s investigation finds that Black communities in the United States are more likely to host data centres and less likely to have a public hearing before projects are approved.

The report describes communities facing potential pollution and other local burdens while receiving fewer of the economic benefits associated with data centre development. Its analysis cites research finding that majority-Black communities are more than twice as likely as majority-white communities to host a data centre. AP also reports that projects with prior public hearings were blocked more often than those without them.

**Why it matters:** For AI infrastructure developers and their customers, community consultation and transparent impact reporting can shape public acceptance and project delivery. The findings point to an equity question that also matters in Asia, where data-centre expansion competes for land, power and water.

### 2. 👥 **AI Hiring Slowdown Raises Entry-Level Concerns**

*Axios · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new US workforce report finds fewer entry-level opportunities in AI-intensive industries, while cautioning that AI’s role in the wider jobs gap is uncertain.

The Burning Glass Institute and RAISE US estimate that white-collar industries had about 1.5 million fewer jobs than their earlier growth path implied. In industries with widespread AI use, entry-level job postings were 3% to 8% below their 2021–2026 average in the year ending June 2026. The researchers say other economic factors may also explain the employment gap.

**Why it matters:** If routine junior work shrinks, employers may need clearer routes for new hires to build experience and advance. Enterprise workforce plans should track hiring and skill development alongside productivity gains.

### 3. 👥 **BCG Finds APAC AI Use High, Clarity Low**

*Boston Consulting Group · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** BCG’s survey suggests that across Asia-Pacific, giving employees strategic clarity about AI is more closely associated with reported business impact than simply providing tools.

The report draws on a survey of 6,349 employees across nine markets, including Singapore. BCG says 85% of respondents use AI regularly, but only 20% have strong clarity about their organisation’s AI strategy and their role in it. Among regular users, employees reporting strong clarity were more likely to report measurable business impact.

**Why it matters:** For regional employers, adoption rates alone may say little about whether AI is improving work. Clear priorities, guidance on how to use time saved, and market-specific workforce plans are practical levers to track.

### 4. 👥 **Fujitsu Tests AI-Linked Patient Transport in Kawasaki**

*Fujitsu · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Fujitsu will test whether AI-based departure-time estimates can help patients coordinate return transport after medical visits.

The Kawasaki pilot will link appointment information with demand-responsive transport. Using electronic medical record information and other data, the system will estimate when patients are likely to leave a facility and arrange return journeys through a patient-facing app. Participants include people attending prenatal check-ups and patients scheduled for endoscopy; the pilot is due to run from December 1, 2026, to January 30, 2027.

**Why it matters:** The project applies AI to a practical access barrier: uncertain clinic departure times can make return transport difficult to plan. Its evaluation should show whether connecting healthcare and local mobility can ease that burden for patients.

### 5. 👥 **Reach3 Finds Consumers Act on AI Advice**

*PR Newswire · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Reach3 Insights survey found that many US adults acted on AI advice, including by purchasing recommended products, even when some respondents said AI had little influence on their decisions.

The company surveyed 2,081 US adults in September and reports that 54% acted on AI advice in the prior three months, while 20% bought a recommended product. Among personal AI users, 40% said they turned to AI for personal advice, compared with 28% who used social media. The findings are self-reported results from a company-sponsored survey.

**Why it matters:** AI assistants are becoming part of consumer decision journeys, giving brands a new discovery channel while raising questions about recommendation quality and data use. Businesses should assess how their customers rely on AI advice as well as what they say about it.

## Key Takeaways

- AI infrastructure’s local impacts and the distribution of its benefits are becoming material questions for communities and project sponsors.
- Workforce adoption does not guarantee clear business gains; employees need direction and routes to develop new skills.
- AI’s influence on consumers may be stronger in practice than survey respondents perceive, underscoring the need to understand how recommendations shape decisions.

## What to Watch

Fujitsu’s Kawasaki pilot starts December 1, with results expected after it ends on January 30, 2027. Employers should also watch entry-level hiring trends and whether stronger AI strategy communication is reflected in employee outcomes across APAC.
