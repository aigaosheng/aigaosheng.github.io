# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: China AI Stocks Shed $480 Billion in Selloff

**Today:** If you invest in, partner with, or buy from Chinese AI companies, today's brief matters: public valuations are falling sharply even as early-stage robotics money keeps flowing.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **China's STAR50 AI and Chip Index Falls 40%, Erasing About $480 Billion**
*Reuters (syndicated via Yahoo Finance) · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's AI equity rally has reversed sharply, and the fallout is likely to slow tech IPOs and equity fundraising.

The mainland STAR50 index of AI and semiconductor leaders has dropped about 40% in under four months, wiping out roughly $480 billion in market value. Robot maker Unitree, heavily oversubscribed by retail investors at its August listing, has halved since it began trading. Traders pointed to high valuations, tax bills and possible foreign bans on Chinese data-centre equipment rather than a single trigger. Even after the fall, the STAR50 trades at about 100 times trailing earnings against roughly 30 times for the Nasdaq 100. Goldman Sachs estimates AI stocks make up 42% of market capitalisation and 57% of turnover across A-shares and Hong Kong H-shares.

**Why it matters:** A Hong Kong-based CIO quoted by Reuters expects the selloff to halt many companies' listing plans, which would lengthen exit timelines for VCs backing Chinese AI startups. Asia-based investors and corporate venture arms should expect tougher pricing in late-stage rounds.

### 2. 📊 **Anthropic's IPO Filing Lifts Asian Tech, but Chinese AI Shares Mostly Lag**
*Fortune · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Anthropic's US IPO filing boosted Korean and Japanese chip stocks, while most Chinese AI names fell. Z.ai was the notable exception.

The KOSPI rose 3.1% and the Nikkei 225 gained 1.7% after Anthropic filed its S-1, while the Hang Seng fell 0.7% and the CSI 300 dropped 1.3%. Unitree slid 4.8% and Cambricon 5.7%. Z.ai, the listed developer of the GLM models, rose 8.9%. A Natixis economist said Chinese markets sit outside the main US AI supply chain, and that users already have cheaper domestic model options such as DeepSeek.

**Why it matters:** Global AI sentiment is not lifting Chinese AI valuations, which strengthens the case for valuing Chinese model developers on their own revenue rather than on US benchmarks. Singapore's Straits Times Index was flat, down 0.1%.

### 3. 🤖 **DeepSeek Reportedly Accelerates Hiring as It Presents Growth Plans to Investors**
*TechNode · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DeepSeek appears to be turning new capital into headcount, with infrastructure and data-centre roles a visible priority.

Citing an Ifeng Tech report from October 27, TechNode says DeepSeek is expanding its team quickly while outlining new growth plans to investors. In June the company said it planned to at least double every department and advertised 33 roles, including data-centre construction jobs in Ulanqab. In September, the head of its Harness team posted about 150 openings.

**What's new:** This is the first report since the company's recent large funding round to describe how DeepSeek is deploying capital internally.

**Why it matters:** Rapid hiring at China's best-funded lab will raise talent costs for smaller Chinese AI startups. Enterprise buyers in Asia should watch whether the added capacity changes DeepSeek's pricing and its support for overseas customers.

### 4. 📊 **Robot E-Skin Startup MoSense Raises About RMB 100 Million in Angel-Plus Round**
*TechNode · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Investors are funding robot-component startups within months of their founding, including one founded only in May.

Shanghai-based MoSense builds full-body tactile sensing systems for robots. CDH Investments and Oasis Capital led the round, joined by LimX Dynamics, Loongson Venture Capital, CAS Star, Hua Capital and Pudong Venture Capital Group. The company was founded in May 2026 by a team with HKUST backgrounds. Its MoSkin hardware builds sensing into structural parts, and MoTact processes the tactile signals. It is also developing MoWorld, a model meant to use touch feedback to improve robot motion control.

**Why it matters:** Robot makers such as LimX are investing in their own suppliers, a pattern of vertical capital-building in China's embodied-AI supply chain that is continuing despite weak public markets.

### 5. 📊 **Desktop AI Robot Maker CyboPal Wins Backing from TCL and Shenzhen Capital Group**
*TechNode · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Corporate and state-linked investors are backing consumer hardware built around personal AI agents.

CyboPal raised an undisclosed strategic round from TCL Industries' investment arm and Shenzhen Capital Group. Its first product, CyboPal ONE, is a desktop robotic terminal that combines movement, vision and voice. The company said presales across overseas crowdfunding and offline channels passed 1,000 units and RMB 10 million in a month. Next it plans to scale deliveries and launch WhisPrompt, a handheld accessory for directing AI-assisted work.

**Why it matters:** Agent-centric hardware is emerging as a distinct funding category in China, with early revenue coming from overseas crowdfunding.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://technode.com/2026/10/27/desktop-robot-startup-cybopal-secures-investment-from-tcl-and-shenzhen-capital-group/)

## Key Takeaways
- Public and private markets are diverging: listed Chinese AI valuations are falling while early-stage robotics and embodied-AI rounds keep closing.
- Corporate investors (TCL, LimX Dynamics) and state-linked funds are a large share of new money, so strategic fit matters more than financial return in many rounds.
- The leading labs are consolidating talent and infrastructure, which raises costs and the competitive bar for second-tier Chinese AI startups.

## What to Watch
Watch whether pending Chinese AI listings in Hong Kong and Shanghai are delayed or repriced. Also watch whether China's securities regulator acts on the market-structure suggestions it gathered from participants this month, as reported by Reuters.
