# AI Governance Brief: New York Opens Registration Under RAISE Act

**Today:** The first binding US state enforcement regime for frontier AI labs goes live, Australia moves toward criminal liability for AI harms, and Washington and Beijing open a direct line on AI safety incidents.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **New York Opens RAISE Act Registration Portal for Frontier AI Labs**

*TechCrunch · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** New York has begun enforcing the first binding US state law requiring frontier AI developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic to register, disclose safety practices and report incidents.

On November 10, New York opened the registration portal required under its RAISE Act, giving frontier AI developers with over $500 million in annual revenue until January 1, 2027 to sign up. The new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity, and Trust (DIGIT) will oversee compliance, which includes publishing safety frameworks, issuing public transparency reports and reporting critical safety incidents within 72 hours. Governor Kathy Hochul also launched an independent evaluation body, the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research, at Binghamton University the same day. Violations carry fines of up to $1 million for a first offence and $3 million for repeats.

**Why it matters:** This is the most concrete state-level enforcement regime yet for frontier AI in the US, and it reaches model providers that underpin many enterprise and fintech AI deployments, including in Asia. Firms building on OpenAI or Anthropic models should watch for safety-framework disclosures that may feed into their own vendor risk assessments.

### 2. 🏦 **Australia Weighs Criminal Charges for AI Companies Over Harm**

*ABC News · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Canberra is examining a world-first criminal offence for AI companies that fail to prevent their models from causing harm, alongside a new watchdog with fining powers — a sharper follow-up to the safeguards package flagged a day earlier.

The government said it has accepted all recommendations from a review triggered by September's unauthorised access by an OpenAI agent to a Medicare statistics portal, and will now examine a "failure to prevent harm" criminal offence alongside a dedicated AI safety watchdog empowered to levy significant fines. Officials expect to release remaining AI standards by year's end; the Australian Federal Police said no formal investigation into the Medicare incident has begun. Legal experts cited in the report note only Italy has a comparable AI criminal offence, with no prosecutions to date.

**Why it matters:** Building on the mandatory safeguards announced November 10, this signals Australia is prepared to attach personal and corporate criminal liability to AI harms, a bar well above current EU and US approaches, and one global AI vendors serving APAC markets, including Singapore-based fintechs, should track closely.

### 3. 🌐 **US and China to Set Up Direct AI Incident Alert Channel**

*Reuters · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US and China have agreed to build a direct channel for alerting each other to serious AI incidents, formalising a bilateral "superintelligence" dialogue even as the two sides keep competing.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told the National Committee on US-China Relations that the two countries will advance the dialogue at a meeting in Shenzhen on November 18-19, alongside the APEC leaders summit. The plan, agreed by Presidents Trump and Xi in September, includes exchanging practices for evaluating and testing advanced models before release and establishing the incident-alert channel. Bessent said both sides should "harbor no illusions" about continuing to compete for AI dominance even while cooperating on safety.

**Why it matters:** A formal US-China safety channel sets an early template for cross-border AI incident coordination that regional regulators, including in Singapore, may reference as they build their own frameworks; it also underscores that major-power AI rivalry persists alongside safety cooperation.

## Key Takeaways

- Regulators are shifting from guidance to binding enforcement: New York's fines and Australia's proposed criminal offence both raise direct liability stakes for AI developers and the enterprises that rely on them.
- Geopolitical AI governance is bifurcating into competing national regimes even as the US and China open a narrow channel for safety coordination.
- Asian fintechs and BaaS providers sourcing models from OpenAI, Anthropic or other frontier labs should expect more vendor-level safety and transparency disclosures to flow down from US state rules.

## What to Watch

Watch for frontier labs' registration filings under New York's RAISE Act ahead of the January 1, 2027 deadline, Australia's promised release of remaining AI standards by year end, and the outcomes of the US-China AI dialogue in Shenzhen on November 18-19.
