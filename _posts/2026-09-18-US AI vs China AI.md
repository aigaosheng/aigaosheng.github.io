# US AI vs China AI Brief — 2026-09-18

## Top Stories 

### 1. **Chinese AI Models Generate Only About 10% of OpenAI and Anthropic Revenue**

* **Source**: South China Morning Post · September 18, 2026
* **Summary**: Seven major Chinese AI developers generated an estimated $10.7 billion in annual recurring revenue between March and August, according to Rhodium Group. That compares with more than $100 billion combined for OpenAI and Anthropic. ByteDance led the Chinese group at roughly $4 billion ARR, followed by Alibaba at $2.4 billion.
* **Why It Matters**: The data highlights an important asymmetry in the US-China AI competition: Chinese models can achieve substantial adoption and technical visibility without yet matching US frontier labs in monetization. The commercial gap remains a critical variable alongside model capability and compute.

### 3. **US-China AI Safety Debate Moves Toward Independent Evaluators**

* **Source**: Axios · September 18, 2026
* **Summary**: US AI companies, policymakers and safety organizations are debating how advanced models should be independently evaluated while federal AI regulation remains limited. The discussion includes possible third-party evaluators, voluntary incident reporting and even proposals for US and Chinese laboratories to review each other's models. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is also expected to discuss AI safety with Chinese officials.
* **Why It Matters**: AI competition is increasingly extending from model performance into the ability to measure and govern frontier capabilities. The difficulty is that the same geopolitical rivalry driving the race also makes shared evaluation standards and cross-border safety cooperation harder to establish.
* **URL**: [https://www.axios.com/2026/09/18/ai-safety-evaluators-metr-white-house-trump](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/18/ai-safety-evaluators-metr-white-house-trump)

### 5. **China's CXMT Targets NAND as AI Server Demand Tightens Memory Supply**

* **Source**: Reuters · September 18, 2026
* **Summary**: China's leading DRAM producer CXMT is preparing an R&D production line for NAND flash memory, according to sources cited by Reuters. AI-server demand has contributed to a global memory shortage, while Chinese memory companies continue expanding under national and local government support. The move would put CXMT into more direct competition with Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron and China's YMTC.
* **Why It Matters**: AI competition increasingly depends on the entire semiconductor stack rather than GPUs alone. China's push into memory could reduce another dependency in its AI infrastructure while intensifying competition for components needed by large-scale AI systems.
* **URL**: [https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-cxmt-eyes-flash-memory-push-amid-global-shortage-firm-take-samsung-ymtc-2026-09-18/](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-cxmt-eyes-flash-memory-push-amid-global-shortage-firm-take-samsung-ymtc-2026-09-18/)

* **Source**: Reuters · September 18, 2026

## Strategic Takeaways

* **The competition is becoming a full-stack contest.** Models remain central, but compute, memory, networking, cloud infrastructure and robotics are increasingly strategic layers.
* **US commercial leadership remains significant.** The latest revenue comparison shows a large monetization gap between major Chinese AI models and leading US frontier labs.
* **China is emphasizing infrastructure sovereignty and deployment.** Huawei's latest announcements illustrate an approach built around domestic compute, cloud, enterprise agents and ecosystem development.
* **Open and closed models are becoming geopolitical instruments.** Model access, distillation, open-weight distribution and third-party routing are increasingly intertwined with national technology policy.
* **AI governance is becoming part of the competitive landscape.** US-China discussions are moving toward questions of evaluation, incident reporting and potentially shared safety standards, even as strategic mistrust remains high.
