**When AI Cuts Out the Middle Men — Is This the Kodak Moment for Global Consulting Firms?**

The consultancy industry is facing a dramatic shift in its business model. In her October 24, 2025 analysis for Reuters’ *Breakingviews*, commentator Aimee Donnellan argues that the rise of artificial intelligence (AI) is creating a “Kodak-moment” for global consulting giants. ([Reuters][1])

---

### The Situation: Consulting Firms vs. Client Self-Service

Consultancies such as Accenture, Deloitte, IBM and Booz Allen Hamilton rode a long boom by offering large enterprises technical and operational services — often at a significant discount to what the client would spend running everything internally. ([Reuters][1])
But now AI is starting to undercut that value proposition. The article gives a telling example: a project that might cost a client $1 million to do in-house, or $200 000 if outsourced to a firm like Accenture historically, could now be done for as little as $10 000 with machine-learning tools. ([Reuters][1])
Clients are noticing. With pressures like the pandemic, inflation, economic uncertainty and public-sector cost control, large clients are demanding sharper value for money from consultants — or simply doing more themselves. ([Reuters][1])

---

### Why the “Kodak Moment” Metaphor Fits

The metaphor refers to Kodak’s collapse when digital photography disrupted its traditional film business. The consulting business is similarly vulnerable: large scale, legacy model, dependent on staffing and human hours, and now threatened by automation and new AI capabilities. ([Reuters][1])
If consultants fail to adapt — by either significantly lowering prices, integrating AI deeply, or radically changing their model — they could face a rapid decline in revenue and valuation. The article warns that just as Kodak emerged from bankruptcy as a much smaller company, consulting firms risk a similar “valuation downer.” ([Reuters][1])

---

### What Are the Options — And Why They’re Tricky

To stay ahead, consulting firms have three main strategic routes:

1. **Acquire AI specialists**: Buying smaller AI-enabled firms (for example, firms like EXL) to accelerate transformation projects. ([Reuters][1])
2. **Merge with other firms**: Consolidate to capture scale and perhaps command better pricing. ([Reuters][1])
3. **Significantly reduce pricing**: Accept that the value of human-hours work has been commoditised, and shift to lower-margin, higher-volume models.

Each option, however, comes with difficulties: acquisitions may not deliver expected synergies; mergers might cause loss of talent (which is critical in consulting); and price cutting pressures margins severely. The article argues that none of these paths guarantee preserving the old business model. ([Reuters][1])

---

### Implications and What to Watch

* **For consulting firms**: They must rethink their revenue model, invest in AI capabilities deeply (not just superficially), redesign service offerings, and be realistic about pricing power.
* **For clients**: The shift may be empowering — more in-house capability, lower cost, faster turnaround. But also risks: new models, less margin for error, integration challenges.
* **For the industry overall**: A potential shake-out looms. Firms that fail to adapt could see sharp declines; survivors may look very different, more tech-centred, slimmer human backbone, and competing more like product firms than staffing firms.
* **For your projects and procurement**: Expect greater demands for transparency, outcomes-based pricing, and possibly more direct use of AI tools rather than heavy consultancy reliance.

---

### Why It Matters to You

If you work in tech, data science, consulting, or the corporate services space (like you Sheng, with a deep R&D and AI background), this is a bellwether. The transformation of service-industries under AI isn’t abstract — it means that business models that rely on arbitraged labour + expertise are under pressure.
It also means there are opportunities: for firms that can embed AI deeply, deliver value faster and cheaper, there’s upside. For those stuck in legacy models, the risk of obsolescence is real.

---

### Glossary

* **Kodak moment**: A metaphor referencing the downfall of Kodak, signalling a sudden disruptive shift that erodes a legacy business model.
* **AI (Artificial Intelligence)**: Computer systems able to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence — e.g., learning, decision-making, language processing.
* **Machine Learning (ML)**: A subset of AI in which systems improve their performance at tasks through experience/data rather than explicit programming.
* **Valuation downer**: A significant reduction in a company’s market value or perceived worth, usually due to structural change.
* **Synergy**: In M&A context, the idea that the combined firm will have greater value than the sum of the two standalone firms.
