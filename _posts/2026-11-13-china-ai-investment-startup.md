# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Moonshot AI Restructures For Hong Kong IPO

**Today:** Moonshot AI's move to unwind its offshore holding structure signals its Hong Kong listing is entering the final preparation stage, while fresh market data shows the first wave of Chinese large-model IPOs cooling sharply from their spring highs.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Moonshot AI Dismantles Offshore Structure Ahead Of Hong Kong IPO**

*Odaily · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Moonshot AI, the maker of the Kimi chatbot, has confidentially filed for a Hong Kong listing and is unwinding its Cayman Islands holding company to clear the path for an early-2027 debut.

Citing Bloomberg and Wall Street Journal reporting, Odaily said Moonshot is repurchasing shares held by its US-dollar investors and redirecting that capital into its China-based operating entity, mirroring a similar "red-chip" unwind that rival StepFun completed earlier. The restructuring follows a pre-IPO round that values the company at roughly $50 billion, with the Hong Kong offering reportedly targeting up to $5 billion.

**Why it matters:** The structural overhaul reflects Beijing's preference for AI champions to list through onshore-friendly vehicles rather than offshore red-chip structures, a template other Chinese AI unicorns eyeing Hong Kong will likely follow. For Asia-based investors, it also signals the IPO timeline is firming up rather than slipping further.

### 2. 📊 **"Space AI" Satellite Firm GuoXing Aerospace Debuts On HKEX**

*The Paper · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** GuoXing Aerospace, marketed as China's first "space AI" listed company, began trading in Hong Kong and closed its debut session down 1.37%.

The Chengdu-based firm, which builds AI-powered satellites and offers smart-city and computing services, priced its offer at HK$24.9 and closed at HK$24.56, valuing it at about HK$14.8 billion after raising roughly HK$6.52 billion net. The company's valuation rose from CNY 95 million at seed stage to CNY 6.76 billion at its 2025 Series E, though it remains unprofitable and posted a 6.3% revenue decline in the first half of 2026.

**Why it matters:** The muted debut adds to evidence that Hong Kong investors are growing more selective on China AI listings even as deal flow continues, a signal relevant to any Asia fund weighing allocations to the next cohort of China AI IPOs.

### 3. 📊 **Zhipu AI And MiniMax Shares Give Back Most Of Post-IPO Gains**

*21jingji · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Shares of Hong Kong-listed large-model pioneers Zhipu AI and MiniMax have fallen sharply from their spring peaks, even though both remain above or near their offer prices.

As of the November 13 close, Zhipu traded at HK$408, still 251% above its HK$116.20 offer price but 86% below its June peak, while MiniMax traded at HK$152.40, below its HK$165 offer price. The report attributed the swing to a shift from an early "scarcity premium" phase, when MiniMax's free float was about 6% of shares outstanding, to pricing now anchored more closely to annualised recurring revenue multiples.

**Why it matters:** As the first Chinese large-model listings to trade a full cycle, Zhipu and MiniMax are becoming the reference points for how Hong Kong prices AI unicorns, directly shaping valuation expectations for Moonshot's and StepFun's pending offerings.

### 4. 📊 **China's Embodied-AI Funding Roughly Halves From Its Summer Peak**

*21jingji · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Financing in China's embodied-AI and humanoid-robotics sector has fallen by nearly half from its June peak, with capital now concentrating on startups that can show commercial traction.

The report tallied 33 disclosed financing deals worth CNY 10.1 billion in October, down from 55 deals worth CNY 30.9 billion in June, and noted that regulatory scrutiny has delayed Zhiyuan Robotics' listing process and slowed embodied-AI Hong Kong IPO filings generally. Several smaller robotics firms, including Yinghe Robotics and Shenlan Robotics, have ceased operations this year even as better-capitalised players such as RoboScience and UniX AI continue to close rounds.

**Why it matters:** The pullback shows investors separating durable embodied-AI businesses from hype-driven ones, a filtering process enterprise buyers and partners in Singapore and the region should watch before committing to long-term robotics deployments.

### 5. 📊 **Meituan Leads Funding Round For RISC-V AI Chip Startup LingRui**

*163.com (NetEase) · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Shanghai-based chip designer LingRui Zhixin raised a Pre-A+ round worth hundreds of millions of yuan led by Meituan's strategic investment arm, taking its cumulative funding to nearly CNY 1 billion.

JD.com-linked funds, Ganbei Fund, SenseTime, SAIC's investment arm and Deepin Software's fund also joined the round. LingRui is developing server-grade RISC-V CPU cores, building on its P100 core launched in January 2026, with a 128-core server chip targeted for fabrication in 2027 and mass production in 2028.

**Why it matters:** Meituan's continued backing of compute, storage and now CPU-architecture startups points to Chinese internet platforms hedging against reliance on Nvidia and other foreign silicon for their AI workloads.

## Key Takeaways

- Hong Kong is becoming the default listing venue for China's AI champions, but investors are now pricing new offerings on revenue multiples rather than scarcity, squeezing valuations for later entrants like Moonshot.
- The embodied-AI funding slowdown shows China's AI capital is rotating from story-driven bets toward companies with proven commercial deployments.
- Domestic platforms such as Meituan and JD.com continue to fund AI chip startups directly, reinforcing China's push for compute self-sufficiency alongside its startup-funding boom.

## What to Watch

Watch for Moonshot AI's formal Hong Kong prospectus filing and underwriter confirmation as the next concrete step toward its targeted early-2027 listing, and monitor whether Zhipu AI and MiniMax share prices stabilise or continue compressing ahead of that offering.
