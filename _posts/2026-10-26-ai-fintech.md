# AI Fintech Brief: Deutsche Bank AI Cuts Loan Decisions to Minutes

**Today:** AI has moved into credit decisions at production scale, and governance is not keeping up. This brief matters if you run lending, risk or compliance.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Deutsche Bank Uses AI to Cut Some Corporate Loan Decisions From Weeks to Minutes**
*Reuters (syndicated by SRN News) · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Deutsche Bank's AI decisioning is now live, and it has built its own platform to control the agents behind it.

Technology chief Marie-Jeanne Deverdun told Reuters that a project launched in the summer has cut decisions on some smaller corporate loans from weeks to minutes. Some multinational lending decisions now take weeks instead of months. The bank built an internal platform called ADA to develop, monitor and control AI agents. ADA also lets the bank switch between AI models as the technology changes. Deutsche Bank plans more than €2.5 billion in technology spending from 2026 to 2028. Deverdun said the bank is preparing for a time when its agents deal directly with clients' agents, and that managing cyber risk is a top priority.

**Why it matters:** A bank-owned agent control layer that can swap models is becoming the standard design for regulated lenders. Asian banks and BaaS providers planning agentic credit workflows will likely face the same buy-versus-build choice on agent oversight.

### 2. 🔒 **SAS and Microsoft Survey: Banks Expect Autonomous AI Credit Decisions, but Governance Lags**
*Lifestyle & Tech · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Most banks expect AI to make credit and risk decisions on its own within five years, but fewer than one in five have mature AI governance today.

FT Longitude, SAS and Microsoft surveyed 300 banking leaders across 31 countries. 76% expect autonomous AI credit and risk decisions at scale by 2031, compared with 10% who say this happens today. Only 18% have fully embedded AI governance processes. 42% lack defined kill switches for AI models, and 26% have no dedicated AI model risk policy. Nearly two-thirds (63%) said they would accept a black-box model if it performed better than an explainable one. 92% reported at least one large language model running outside formal governance.

**Why it matters:** These gaps are what supervisors such as the EBA say they are watching. Banks that put kill switches, drift monitoring and model inventories in place now are likely to face less friction when regulators issue agent-specific expectations.

### 3. 🏦 **Indian Bank CEOs Push AI From Pilots to Production, With Audit Trails**
*Business Standard · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's private-bank CEOs say the RBI now expects banks to implement AI, not just test it, and that the bank stays accountable for every AI outcome.

At the Business Standard BFSI Insight Summit in Mumbai, three CEOs spoke on a panel about AI in banking: Rajiv Anand of IndusInd Bank, R Subramaniakumar of RBL Bank and V Vaidyanathan of IDFC First Bank. Subramaniakumar said AI can help banks scale credit delivery. He added that faster decisions need traceability and strong audit trails so banks can explain what drove each outcome. Vaidyanathan argued that every bank should have an AI policy. The panel agreed that accountability stays with the bank whatever operating model it uses.

**Why it matters:** India is one of Asia's largest AI-in-lending markets. Its regulator's signal favours implementation with explainability, not open-ended experimentation. Regional fintechs partnering with Indian lenders should expect audit-trail requirements to appear in their contracts.

### 4. 📊 **Qatar's Commercial Bank Puts an In-House AML AI Agent Into Production**
*Investing.com · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A mid-sized Gulf lender is now running AI in production across lending, relationship management and AML alert review.

In its nine-month 2026 results, Commercial Bank presented three live AI initiatives:

- **AML AI Agent:** built in-house, it produces structured analysis and recommendations on transaction-monitoring alerts.
- **AI Personal Loan Engine:** ranks eligible customers and recommends risk-aware loan amounts, with human oversight.
- **RM Cockpit:** an AI tool for premium relationship managers.

Net profit after tax was QAR 1.54 billion, down 13.8% year on year, mainly because of higher provisions. Operating expenses rose 10.1%, partly due to continued spending on digital and AI.

**Why it matters:** AI agents that triage AML alerts while humans keep the decision are becoming common outside the global tier-one banks. They are a realistic benchmark for Asian payment institutions working through alert backlogs.

## Key Takeaways
- AI credit decisioning has moved from pilots to production at banks in Germany, Qatar and Canada. Speed gains are now measured in orders of magnitude, not percentages.
- The industry's own data shows governance trailing deployment. Missing kill switches, ungoverned LLMs and a willingness to accept black-box models are the most likely targets for future supervisory findings.
- Banks increasingly own the agent control layer themselves, through internal platforms and in-house agents, rather than relying fully on vendor tooling.

The Business Standard BFSI Insight Summit continues in Mumbai until October 28. Watch for remarks from Indian regulators and public-sector bank chiefs on AI accountability in lending.
