# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Memory-Chip Tycoon Zhu Yiming Backs DeepSeek

**Today:** Fresh disclosures show China's chip industry and foreign investment banks both leaning further into domestic AI, a signal worth tracking for anyone pricing China AI exposure or counterparty concentration.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Changxin Memory Chairman Zhu Yiming Joins DeepSeek's Funding Round**

*21st Century Business Herald · November 8, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's chipmaking elite are putting personal capital behind DeepSeek, treating its financing as a hedge on future memory-chip demand rather than a pure financial bet.

21st Century Business Herald reported that Zhu Yiming, chairman of DRAM maker Changxin Memory Technologies (CXMT), personally participated in DeepSeek's second funding round alongside Tencent, CATL, China Mobile and CITIC Securities. The round had earlier been reported at roughly RMB105.5 billion, valuing DeepSeek near RMB615.5 billion, though this report did not disclose the size of Zhu's own stake. Zhu, who also runs semiconductor-focused Shixi Capital, framed the move as "demand insurance": large-model training and inference consume computing power, which in turn drives demand for the memory chips his companies supply.

**Why it matters:** A chip-industry veteran backing a model developer directly shows China's semiconductor supply chain treating AI compute demand as durable enough to invest equity in, not just sell hardware into. For Singapore and regional fintech and enterprise-AI buyers, it underscores how China's AI stack, compute, memory and models, is consolidating around a small circle of state-aligned, cross-invested players.

### 2. 📊 **Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and UBS Turn Bullish on China AI Stocks**

*21st Century Business Herald · November 8, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Major foreign banks say the recent valuation pullback has made China's AI-linked equities attractive again, pointing to durable policy support and improving risk-reward.

Goldman Sachs' trading desk told clients it favours domestic AI-related shares after the recent correction, citing sustained policy backing, while JPMorgan strategist Zhang Xiaoning noted the CSI 300 trades near 11.5 times forward earnings, about 0.5 standard deviations below its historical average. UBS Asia vice-chair Zhu Zhengqin cited 3.2 times international subscription demand in Dongshan Precision's recent Hong Kong listing as evidence of sustained foreign appetite. Goldman estimates China holds roughly half of global market share in select AI power and infrastructure segments, across more than 1,700 listed AI-linked companies it tracks.

**Why it matters:** Renewed foreign-bank conviction could unlock fresh portfolio inflows into China's listed AI supply chain just as AI valuations wobble elsewhere globally, potentially easing the path to public listings for later-stage Chinese AI startups. Singapore-based asset managers and family offices with Asia AI exposure should treat this as a prompt to revisit, not reflexively trim, China AI allocations.

## Key Takeaways

- Semiconductor capital is moving directly into AI model developers, blurring the line between hardware suppliers and AI financiers.
- Foreign institutional sentiment on listed China AI equities is turning constructive again after a valuation reset, reversing recent caution.
- Both developments point to capital concentrating around a small set of state-aligned, vertically-connected AI champions rather than spreading broadly across the startup field.

Watch for confirmation of the full investor roster and size of DeepSeek's Series B round, and whether CITIC Securities' involvement advances DeepSeek's reported preparations for a future listing.
