# AI Fintech Brief: Schwab Blocks AI Agents From Moving Client Money

**Today:** Only two stories cleared the window since Friday evening Singapore time: a major brokerage walling off outside AI agents, and a regional bank cutting its SBA loan origination steps by two-thirds.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Schwab Blocks Unauthorized AI Agents From Moving Client Money**
*Bloomberg · October 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Schwab's CEO says the firm is actively blocking unauthorized AI agents, including Meta's Muse, that try to move clients' money.

Charles Schwab CEO Rick Wurster told Bloomberg that the brokerage, wealth and banking group is blocking unauthorized AI agents that attempt to move client funds, and he named Meta's Muse among them. Bloomberg frames the stance as part of a wider pattern: US bank executives say they welcome consumers using AI to help manage their finances, provided the tools operate within the institution's rules.

**Why it matters:** For Asia-based banks, wallet providers and BaaS platforms, this is an early signal of how incumbents may treat consumer-grade agents: tolerated inside controlled, authenticated channels, blocked when they act on their own. Our read, not a claim from the source: anyone building agent-facing APIs or partnering with agent providers should expect access terms, authentication and liability allocation to become contract issues rather than afterthoughts.

### 2. 🤖 **Colony Bank Cuts SBA Loan Origination Steps by 67% With Gen AI**
*American Banker · October 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Colony Bank says generative AI origination software cut its SBA lending process from 92 steps to 32 while keeping a human reviewer on each recommendation.

Georgia-based Colony Bank reports that Lama AI's origination platform reduced its SBA lending process by 67% as revised SBA rules, effective October 1, tightened criteria and documentation requirements. The software reads documents, fills application fields and checks applicant data against outside sources for KYC and KYB, then makes a recommendation that staff review; approvals now take about seven business days with a responsive customer, against a typical 10-day process the bank had expected the new rules to stretch by five days. Colony now runs all its SBA Express loans and some non-SBA commercial products on the platform and plans to add larger SBA Standard loans within a couple of weeks.

**Why it matters:** This is a concrete, human-in-the-loop efficiency case in regulated credit: the gains come from removing document handling and data validation, not from automating the credit decision. American Banker also notes that using AI to decline applicants raises fair-lending questions, which this design largely avoids. Lenders in Asia facing heavy KYC, KYB and documentation loads could apply the same pattern.

## Key Takeaways
- Institutions are separating AI they control from AI they do not: governed, human-reviewed internal tools are being scaled while unauthorized outside agents are blocked.
- Measurable gains are showing up in back-office workflow (steps removed, days saved) rather than in autonomous financial decisions.
- Regulatory change can accelerate adoption: Colony's automation push was driven in part by new SBA rules.

## What to Watch
Colony Bank's go-live of larger SBA Standard loans on Lama, expected within weeks, will show whether the efficiency gains hold on more complex credits. Also watch whether other large US banks and brokerages publish explicit access rules for outside AI agents.
