# Quantum Brief: Quantum Software Tested on Energy Grids and Materials

**Today:** Only one in-scope quantum story could be verified as published after the cut-off, but it bears on a question executives keep asking: where quantum computing might first pay off commercially.

## Top Stories

### 1. ⚛️ **Quantum Software Firms Test Energy Use Cases Ahead of Drug Discovery**

*CNBC · November 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Quantum software companies are pointing to energy-transition problems such as grid operation and clean-energy materials as a nearer-term commercial opening than drug discovery or logistics.

CNBC reports that Phasecraft, a Bristol-founded quantum software company, is working with NESO, the operator that balances Great Britain's electricity system, on tasks such as managing power exchanges and isolating parts of the network in emergencies. Phasecraft claims its software could run these tasks up to 1,000 times faster than conventional computing, a company assertion the article does not independently verify. It is also working with solar cell maker Oxford PV on more efficient photovoltaic materials, and, under US ARPA-E oversight, with Johnson Matthey, Harvard and QuEra on catalysts that reduce reliance on scarce iridium in hydrogen production.

The article adds that Quantinuum is modelling materials for hydrogen fuel cells and carbon capture. It also says ORCA Computing has worked with BP on molecular properties relevant to biofuel combustion, and with Japan's JIJ on power-generator scheduling alongside BP and the UK's National Quantum Computing Centre.

**Why it matters:** Energy is a sector where grid operators, utilities and oil majors already fund pilots, so these projects give quantum vendors a credible commercial story beyond the usual long-dated promises. Phasecraft told CNBC it sees potential demand in Asia, where electrification and renewables integration are expanding. That points to a possible opening for Singapore and regional utilities and energy firms. The speed-up figures are still vendor claims, and no production deployments are reported.

## Key Takeaways

- Quantum vendors are shifting their pitch toward concrete, domain-specific problems in energy and materials, where partners with real operating data and budgets are already engaged.
- The cited work is still at the partnership and pilot stage, and performance claims come from the companies themselves, so buyers should ask for independent benchmarks before committing budget.
- Government bodies such as the UK's NQCC and the US ARPA-E are part of these projects, which suggests public funding and sponsorship will continue to shape early quantum applications.

## What to Watch

Look for published results or benchmarks from the NESO, Oxford PV and ARPA-E catalyst projects, and for any Asian utility or energy-company pilots announced by quantum software vendors.
