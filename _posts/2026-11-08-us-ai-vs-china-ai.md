# US AI vs China AI Brief: Shell Company Scandal Fuels Nvidia Export Bills

**Today:** Fresh evidence that Washington's own chip-export vetting broke down is reshaping the US-China AI contest from a story about bans to a story about enforcement credibility, a shift payments and fintech leaders should track given their reliance on US-origin compute and cloud infrastructure.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Nvidia's China Chip Licensees Draw Scrutiny Over Vetting Gaps**

*Seoul Economic Daily · November 8, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US-approved handlers of Nvidia's H200 chips in China were found to have no real operations, intensifying congressional pressure on Nvidia ahead of a closed-door briefing with CEO Jensen Huang on November 18.

Two Commerce Department-cleared distributors, New Matrix and Mag Infra, were reported to occupy effectively empty offices despite links to previously blacklisted entities, Wise Road Capital and server maker Inspur respectively. The findings add urgency to three pending bills — the AI Overwatch Act, the MATCH Act and the Chip Security Act — that would tighten congressional and technical oversight of chip export licensing.

**Why it matters:** A Democratic-controlled Congress taking office in January is expected to use subpoena power to press Nvidia and Commerce on export compliance, raising the odds of stricter licensing and end-use verification that could slow China-bound chip flows. Singapore-based Megaspeed, an approved Nvidia customer already under separate scrutiny, keeps the spotlight on how export-control enforcement touches Asia-based intermediaries that banks and BaaS providers may rely on indirectly through cloud and data-center partners.

### 2. 🌐 **"Plugged In, Not Locked In": Asia Charts a Middle Path on AI**

*South China Morning Post · November 8, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Policymakers and academics meeting in Shenzhen argued that Southeast Asian and other middle powers should keep selectively engaging both US and Chinese AI technology stacks rather than aligning exclusively with either Washington or Beijing.

At a November 7 governance panel, Alejandro Reyes of the University of Hong Kong said middle powers are exercising agency by partnering with both superpowers "issue by issue," and called for a "collaboration stack" spanning governments, industry, universities and local communities to test governance frameworks in practice. He pointed to Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area as a testbed, citing its research base, legal system and financing links.

**Why it matters:** For Singapore and ASEAN regulators and fintechs, the framing reinforces a vendor-neutral posture already common in the region — sourcing models, chips and cloud services from both US and Chinese providers — which carries direct implications for how MAS-regulated entities assess concentration and sovereignty risk in their AI supply chains.

## Key Takeaways

- Export-licensing integrity, not just headline chip bans, is emerging as the next flashpoint in US-China AI competition, with concrete enforcement consequences for approved distributors.
- A Democratic-controlled Congress is positioning chip export oversight as a recurring pressure point on Nvidia and the Commerce Department into 2027.
- Asian middle powers, including Singapore-linked voices, are converging on a "plugged in, not locked in" doctrine that favours multi-vendor AI stacks over exclusive alignment with either Washington or Beijing.

## What to Watch

Jensen Huang's closed-door congressional briefing on November 18, and whether the AI Overwatch Act, MATCH Act and Chip Security Act advance through the Senate NDAA conference process, will signal how far Congress pushes new export-licensing requirements.
