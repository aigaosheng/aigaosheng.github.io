# Enterprise AI Brief: DXC and LangChain Advance Agentic AI

**Today:** Enterprise AI is shifting from standalone copilots towards governed agents embedded in business-critical workflows, with deployment flexibility, operational control and measurable outcomes becoming central buying criteria.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 DXC and LangChain Advance Agentic AI for Critical IT Operations

*DXC Technology · October 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DXC Technology and LangChain are expanding their collaboration to help enterprises deploy AI agents in mission-critical IT operations with stronger governance and operational oversight.

The partnership builds on DXC OASIS, an orchestration platform that uses LangGraph to coordinate agentic workflows. DXC says the platform is scaling towards 125 customer environments across regulated industries and multiple geographies. Across 500 measured use cases, the company reports an average 95% reduction in resolution time and diagnostic accuracy of at least 92%.

**Why it matters:** For banks, payment providers and large enterprises, production AI must fit existing operating environments rather than require wholesale infrastructure replacement. The partners' emphasis on model choice, interoperability and deployment across public, private, dedicated and sovereign environments is particularly relevant to organisations balancing performance, privacy and data-residency requirements.

### 2. 🤖 TCS Develops an Enterprise AI Control Layer for Multi-Vendor Agents

*Financial Express · October 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Tata Consultancy Services is developing an enterprise AI architecture that centrally coordinates agents built internally, by clients and by third-party vendors.

The proposed governance layer is intended to manage agents across enterprise applications, including SAP, ServiceNow and Oracle. TCS sees the control layer as a way to coordinate deployments while managing security, operating costs and responsible use. The approach reflects demand for enterprise-wide orchestration instead of isolated agent deployments managed separately by each application or team.

**Why it matters:** Enterprises adopting agents from multiple vendors need a consistent view of permissions, performance, cost and accountability. A shared control layer could help technology leaders manage that complexity, particularly in regulated financial services where auditability and operational consistency are essential.

### 3. 🤖 HCLTech and Anthropic Partner to Expand Enterprise Agentic AI

*Reuters · October 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** HCLTech and Anthropic have formed a strategic partnership to promote enterprise adoption of agentic AI, with an emphasis on security and governance.

Under the agreement, HCLTech plans to establish a dedicated business unit focused on integrating and supporting agentic AI solutions. The partnership combines Anthropic's AI technology with HCLTech's enterprise implementation capabilities, targeting organisations seeking to incorporate agents into existing business processes.

**Why it matters:** The partnership highlights the growing role of systems integrators in translating AI capabilities into production deployments. For enterprises in Singapore and across Asia, implementation expertise, integration with legacy systems and operational safeguards may be as important as the underlying model when selecting an AI partner.

### 4. 🤖 Boomi and Jade Global Target Governed AI Agents in Manufacturing

*Boomi · October 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Boomi and Jade Global are expanding their partnership to help manufacturers move AI agents into production across plant operations, suppliers and enterprise systems.

The collaboration combines Boomi's data integration capabilities and Agent Control Plane with Jade Global's manufacturing expertise. ## Key Takeaways

- **Governance is becoming part of the deployment architecture.** Enterprises increasingly need central coordination, policy enforcement and visibility across agents from multiple vendors.
- **Production readiness matters more than standalone demonstrations.** Integration, operational reliability, human oversight and measurable outcomes are critical when agents enter business-critical workflows.
- **Deployment flexibility is a strategic requirement.** Model choice, interoperability and control over infrastructure help organisations manage privacy, cost and regulatory obligations across regions.

## What to Watch

Watch for customer deployments that independently validate reported productivity and diagnostic improvements, and for clearer evidence of how enterprise agent control layers manage permissions, costs and incidents across vendors. For Asia-based enterprises, the next important signal will be whether these platforms can support regional data-residency and operational requirements without sacrificing interoperability.

### 3. 🤖 Kore.ai Launches Autoloop for Continuous AI Agent Optimisation

*Express Computer · October 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Kore.ai has introduced Autoloop, an optimisation engine designed to continuously evaluate and improve AI agents operating on its Artemis platform.

The system uses a cycle of building, evaluating, diagnosing, optimising and re-verifying agents. Its stated evaluation dimensions include task completion, accuracy, safety and cost efficiency, with changes assessed to reduce the risk of improving one metric at the expense of another.

**Why it matters:** Enterprise AI teams need ongoing performance management after deployment, not just pre-launch testing. Continuous evaluation could help organisations detect regressions and manage trade-offs between reliability, safety and operating costs as agents encounter real-world workloads.
