# **Payments Brief: Antom Launches PayNow Deep-Linking for Lazada**

**Today:** Singapore's PayNow ecosystem is moving towards simpler e-commerce checkout, while stablecoin-funded payout infrastructure is gaining traction as payment providers seek faster and more flexible cross-border liquidity.

## **Top Stories**

### **1. 💳 Antom Launches PayNow Deep-Linking for Lazada Checkout**

*Fintech Singapore · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Ant International's Antom has introduced PayNow deep-linking for Lazada Singapore, with early results showing an approximately 9% improvement in payment success rates.

The feature connects Lazada checkout directly to a customer's preferred banking application, automatically populating payment details and removing the need to save and manually upload a QR-code screenshot. Following payment approval, customers return to the marketplace's confirmation page.

Ant International also announced that its subsidiary, 2C2P by Antom, is working with GovTech Singapore to explore the same capability through GovWallet. A proof of concept is being demonstrated at Singapore FinTech Festival 2026.

**Why it matters:** Payment conversion is an important commercial metric for e-commerce platforms. Deep-linking reduces friction in account-to-account payments and demonstrates how PayNow can become more competitive with card-based checkout. The approach also offers a potential template for government collections and other digital services.

### **2. 💳 StraitsX, OCBC and Standard Chartered Demonstrate Interoperable Tokenised Deposits**

*StraitsX · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** StraitsX, OCBC and Standard Chartered completed a proof of concept demonstrating cross-bank payments using tokenised deposits issued on separate banking infrastructure.

Conducted under the Monetary Authority of Singapore's BLOOM initiative, the experiment used StraitsX's shared settlement infrastructure to coordinate payment initiation, compliance screening, liquidity checks and settlement between the two banks.

The participants successfully completed US-dollar payment flows outside conventional banking hours, with settlement taking place within minutes. The approach also demonstrated potential reductions in bilateral prefunding requirements.

**Why it matters:** Interoperability is a major obstacle to institutional adoption of tokenised deposits. This experiment demonstrates how shared settlement infrastructure could connect otherwise isolated banking networks. For Singapore, it strengthens the country's position in developing institutional-grade programmable money and next-generation cross-border settlement infrastructure.

### **3. 🌐 Alipay+ Connects More Than 50 Wallets to Brazil's Pix**

*Alipay+ · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Ant International's Alipay+ has connected more than 50 international digital wallets and banking applications to Brazil's national instant payment network, Pix.

The integration allows international travellers to scan Pix QR codes at participating merchants using their existing payment applications, without installing another wallet. Pix is operated by Brazil's central bank and supports payments across a substantial domestic merchant ecosystem.

Alipay+ now connects with 16 national QR payment schemes across Asia, the Middle East and Latin America. Its broader network connects 150 million merchants with 2.1 billion user accounts globally, according to the company.

**Why it matters:** Cross-border QR interoperability is becoming an important competitive advantage for digital wallet operators. The expansion demonstrates how domestic instant payment infrastructure can become an international acceptance network without requiring consumers to adopt additional applications. Asian payment providers can learn from this model when expanding regional merchant acceptance and tourist payment services.

### **4. 💳 Standard Chartered Expands Tokenised Deposits Between Singapore and Hong Kong**

*Standard Chartered · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Standard Chartered expanded its tokenised deposit solution to enable near-real-time transfers between Singapore and Hong Kong.

Built on the bank's proprietary blockchain infrastructure, the service allows eligible institutional clients to use commercial bank deposits in digital treasury and investment workflows, including intragroup liquidity management.

Supported currencies include SGD, HKD, USD, EUR, CNH and GBP. The bank plans to extend the solution to additional markets.

**Why it matters:** Tokenised commercial bank deposits offer institutions an alternative to conventional payment arrangements while retaining the underlying bank deposit relationship. Singapore and Hong Kong are particularly relevant markets because of their roles as regional treasury, banking and digital-asset centres. The development could support more flexible institutional liquidity management across Asia.

### **5. 🌐 NETS Expands Singapore QR Payments for International Visitors**

*移动支付网 · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore payment network NETS is upgrading its NETS App to support international visitors paying through local SGQR merchant acceptance points.

The upgraded application enables visitors whose home payment applications are not connected to Singapore's cross-border QR networks to access participating NETS merchants. The network covers more than 50,000 SGQR acceptance points.

NETS is also planning a trial of NFC-triggered QR payments in Toa Payoh from December, initially lasting two months. Additional features include mini-programmes and family wallets with configurable spending limits.

**Why it matters:** Singapore's payment ecosystem is expanding beyond domestic account-to-account transfers towards more inclusive acceptance for international visitors. The combination of QR interoperability, NFC interaction and controlled family spending also illustrates how payment infrastructure can evolve into broader consumer financial services.

## **Key Takeaways**

- **Interoperability is the defining infrastructure challenge:** Tokenised deposits, instant payment networks and international QR schemes increasingly require shared connectivity rather than isolated platforms.
- **Singapore is becoming an institutional payments innovation hub:** PayNow, BLOOM, NETS and bank-led tokenisation initiatives demonstrate the country's commitment to modernising domestic and cross-border payment infrastructure.
- **Payment experiences are becoming increasingly seamless:** Deep-linking, QR interoperability and programmable settlement are reducing friction for consumers, merchants and institutional treasury operations.

## **What to Watch**

Watch for the next stages of MAS's BLOOM initiative, particularly whether tokenised deposit interoperability progresses from controlled proofs of concept to commercial institutional settlement. Also monitor the rollout of PayNow Gen2, NETS NFC payment trials and Alipay+'s expansion into additional national instant payment networks.

**MY TAKE**

The global payments industry is gradually moving towards an interoperable, multi-rail financial infrastructure.

Three developments are particularly important:

1. **Instant payment interoperability:** Domestic systems such as PayNow and Pix are increasingly becoming gateways for international payments rather than isolated national networks.
2. **Tokenised institutional money:** Tokenised deposits and stablecoins could enable faster settlement, improved liquidity management and programmable financial transactions.
3. **Intelligent payment orchestration:** AI, APIs and programmable payment infrastructure will increasingly coordinate payment initiation, routing, compliance and settlement.

For Singapore, the strategic opportunity is to connect traditional banking infrastructure, instant payment networks and regulated digital assets into a unified financial ecosystem.

**My central thesis: The future of payments will not be defined by a single winning rail. It will be defined by interoperability across multiple forms of money, networks and institutions.**
