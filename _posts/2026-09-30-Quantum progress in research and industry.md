# Quantum progress in research and industry Brief — 2026-09-30

**Today:** Quantum computing is shifting from a technology-selection question into a business strategy issue, while companies focus on scaling, talent retention, and execution.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **BCG maps the business trade-offs across five quantum computing hardware approaches**

*Boston Consulting Group · September 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** BCG finds that superconducting, trapped-ion, neutral-atom, photonic and spin-qubit systems each offer different performance trade-offs, with no single architecture leading across all major business-relevant dimensions.

BCG's latest analysis compares the five principal quantum hardware approaches currently reaching the market. It highlights differences in factors including scalability, performance, connectivity and operating requirements, emphasizing that hardware selection increasingly depends on the intended application rather than a universal technology winner.

**Why it matters:** As enterprises move from quantum experimentation toward application planning, hardware architecture becomes a strategic procurement and technology-roadmap decision. The analysis also underscores why organizations should evaluate quantum use cases against specific hardware characteristics rather than treating "quantum computing" as a single technology category.

### 2. 📊 **IQM introduces long-term share-based incentive plan through 2032**

*IQM Quantum Computers · September 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IQM has established a 2026–2032 performance-share program designed to retain key employees and align management and staff incentives with the company's long-term value creation.

The program contains three four-year performance periods covering 2026–2030, 2027–2031 and 2028–2032. Awards are tied to performance conditions and are intended to encourage employees to remain with the company while aligning their interests with shareholders over multiple years.

**Why it matters:** Quantum hardware development requires long engineering cycles, specialized talent and sustained capital investment. A multi-year incentive structure signals that IQM is planning around the long commercialization timelines characteristic of fault-tolerant quantum computing.
