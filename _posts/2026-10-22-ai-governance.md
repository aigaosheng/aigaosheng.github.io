# AI Governance Brief: UBS Expects Light-Touch US AI Regulation to Persist

**Today:** A quiet day for binding rules: one bank forecast says Washington will stay light-touch on AI, and one Indian state has published a responsible-AI policy.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **UBS Says US Midterms Are Unlikely to Bring Major AI Rules**
*ANI · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** UBS expects the US to keep a light-touch approach to AI regulation through the midterms, largely because it is counting on AI to support economic growth.

ANI reports that UBS expects the Democrats to retake the House and possibly the Senate. It still does not expect notable new AI regulation to follow. UBS ties this to AI's economic weight: hyperscaler capital spending is projected at nearly 2% of US GDP in 2026. It estimates that AI investment and the related wealth effect from higher equity prices added around 100 basis points to first-half 2026 growth. ANI also notes that the voluntary agreement the White House announced with six major AI developers this month does not create legally enforceable requirements.

**Why it matters:** For Asia-based fintech and enterprise teams selling into the US, the forecast suggests near-term compliance pressure is more likely to come from outside Washington, such as the EU and Asian regimes, than from a new federal statute. This is one bank's forecast, not a policy decision.

### 2. 🏦 **Goa Launches State AI Policy With a 50,000-Person Skilling Target**
*The Goan · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Indian state of Goa has launched an AI policy that pairs a large skilling target with a pillar on responsible and ethical AI.

Chief Minister Pramod Sawant and IT Minister Rohan Khaunte unveiled the Goa AI Policy 2026 at the state's first AI Tech Conclave. It aims to train and certify 50,000 people in AI by 2030. The policy covers 2026–2029 and rests on five pillars: skilling, startups and industry, capital and investment, policy and infrastructure, and responsible and ethical AI. It also plans a technology hub at Porvorim.

**Why it matters:** As reported, the policy sets goals and institutions rather than binding duties for companies, so it is mainly a signal of how Indian states are positioning on AI adoption. Fintechs operating in India should watch for any implementing rules that follow.

## Key Takeaways

- Neither story creates a new binding obligation for companies. Both reflect promotion-first, voluntary approaches to AI.
- Economic growth arguments are shaping how much appetite policymakers have for tighter AI rules.
- Analyst forecasts and state-level policies are filling the gap while federal US legislation remains stalled.

## What to Watch

Watch how the US midterm results shape any AI bills, and whether the voluntary six-company safety agreement is followed by enforceable requirements. In India, look for implementation details behind Goa's policy.
