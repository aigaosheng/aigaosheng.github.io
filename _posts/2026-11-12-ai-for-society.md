# AI for Society Brief: ANZ Chief Warns AI Job Cuts Risk Crisis

**Today:** Two stories frame the same tension from opposite ends of the system — a major bank CEO calling for global rules to manage AI-driven job losses, and a Southeast Asian city moving ahead with compulsory AI education before those rules exist.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **ANZ Chief Warns of Global Crisis Risk From AI Job Cuts**

*Insurance Journal · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** ANZ CEO Nuno Matos told an Australian parliamentary inquiry that unchecked AI-driven job losses could trigger a "global crisis" and called for coordinated international regulation to manage the fallout.

Testifying in Canberra, Matos said large-scale cuts of up to 50% of some workforces would not stay confined to Australia, describing AI as "one of the few examples" where global rules are needed so "society does not go into trouble." He pointed to Canva's 22% staff reduction and Microsoft's buyout offer to roughly a quarter of its 223,000 employees as evidence of the scale of disruption already underway, while noting ANZ is building its own AI control platform and using the technology for fraud and cyber-risk detection.

**Why it matters:** For banks and enterprises across Asia-Pacific, a bank CEO publicly framing AI workforce disruption as a systemic risk — not just an efficiency gain — signals how boards may justify AI-linked restructuring and how regulators in the region could approach oversight of AI-driven labour shifts.

### 2. 👥 **Ho Chi Minh City Mandates AI Lessons for All School Grades**

*Sài Gòn Giải Phóng (SGGP) · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Ho Chi Minh City will roll out compulsory AI education across grades 1 to 12 from the 2026–2027 school year, woven into existing subjects rather than taught as a standalone class.

Announced at a workshop by the city's Department of Education and Training, the curriculum covers four competency areas — human-centric thinking, AI ethics, technical application and system design — delivered over 12 teaching periods a year. Officials and academics at the workshop flagged unresolved bottlenecks, including unclear guidance on which teachers should deliver the content, gaps in teacher training and infrastructure, and student data-safety concerns.

**Why it matters:** As Southeast Asian economies race to build AI-literate workforces, Vietnam's top-down, system-wide mandate contrasts with the patchier, opt-in guidance seen in many other markets, making it an early test of whether a developing-economy school system can scale AI literacy without widening gaps between well-resourced and under-resourced schools.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://en.sggp.org.vn/ho-chi-minh-city-brings-ai-education-into-classrooms-post130954.html)

## Key Takeaways

- Bank executives are increasingly framing AI-driven job displacement as a systemic, cross-border risk rather than a purely operational matter, raising pressure for coordinated policy responses.
- Governments in Asia are moving at different speeds on AI literacy — Vietnam's mandatory, system-wide curriculum rollout contrasts with slower, guidance-light approaches seen elsewhere.
- Both stories point to the same underlying gap: AI adoption in finance and education is outpacing the governance, training and safety-net structures needed to manage its social impact.

## What to Watch

Commonwealth Bank and NAB were due to testify before the same Australian parliamentary inquiry the following day, which should show whether other major lenders echo Matos's call for global AI regulation. Separately, Ho Chi Minh City's rollout will face its first real test as the 2026–2027 school year gets underway, particularly on teacher readiness.
