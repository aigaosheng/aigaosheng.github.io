# Payments Brief: Gnosis Pay Breach Exposes Customer Card Data

**Today:** A card-data breach at a crypto debit card provider and fresh Southeast Asian e-commerce payments data headline a quiet news day, both carrying direct lessons for card programs and payment-method strategy in Asia.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Gnosis Pay Data Breach Exposes Card Numbers and Wallet Links**

*CoinDesk · November 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Gnosis Pay disclosed a data breach that tied customers' card numbers and home addresses to their linked crypto wallets, raising fresh fraud risks for card-based stablecoin products.

Gnosis Pay, a crypto debit card provider, said it detected unauthorized access to its consumer card system on November 3 and contained the breach within two hours, but the exposure included card numbers, home addresses, customer names and past purchase data tied to linked crypto wallets. The company said customer funds were not affected, has since made 3-D Secure verification mandatory for online card payments, and stepped up fraud monitoring; it had previously disclosed a June 2026 exploit costing roughly $1.5 million, which it covered.

**Why it matters:** The incident shows exposed card-to-wallet mappings create a new targeted-scam vector as stablecoin-linked card programs scale. Issuers and program managers expanding similar products across Asia should expect comparable pressure to harden authentication and monitoring.

### 2. 💳 **QR Payments Surge In Philippines, Cash-On-Delivery Still Leads**

*Manila Times · November 8, 2026*

**Bottom line:** QR Ph and InstaPay transactions nearly tripled on Xendit's platform, yet cash-on-delivery remains the single largest payment method in Philippine e-commerce.

Citing the Xendit-Cube Ecommerce Payments Momentum Report 2026, QR Ph and InstaPay payment value grew 198% between January 2025 and April 2026, now representing about 13% of Philippine e-commerce payment value, while cash-on-delivery held roughly 23% of value — the highest share among six Southeast Asian markets studied. The report also found 41% of Filipino shoppers abandoned a purchase in the past three months because their preferred payment method wasn't offered, and 92% of surveyed business leaders called payment-method diversity important or critical to growth.

**Why it matters:** For payment providers and BaaS platforms operating in the Philippines, the data confirms QR and instant-payment rails are gaining real traction, but merchants dropping cash-on-delivery risk losing price-sensitive, trust-cautious shoppers — a tension distinct from more digitally mature ASEAN markets like Singapore.

## Key Takeaways

- Card-linked stablecoin and crypto payment products are becoming a visible fraud target as adoption scales, raising the bar for authentication and monitoring across issuers.
- Southeast Asia's payment mix remains uneven: instant and QR rails are gaining share fast, but cash-dependent habits persist strongly in markets like the Philippines, requiring multi-rail merchant strategies.
- Payment-method availability is a direct conversion lever — survey data shows a meaningful share of shoppers abandon purchases outright when their preferred method is missing.

Separately, watch whether other crypto-linked card issuers disclose similar exposures before Gnosis Pay's planned legacy system shutdown on December 20, 2026.
