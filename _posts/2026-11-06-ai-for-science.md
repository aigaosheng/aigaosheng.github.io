# AI for science Brief: Roche Taps Chai Discovery for Protein Design

**Today:** A cluster of fresh pharma and materials deals shows AI-native drug design moving from pilot projects to production pipelines at major enterprises, with one Korean drugmaker and a YC-backed challenge testing how far current models can go.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔬 **Roche Deploys Chai Discovery's AI Models Across Drug Pipelines**

*BioSpace / Business Wire · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Roche will embed Chai Discovery's generative protein-design platform into its core research and early development programmes, extending AI-native biology into a top-five global pharma pipeline.

Chai Discovery said Roche will use its computer-aided design suite for protein-based therapeutics, anchored by the Chai-3 foundation model, which the company says achieves high target hit rates and nanomolar-range binding affinities when generating novel molecular binders from scratch. Financial terms were not disclosed. The deal follows Chai's 2026 agreements with Novartis, argenx, Bristol Myers Squibb and GSK, and comes four months after the startup closed a $400 million Series C at a $3.8 billion valuation.

**Why it matters:** Roche's adoption signals that generative protein design is shifting from predictive folding tools to active molecule synthesis inside mainstream pharma R&D, intensifying competition with DeepMind-linked Isomorphic Labs and China's XtalPi for enterprise drug-discovery contracts.

### 2. 🔬 **YC-Backed Startup Puts $1 Million on AI Solving Materials Puzzles**

*TechCrunch · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Discovered Materials is challenging AI models with a $1 million prize to crack five long-unsolved materials science problems, after finding that frontier models made little headway working autonomously for days.

The Y Combinator-backed startup, working with an academic advisory group including Stanford's Felipe Jornada, structured the contest around $200,000 prizes for problems such as explaining cuprate superconductivity and modelling silicon-silicon dioxide interfaces used in most semiconductors. Founder Advaith Sridhar said materials science is harder for AI than mathematics because problems are less precisely specified, though he is "more confident now" of reaching industrially useful materials within a year.

**Why it matters:** The contest sets a measurable public benchmark for AI's biggest current weak spot — physics-grounded materials discovery — at a time when chipmakers and battery developers are racing to find next-generation semiconductor and energy-storage materials.

### 3. 🔬 **SK Biopharmaceuticals Builds AI Model Trained on 7 Million Drug Records**

*Seoul Economic Daily · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea's SK Biopharmaceuticals unveiled a government-backed AI model co-developed with Trillion Labs that predicts drug toxicity, target interactions and clinical trial outcomes.

The model was trained on roughly 7.14 million data entries spanning small molecules, proteins and genomes, and was evaluated across 66 tasks from toxicity prediction to clinical outcomes, drawing on proprietary pharmaceutical data for validation. The company also built a digital twin forecasting Alzheimer's cognitive decline and secured Nvidia B200 GPU infrastructure to scale AI use across research, candidate discovery and clinical development; quantitative accuracy figures were not disclosed.

**Why it matters:** The project adds South Korea, alongside China's XtalPi and Insilico Medicine, to the list of Asian drugmakers building proprietary foundation models rather than relying solely on Western AI vendors, broadening the region's stake in AI-native pharma R&D.

### 4. 📊 **Fathom Therapeutics Raises $55 Million for Physics-Based AI Drug Design**

*PR Newswire · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Fathom Therapeutics closed a $55 million Series A led by Sutter Hill Ventures, with Kos Biotechnology Partners joining, to scale its Microcosmos platform for discovering "undruggable" small-molecule targets.

Microcosmos pairs quantum chemistry and physics-based molecular simulations with AI to model protein movement and drug-molecule interactions at atomic resolution, an approach distinct from purely data-driven generative models. Kos co-founder Alex Tzoukas said the next wave of biopharma innovation will come from combining computational science, AI and experimental biology.

**Why it matters:** The round underscores continued investor appetite for physics-grounded AI drug-discovery approaches even as valuations for generative-only platforms climb, giving enterprise pharma buyers a wider menu of computational methods to evaluate.

## Key Takeaways

- Big pharma is moving from AI pilots to production deployments, with Roche joining Novartis, argenx, BMS and GSK as Chai Discovery customers in a single year.
- Materials science remains AI's hardest frontier: even frontier models made little progress on Discovered Materials' five benchmark problems, highlighting a gap enterprise R&D teams should factor into timelines.
- Asian drugmakers are building proprietary AI infrastructure rather than only licensing Western platforms, with SK Biopharmaceuticals' government-backed model a notable data point.
- Capital continues to flow into both generative (Chai, $3.8B valuation) and physics-based (Fathom, $55M Series A) AI drug-discovery approaches, suggesting no single method has yet won out.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Discovered Materials' $1 million challenge attracts verifiable solutions from frontier labs, which would be a concrete signal of AI's readiness for physics-heavy materials problems rather than biology-only tasks. Also monitor whether Roche discloses specific therapeutic programmes using Chai-3, which would clarify how far generative protein design has moved beyond early-stage screening.
