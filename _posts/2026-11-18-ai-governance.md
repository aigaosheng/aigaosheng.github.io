# AI Governance Brief: New Zealand Commerce Commission Issues AI Guidance

**Today:** A competition and consumer regulator in Asia-Pacific has put boards on notice that existing law already governs their AI use, a signal for any enterprise or fintech deploying AI across the region.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **New Zealand Commerce Commission Issues AI Guidance for Businesses**

*New Zealand Commerce Commission · November 18, 2026*

**Bottom line:** New Zealand's competition and consumer regulator says AI does not change businesses' legal duties, so firms stay accountable for outcomes whether a person or an AI system made the decision.

The Commission published guidance explaining how the Fair Trading Act 1986 and the Commerce Act 1986 apply when businesses use AI. It sets out the main risks that could lead to a breach and practical steps to manage them. Deputy Chair Anne Callinan said the Commission's focus is AI's effect on consumers, competition and markets rather than regulating the technology itself, and that policymakers may consider further tools or safeguards as AI evolves.

The Commission urges boards and senior leaders to treat AI as a governance issue. That means knowing where AI is used, understanding the risks and building competition and consumer protection into AI risk management. It has scheduled a free webinar with the AI Forum on 24 November.

**Why it matters:** The guidance relies on existing consumer and competition law instead of a new AI statute, which is a common approach across Asia-Pacific. For firms operating in New Zealand, including fintechs and payments providers, AI-driven pricing, marketing claims and customer communications now sit squarely within regulatory scrutiny.

## Key Takeaways

- Regulators are increasingly applying existing consumer protection and competition law to AI instead of waiting for dedicated AI legislation.
- Accountability is being placed with the deploying business and its board, and the Commission's guidance frames this as a governance and risk-management matter.
- Compliance teams should inventory where AI is used and map each use to the relevant consumer protection and competition obligations.

## What to Watch

Watch the Commerce Commission and AI Forum webinar on 24 November for further detail on how the guidance will be applied in practice. Also watch whether other Asia-Pacific competition and consumer regulators issue comparable AI guidance.
