# AI Finance in Singapore Brief: AI Data-Centre Lending Faces Chip-Curb Risks

**Today:** New US chip restrictions are prompting lenders to reassess AI infrastructure exposure, with Singapore banks and UOB among those facing financing decisions.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **AI Data-Centre Lending Faces Chip-Curb Risks**

*The Straits Times · November 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** New US chip restrictions are delaying or jeopardising billions of dollars in Asia-Pacific data-centre loans, while Singapore banks review their exposure.

The Straits Times, reporting Bloomberg’s findings, says lenders are revisiting financing tied to data centres serving Chinese technology firms. UOB is seeking a roughly US$1 billion significant risk transfer involving data-centre and infrastructure loans, though the details remain uncertain, according to people familiar with the matter. The report says regional data-centre lending has exceeded US$49 billion since 2023, based on Bloomberg data.

**Why it matters:** Financing for AI infrastructure is increasingly exposed to export rules and the identity of data-centre customers, not just project economics. For lenders and AI infrastructure operators across Asia, closer scrutiny could affect deal timing, risk transfer and access to capital.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.straitstimes.com/business/billions-in-data-centre-deals-grind-to-halt-after-ai-chip-curbs)

## Key Takeaways

- AI infrastructure financing in Asia is sensitive to policy changes affecting chip access and compute customers.
- Singapore banks are reassessing data-centre exposure as lenders weigh legal, customer and concentration risks.
- UOB’s proposed risk transfer is a signal to watch, but the reported deal details are not final.

## What to Watch

Monitor whether UOB completes the reported risk transfer and whether lenders resume, restructure or withdraw from financing for data-centre projects serving Chinese technology firms.
