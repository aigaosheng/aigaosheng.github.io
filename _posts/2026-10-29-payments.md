# Payments Brief: Tencent and Visa Expand Cross-Border Payments Pact

**Today:** Two of the largest consumer payment ecosystems in Asia and the West are wiring themselves together, while tokenised money moves from pilot decks into live settlement and retail refunds.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Tencent and Visa Sign MoU Covering Cross-Border Payments and Agentic Commerce**

*Tencent · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Tencent and Visa have agreed a framework to connect Weixin Pay and TenPay Global with Visa's networks for traveller payments, remittances and AI-driven commerce.

The memorandum covers inbound and outbound traveller payments through TenPay Global and Visa Pay, deeper remittance flows into the Weixin Pay ecosystem via Visa Direct, and exploration of agentic payments using Visa Intelligent Commerce. The two companies also said they will extend their existing work on global payments, joint marketing and risk management for Tencent's gaming and AI businesses. No volumes, investment figures or launch dates were disclosed, and the agreement is framed as intent rather than a live deployment.

**Why it matters:** Travel and remittance corridors between China and the rest of Asia are among the region's largest payment flows, and a single commercial framework spanning wallet, card and agentic rails would reshape acceptance economics for banks and acquirers across Singapore, Hong Kong and Southeast Asia.

### 2. 💳 **DTCC Switches On Tokenised Securities Service With 20 Banks**

*CoinDesk · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DTCC's Tokenization Service went live, letting 20 major institutions mint and move tokenised versions of US equities, ETFs and Treasuries around the clock.

The service runs on the Canton Network and DTCC's private Besu network, with 34 eligible securities at launch and tokens backed one-for-one by assets held at DTCC's depository subsidiary. Participants signed up include JPMorgan, Citigroup, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America and UBS, and transfers between registered wallets can run 24/7. DTCC's roadmap adds further chains in 2027 alongside tools for digital cash settlement and collateral use.

**Why it matters:** Once collateral and digital cash settle on the same infrastructure, the gap between securities settlement and payment settlement narrows, which is the practical precondition for the always-on treasury operations that corporates in Asia have been piloting with tokenised deposits.

### 3. 💳 **Japan to Pay Tourist Tax Refunds in JPYC Stablecoin From 1 November**

*UPBOND via PR Times · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Japan Tax-Free and UPBOND will issue consumption tax refunds to visiting tourists as JPYC, a yen-denominated stablecoin, settled on the Avalanche C-Chain.

The service starts on 1 November, the same day Japan's duty-free regime switches to a post-departure refund model in which shoppers pay the full tax-inclusive price and claim back the tax after customs confirmation. UPBOND handles wallet creation, authentication and transfer confirmation, so travellers without an existing wallet can be paid without retailers handling card or bank account details. Recipients can hold the JPYC for a future trip or swap it on-chain for other stablecoins.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the first consumer-scale, regulated uses of a fiat-referenced stablecoin for cross-border disbursement in Asia, and it gives payout providers a concrete benchmark against card refunds and international transfers.

### 4. 📊 **Thunes Study Finds Fee and Speed Gaps in China's Cross-Border Flows**

*Thunes · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Research from Thunes and Juniper Research finds Chinese consumers are overwhelmingly digital-first in domestic payments but still face high fees and slow settlement on cross-border transfers.

The study, part of Thunes' Cross-Border Payments Interoperability Index for Asia Pacific, reports that only 15% of Chinese respondents rely on cash daily and 28% of Gen Z opened a super-app as their first financial account. On cross-border flows, 51% of receivers and 44% of senders reported fees above 3%, while 31% said international transfers took more than two days. E-commerce was the most common reason for sending money abroad, cited by 51% of senders.

**Why it matters:** The findings quantify the pricing headroom that wallet-to-wallet and stablecoin-backed payout networks are targeting in the region's largest consumer market, though the data comes from a vendor-commissioned survey and should be read accordingly.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://thunes.com/news/thunes-and-juniper-research-study-highlights-powerful-opportunities-to-unlock-global-commerce-growth-in-china)

### 5. 🌐 **Maybank Opens Ringgit Corporate Accounts to Singapore Companies**

*Fintech News Singapore · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Maybank Singapore has extended its Malaysian ringgit current account to Singapore-registered corporates, letting them hold and convert ringgit ahead of scheduled payments.

The account is open to non-financial companies registered outside Malaysia that already hold a Maybank Singapore dollar corporate account, with no initial deposit, minimum balance or monthly fee, and no interest paid. Permitted uses include paying Malaysian suppliers, asset transactions in Malaysia and collecting dividends and interest, accessed through the bank's corporate banking platforms. Maybank linked the launch to rising cross-border activity following the formalisation of the Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone.

**Why it matters:** Pre-funding in local currency reduces FX timing risk for treasurers running Singapore-Malaysia supply chains, and signals that banks see the JS-SEZ corridor as a durable corporate payments opportunity rather than a policy headline.

### 6. 💳 **JCB Signs Hanoi Metro Deal for Contactless Transit Fares**

*JCB via JCN Newswire · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** JCB International and Hanoi Metro have signed a memorandum of understanding to introduce open-loop contactless fare payments on Hanoi's urban rail network from early 2027.

Under the planned JCB transit service, cardholders would tap a contactless JCB card or compatible device at fare gates instead of buying a separate ticket. JCB positioned the service as a convenience for international visitors, including travellers from Japan, Korea and Taiwan, and as a contribution to Vietnam's cashless transport agenda. The scheme already supports contactless transit in several other cities.

**Why it matters:** Open-loop transit is a reliable on-ramp for contactless habit formation in cash-heavy markets, and card networks are using it to secure acceptance footholds in Vietnam ahead of broader retail rollouts.

### 7. 📊 **Former Swift Chief Joins Stablecoin Yield Platform OpenTrade**

*CoinDesk · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Lazaro Campos, who previously led Swift, has become chairman of London-based OpenTrade as the stablecoin yield platform pushes further into institutional infrastructure.

OpenTrade provides infrastructure that lets businesses offer dollar- and euro-denominated returns backed by real-world assets without building their own investment systems. CoinDesk reports the firm holds about US$300 million in total value locked, has passed US$1 billion in annual transaction volume and has raised US$32 million to date from backers including a16z crypto, Notion Capital and CMCC Global. Campos also chairs Payoneer Europe and has served on Bank of England payment system boards.

**Why it matters:** Senior traditional-rails operators moving into stablecoin infrastructure is a useful signal of where institutional treasury yield products are heading, and of the governance expectations those products will have to meet.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/29/former-swift-ceo-joins-stablecoin-firm-opentrade-as-chairman-after-a-usd17-million-raise)

## Key Takeaways

- Tokenised money is shifting from proof-of-concept to production in two different directions at once: wholesale settlement and collateral at DTCC, and retail disbursement through Japan's stablecoin tax refunds.
- Asia's cross-border value is being captured at the edges — traveller QR, transit fares and corridor-specific corporate accounts — rather than through a single universal rail.
- Agentic commerce has moved into formal commercial frameworks between incumbents, with Tencent and Visa treating AI-initiated payments as a scoped workstream rather than an experiment.

## What to Watch

Whether Japan's JPYC refund service operates smoothly from its 1 November start, and whether Tencent and Visa convert their memorandum into named products with disclosed launch markets; also watch DTCC's stated plan to add further blockchain networks and digital cash settlement tooling in 2027.
