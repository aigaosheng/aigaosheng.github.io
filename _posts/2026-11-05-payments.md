# Payments Brief: Finmo Launches Multi-Currency SME Account

**Today:** A Singapore SME banking launch, an early agentic-commerce test case and a cross-border UPI incentive show payments innovation continuing to move faster at the product level than at the regulatory level.

## Top Stories

### 1. 💳 **Finmo Launches Multi-Currency Business Account for SMEs**

*Fintech Singapore · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Finmo, a Singapore-licensed fintech, launched a Business Account letting SMEs collect, hold and pay across dozens of currencies without routing through a traditional bank.

Singapore-incorporated businesses can collect payments in more than 45 currencies using local account details in their own name, hold over 27 currencies, and send funds to more than 195 countries and territories. The account integrates Finmo Treasury for multi-entity balance consolidation and cash-flow forecasting, plus bulk payments, scheduled transfers and approval controls; applicants apply via MyInfo Business using Corppass or Singpass. Finmo holds 11 licences across eight markets, including an MAS Major Payment Institution licence.

**Why it matters:** Non-bank platforms offering embedded multi-currency accounts are competing directly with transaction banks for SME cross-border trade flows, a shift that regional banks and BaaS providers serving Singapore, the Philippines and Indonesia will need to respond to.

### 2. 🤖 **Reap, Meitu and Pivota Pilot Agentic Beauty Payments**

*Cision PR Newswire (via newswire.ca) · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Hong Kong fintech Reap signed a non-binding agreement with Meitu's EveLab Insight and commerce platform Pivota to test an AI-driven, end-to-end beauty shopping and payment flow.

Under the MOU, Meitu's MakeupPlus app would surface personalised skincare and beauty recommendations, Pivota would match shoppers to merchants and products, and Reap would execute payment with explicit user approval and spending controls through its stablecoin-enabled payment infrastructure. The three companies describe the goal as closing the gap between AI-assisted product discovery and checkout.

**Why it matters:** This is a concrete, narrow test of agentic commerce rather than a platform-wide rollout, offering a template other Asian payments fintechs may copy as card networks push agent-initiated payments toward commercial scale.

### 3. 🌐 **NPCI Sweetens UPI Payments for Dubai Shoppers**

*Business Today · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** NPCI International Payments Limited introduced a 10% UPI discount at Dubai Duty Free to drive adoption of India's real-time payment rail among outbound travellers.

Indian travellers paying via UPI International or UPI Global at Dubai Duty Free outlets in Dubai International and Al Maktoum airports can get a discount capped at AED 100 across categories including fashion, electronics and jewellery, with Network International acting as acquirer. NIPL's chief executive said the offer pairs real-time UPI payments with added traveller value.

**Why it matters:** The promotion extends NPCI's push to normalise UPI usage outside India through commercial incentives rather than interoperability deals alone — a tactic other Asian real-time payment schemes may adopt as they compete for outbound travel and remittance spend.

### 4. 🏦 **WasabiCard Joins Macau Stablecoin Policy Roundtable**

*Manila Times (GlobeNewswire) · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** WasabiCard co-founder Ray Yang presented the firm's stablecoin-funded card and payout infrastructure at a University of Macau roundtable on cross-border stablecoin regulation.

The event, hosted by the University of Macau's Institute of Global and Public Affairs, gathered academics from Tsinghua and Macau alongside banking and payments industry representatives to discuss stablecoin oversight, implementation hurdles and Macau's potential role in regional dialogue. Yang argued that compliant stablecoin growth needs practical commercial use cases — corporate spend, payroll and supplier payments — alongside clear rules; this reflects an industry participant's view, not a regulatory position.

**Why it matters:** As Greater China and Southeast Asian regulators continue shaping stablecoin frameworks piecemeal, industry input at forums like this signals where compliant commercial use cases are emerging first, relevant for any payments provider weighing a stablecoin strategy in the region.

## Key Takeaways

- Non-bank platforms like Finmo are closing the gap with banks on multi-currency SME accounts, raising competitive pressure on regional transaction banking.
- Agentic commerce is advancing through narrow, vertical-specific pilots such as Reap-Meitu-Pivota rather than broad platform launches, suggesting faster real-world experimentation ahead of scale.
- National payment schemes are increasingly using commercial incentives, not just scheme interoperability, to drive outbound adoption — a tactic likely to spread across other Asian real-time rails.
