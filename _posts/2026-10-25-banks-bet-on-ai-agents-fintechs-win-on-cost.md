## 1. Top Headlines

- [Standard Chartered to acquire digital asset custodian Zodia Custody](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/standard-chartered-to-acquire-zodia-custody) (FinTech Futures): The bank is moving to take full ownership of the crypto custodian it incubated through SC Ventures. A full analysis follows below.

- [Fintechs top banks and stablecoins for cheap cross-border payment - BIS research](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48630/fintechs-top-banks-and-stablecoins-for-cheap-cross-border-payment---bis-research) (Finextra): New BIS research finds that fintech transfer operators such as Wise and Remitly cut cross-border costs well below traditional bank wires. Stablecoins lag because of conversion fees and thin liquidity outside the US dollar, which tempers the hype around crypto rails.

- [BBVA works with Nvidia to boost AI agent security](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48628/bbva-works-with-nvidia-to-boost-ai-agent-security) (Finextra): BBVA will test Nvidia's newly unveiled OpenShell Reference Architecture, which monitors and contains AI agents while they run. For banks scaling agents, the lesson is that controls built in at design time are not enough on their own.

- [UK sets out proposal to open up access to iPhone contactless payments](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48618/uk-sets-out-proposal-to-open-up-access-to-iphone-contactless-payments) (Finextra): The UK competition regulator wants Apple Pay rivals to reach the iPhone's contactless technology with lower fees and fewer technical barriers. If adopted, banks and fintechs could launch their own tap-to-pay wallets in the UK.

- [Klarna expands Fiserv partnership to enterprise merchants](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111349/klarna-expands-fiserv-partnership-to-enterprise-merchants) (Finextra): Klarna will be available on Fiserv's Commerce Hub across the US, Canada, the UK and select European markets, in store and online. It follows a launch at 100,000 US in-store locations via Clover, showing buy now, pay later moving deeper into physical retail.

- [Barclays to axe around 5,000 jobs this year](https://www.fintechfutures.com/job-cuts-new-hires/barclays-to-axe-around-5000-jobs-this-year) (FinTech Futures): Barclays plans to cut about 5% of its workforce, with 1,000 roles already gone and 4,000 more by year-end. The bank ties part of the reduction to AI-driven productivity gains.

- [LSEG to acquire Octus in $3.5bn all-cash deal](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/lseg-to-acquire-octus) (FinTech Futures): LSEG will buy the credit intelligence platform from Permira and management for US$3.535 billion. Octus serves more than 750 institutions, so the deal deepens LSEG's reach into private credit data.

- [Ramp applies for US national trust bank charter](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulations-compliance/ramp-applies-for-us-national-trust-bank-charter) (FinTech Futures): The expense management fintech has applied to the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for a national trust charter. It would offer fiduciary investment management and digital asset custody.

- [Cross River Bank to buy customer loyalty-focused paytech Accrue](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/cross-river-bank-acquires-accrue) (FinTech Futures): The deal will let Cross River's business customers embed own-branded wallets in their offerings. It points to banking-as-a-service providers bundling wallets and loyalty into their core proposition.

- [Money20/20 USA 2026 wraps with cross-border payments and open banking in focus](https://www.fintechfutures.com/fintech/money20-20-usa-2026-wraps-with-cross-border-payments-and-open-banking-in-focus) (FinTech Futures): The event closed with cross-border payments and open banking high on the agenda. Day two had been dominated by agentic commerce and autonomous transactions.

## 2. In-Depth Highlight

Standard Chartered has signed definitive agreements to acquire Zodia Custody, following shareholder acceptance of its non-binding offer earlier this year. According to the [bank's announcement](https://www.sc.com/en/press-release/t/), a Standard Chartered entity will buy 100% of Zodia Custody Limited, the parent of regulated custody entities in Singapore, Hong Kong, Australia, the UAE and Luxembourg. The regulated custody business will be folded into the bank's existing digital asset custody activities within Securities Services, while the Zodia Solutions software business continues separately and keeps serving institutions, including Standard Chartered. The bank says no disruption is expected for existing clients.

Why it matters: a global bank is bringing regulated crypto custody in-house rather than leaving it in an incubated venture. That is a sign that digital asset custody is becoming a mainstream banking service. The deal still needs regulatory approvals, and with entities across several Asia-Pacific hubs, supervisors in multiple jurisdictions will be part of that process. More coverage: [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/standard-chartered-to-acquire-zodia-custody).

## 3. Market & Industry Insight

AI is moving from pilot to payroll and, increasingly, to governance. Barclays says 60% of its planned cuts fall in support functions, and National Australia Bank expects to [reduce its workforce by up to 2,500 roles, about 6%, in FY27](https://www.fintechfutures.com/job-cuts-new-hires/nab-plans-to-cut-2500-jobs-in-fy27), as part of an operating-model restructure. At the same time, banks are asking how to keep autonomous software in check. [BBVA's work with Nvidia](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48628/bbva-works-with-nvidia-to-boost-ai-agent-security) focuses on watching and containing agents while they act, and FinTech Futures reported that [AI payments dominated day two of Money20/20 USA](https://www.fintechfutures.com/ai-in-fintech/money2020-usa-2026-agentic-commerce-take-centre-stage-on-day-two).

Cross-border payments tell a parallel story about architecture over hype. The [BIS working paper](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1385-competing-rails-crossborder-payments-banks-fintechs-and-stablecoins) compares banks, fintech money transfer operators and stablecoins, and finds that fintechs have compressed costs the most. Stablecoins are not a cure-all: their competitiveness also depends on market liquidity, access to fiat currencies, regulatory compliance and trust in intermediaries. For payment providers, the practical takeaway is that winning on cost depends on the whole payment chain, not just the settlement rail.

## 4. Company & Startup Spotlight

**Salmon (Philippines):** The digital banking and lending group has [raised US$90 million in financing plus US$50 million in bond proceeds](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48608/salmon-raises-funds-to-double-down-in-the-philippines), committed by Spice Expeditions, WashU and Moore. It uses an AI-powered credit engine to lend to customers with limited credit history, and the funds will scale credit line and in-store lending and support new motorcycle and MSME lending. It is worth watching as a test of whether AI-led underwriting can serve the Philippines' underbanked population at scale.

**Whop (United States):** The platform that helps entrepreneurs run digital storefronts, accept fiat and digital payments, and automate with AI has [landed a US$200 million Series D at a US$5.1 billion valuation](https://www.fintechfutures.com/venture-capital-funding/whop-200m-series-d). Sequoia Capital led, with Shopify Ventures participating. It is the company's second raise this year, after a February round from Tether at US$1.6 billion.

## 5. Regulatory & Policy Watch

- The UK competition regulator is [consulting on opening iPhone contactless access](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48618/uk-sets-out-proposal-to-open-up-access-to-iphone-contactless-payments) to rival wallets through both main contactless standards. It is not proposing to set the price of access, and the consultation runs until 17 November.

- Ramp's [national trust bank charter application](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulations-compliance/ramp-applies-for-us-national-trust-bank-charter) is now with the US OCC. The proposed trust bank would not take deposits or make commercial loans, and would bring fiduciary and digital asset custody services under a federal framework.
