# US AI vs China AI Brief: Beijing Opposes US DeepSeek Ban

**Today:** Beijing has pushed back on Washington's ban on DeepSeek in federal systems, while Chinese equities slide on doubts about AI profitability.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Beijing Opposes US Government Ban on DeepSeek**
*Agence France-Presse (via Daily Tribune) · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's foreign ministry has rejected the US order to strip DeepSeek from federal civilian systems, calling it an overreach of national security, even as both sides keep an AI incident channel open ahead of a leaders' meeting next month.

The Department of Homeland Security banned DeepSeek products, and third-party tools built on its models, from civilian agencies on Monday. It said the models could send sensitive data to China, where DeepSeek's servers sit under a legal system that gives the state broad access to private infrastructure. The order does not cover the military or intelligence services. On Tuesday, foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said Beijing opposes politicising technology and trade and will protect Chinese companies' rights. AFP notes that DeepSeek released its V4 model in April and updated a lighter, faster version this month.

**Why it matters:** The order reaches products built on DeepSeek models, not only DeepSeek's own apps. Vendors, including Asia-based fintech and enterprise software firms that sell to US government customers, may need to check which models sit inside their products. This is an analyst's reading, not a finding of the source.

### 2. 📊 **Chinese Stocks Enter Bear Market as AI Profit Doubts Mount**
*Bloomberg · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Chinese equities have fallen into a bear market, and investor doubts about AI companies' profitability are now one of the drags alongside weak consumption.

Bloomberg reports that roughly $2.4 trillion of market value has been wiped out since the market peaked earlier this year. The CSI 300 Index closed 1.8% lower on Thursday and now sits about 20% below its June high. Major Hong Kong-listed company and tech gauges also fell sharply, in a selloff that shows few signs of easing.

**Why it matters:** Policy rivalry with the US is only part of the picture. Markets are also questioning whether China's AI buildout will pay off, which could affect funding appetite and valuations for Chinese AI firms and the regional investors exposed to them. This is an analyst's reading, not a finding of the source.

## Key Takeaways

- Washington is extending the rivalry from chips to software supply chains, with procurement rules that reach third-party products built on Chinese models.
- Beijing's response so far is diplomatic protest, and both governments are still keeping their AI incident channel alive.
- Financial markets are starting to price in doubts about Chinese AI returns, separate from geopolitics.

## What to Watch

The leaders' meeting in Shenzhen next month, where AFP expects the AI incident channel to come up again. Any concrete Chinese countermeasure to the DeepSeek order would also be a signal.
