# Quantum Brief: QuEra, Harvard, Anthropic Cut Circuit Costs

**Today:** A cluster of hardware, funding and AI-assisted research updates shows quantum computing shifting from lab milestones toward commercially credible roadmaps, with direct implications for how enterprises should plan post-quantum security and compute partnerships.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **QuEra, Harvard and Anthropic Use Claude to Shrink Quantum Circuits**

*The Quantum Insider · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Researchers paired Anthropic's Claude with an automated evaluator to redesign quantum circuits, cutting resource requirements by up to 39 times on some tasks.

QuEra Computing, Harvard University and Anthropic reported improvements across seven of eight tested computational building blocks, including a Reed-Solomon decoder that needed roughly 12 times fewer Toffoli gates and a Gaussian-function circuit that needed about 39 times fewer combined gate-and-qubit resources. The team also surfaced a previously undocumented reversible modular-inversion technique offering new gate-versus-qubit trade-offs.

**Why it matters:** If AI-assisted circuit design generalises, it could let fault-tolerant quantum computers tackle useful workloads on smaller, less error-prone hardware sooner than hardware scaling alone would allow, a timeline question that matters directly to banks and insurers budgeting for quantum-era risk.

### 2. 📊 **Rigetti Hits Fidelity Goal, Banks $100M Government Deal**

*The Quantum Insider · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Rigetti's Q3 2026 results combined a hardware milestone with fresh government backing, even as near-term revenue stayed modest.

The company's Cepheus-1-108Q system reached its targeted 99.5% median two-qubit gate fidelity across 108 qubits built from twelve interconnected chiplets, with gate speeds of 50–70 nanoseconds. Rigetti reported $1.4 million in Q3 revenue and a $33.2 million operating loss, while finalising a $100 million CHIPS Act agreement with the US Department of Commerce that gives the department a minority equity stake in exchange for R&D funding in fabrication and cryogenics.

**Why it matters:** Government co-investment alongside fidelity gains signals state-level confidence in superconducting roadmaps, a pattern Singapore and other Asian governments are watching as they weigh their own quantum infrastructure bets.

### 3. 📊 **Gartner Names IonQ a Market Shaper, Cites South Korea Deployment**

*The Quantum Insider · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Gartner's first Emerging Market Quadrant for quantum computing placed IonQ among vendors it considers able to reliably deliver commercial systems today.

Gartner's report pointed to IonQ's progress in DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, worth up to $300 million if milestones are met, alongside US Air Force funding and system deployments at Florida International University, NVIDIA's research centre and South Korea's SDT. Gartner's framing emphasised that quantum computing is moving from experimental use into measurable commercial deployment.

**Why it matters:** Independent analyst validation and an active Asian deployment give enterprise buyers in the region a reference point for vetting quantum vendors, though Gartner itself cautions against treating ratings as purchase advice.

### 4. 📊 **Dutch Consortium Backs QuiX Quantum to Build 100+ Qubit Photonic Machine**

*The Quantum Insider · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** QuiX Quantum will lead a government-funded national mission to build the technology foundations for a universal photonic quantum computer exceeding 100 qubits.

The project, backed under the Dutch-German PhotonDelta National Growth Fund programme, runs from January 2026 to December 2028 and brings together QuiX Quantum with nine Dutch industrial and academic partners developing twelve component technologies for the system.

**Why it matters:** The multi-year, multi-partner structure reflects how governments are now funding quantum hardware as strategic infrastructure rather than isolated research grants, a model Asian science ministries are replicating for their own photonic and superconducting programmes.

### 5. ⚛️ **Xanadu and HyperLight Cut Photonic Switch Loss Nearly Threefold**

*The Quantum Insider · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Xanadu and HyperLight reduced optical-switch insertion loss to 7 millidecibels, down from 20 mdB in 2025, on a thin-film lithium niobate chiplet platform.

The companies said the result is roughly ten times better than published competitor benchmarks and is manufacturable at high-volume wafer scale. Because quantum signals cannot be conventionally amplified without destroying the quantum state, lower insertion loss directly preserves signal fidelity as systems scale.

**Why it matters:** Component-level advances like this reduce the laser power and hardware redundancy needed for photonic quantum computers, a prerequisite for the utility-scale systems that cloud and fintech partners would eventually access commercially.

### 6. 🔬 **Osaka Links Supercomputer and Quantum Computer in Single Workflow**

*The Quantum Insider · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** University of Osaka researchers launched SEAFOOD, Japan's first platform integrating a domestically built quantum computer with the OCTOPUS supercomputer in one research workflow.

The system uses open-source software called OQTOPUS to route tasks between the two machines, with a proof-of-concept quantum-classical calculation on four hydrogen atoms matching conventional reference results. Full operations begin in December 2026, with trial access for University of Osaka research teams planned for fiscal year 2027.

**Why it matters:** Hybrid quantum-classical workflows are the near-term path to practical chemistry and materials applications, and Japan's early domestic integration adds to a wider Asian push, alongside Quantinuum's RIKEN and SoftBank partnership, to build regional quantum-AI compute capacity.

## Key Takeaways

- AI is becoming a quantum R&D tool in its own right: the QuEra-Harvard-Anthropic result shows large language models can shrink circuit resource needs, potentially compressing the path to useful fault-tolerant computing.
- Government co-funding is now standard across quantum hardware bets, from Rigetti's CHIPS Act deal to the Dutch national photonic mission, reinforcing quantum as strategic state infrastructure rather than purely private-sector research.
- Asia's quantum footprint is broadening beyond announcements into deployments, with IonQ's South Korea installation and Japan's Osaka hybrid platform both cited as evidence of regional commercial and research traction.
