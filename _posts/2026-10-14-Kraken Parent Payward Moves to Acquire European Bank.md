# Payments Brief: Kraken Parent Payward Moves to Acquire European Bank

**Today:** Crypto firms are pushing deeper into regulated banking, while India adjusts its merchant payment economics and European banks advance a euro-denominated stablecoin. Together, these developments highlight the convergence of traditional banking, digital assets and payment infrastructure.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Kraken Parent Payward Moves to Acquire Lithuania's Saldo Bank**

*Financial Times · October 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Payward, the parent company of crypto exchange Kraken, is moving to acquire Saldo Bank, marking a significant step towards combining regulated banking services with digital-asset products.

The Lithuania-based bank offers consumer and business lending alongside term deposits. The proposed acquisition would give Payward a route to integrate traditional financial services, including lending and card issuance, with crypto, tokenised stocks and cash management. Completion is expected in 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

**Why it matters:** Crypto platforms are increasingly seeking direct access to regulated financial infrastructure rather than relying exclusively on banking partners. If completed, the deal could strengthen Payward's ability to offer integrated financial products, although regulatory permissions and the operational integration of banking and crypto services remain critical hurdles.

### 2. 💳 **India Delays UPI Merchant Discount Rate Rollout Beyond Diwali**

*The Financial Express · October 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has postponed the planned introduction of merchant discount rates on eligible UPI transactions until after Diwali, leaving the revised implementation date to be announced.

The proposed framework includes a 0.4% charge on eligible person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000, subject to a ₹300 cap per transaction. The postponement gives merchants and payment providers additional time to prepare during the busy festive shopping period. Industry groups have also sought changes to the proposed thresholds, exemptions and fee structure.

**Why it matters:** India's decision illustrates the policy challenge of sustaining large-scale real-time payment infrastructure without undermining merchant adoption. For payment service providers and merchants operating in India, the final pricing framework will affect transaction economics, technology readiness and potentially the balance between digital payments and cash.

### 3. 🏦 **European Banking Consortium Qivalis Secures Approval for Euro Stablecoin**

*Cinco Días · October 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Qivalis, a consortium backed by 37 financial institutions across 15 European Union countries, has received authorisation from the Dutch central bank to operate as an electronic money institution, enabling it to issue a euro-linked stablecoin.

The consortium plans to launch the stablecoin on Ethereum in November, with potential expansion to other platforms. Its initiative aims to strengthen Europe's digital payments infrastructure and provide an alternative to the dollar-dominated stablecoin market. The project brings established financial institutions together around a shared digital-money initiative.

**Why it matters:** A bank-backed euro stablecoin could create another settlement option for cross-border transfers and digital commerce, particularly where programmable payments and blockchain-based infrastructure offer operational advantages. For Asian payment providers, the development is worth monitoring as regional institutions assess how fiat-backed digital currencies might complement existing payment rails and cross-border settlement networks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Financial infrastructure is becoming a competitive differentiator.** Crypto platforms are pursuing banking capabilities, while established financial institutions are organising around stablecoin issuance and settlement.
- **Payment economics remain a policy decision, not just a technology question.** India's UPI fee postponement demonstrates the tension between funding payment infrastructure and preserving merchant participation.
- **Stablecoin competition is broadening beyond dollar-linked assets.** Qivalis's plans point to growing interest in euro-denominated digital money, although adoption will depend on distribution, liquidity, interoperability and regulatory execution.

## What to Watch

Watch for the revised UPI merchant discount rate implementation date and any changes to the proposed fee structure. In Europe, monitor Qivalis's planned November launch and the regulatory milestones for Payward's proposed bank acquisition. For Asia-based payment providers, the key signal will be whether these developments translate into practical cross-border settlement, merchant acceptance and integrated financial services.
