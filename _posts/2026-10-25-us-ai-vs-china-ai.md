# US AI vs China AI Brief: Pax Silica and WAICO Force Countries to Hedge

**Today:** If you buy chips, models or capital across the US-China line, today's brief covers middle powers hedging between two AI blocs and fresh rules on both sides, plus a Chinese open-weight model closing in on US rivals.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Countries Hedge Between US-Led Pax Silica and China's WAICO**
*The Associated Press (via ABC News) · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Countries are trying to join both AI blocs rather than choose, even as Washington signals that dual membership undermines trust.

The AP reports that Kazakhstan joined the US-led Pax Silica in June and, three weeks later, became a founding member of China's World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO), which has 29 founders including Pakistan and Russia. Indonesia has joined WAICO, and Singapore says it is considering joining while Anthropic sets up its latest Asia office there. The US State Department said countries cannot be trusted partners in Pax Silica while engaging in initiatives that advance a competing vision for AI. Beijing counters that AI should not be owned by major countries, and AidData research cited by the AP finds Chinese grants and loans supporting AI-related projects in at least 77 low- and middle-income countries.

**Why it matters:** For Singapore-based fintechs and enterprises, the bloc choice may eventually shape access to US chips and cloud platforms versus cheaper Chinese open-source models. Keeping model and vendor choices swappable is a sensible hedge (this is an implication, not a finding from the article).

### 2. 🏦 **US Treasury Proposes Wider Curbs on US Investment in Chinese AI and Chips**
*The Next Web · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Treasury's draft outbound investment rule would widen limits on American money flowing into Chinese chips and AI, add biotech and more countries, and ban some deals that today only need reporting.

TNW reports that Treasury published the proposal on Thursday to implement the COINS Act, passed in Congress's 2026 defense bill. The draft covers chips, AI, quantum, supercomputing and hypersonics, adds biotechnology, and names China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Russia, Iran, North Korea and Cuba. It also swaps the term "AI systems" for "super intelligence systems", following President Trump's 29 September executive order, and would remove the reporting-only option for investment in those systems. Penalties could reach the IEEPA maximum, and Treasury could force divestment. The Federal Register is due to publish the draft on October 26, with comments open until December 7.

**Why it matters:** Funds, startups and corporate venture arms in Asia with US investors should map any exposure to Chinese AI and chip companies before the comment window closes, since final rules will set the start date.

### 3. 🏦 **DeepSeek and MiniMax Reportedly Clear China's New AI Export Registration**
*The Next Web · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A CCTV-linked account says DeepSeek and MiniMax have already completed Beijing's AI export registration, and that API, app and SaaS access to Chinese models stays outside the proposed controls.

TNW reports that the WeChat account Yuyuan Tantian, affiliated with state broadcaster CCTV, described how China's draft controls (proposed October 16, comments open until November 15) would work. The rules target models trained on sensitive Chinese data, high-performance agents, and computing power such as AI chip design. Large models are defined by thresholds including over 2 trillion parameters or a top-ten global ranking, while open-source releases get automatic licensing. What is new since the draft: two named companies are said to have completed registration, and serving a model through APIs, apps or SaaS is described as not an export. TNW has not independently verified the post.

**Why it matters:** For Asia-based enterprises that consume Chinese models through APIs, the signal is continuity of access, though the draft is still open to comment and the claims rest on a single state-linked source.

### 4. 🤖 **Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro Ranks Fourth on Independent Index at Lower Price**
*VentureBeat · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Chinese open-weight model now sits near the top of an independent AI index, at about half the token price of Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, with weights due on November 4.

VentureBeat reports that the 3-trillion-parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro scored 55 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, fourth overall and ahead of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, though Claude Sonnet 5.5 is one point higher. Moonshot lists API pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 for output, against $4 and $20 for Opus 5.5. Opus still leads on Humanity's Last Exam, 61% to 52%, and the article notes unauthenticated allegations that influencers were told not to disclose paid promotion. VentureBeat adds that Moonshot's privacy policy points to Singapore-based servers, and that the DHS order excluding DeepSeek from federal civilian systems does not name Kimi.

**Why it matters:** The US-China capability gap is narrowing at lower cost, so Asia-based buyers should test Kimi on their own workloads and confirm data-processing locations and contract terms. Independent replication after the weights ship matters more than launch-day scores.

## Key Takeaways
- Both governments are writing rulebooks at once: US limits on outbound capital and China's export registration, each while promoting its own AI stack.
- Middle powers, including Singapore and Indonesia, are keeping options open, and Washington is signaling that this has a cost.
- Chinese open-weight models are closing the gap on price and benchmarks, which raises the stakes of the dual-stack choice for enterprises.

## What to Watch
The Federal Register publishes Treasury's outbound investment draft on October 26 (comments due December 7), and Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro weights are due by November 4, followed by the close of China's export-control comment period on November 15.
