# AI Finance in Singapore Brief: Qashier Launches AI General Manager for Merchants

**Today:** A MAS-licensed payments firm puts an AI assistant in front of Singapore merchants, and fresh Citi data shows how far wealthy clients will trust AI with their money.

## Top Stories

### 1. 💳 **Qashier Launches QashierGM, an AI General Manager for Singapore Merchants**
*Fintech News Singapore · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A licensed Singapore payments company is bundling an AI assistant that reads merchants' own sales and settlement data, at no extra charge.
Qashier has released QashierGM to all its Singapore merchants through its QashierHQ platform. The assistant answers questions on sales, payouts and products, and can explain line by line why a payout differs from the day's takings. Owners can also ask it to update prices, mark items sold out or add products, but every change is shown on a card and only goes live after the owner confirms. Qashier says it ran an open beta from September, plans to extend the tool to Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines in November, and will add connectors to apps such as Gmail and Google Business Profile.

**Why it matters:** Qashier holds a MAS Major Payment Institution licence and reported US$1 billion in annualised payment volume at its Series A+ close, so this is an AI feature built on regulated payments data rather than a pilot. The confirm-before-change design and permission-limited access (the assistant acts only with the rights of the person who tagged it) are a practical template for regional payment and BaaS providers designing agent controls.

### 2. 📊 **Citi Study: 58% of Singapore HNW Clients Open to AI, but Control Comes First**
*Fintech News Singapore · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Wealthy Singapore investors are willing to use AI for research and screening, provided they keep the final say and access to a human adviser.
A Citi Wealth study, prepared with research firm RFI Global, puts average assets of Singapore high-net-worth (HNW) clients at US$1.50 million, up 6% from US$1.41 million in 2025. Some 58% of HNW clients and 51% of Affluent clients said they are comfortable exploring AI for personalised insights, mainly for product comparison and portfolio screening. Staying in full control of decisions was the top consideration for adopting AI (31% of respondents), followed by the option to consult a human adviser (22%). The study also found that 72% of onshore Affluent assets and 74% of offshore booked assets sit in cash and liquid deposits.

**Why it matters:** The findings back a hybrid model in which AI handles analysis and preparation while advisers provide judgement and execution. For banks and wealth platforms in Singapore, the large cash balances are the commercial opening, and client-controlled, explainable AI is the likely route to it.

## Key Takeaways
- AI is reaching Singapore's financial users through licensed intermediaries, from merchant payment platforms to private-wealth advisers, not only through large banks.
- Human control is a product requirement on both the merchant and the investor side: confirmation steps, scoped permissions and access to human advisers.
- Regional expansion is part of the plan from day one, with Qashier already scheduling three more markets.

## What to Watch
Qashier's November rollout to Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines, along with its planned Gmail and Google Business Profile connectors, will show whether the assistant scales across markets and data sources.
