# AI and Finance in Singapore Brief — 2026-10-19

**Today:** A light news day: banks are finding that human review of AI agents needs deliberate design, and iCapital says heavy AI coding is increasing its Singapore hiring rather than reducing it.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Banks Are Rethinking Human Oversight in the Age of Agentic AI**

*Fintech News Singapore · October 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Putting a human in the loop is not enough, because reviewers can stop checking carefully once an AI agent proves reliable.

The article draws on a GFTN and Zühlke report, "Moving from Hype to Impact: Preparing for an Agentic Future." The report summarizes a closed-door roundtable at last year's Singapore Fintech Festival. One participant said AI agents cut suspicious activity report processing from six hours to five minutes, but human reviewers began approving submissions without proper scrutiny. The team now deliberately inserts errors to keep reviewers alert. The report also cites KPMG data that 99% of companies plan to deploy agents but only 11% have done so. It names data accessibility as the most-cited regulatory constraint on AI adoption. Standard Chartered's head of AI and research says the bank expects to manage growing numbers of agents through a bankwide control plane and performance metrics. The publisher marks the piece as sponsored content.

**Why it matters:** The article ties these oversight questions to the MAS Guidelines on AI Risk Management issued on 7 October 2026. Firms scaling agents in AML, fraud and compliance workflows appear to need evidence that human review is effective, not just present. Compliance and risk teams should treat this as input for their own review rather than as a regulatory interpretation.

### 2. 📊 **iCapital to Hire in Singapore Despite AI Writing 75% of Code**

*Fintech News Singapore · October 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** iCapital says AI generates about three-quarters of its code, yet it plans to add product and engineering staff in Singapore.

CEO Lawrence Calcano told The Business Times that AI helps the firm build more capabilities efficiently. The alternative investments platform uses AI for code generation, testing and quality assurance, and says it has invested more than US$1 billion in technology. Its 2026 Asia Advisor Survey ranks risk analytics as advisers' top technology priority, with compliance the fastest-growing challenge. Interest in private credit among surveyed Asian advisers fell from 71% to 48%, as interest in real assets rose. iCapital is also exploring annuities and insurance, and may build capabilities for these in Singapore or Zurich.

**Why it matters:** The story is a data point that AI-assisted engineering can coexist with local hiring in Singapore's wealth and fintech sector. The survey findings also suggest advisers are asking for analytics and compliance tooling, which is where AI-enabled platforms may compete.
