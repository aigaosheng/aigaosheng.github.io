# Payments Brief: Mastercard Expands Agentic Trust and B2B Analytics

**Today:** Card networks and issuers are setting the control rules for AI-agent payments, while a bank-issued Hong Kong dollar stablecoin reaches a mass-market wallet.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Mastercard Adds Agent Trust Signals and Supplier Analytics**

*Forbes · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Mastercard is building tools to judge whether an AI agent's payment matches what the buyer actually asked for, and to find B2B suppliers that will accept cards.

Mastercard's Advanced B2B Analytics combines a company's payables data with network insights to estimate which suppliers will accept card payments; Absa Group and Emirates NBD are among the first bank customers. A separate Agent Pay service estimates the probability that an AI agent initiated a transaction and is rolling out for US testing. Mastercard also described a Singapore pilot with HSBC using tokenized payments between a corporate buyer, a procurement platform and an e-commerce supplier. The article notes that Mastercard shared no detection-accuracy results, and that the $80 trillion figure is its estimate of the addressable market, not spending agents control today.

**Why it matters:** Banks and merchants still need proof of who delegated a task and within what limits, and Visa, American Express, Stripe and PayPal are building rival answers. For Asia-based issuers and BaaS providers, the Singapore pilot shows agentic B2B payments already being tested in the region.

### 2. 💳 **HSBC Launches RedCoin Hong Kong Dollar Stablecoin on PayMe**

*PYMNTS · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** HSBC has put a regulated Hong Kong dollar stablecoin into its PayMe wallet, giving it consumer-scale distribution from day one.

According to HSBC's press release as reported by PYMNTS, RedCoin (RCHKD) is licensed and regulated by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and became available on PayMe on Friday. Users who activate a stablecoin wallet in the app can send funds instantly to contacts and pay at more than 100,000 merchants. HSBC plans to add wallet functionality to its Hong Kong mobile banking app and tokenized investment subscriptions later this year, with wholesale corporate and institutional use cases to follow.

**Why it matters:** A bank-issued stablecoin running inside an established payments app tests everyday demand rather than trading demand. Singapore-based payments teams watching regional stablecoin regimes now have a live Hong Kong retail reference point.

### 3. 📊 **American Express Cuts 5% of Roles and Restricts Agent Access**

*PYMNTS · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** American Express will cut about 5% of positions after a $336 million restructuring charge and will let only approved AI agents reach its systems.

On its third-quarter call, management said revenue grew 9% to $20.1 billion and reaffirmed its 10% full-year revenue growth outlook. US consumer spending by Gen Z cardholders rose 35%, and commercial spending grew 3%. CEO Steve Squeri said outside agents will be admitted only through approved API or MCP channels, and that agentic payment volumes remain small. Amex is developing purchase-intent authentication and protection for eligible purchases made by registered agents. Shares were down about 2% in early Friday trading.

**Why it matters:** A major issuer is positioning itself as gatekeeper for agent access to cardholder accounts. Card-programme operators should expect similar approved-channel requirements to shape how agent payments reach their own issuing stacks.

### 4. 🌐 **LianLian Global Adds PayPal and Venmo Cross-Border Payouts**

*PYMNTS · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** LianLian Global clients can now send payouts directly to PayPal accounts worldwide and to Venmo accounts in the US.

In a press release dated October 22, the cross-border payments firm said its social commerce, marketplace and e-commerce clients get three new options: Pay to PayPal, Pay to Venmo and PayPal Payouts for Platforms. Pay to PayPal reaches accounts in 96 countries using only the recipient's email address or phone number. Pay to Venmo covers US domestic payouts, and Payouts for Platforms handles mass disbursements.

**Why it matters:** Wallet-based payouts let platforms pay small, frequent amounts to sellers and gig workers without collecting bank details. That is a common pain point for cross-border payout providers serving creator and marketplace economies.

## Key Takeaways

- Control and evidence layers, not the payment rail itself, are becoming the contested ground in agentic payments, with both Mastercard and American Express focused on proving delegation and limiting access.
- Regulated stablecoins are reaching users through existing wallets and channels rather than standalone crypto apps.
- Distribution reach, whether through PayMe's merchant base or PayPal's payout network, is differentiating providers more than new technology.

## What to Watch

Mastercard's US tests of its agent-initiated transaction score, and HSBC's planned RedCoin wallet in its Hong Kong mobile banking app and tokenized investment offering before year-end.
