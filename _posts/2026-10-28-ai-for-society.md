# AI for Society Brief: JED Study Flags Teen AI Mental Health Risks

**Today:** New evidence on teen wellbeing, data-centre power use and workplace productivity shows that AI's social costs and benefits are becoming measurable, which matters for anyone deploying consumer-facing AI or planning AI-driven capacity gains.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **JED Foundation Links Some Teen AI Uses to Poorer Mental Health**

*The Jed Foundation · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** More than half of the US middle and high school students surveyed turn to AI when emotionally struggling, and certain uses are associated with worse mental health outcomes.

The youth mental health nonprofit surveyed more than 5,500 students across 21 US schools between October 2025 and April 2026, with input from Boston Children's Hospital's Digital Wellness Lab and funding from the Nielsen Foundation. Students who used AI for companionship, relationship advice or while feeling distressed reported higher anxiety, depression and loneliness, and a greater history of suicidal thoughts. The findings show association, not causation. Middle schoolers, girls and several historically marginalised groups were more likely to use AI during emotional struggles, and the report includes recommendations for the tech industry, policymakers and parents.

**Why it matters:** Any firm running consumer chatbots, including banking and wallet assistants in Asia, should expect distress detection and handoff to human support to become a baseline design requirement rather than an optional safeguard.

### 2. 🌐 **Malaysia's Data Centres Use Only 58.5% of Contracted Power**

*The Star · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysia is tightening how it allocates electricity and water to data centres after finding that operators use far less power than they contract for.

Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof told Parliament that, as of August, data centres were drawing 1,374 MW against 2,346 MW of contracted demand. A Data Centre Task Force is now reviewing actual consumption, and the Energy Commission is developing guidelines for data centres to generate their own electricity and use at least 30% renewable energy. Projects in areas with low water reserve margins will not be considered, and nuclear power remains under study, subject to Malaysia meeting its treaty and IAEA obligations first.

**Why it matters:** For the Johor–Singapore data centre corridor, power and water allocation is shifting from first-come contracting to demand verification, which could change hosting costs and timelines for AI workloads serving Singapore firms.

### 3. 👥 **Hubstaff Survey Finds AI Time Savings Fuel Higher Expectations**

*Hubstaff (PR Newswire) · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Workers say AI saves them time, but much of that time is absorbed by rising expectations and by fixing colleagues' AI-generated work, not turned into extra output.

In Hubstaff's survey of 1,068 full-time US computer-based workers, conducted in October 2026, 79% used AI at work. Among those who reported time savings, 76% saved at least an hour a week. However, only 26% of AI users said they take on more work with that time, and 46% said their expected workload had risen since adopting AI. Some 45% reported spending an hour or more each week correcting other people's AI output. The results come from a vendor-commissioned, unweighted online poll.

**Why it matters:** Business cases that assume AI hours saved convert directly into headcount or capacity gains may be overstated, and rework and burnout risks belong in AI rollout plans.

### 4. 👥 **SAP Expands Plan to Give 2 Million Indians AI-Ready Skills**

*SAP India News Center · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** SAP announced additional programmes towards its goal of training two million people in India in AI-ready skills by 2030.

The India commitment is part of SAP's global pledge to skill 12 million people by 2030. The company says nearly 13% of the India target has already taken part through certification, student and community programmes. The new push spans state government partnerships, vocational training through the Future Right Skills Network for Industrial Training Institute students, university alliances, and inclusive programmes aimed at women, unemployed graduates and learners in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. Progress figures are company-reported.

**Why it matters:** Vendor-led skilling is increasingly filling gaps in public programmes across Asia, and India's AI-ready talent pool directly affects offshore delivery capacity for regional banks, fintechs and payment firms.

### 5. 🌐 **Industry Leaders Argue AI Load Growth Can Modernise Power Grids**

*TechCrunch · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Grid-technology executives argue that AI-driven power demand, while straining grids, is also speeding adoption of technologies that could ease costs for households.

Speaking at TechCrunch Disrupt, Weavegrid CEO Apoorv Bhargava described software that orchestrates electricity loads to help utilities make better use of existing capacity and reduce bills. Veir CEO Tim Heidel pointed to superconducting power lines, which the company says carry power five times further using 20 times less space; Veir recently raised a US$110 million Series C. Rewiring America's Kristen Eberhard said the urgency of AI demand has made the industry more open to new approaches alongside new transmission and generation. Several panellists sell products tied to this thesis.

**Why it matters:** Who pays for grid upgrades is becoming a central public question around AI, and Asian markets facing similar load growth will need both new capacity and better demand management.

## Key Takeaways

- AI's social effects are moving from speculation to measurement: teen wellbeing, worker workload and grid usage are now being tracked with data, though much of it is still correlational or vendor-sourced.
- Infrastructure governance is tightening in Asia, with Malaysia shifting power and water allocation for data centres towards verified demand, renewable requirements and water-stress screening.
- Productivity gains from AI are not automatic: rework, rising expectations and skills gaps can absorb the hours saved unless organisations redesign work and invest in training.

## What to Watch

Watch for publication of Malaysia's Energy Commission framework on data centre self-generation and the 30% renewable requirement, and for whether chatbot providers adopt distress-detection and escalation measures in response to the JED recommendations.
