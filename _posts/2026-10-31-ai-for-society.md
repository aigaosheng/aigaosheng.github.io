# AI for Society Brief: AI-Displaced Workers Return With New Demands

**Today:** New data on AI-driven layoffs, a real-world demonstration of AI-powered election manipulation, and fresh healthcare and skills initiatives show the societal costs and benefits of AI adoption are becoming concrete rather than theoretical, with direct implications for workforce planning and risk management.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **Nearly One in Five US Workers Rehired After AI Cuts Went Too Far**

*CNBC · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Employers who replaced staff with AI are now rehiring them, but returning workers are demanding higher pay, job-security guarantees and transparency about how AI will be used.

New research from talent-assessment firm SHL found that nearly 1 in 5 surveyed US workers were laid off or replaced by AI and later asked to return by the same employer, with AI cited as the primary reason for almost 40% of job cuts announced in May 2026. Of workers willing to return, roughly half want a significant pay increase, 43% want written job-security guarantees, and 40% want full transparency on how AI will be used in their role; workers aged 18-24 were hit hardest, with 24% reporting they were personally "AI-rebounded."

**Why it matters:** The pattern signals that premature AI-driven headcount cuts carry a rehiring and trust-rebuilding cost that enterprise leaders should factor into workforce planning; Singapore and regional employers running their own AI-linked restructuring, amid SkillsFuture-backed reskilling pushes, face the same risk of misjudging which roles AI can genuinely replace.

### 2. 👥 **University Wargame Shows AI Bots Can Swing a Simulated Election**

*ABC News (Australia) · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A UNSW competition that let students build AI bots to manipulate a fictional election found teams swung the simulated result by nearly two percentage points in just four weeks.

Computer science researchers at UNSW ran a social-media wargame set in a fictional nation called "Kingstonia," where student teams earned points for using AI-generated posts to shift simulated voter opinion and for detecting rival teams' bots. Despite limited resources and a short timeframe, participants successfully moved the mock election outcome, with researchers framing the exercise as training for students to recognise real manipulation tactics.

**Why it matters:** The exercise is a concrete, measured illustration of how cheaply AI can be used to distort public opinion, a risk regulators and platforms across Asia should weigh as several regional election cycles approach and as deepfake-driven disinformation already disrupts campaigns elsewhere in 2026.

### 3. 👥 **Manitoba Researchers Test AI Tool to Cut Surgical Wait Times**

*CTV News / BNN Bloomberg · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** University of Manitoba researchers are piloting an AI system called ORION that aims to better predict surgical demand and ease a backlog affecting more than 1,300 orthopedic patients with a median wait of 170 days.

The project will analyse ten years of historical patient data from Winnipeg's Health Sciences Centre to test how accurately the model could have predicted past surgical demand, with researchers "very optimistic" it could help scheduling. The story is framed around patient Guy Arnel, who faced an 18-month wait for hip surgery and travelled to Mexico for a paid procedure rather than wait.

**Why it matters:** Public health systems in Singapore and across Asia face similar capacity-planning pressure, and predictive scheduling tools like ORION offer a template for improving access without new clinical headcount, provided accuracy holds up against real-world outcomes rather than historical data alone.

### 4. 👥 **White Paper Warns Rural Workers Risk Being Left Behind on AI Skills**

*PR Newswire (University of Phoenix) · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A University of Phoenix College of Doctoral Studies white paper finds rural workers are teaching themselves AI skills faster than small employers can build formal training, risking a widening career-pathway gap.

The paper reports that 50% of workers are learning AI independently without employer training, while 62% of employers say staff are developing AI skills faster than their organisations can adapt. It recommends regional training collaboration among employers and institutions, clearer skills assessment, and manager training to guide AI adoption.

**Why it matters:** The same informal-adoption gap is visible in Southeast Asia, where UN ESCAP has warned that AI-native infrastructure could widen the region's digital divide without parallel investment in skills and governance; employers that formalise AI training now may gain a retention advantage over those relying on self-taught staff.
