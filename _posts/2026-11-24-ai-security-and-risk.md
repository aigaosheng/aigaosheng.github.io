# AI Security & Risk Brief: EU Commission Confirms Academy Data Leak

**Today:** The EU Commission’s disclosure of exposed Academy user data adds a concrete incident to fresh evidence that AI-enabled attack frameworks are accelerating intrusions and raising pressure on defenders.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **EU Commission Confirms Academy Data Leak**

*AP News · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The European Commission said personal data belonging to EU Academy users was taken during a cyberattack, with the scale still under investigation.

The Commission said names and email addresses were among the exposed data and disabled access to the learning platform while it contained the incident. The platform serves people outside EU institutions, and the Commission advised users to watch for suspicious messages.

**Why it matters:** The incident shows how an attack on public-facing services can create downstream phishing risk for users beyond an organisation’s own workforce. Enterprises should include external learning and partner platforms in incident response and user-notification plans.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://apnews.com/article/european-commission-cyberattack-website-artex-ai-02e1f69e46ad9f82484b66b2f86df53b)

### 2. 🔒 **Wiz Reports More Agentic Cloud Intrusions**

*Wiz Research · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Wiz says it has seen a rise in successful intrusions using agentic offensive frameworks, including attacks that moved from stolen credentials into cloud and CI/CD environments.

Wiz described activity involving ARTEX and other tools, while cautioning that the activity attributed to ARTEX involves multiple unrelated operators. In one case, attackers used compromised source-code credentials and CI trust relationships to access secrets and cloud resources. Wiz reported a threefold increase in incident response requests and said vendors had disclosed more than 20 related incidents in the previous three weeks.

**Why it matters:** The cases highlight the risk in credentials, build pipelines and cloud identities that connect development environments to production. Asia-based firms should review those pathways alongside perimeter controls, particularly where distributed engineering teams and cloud services span multiple jurisdictions.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.wiz.io/blog/agentic-attacks-artex-offensive-ai-frameworks)

### 3. 🔒 **UK NCSC Warns Agentic Attacks Exploit Familiar Weaknesses**

*UK National Cyber Security Centre · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The NCSC says AI-assisted attack frameworks are speeding up the discovery and exploitation of common security gaps across multiple sectors.

The agency reports a marked rise in AI-assisted cyberattacks since September. It says attackers are using frameworks to probe internet-facing services quickly and persistently, often targeting exposed credentials, misconfigurations, default passwords and weak APIs. The NCSC advises organisations to maintain asset inventories, restrict public access where possible, and improve monitoring and basic cyber hygiene.

**Why it matters:** Faster, cheaper probing makes neglected systems and identity weaknesses more urgent risks for enterprises. The NCSC’s recommendations also apply to organisations in Asia that rely on interconnected cloud, API and CI/CD environments.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/familiar-weaknesses-exploited-faster-defending-against-agentic-cyber-attacks)

### 4. 🔒 **DXC Launches Claude Mythos Code Security Service**

*DXC Technology · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DXC has launched a managed service that uses Anthropic’s Claude Mythos to identify software vulnerabilities, with DXC specialists validating findings and supporting remediation.

The company says Frontier Code Assurance is available now and can assess mission-critical software, rank potential flaws by business impact and provide a remediation roadmap. The launch extends DXC’s existing partnership with Anthropic; the service sits within DXC’s cybersecurity practice.

**Why it matters:** The offer reflects growing demand to turn AI-assisted vulnerability discovery into verified fixes that fit enterprise operations. For buyers, the key evaluation points will be validation quality, evidence trails, and how remediation work integrates with existing application security processes.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://dxc.com/newsroom/11242026-dxc-introduces-frontier-code-assurance-a-managed-code-security-service-built-on-claude-mythos)

## Key Takeaways

- AI-enabled attacks are using familiar weaknesses at greater speed, putting exposed assets, credentials and identity controls under more pressure.
- The EU Academy incident highlights the need to include external platforms and their users in breach response and phishing protection.
- New AI security services are emerging, but enterprises still need clear validation and remediation processes around machine-generated findings.

## What to Watch

Watch for the Commission’s further findings on the scale of the Academy data exposure and any changes to platform access. For enterprise security teams, track whether agentic attack reports translate into faster incident response demand and whether AI-assisted code-security services produce validated fixes at scale.
