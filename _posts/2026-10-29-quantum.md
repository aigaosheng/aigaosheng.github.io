# Quantum Brief: PsiQuantum Takes Photonics Into AI Data Centers

**Today:** A quantum hardware builder found its first commercial opening in AI infrastructure, a major bank moved its encryption-break estimate closer, and cloud quantum capacity took a measurable step up — all of which shorten the planning horizon for banks and payment firms.

## Top Stories

### 1. ⚛️ **PsiQuantum Unveils an Optical Circuit Switch Built From Its Quantum Photonics Stack**

*Semafor · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** PsiQuantum has demonstrated an 8×8 optical circuit switch for AI data centers, using the low-loss silicon photonics platform it developed for quantum computing.

The prototype routes signals between GPU clusters without repeated optical-to-electrical conversion, and the company reports 1.2 dB insertion loss against an industry requirement of roughly 2–3 dB, alongside 40 dB crosstalk and sub-3-millisecond reconfiguration. PsiQuantum says it is sharing samples with selected prospective customers ahead of commercial products. The company signed documentation with the US Department of Commerce in September 2026 covering domestic manufacturing of optical components.

**Why it matters:** This is the clearest example yet of quantum hardware investment generating near-term classical revenue, and it puts a quantum company into the AI networking supply chain that Asian data centre operators and cloud buyers are already spending heavily on.

### 2. 🔒 **Bank of America Moves Its Encryption-Break Estimate Forward to 2030**

*The Jerusalem Post · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Bank of America's senior global strategist Haim Israel said the bank has brought forward its estimate of when quantum computers could break standard encryption to around 2030.

Israel argued that AI is helping solve quantum computing's hardest engineering problem — qubit control and error reduction — and pointed to 2022 as the inflection point where qubit counts rose while error rates fell. The bank sizes the quantum industry at above US$1 billion in annual revenue today, projecting roughly US$15 billion by 2030 and US$80 billion by 2035. Israel also flagged limited government oversight of the technology relative to its dual-use risk.

**Why it matters:** A 2030 estimate from a major bank sits uncomfortably close to Singapore's CSA requirement that critical information infrastructure — which includes banking and finance — complete quantum-safe migration by 2031, and it strengthens the case for treating cryptographic inventory work as a current-year programme rather than a later one. Note this is one bank's forecast, not a verified technical timeline.

### 3. ⚛️ **IQM Entangles All 54 Qubits on Its Cloud-Accessible Emerald Processor**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IQM reported genuine entanglement across the full 54-qubit Emerald chip, roughly doubling the number of qubits a cloud user can reliably put to work in a single algorithm.

The company benchmarked a Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger state and measured fidelity of 0.516 across the full chip with readout error mitigation, and 0.515 at 40 qubits without it — both above the 0.500 threshold that separates entanglement from noise. IQM attributes the result to automated state preparation, improved calibration, optimised gate selection and dynamical decoupling, reporting error reductions of up to 50%. Previously, comparable demonstrations on Emerald were reliable at around 30 qubits.

**Why it matters:** Usable width, not headline qubit count, determines whether an enterprise pilot in optimisation or risk modelling can run at all, and this kind of incremental calibration gain is what moves cloud quantum from demonstration to repeatable benchmarking.

### 4. 📊 **Nord Quantique Secures a US$10 Million DARPA Stage B Extension**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Canada's Nord Quantique received a US$10 million extension to its DARPA Quantum Benchmarking Initiative Stage B agreement to continue work on bosonic error correction.

The company builds superconducting circuits that encode error protection inside individual physical qubits, aiming to cut the hardware overhead that fault-tolerant machines normally require. DARPA's programme is assessing whether competing architectures can reach utility-scale operation by 2033. CEO Julien Camirand Lemyre described the award as a milestone reflecting momentum behind the approach.

**Why it matters:** DARPA's staged funding is becoming the de facto filter investors use to judge which quantum architectures are credible, and continued government validation of error-correction shortcuts directly affects how soon commercially relevant machines arrive.

### 5. ⚛️ **E.ON and Qoro Test Hybrid Quantum Optimisation for Peer-to-Peer Energy Trading**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** E.ON, Qoro, the UK's National Quantum Computing Centre and the STFC Hartree Centre built a proof of concept that decomposes large grid-scheduling problems into quantum-sized pieces.

The team used spectral graph partitioning to break optimisation problems into subproblems of up to 50 qubits, ran fragments on IBM hardware, and reassembled results with classical feasibility repair — reporting roughly a threefold reduction in quantum hardware execution time through spatial circuit packing. A realistic grid model would require around 1,710 qubits outright, well beyond current machines. The work was funded under the UK SparQ 2025 programme and remains a proof of concept, not an operational deployment.

**Why it matters:** Decomposition is the practical route to using today's small processors on real industrial problems, and the same pattern — partition, run fragments, repair classically — maps onto settlement netting, routing and portfolio optimisation in financial services.

### 6. 🔬 **Pasqal's 256-Qubit Processor Finds Behaviour Classical Simulation Could Not Predict**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Pasqal-led team observed unexpectedly slow relaxation in a two-dimensional quantum magnet on a 256-qubit neutral-atom processor, in a regime where three leading classical simulation methods disagreed with each other and with the experiment.

Researchers from Pasqal, the Flatiron Institute, the University of Augsburg, Forschungszentrum Jülich, the University of Regensburg and Institut d'Optique–CNRS ruled out noise and experimental artefacts, but the mechanism remains unexplained. The work is posted as an arXiv preprint. The authors frame the result as quantum processors acting as complementary scientific instruments rather than as a claim of quantum advantage.

**Why it matters:** Quantum simulation of materials is the application area with the clearest near-term scientific payoff, and results that classical methods cannot reproduce are the strongest available evidence that the hardware is doing something useful.

### 7. 🔬 **German Team Reports 6.8-Nanosecond Qubit Gates With 99.98% Accuracy**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Researchers from Forschungszentrum Jülich, RWTH Aachen University and the University of Cologne performed single-qubit operations in 6.8 nanoseconds while keeping error per operation below 0.002%.

Their pulse-shaping method, R2D, suppresses specific frequency components in the microwave control signal and reduced unwanted multiphoton transitions by more than twentyfold compared with the established DRAG technique. The work was published in *Nature Communications*. Testing so far covered isolated qubits only, and the team notes that validation in larger processors and on two-qubit gates is still needed.

**Why it matters:** Faster gates fit more operations inside a qubit's coherence window, which is the binding constraint on both algorithm depth and the error-correction cycles that fault tolerance depends on.

### 8. 🔬 **Delft Researchers Control a Single Atom's Nuclear Spin With Radio Signals**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A TU Delft team directly manipulated the nuclear spin of a single atom using radio-frequency signals, an effect physicists had not expected to be achievable this way.

Using a refined double magnetic resonance technique, the researchers controlled electron and nuclear spins independently even when the two were largely disentangled. The findings appear in *Nature Communications*. Lead researcher Sander Otte said the underlying mechanism is not yet understood and raises new questions.

**Why it matters:** Nuclear spins are far better shielded from environmental noise than electron spins, making them a candidate for long-lived quantum memory — though this remains basic research with no near-term commercial path.

## Key Takeaways

- Quantum companies are monetising their adjacent engineering before their computers are ready: PsiQuantum's photonics switch sells into AI data centres today, which changes how investors should read quantum capex.
- The commercial signal has shifted from qubit counts to usable capacity — IQM's full-chip entanglement and the Jülich gate-speed result both matter because they expand what a single circuit can actually execute.
- Forecast timelines for cryptographically relevant quantum computing keep compressing, while decomposition techniques like E.ON and Qoro's show that near-term value comes from hybrid classical-quantum pipelines rather than standalone quantum runs.
