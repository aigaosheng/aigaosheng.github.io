# AI Fintech Brief: Marsh Pushes Fed on AI Loan Capital Relief

**Today:** Regulators and risk managers are racing to keep pace with banks' AI-lending exposure, while vendors push "accountable" AI tools and Asian regulators tighten the rules banks must deploy agentic AI under.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Marsh Urges Fed to Ease Capital Rules on AI Data-Center Loan Insurance**

*Insurance Journal · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Insurance broker Marsh & McLennan is lobbying the Federal Reserve to let banks hold less regulatory capital against AI data-center loans that are hedged with credit risk insurance.

Marsh argues that US rules currently deny banks the capital relief that European regulators already grant for insured loan exposure, even as data-center debt is projected to keep surging toward a global AI infrastructure spend of $6.7 trillion by 2030. The push follows banks including Barclays exploring credit insurance cover on more than $10 billion of data-center debt, and comes as the Fed is finalizing new capital rules in the coming weeks.

**Why it matters:** A capital-relief decision would materially change how much AI-lending risk banks can absorb and how aggressively they compete for data-center financing; Asia-based lenders and insurers watching US capital treatment will look to replicate any relief locally if it is granted.

### 2. 🌐 **Vietnam's New AI Banking Rules Push Lenders Beyond Chatbots**

*TNGlobal (TechNode Global) · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Vietnam's central bank is forcing local lenders to move past customer-service chatbots toward governed, agentic AI as Circular 53/2026/TT-NHNN takes effect on November 15.

The circular sets safety, risk-management and deployment requirements for AI systems in Vietnamese banking. Singapore-based digital banking platform audax told the outlet that most Vietnamese bank AI projects still stall before production, and argued lenders need stronger governance frameworks to scale AI safely across credit risk, fraud management and customer relationships, noting similar guidance already issued by Singapore's MAS, Hong Kong's HKMA and Australia's APRA.

**Why it matters:** Vietnam joins a wave of Asian regulators moving from guidance to binding rules on bank AI use, raising compliance stakes for regional and multinational lenders operating across Southeast Asia ahead of the November 15 effective date.

### 3. 🤖 **Dimply Launches Accountable AI Advisor Holi for Banks**

*Fintech.Global · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Dimply unveiled Holi, an AI financial advisor, alongside an AI Experience Builder designed to let banks embed AI into customer journeys while keeping humans and auditors in control.

Holi answers customer questions using the institution's own data rather than performing calculations itself, escalates requests beyond its scope to staff, and keeps full audit trails of its replies and sources. The Experience Builder lets product teams generate customer journeys from plain-language descriptions, with design, legislative and suitability compliance checkpoints before rollout.

**Why it matters:** The launch reflects growing bank demand for AI tools that preserve auditability and human sign-off, addressing supervisors' recurring concern that institutions must be able to stand behind AI-generated answers to customers.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://fintech.global/2026/11/12/dimplys-holi-puts-accountable-ai-at-the-heart-of-banking/)

### 4. 📊 **Planky Raises €6.9M to Scale Real-Time AI Credit Decisioning**

*Open Banking Expo · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** UK-based Planky raised €6.9 million, co-led by Innovation Global and Azimut Venture Capital's Digitech Europe strategy, to pivot from transaction-based credit intelligence toward a real-time AI decisioning layer.

The funding will accelerate AI-driven underwriting, spend-intelligence and early-warning tools that continuously monitor borrower behaviour for financial stress signals, targeting the "non-scorable" segment of applicants that traditional credit bureaus struggle to assess. Planky, part of BKN301 Group, is expanding into the Middle East alongside its European base.

**Why it matters:** The round underscores continued investor appetite for AI credit-decisioning infrastructure serving digital banks and embedded-finance platforms, a model increasingly mirrored by alternative-data lenders across Asia's underbanked markets.

## Key Takeaways

- Capital treatment of AI-related lending risk is becoming a live regulatory battleground, with Marsh's push to the Fed testing whether insurance-backed capital relief can keep pace with surging data-center debt.
- Asian regulators are shifting from AI guidance to binding deployment rules, with Vietnam's Circular 53 following Singapore, Hong Kong and Australia in setting governance conditions for bank AI use.
- Vendors are converging on "accountable" and auditable AI design, embedding human escalation, audit trails and compliance checkpoints directly into advisor and decisioning tools rather than treating them as add-ons.

## What to Watch

Watch for the Federal Reserve's finalized bank capital rule, which will determine whether Marsh's ask for AI-loan insurance relief is adopted, and for early bank compliance filings as Vietnam's Circular 53/2026/TT-NHNN takes effect on November 15.
