# AI Governance Brief: Australia Passes Digital Duty Of Care

**Today:** Australia’s new online safety law, China’s international standards push and New York’s chatbot toy moratorium show regulators applying different tools to AI-related risks.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Australia Passes Digital Duty Of Care**

*Reuters · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Parliament has passed Australia’s Digital Duty of Care, a new legal obligation requiring online services to address serious harms.

This is a material step beyond the earlier legislative changes: the bill has now passed Parliament. The law covers online services including AI chatbots, carries maximum penalties of 5% of global turnover or A$109.2 million, whichever is greater, and takes effect in 12 months.

**Why it matters:** Platforms and AI service providers serving Australia will need to prepare for a duty focused on preventing harm, backed by substantial penalties. The law’s scope and enforcement approach will be watched across the Asia-Pacific as governments weigh broader obligations for digital services.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/australia-passes-law-forcing-internet-platforms-take-down-harmful-content-2026-11-25/)

### 2. 🏦 **China Pushes Generative AI Governance Standard**

*China Government · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China says it is working with other countries on a proposed international standard for managing generative AI risks.

The State Administration for Market Regulation described a proposal addressing risks including deepfakes and model poisoning. It also reported that China has led work on 78 international AI standards, including foundational standards and sector-specific standards; the generative AI governance proposal is not yet an adopted international standard.

**Why it matters:** International standards can shape technical practices and market expectations across borders. Companies operating in China and elsewhere in Asia should monitor how the proposal develops and whether it gains wider participation.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202611/25/content_WS6b0643ecc6d00ca5f9a0e96d.html)

### 3. 🏦 **New York Enacts Five-Year Pause On AI Chatbot Toys**

*ABC7 New York · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** New York has enacted a five-year pause on manufacturing, distributing and selling toys with AI chatbots.

Governor Kathy Hochul signed the legislation on November 24. The law targets toys with internet access and integrated AI capabilities, and non-compliance could draw penalties of up to $15,000 per day.

**Why it matters:** The law sets a targeted restriction around children’s products, adding to the growing range of ways governments are addressing AI risks. Toy makers and retailers selling across jurisdictions will need to track how the pause is implemented and whether other lawmakers follow this approach.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://abc7ny.com/post/new-york-pauses-ai-chatbot-toy-sales-citing-child-safety-concerns/20076376/)

## Key Takeaways

- Australia’s law moves a broad online safety duty from proposal to enacted legislation, with AI chatbots explicitly within scope.
- China’s standards work shows how AI governance is also being shaped through technical standards and international proposals.
- New York’s pause applies a product-specific restriction, illustrating the range of regulatory responses to AI-related risks.

## What to Watch

Monitor Australia’s implementation ahead of the law taking effect in 12 months, and whether China’s generative AI risk proposal advances through international standards bodies. For New York, watch for implementation details and any comparable measures in other jurisdictions.
