# AI for Science Brief: AI Reshapes Mathematical Discovery

Today: New developments highlight two important dimensions of AI-driven science: increasingly sophisticated mathematical reasoning and the development of structured biomedical knowledge infrastructure for accelerating scientific discovery.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔬 Nature Examines AI's Emerging Role in Mathematical Discovery

Nature Computational Science · November 17, 2026

Bottom line: Nature examines how increasingly capable AI systems could contribute to mathematical and scientific discoveries, while emphasising the importance of independent verification, reproducibility and human accountability.

In its latest editorial, Nature Computational Science discusses the implications of OpenAI's September announcement that its AI agents had produced an analytical solution addressing the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem. The claim remains controversial and has generated debate within the mathematical community. The editorial places this development within a broader trajectory that includes DeepONet's applications to differential equations and Google DeepMind's AlphaProof mathematical reasoning capabilities.

The authors argue that AI could move beyond accelerating calculations and simulations towards contributing to solutions for previously unresolved scientific questions. However, researchers must remain responsible for evaluating results, establishing reproducibility and determining whether proposed discoveries withstand independent scrutiny. The editorial also calls for transparent disclosure of AI contributions in scientific publications.

Why it matters: AI-assisted mathematical reasoning could expand the scope of computational science, potentially accelerating research in fluid dynamics, engineering, climate modelling and complex physical systems. For enterprise AI and research organisations in Asia, the opportunity lies in combining AI's computational capabilities with domain expertise, rigorous validation and reproducible research workflows.

### 2. 🔬 Harvard and Oxford Researchers Introduce OptimusKG for Biomedical Discovery

Scientific Data · November 17, 2026

Bottom line: Researchers introduce OptimusKG, an open multimodal biomedical knowledge graph designed to support machine learning, knowledge-grounded AI and scientific hypothesis generation.

The research team, involving Harvard Medical School, the University of Oxford and Edison Scientific, developed a structured biomedical knowledge graph integrating information across molecular, anatomical, clinical and environmental domains. The resource contains 190,939 nodes, 21.8 million edges and approximately 109.7 million property values, assembled from 18 ontologies and controlled vocabularies.

The researchers evaluated its relationships using PaperQA3, a multimodal AI agent that assesses whether graph connections are supported by scientific literature. The system identified supporting evidence for 70% of sampled relationships, while 83.4% of sampled false relationships received no supporting evidence. The researchers have released the dataset in Apache Parquet format to facilitate downstream research and computational applications.

Why it matters: Reliable scientific knowledge infrastructure is an important foundation for AI-assisted drug discovery, biomedical research and hypothesis generation. Structured knowledge graphs can help researchers connect evidence across biological domains while making relationships and their provenance more accessible to computational systems. The involvement of the University of Oxford's Suzhou research centre also provides a relevant connection to Asia's growing biomedical research ecosystem.

## Key Takeaways

- AI is moving towards higher-order scientific reasoning. Its potential contribution increasingly extends beyond pattern recognition and computational acceleration to mathematical reasoning and scientific problem-solving.
- Scientific data infrastructure remains essential. Knowledge graphs, structured datasets and evidence-grounded retrieval systems are important building blocks for reliable AI-assisted research.
- Human verification remains indispensable. Scientific credibility depends on reproducibility, transparent AI disclosure and independent validation, regardless of whether discoveries originate from humans or AI systems.

## What to Watch

Monitor independent mathematical assessments of AI-generated solutions to longstanding scientific problems, particularly the Navier–Stokes work.
