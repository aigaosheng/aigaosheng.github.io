# Quantum Brief: South Korea Issues Post-Quantum Migration Guidebook

**Today:** Governments in Seoul, Tokyo and Doha are moving quantum readiness from strategy into concrete standards, test programs and pilots, which matters to banks and payment providers planning their own cryptographic migration.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **South Korea's NIS Publishes Draft Cryptography Standards and Post-Quantum Migration Guidebook**

*DigitalToday · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea has put out draft rules and a migration guide that tell public agencies which cryptographic algorithms to keep, restrict or retire as quantum risk grows.

The National Intelligence Service released the drafts for state and public institutions, together with the National Security Research Institute, on the National Cybersecurity Center website. The standards assign each algorithm an allowed, restricted or prohibited period of use. The guidebook covers preparation, phased migration steps and prioritisation by system importance and risk. Per the report, the drafts are open for feedback, final versions are due in the first half of 2027, and a key management guideline is expected by year-end.

**Why it matters:** Public-sector mandates in major Asian economies tend to become the baseline that regulated financial institutions and their vendors follow. Banks and payment operators with Korean exposure should begin cryptographic inventories now.

### 2. ⚛️ **JIJ and Mitsubishi Electric Selected for Japan's JHPC-quantum Test Program**

*The Quantum Insider · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Japanese industrial and software pairing will test whether quantum-classical hybrid methods can help with engineering simulation on a platform that links quantum machines to supercomputers.

JIJ and Mitsubishi Electric, with Mitsubishi Electric as lead proposer, were chosen for the JHPC-quantum Test User Program. Their proposal evaluates hybrid linear solvers for thermal-fluid simulations. The program is advanced by RIKEN and SoftBank Corp. under a project commissioned by NEDO. The article reports no performance or funding figures.

**Why it matters:** The work is a test of practical use rather than a performance claim, and it shows Japan building a path from quantum hardware access to industrial use cases. Enterprises weighing hybrid quantum-HPC workloads should watch the results for realistic benchmarks.

### 3. 🔒 **Qatar's MCIT Partners with SignQuantum on Post-Quantum Signatures**

*The Quantum Insider · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Qatar's communications ministry has agreed to assess post-quantum signature use cases, an early-stage step with no committed deployment.

The agreement was announced at MWC26 Doha on November 10, 2026. It covers evaluating post-quantum cryptography use cases and preparing for quantum risks. Any pilot, deployment or commercial engagement would need separate evaluation and approval. SignQuantum combines post-quantum signatures with quantum-resistant timestamping on QANplatform's blockchain.

**Why it matters:** Gulf governments are positioning as early adopters of quantum-safe infrastructure, which could shape vendor choices for digital identity and payments in the region. The agreement is exploratory, so near-term commercial impact is limited.

- Post-quantum migration is becoming a compliance exercise, with Korea's draft standards using algorithm-by-algorithm timelines.
- Asian and Gulf governments are building test programs and pilots, but most remain exploratory with no disclosed budgets or performance results.
- Listed quantum vendors are strengthening finance and governance leadership as the sector matures.
