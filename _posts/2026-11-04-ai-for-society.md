# AI for Society Brief: Data Centre Backlash Reshapes US Midterms

**Today:** Voter anger over AI infrastructure, a wave of AI-driven layoffs and a flood of undisclosed AI political ads show how AI's social costs are now moving markets, elections and workplaces at once.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **Voter Backlash Against AI Data Centres Shapes US Midterm Results**

*Euronews · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Opposition to AI data-centre expansion became a decisive factor in the US midterms, with candidates who campaigned against new projects winning races in Texas, Georgia and Ohio.

More than half of voters called AI and data centres a "crucial or major factor" in their vote, according to NBC exit polling cited by Euronews. At least 45 data-centre projects faced community disruption in the second half of 2026, with 15 Ohio municipalities imposing bans or restrictions, and Texas Governor Greg Abbott paused new data-centre permits pending an audit, reversing his earlier pro-development stance despite billions in planned Meta and Amazon investment in the state.

**Why it matters:** For fintech and payments firms planning cloud and AI capacity in Asia, the result is an early warning that public resistance to AI's energy, water and land footprint can outpace corporate build-out timelines — a factor worth weighing in site selection and community engagement, not just regulatory approval.

### 2. 👥 **Canva Cuts 22% of Workforce, Citing AI-Driven Disruption**

*Bloomberg · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Canva announced its first mass layoffs in its 13-year history, cutting 1,271 roles — 22% of staff — after growth slowed for the first time as AI reshapes product expectations and costs.

CEO Melanie Perkins told employees the company must rethink its structure, staffing and operating model as "new technology" transforms what customers expect from creative tools; the move follows Canva cutting its revenue-growth forecast from 30% to 20% in August after AI feature costs ran higher than planned. Affected staff receive at least six months' salary and twelve months of healthcare coverage, and Canva will consolidate product teams around its Sydney hub.

**Why it matters:** Canva is a widely used productivity tool across Southeast Asian SMEs and enterprises, making this a concrete signal that AI-linked cost pressure is now reaching consumer software firms, not just banks — a trend worth tracking for vendor stability and pricing.

### 3. 👥 **$187 Million in Undisclosed AI Political Ads Floods US Midterms**

*CNBC · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Campaigns spent an estimated $187 million on nearly 300 AI-generated or AI-enhanced political ads this cycle, with most lacking required AI-use disclosures.

The Wesleyan Media Project, cited by CNBC, found over 90% of the spending came from Republican candidates, committees or allied groups, and called its tally "almost certainly an undercount." Platforms responded unevenly: YouTube labels realistic AI content and lets candidates request removal of altered faces, Meta added "about this ad" AI labels in June, while X leaves compliance to advertisers with no platform-level enforcement detailed.

**Why it matters:** With no comprehensive US federal disclosure law and only patchy state rules, the episode is a preview of the enforcement gap that election authorities and platforms across Asia will also need to close before AI-generated political content reaches similar scale.

### 4. 👥 **Google Forces Schools to Choose Between AI and Search Access**

*TechXplore · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Google will switch off Gemini, Gemini in Classroom and standard Search for K-12 student accounts by default, meaning schools that reject AI tools lose search access too.

Administrators must explicitly opt in to either standard Gemini or a new "Guided Learning" tutoring mode that Google says will begin testing in select US schools this month; former FTC chair Lina Khan criticised the move as using Google's "search monopoly to coerce schools into adopting AI," while child-safety group Fairplay warned the policy could backfire. New York City and Los Angeles have already imposed temporary generative-AI bans in elementary and middle schools pending review.

**Why it matters:** The policy highlights how a handful of dominant platforms can bundle AI adoption with essential services, a dynamic regulators and school systems in Singapore and the wider region will want to watch as they weigh AI literacy goals against vendor lock-in and student data concerns.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-11-google-access-students-schools-ai.html)

### 5. 📊 **Philippine Consumers Outpace Businesses in AI Adoption**

*Context.ph · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Consumers account for 80-85% of AI usage in the Philippines, far ahead of enterprise deployment, which represents only 15-20% of usage measured in tokens.

The figures come from the e-Conomy SEA 2026 report by Google, Temasek and Bain & Company, which found Filipinos already weaving AI into daily life while companies have been slower to convert that familiarity into workplace tools. The report points to unclear returs on investment and operating costs, rather than compute access alone, as the main barriers to business adoption.

**Why it matters:** The consumer-ahead-of-business pattern mirrors trends seen elsewhere in Southeast Asia and suggests fintech and payments providers have a ready base of AI-literate customers to design services for, even where back-office AI adoption lags.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://context.ph/2026/11/04/consumers-drive-philippine-ai-use-as-businesses-lag/)

## Key Takeaways

- AI's social costs — energy and land use, job displacement, and unregulated political content — are converging into a single political and reputational risk that enterprises can no longer treat as separate workstreams.
- Public backlash against AI infrastructure is translating directly into electoral outcomes and policy reversals (Texas permit pause), a signal that community engagement now matters as much as regulatory approval for data-centre siting.
- Consumer-led AI adoption in markets like the Philippines is outrunning enterprise deployment, creating an opening for financial services firms to meet AI-literate customers with new products before competitors catch up internally.

## What to Watch

Watch whether other US states follow Texas in pausing or reviewing data-centre permits, and whether Google's opt-in schools policy prompts further district-level bans beyond New York City and Los Angeles.
