# Payment Brief — 2026-08-08

## Top Stories

### 1. **India Keeps UPI Free for Consumers and Small Merchants Amid MDR Debate**

* **Source**: News On AIR · August 8, 2026
* **Summary**: The Payments Council of India clarified that consumers will continue to make UPI payments without transaction charges, while small merchants will remain exempt from merchant discount rates. The clarification follows India's recent legislative changes that remove restrictions on charging certain electronic payment modes, reopening debate over how UPI infrastructure should be funded. PCI emphasized that banks, fintechs, NPCI and the RBI continue to invest heavily in cybersecurity, fraud prevention, resilience and infrastructure.
* **Why It Matters**: The debate highlights the central challenge of India's payments model: preserving near-universal, low-cost access while creating sustainable economics for the infrastructure behind UPI. Large merchants could increasingly become the primary source of monetization without undermining UPI's consumer proposition.

### 2. **Sudan Reactivates National Electronic Payment Switch After Years of War Disruption**

* **Source**: Sudan Tribune · August 7, 2026
* **Summary**: The Bank of Sudan has reactivated its national electronic payment switch after more than three years of war-related disruption. The switch is a key piece of national financial infrastructure, reconnecting banks and supporting the restoration of electronic payments and banking services. The move comes as Sudan works to rebuild financial infrastructure and expand access to formal financial services.
* **Why It Matters**: Restoring domestic payment infrastructure is foundational to economic normalization, financial inclusion and the eventual rebuilding of commerce. Sudan also illustrates how payment rails can become critical national infrastructure during post-conflict reconstruction.
* **URL**: [https://sudantribune.com/article/317125](https://sudantribune.com/article/317125)

### 3. **Maya Pushes Financial Inclusion Beyond Payments Toward Financial Health**

* **Source**: Back End News · August 8, 2026
* **Summary**: Philippine fintech Maya is calling for financial inclusion to evolve from simply giving consumers accounts toward improving savings, responsible borrowing, business growth and resilience against financial shocks. The company highlighted the growing importance of national payment rails, digital identity and better credit information, while using payment activity as a potential signal for expanding access to savings and credit. Maya is also applying AI to onboarding, personalization, credit assessment, fraud detection and customer protection.
* **Why It Matters**: Payments are increasingly becoming the data layer for broader financial services rather than a standalone transaction product. The combination of payment data, AI and digital identity could allow fintechs to deepen customer relationships while expanding access to credit and other financial products.
