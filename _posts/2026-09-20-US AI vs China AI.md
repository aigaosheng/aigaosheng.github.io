# US AI vs China AI Brief — 2026-09-20

## Top Stories 

### 1. **US and China Launch High-Level Talks on AI Ahead of Trump-Xi Summit**

* **Source**: Reuters · September 20, 2026
* **Summary**: US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng are meeting in New York for talks covering artificial intelligence, trade and critical minerals ahead of the planned September 24 Trump-Xi summit. The AI discussions are expected to address safeguards for powerful systems, risks from malicious use and the possibility of avoiding a deeper bifurcation between the two countries' AI ecosystems. Bessent said discussions would cover both open- and closed-weight models.
* **Why It Matters**: AI has moved directly into the highest-level US-China economic negotiations. The talks could shape future rules around model access, AI safety, technology controls and the degree to which the world's two largest AI ecosystems remain interoperable.

### 2. **Trump Plans New “AI Force” and AI Czar as US-China Competition Intensifies**

* **Source**: South China Morning Post · September 20, 2026
* **Summary**: President Donald Trump said he plans to appoint a new artificial-intelligence adviser, or “AI czar,” and establish an “AI force,” although he provided few implementation details. Trump said the administration would avoid hindering the growth of the AI industry and rely on existing criminal and civil laws to address misuse.
* **Why It Matters**: The proposal indicates that US AI strategy is increasingly being organized around national competitiveness as well as technology policy. The institutional structure eventually adopted could affect how Washington coordinates AI development, safety, regulation and competition with China.

### 3. **China’s CXMT Begins Mass Production of New-Generation DRAM**

* **Source**: Reuters · September 20, 2026
* **Summary**: Chinese DRAM manufacturer CXMT announced that its fifth-generation technology platform has entered mass production. The company says the process reduces key feature spacing to 11.95 nanometres and can produce at least 50% more gross chip dies per wafer than its previous generation. CXMT also unveiled two 24-gigabit LPDDR5X products, with the company positioning the technology as a step toward competing with Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron.
* **Why It Matters**: AI infrastructure depends on a broad semiconductor stack, not just accelerators. Progress in domestic memory manufacturing could strengthen China's ability to build AI computing infrastructure while reducing exposure to foreign semiconductor supply constraints and US technology restrictions.

# Strategic Takeaway

The September 20 developments show the US-China AI competition expanding across **three interconnected layers: diplomacy, institutional policy and semiconductor self-reliance**.

At the diplomatic level, Washington and Beijing are now discussing AI safeguards and ecosystem bifurcation alongside trade and critical minerals. In the US, the proposed AI Force and AI czar point toward greater institutional coordination around national AI strategy. In China, CXMT's DRAM milestone illustrates the continuing effort to strengthen the domestic semiconductor stack that underpins large-scale AI deployment.

The competitive picture is therefore increasingly about **control of the complete AI stack — models, compute, memory, chips, infrastructure, standards and governance — rather than model benchmarks alone**.
