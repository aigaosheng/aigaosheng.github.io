# AI finance in Singapore Brief — 2026-09-24

## Top Stories 

### 1. **23 Financial Institutions Commit to Train 80,000 Singapore Finance Workers in AI**

* **Source**: The Business Times · September 24, 2026
* **Summary**: The Institute of Banking and Finance (IBF) launched an AI Workforce Co-Lab involving 23 financial institutions, including DBS, OCBC, UOB, Goldman Sachs, UBS, AIA, Schroders and SGX. The institutions collectively represent about 40% of Singapore's financial-services workforce and have committed to training more than 80,000 employees in critical AI skills by 2028. Initial programmes target leaders, wealth managers and operations staff, alongside job-redesign and career-progression initiatives.
* **Why It Matters**: Singapore's financial-sector AI strategy is moving beyond experimentation toward workforce-scale adoption. The emphasis on governance, role redesign and AI-enabled productivity suggests that organisational capability—not simply model access—is becoming a core competitive variable for financial institutions.

### 2. **Singapore Pushes Companies to Convert AI Spending Into Measurable Business Value**

* **Source**: FinTech Singapore · September 24, 2026
* **Summary**: Senior Minister of State Tan Kiat How said the next constraint on AI growth may be organisations' ability to convert AI capabilities into economic value. Speaking at NVIDIA AI Day Singapore, he highlighted the need to redesign workflows around human-AI collaboration rather than deploy AI as isolated tools. Singapore is also using initiatives such as SEA-LION and National AI Missions to test applications across sectors including finance.
* **Why It Matters**: The message is particularly relevant to banks and fintechs facing growing AI investment requirements. The strategic shift is from proof-of-concept counts toward measurable improvements in revenue, risk, productivity and customer outcomes.

### 3. **Sea Expands AI Infrastructure Across Monee, Shopee and Garena With NVIDIA Vera Rubin**

* **Source**: Sea · September 23, 2026
* **Summary**: Singapore-founded Sea announced that it will become the first enterprise in Southeast Asia to adopt NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform, expanding AI infrastructure across its businesses. Within Monee, AI is already used for fraud detection and credit-risk assessment; Sea says newer transformer-based models using behavioural and transactional data have increased approval rates by around 10% while maintaining a similar level of risk. The company plans to use the additional computing capacity to develop AI models and intelligent agents at greater scale.
* **Why It Matters**: The development connects AI infrastructure directly with financial-services economics: credit underwriting, fraud detection and financial inclusion. It also illustrates how Singapore-based fintech platforms are increasingly building AI capabilities as core operating infrastructure rather than as standalone features.
* **URL**: [https://www.sea.com/news/418](https://www.sea.com/news/418)

### 4. **Deutsche Bank Deploys Agentic AI for Source-of-Wealth KYC in Singapore**

* **Source**: Deutsche Bank Private Bank · September 22, 2026
* **Summary**: Deutsche Bank Private Bank announced an agentic AI solution for Source-of-Wealth processes that went live in its Singapore and Hong Kong booking centres at the beginning of September. The system automates research, documentation and preparation of Source-of-Wealth assessments using client information and approved external sources, while identifying gaps and inconsistencies for human review. Deutsche Bank says its Emerging Markets coverage region is forecast to onboard about 30% more clients in 2026 than in 2025.
* **Why It Matters**: This is a concrete example of agentic AI moving into a regulated banking workflow in Singapore. The architecture is notable because automation is bounded by approved data sources, governance controls and human accountability rather than replacing the final KYC decision.

### 5. **Singapore Captures 92% of Southeast Asia's H1 Startup Funding as AI Attracts Capital**

* **Source**: The Business Times · September 24, 2026
* **Summary**: Singapore-headquartered companies captured US$6.7 billion, or 92% of Southeast Asia's US$7.25 billion in startup funding during the first half of 2026, according to a Kickstart Ventures and DealStreetAsia report released September 24. Capital was concentrated in AI, compute infrastructure and semiconductors, with the five largest regional rounds accounting for 75.5% of total funding. At the same time, regional deal volume declined 5% year on year and early-stage transactions fell 11%.
* **Why It Matters**: Singapore's role is increasingly that of a financing and infrastructure hub for the regional AI economy, rather than simply a domestic fintech market. The concentration of capital also highlights a widening gap between large AI infrastructure transactions and the broader early-stage funding pipeline.

### 6. **Grab and OpenAI Launch AI Skills Programme for 30,000 Southeast Asian Platform Partners**

* **Source**: FinTech Singapore · September 24, 2026
* **Summary**: Grab and OpenAI launched the GO Forward with AI programme in Singapore, with plans to expand to Thailand, Indonesia and the Philippines. The two-year programme will train 30,000 drivers, delivery partners and merchants in practical AI applications including business planning, sales analysis, demand forecasting and inventory planning. Grab said 50% of its Singapore driver and delivery partners already use AI tools.
* **Why It Matters**: While broader than financial services, the programme demonstrates how AI adoption is spreading through the digital-platform economy that underpins payments, commerce and embedded finance. For fintech providers, AI literacy among merchants and consumers can become an important prerequisite for AI-enabled financial products.
