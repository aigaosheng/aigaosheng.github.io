# AI governance Brief — 2026-10-19

**Today:** The UK's FCA asks AI providers to warn users about the limits of AI financial advice and publishes good and poor AI practice for firms, while government advisers urge a UK AI sovereignty plan.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **FCA Calls on AI Providers to Add Financial-Advice Disclaimers and Publishes Good and Poor AI Practice**

*Financial Conduct Authority · 2026-10-19*

**Bottom line:** The FCA is steering AI governance through existing rules, asking general-purpose AI providers for clear disclaimers and telling regulated firms to fix accountability, testing and resilience before they scale AI.

In a speech at Bloomberg's UK Policy Series, FCA chief data, information and intelligence officer Jessica Rusu asked providers of general-purpose AI models to give consumers clear, proportionate and consistent warnings about the limits of the financial information and advice they generate. The FCA also published good and poor practice drawn from this year's firm testing, grouped under consumer outcomes, accountability and governance, and operational resilience. Rusu said incident reporting should extend to significant AI failures and near-misses, and that the FCA will work with industry and frontier labs on a framework for agentic finance covering agent identity, delegated authority and accountability.

**Why it matters:** Firms using AI in customer-facing or payments journeys appear to face closer supervisory questions on named senior accountability, governance built before deployment rather than after, and fallback plans for model or supplier failure. The agent identity and delegated authority work signals where rules for AI agents acting on customers' behalf may head.

### 2. 🌐 **UK Council for Science and Technology Urges a National AI Sovereignty Plan**

*GOV.UK, Council for Science and Technology · 2026-10-19*

**Bottom line:** The UK's top independent science advisory body wants the government to decide which AI capabilities to own, share or secure access to, and to put one accountable owner in charge.

The Council published advice to the Minister for AI with four recommendations: a UK AI Sovereignty Plan built on a map of the AI ecosystem, clear governance and coordination for sovereignty choices, early investment in skills, data and public trust, and trusted access to leading global models alongside selected domestic capability. The letter dates from July 2026 and was made public today. It is advice to government, not policy.

**Why it matters:** Governance debates are widening from model safety to dependency, procurement and who controls critical AI capability. Organisations selling AI into UK public-sector or sensitive-data use cases should watch for sovereignty requirements to shape buying decisions.
