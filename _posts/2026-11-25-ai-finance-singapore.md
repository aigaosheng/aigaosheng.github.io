# AI Finance in Singapore Brief: Singapore Banks Weigh AI Agent Access

**Today:** Singapore's banks are weighing how to manage third-party AI access as executives and technology providers map out where AI and connected payments can create value.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Singapore Banks Weigh AI Agent Access**

*The Business Times · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore banks are keeping third-party AI agents at arm’s length while the industry works out how to verify their authority and manage the risks.

The Business Times reports that DBS, OCBC and UOB have raised concerns about agents accessing customer accounts. Its analysis highlights risks including mistaken or unauthorised transactions, limited bank visibility into agent mandates and unresolved liability. MAS is developing a “Know Your Agent” protocol to help financial institutions assess agent identity and authority.

**Why it matters:** Agent access could affect customer security and banks’ ability to control how funds move. For Singapore institutions, reliable identity, permissions and transaction limits will be central to moving from restricted access to supervised use.

### 2. 🤖 **DBS and OCBC See AI Edge Beyond Models**

*Computer Weekly · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Bank executives at the Singapore FinTech Festival argued that lasting advantage will depend on how firms integrate AI with their data, operations and customer relationships.

A panel featuring executives from DBS, OCBC, FIS and Microsoft discussed potential sources of advantage in agentic AI, including proprietary data, distribution, deep integration, partnerships, trust and execution. The discussion pointed to institutional knowledge and operational fit as important complements to access to AI models.

**Why it matters:** Singapore banks can differentiate through the way they connect AI to their existing systems and expertise. That puts implementation, governance and workforce knowledge alongside model choice in the competitive equation.

### 3. 💳 **Tencent Showcases AI and Connected Payments at SFF**

*PR Newswire · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Tencent used the Singapore FinTech Festival to present AI tools and payment connectivity aimed at business workflows, customer services and cross-border transactions.

The company said more than 60 overseas wallets across 16 countries and regions are linked to Weixin Pay. Its SFF showcases included TenPayGo’s AI-assisted travel services, a Weixin Pay merchant tool and WorkBuddy, which Tencent said had surpassed 10 million daily active users.

**Why it matters:** The announcement illustrates how AI features are being paired with payment networks and services used by customers across borders. For financial institutions in Singapore and the region, the practical test will be whether these combinations improve user experience and business operations while preserving trust and control.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/tencent-spotlights-how-ai-and-connected-payments-simplify-everyday-services-at-sff-2026-302949967.html)

## Key Takeaways

- Singapore banks are balancing customer demand for AI assistance against the risks of granting agents access to financial accounts.
- Competitive advantage may come from integrating AI into bank data, workflows and customer channels, rather than model access alone.
- Connected payments offer a route to combine AI services with cross-border commerce, though safeguards and user trust remain essential.

## What to Watch

Watch for further detail on how MAS’s proposed agent protocol could translate into practical identity, authority and transaction controls. Also watch for evidence from banks and payment providers on whether AI tools improve customer or operational outcomes.
