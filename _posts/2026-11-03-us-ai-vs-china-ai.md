# US AI vs China AI Brief: Nvidia Caps China Cloud Business at 49%

**Today:** Nvidia's new cloud contract rule and a sharpening US-China bloc split in Southeast Asia show that compute access, not just model capability, is now the front line of the AI rivalry — with direct consequences for data centre operators and AI infrastructure buyers in Singapore and the region.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Nvidia Tells Cloud Partners to Cap China Business at 49%**

*Financial Times, via Malay Mail (AFP) · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nvidia is restricting how much revenue its data centre and cloud partners can earn from Chinese customers, effectively closing a loophole that let Chinese firms access controlled chips through overseas cloud rentals.

Nvidia has verbally instructed cloud and data centre partners, via its sales and regional teams, that new contracts must derive at least 51% of revenue from non-Chinese customers, according to five sources cited by the Financial Times. The rule applies only to future contracts, not existing agreements, and comes as Chinese AI firms have reportedly used remote cloud connections to access Nvidia chips housed in overseas facilities, including in Singapore and Malaysia, without the hardware ever crossing a border. Nvidia did not confirm or deny the directive but said infrastructure built on American chips is "the foundation that American model and application makers need to compete worldwide."

**Why it matters:** This shifts US export enforcement from where chips physically ship to how overseas compute capacity is commercially allocated, directly affecting Singapore- and Malaysia-based data centre operators that serve regional and Chinese clients. Enterprises relying on GPU cloud capacity sourced through the region should expect tighter customer-mix scrutiny and potential capacity reallocation.

### 2. 🌐 **Southeast Asia Pressed to Pick Between US and China AI Blocs**

*Financial News (fnnews.com) · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Intensifying US-China AI competition is forcing Southeast Asian governments away from "strategic ambiguity," splitting the region between the US-led Pax Silica initiative and China's Shanghai-based World AI Cooperation Organisation (WAICO).

Singapore and the Philippines have aligned with Pax Silica, which coordinates semiconductor supply chains and GPU export rules among the US and partners including Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, while Malaysia, Indonesia and Vietnam are leaning toward WAICO's lower-cost, open-source approach, the report said. Singapore reportedly received a pointed warning from Washington that it "can't have it both ways," even as the city-state's AI Singapore programme continues to build its SEA-LION models on both Alibaba and Google open-weight foundations. Indonesian trade envoy Mari Pangestu was quoted rejecting the binary framing, warning the region "must never be pushed into such an either-or situation."

**Why it matters:** For Singapore-based fintechs and banks, this bloc pressure raises the stakes around vendor and chip sourcing choices, and could complicate the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement that underpins cross-border digital and data rules the payments industry depends on.

### 3. 🤖 **Huawei's Next AI Chip Hits HBM Shortage as Smugglers Fill the Gap**

*TrendForce · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Huawei's upcoming Ascend 960DT chip depends heavily on Samsung and SK hynix memory that US export controls restrict, pushing some buyers toward a smuggling workaround and domestic suppliers that lag badly on yield.

According to reporting cited by TrendForce, smugglers package South Korean high-bandwidth memory (HBM) with low-performance server processors to exploit rules that classify shipments by processor power rather than memory specs, then strip the memory out once it reaches China. Domestic alternatives from Fujian Jinhua and CXMT reportedly post HBM3E yields of just 20–30%, versus roughly 70% at SK hynix, and Huawei has asked some customers to source memory themselves for the 960DT, due in Q1 2027. Huawei has reportedly already raised pricing on its current Ascend 950DT chip to roughly US$37,000 despite roughly half the raw compute of an Nvidia H100.

**Why it matters:** The memory bottleneck shows export controls are still constraining China's highest-end AI hardware roadmap even as Chinese chipmakers scale output, meaning enterprises evaluating China-based AI infrastructure should expect continued price volatility and supply uncertainty through 2027.

### 4. 🤖 **DeepSeek Plans to Train Next Model on Huawei Chips, Not Nvidia**

*TokenPost · November 3, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DeepSeek is reportedly planning to train its next flagship model, V5, on Huawei Ascend chips rather than Nvidia hardware, a step beyond its current use of Huawei chips mainly for inference.

Multiple people in the chip supply chain told reporters that DeepSeek aims to move core training workloads to Huawei's platform, after an earlier attempt to train its R2 model on Huawei chips reportedly failed due to stability, communication and software problems and had to be completed on Nvidia hardware instead. DeepSeek released open-source Ascend tooling in late September, including compiler and distributed-communication software mirroring what it previously built for Nvidia's platform.

**Why it matters:** A successful shift would be a significant proof point for China's domestic AI compute stack and reduce DeepSeek's reliance on restricted Nvidia hardware, though the earlier failed attempt on R2 underscores that Huawei's training-grade software stack still has real gaps enterprises should weigh before assuming near-term parity with Nvidia.

## Key Takeaways

- Export-control enforcement is moving from hardware shipment points to downstream commercial terms, as seen in Nvidia's new cloud revenue-mix rule, making compliance harder to monitor and easier to circumvent.
- Southeast Asia's room for AI non-alignment is narrowing, with Singapore facing explicit pressure to choose between US- and China-led technology blocs, a dynamic with direct implications for regional data centre and cross-border digital policy.
- China's AI hardware stack remains memory-constrained despite domestic chip progress, meaning Huawei and DeepSeek's push toward full self-sufficiency is still a multi-year effort rather than an accomplished fact.

## What to Watch

Watch for Nvidia's cloud partners' response to the 51% non-China revenue rule, and for any Singapore government statement on its Pax Silica versus WAICO positioning following the reported US pressure. The next US-China AI dialogue session, previously flagged for November, is also worth monitoring for any movement on chip-related issues.
