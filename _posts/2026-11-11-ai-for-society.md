# AI for society Brief: Stanford AI100 Study Warns Public Lacks AI Input

**Today:** A landmark Stanford review of AI's societal toll lands alongside fresh evidence that the public backlash over AI's costs, from data-centre energy use to compute access, is intensifying rather than fading, with direct implications for how enterprises plan AI rollouts and workforce strategy in Asia and beyond.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **Stanford AI100 Study Warns Public Lacks Say in AI's Rise**

*Stanford HAI (One Hundred Year Study on AI) · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Stanford's third five-year AI100 assessment finds that AI has gone mainstream faster than society's ability to govern it or have meaningful input into its deployment.

The report, led by Oxford's Michael Wooldridge, warns that fields once seen as automation-proof, including creative work, coding and mathematics, now face disruption echoing the upheaval journalism and retail saw over the past three decades. It also flags synthetic audio and video as having undermined public trust in visual evidence, including use in electoral interference, while international governance remains fragmented and shaped more by geopolitical competition than collective welfare.

**Why it matters:** For enterprise and fintech leaders, the report is an early signal that scrutiny of AI's social costs, on jobs, education and democratic trust, is building toward firmer rules; regulators across Asia, including Singapore's, are likely to draw on this kind of research as they calibrate AI governance frameworks.

### 2. 👥 **US Voters' Data-Centre Backlash Outlasts the Midterms**

*CNN · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Community organisers who helped defeat 40 of 41 local AI data-centre ballot measures say their campaign against the industry's energy, water and noise footprint continues well beyond the midterm elections.

Organisers in Texas and Kentucky told CNN the fight "doesn't change" despite Democrats flipping statehouses and several newly elected governors promising construction moratoriums. The report follows OpenAI's November 5 "National Community Compact," in which it pledged to pay full infrastructure and power costs and drop non-disclosure agreements with local officials after the ballot defeats.

**Why it matters:** Sustained local resistance raises real cost and timeline risk for hyperscale AI buildouts that fintech and cloud-dependent businesses rely on; Asian markets weighing their own data-centre expansion, including Singapore's power-constrained sites, are watching how this public-trust dynamic plays out.

### 3. 🌐 **Malaysia Warns AI Risks Deepening Global Compute Divide**

*Bernama · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysia's Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil told the ITU's Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha that unequal access to computing power, not just internet connectivity, is becoming the next global AI fault line.

Fahmi said developing nations, particularly in the Global South, risk falling further behind as countries race to build AI capability without equal access to the underlying compute infrastructure. He also called for international cooperation on guardrails for agentic AI, linking Malaysia's ITU Council candidacy to amplifying Southeast Asian and Global South concerns.

**Why it matters:** For BaaS and fintech providers operating across ASEAN, the "compute divide" is a proxy for how unevenly AI-driven products can be localised and priced across the region, a gap that outlasts conventional digital-inclusion gaps.

### 4. 👥 **UK Watchdog Finds Universities Unready for Student AI Use**

*Office for Students · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new Office for Students review of over 300 staff and students across 120-plus English institutions finds most universities still lack coordinated AI policies, reliable assessment safeguards or adequate staff training.

The review found AI helps with summarising and revision, including for neurodivergent and non-native-English students, but unsupervised assessments remain vulnerable since detection tools are unreliable. Participants favoured sector-wide collaborative guidance over new compliance rules, and staff AI training remains largely optional with low uptake.

**Why it matters:** The findings foreshadow how education regulators and employers elsewhere, including in Singapore, may need to rethink assessment design and graduate-skills verification as AI use becomes the default for students rather than the exception.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/publications/making-ai-work-for-students-reflections-on-institutional-practice/)

### 5. 👥 **Singapore Employers Flag Widening AI Skills Mismatch**

*PR Newswire (AmCham Singapore) · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** AmCham Singapore's 2026 Manpower Survey, developed with Gallup, finds 46% of employers now cite skills mismatch as a top manpower concern, up from 34% a year earlier, as AI reshapes roles faster than hiring pipelines adjust.

Some 68% of employers expect AI to lift productivity and 80% are investing in reskilling, yet only 35% feel confident accessing needed talent under the government's COMPASS work-pass framework, and 41% struggle to hire locally. Hiring overall is described as stable but cautious, with headcount cuts expected by just 13% of respondents.

**Why it matters:** The survey underscores mounting pressure on Singapore-based financial and technology employers to combine internal AI upskilling with continued advocacy on talent-access policy as regional competition for AI skills intensifies.

### 6. 👥 **Study Links Korea's AI Subsidies to Youth Hiring Drop**

*Seoul Economic Daily · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Konkuk University analysis of South Korean AI-voucher recipients finds government AI subsidies correlate with a 10.1% drop in hiring of workers aged 19 to 34 within three years.

The study compared 636 firms that received AI vouchers from the Ministry of Science and ICT against 1,603 rejected applicants between 2015 and 2024, finding firms froze entry-level hiring rather than cutting existing staff, reflecting Korea's strict dismissal rules. The researcher recommends tracking hiring and departures separately and designing AI training-to-hiring pathways for young workers.

**Why it matters:** The research is a cautionary data point for governments across Asia, including Singapore's SkillsFuture and productivity grant schemes, designing AI-adoption subsidies without matching incentives to protect entry-level hiring.

### 7. 👥 **ISCA Partners Polytechnics to AI-Train Future Accountants**

*Media OutReach Newswire (ISCA) · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore's Institute of Singapore Chartered Accountants signed a five-year agreement with all five polytechnics and ITE to embed AI fluency training for more than 3,000 students, part of a push to AI-skill 60,000 accountancy and finance professionals within three years.

Developed with the Infocomm Media Development Authority under the National AI Impact Programme, the partnership adds industry projects, mentorship, internships and job shadowing to existing coursework. EY and Nexia Singapore were separately recognised for workforce-transformation efforts as firms redesign accounting roles around AI tools.

**Why it matters:** The initiative is a concrete model of structured workforce-pipeline response to AI disruption that other regulated Singapore sectors, including financial services, may look to replicate rather than relying on ad hoc reskilling.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.media-outreach.com/news/singapore/2026/11/11/as-ai-reshapes-accounting-jobs-isca-partners-with-all-five-polytechnics-and-ite-to-prepare-future-talent/)

## Key Takeaways

- Public trust and governance gaps, not technical limits, are now the binding constraint on AI's spread, from Stanford's flagship review to grassroots opposition over data-centre impacts.
- Compute and skills access, not just connectivity, are emerging as the defining inequality of the AI era, from Malaysia's Global South warning to Singapore and Korea's labour-market strains.
- Singapore's response, pairing sector-specific workforce deals like ISCA's with broader employer surveys, illustrates a structured-pipeline approach that contrasts with the ad hoc policies flagged in the UK's university review.

## What to Watch

Watch for state-level follow-through on the data-centre construction moratoriums promised by newly elected US governors, and for whether Singapore's COMPASS framework or SkillsFuture grants are adjusted in response to the AI talent-access and youth-hiring concerns raised this week.
