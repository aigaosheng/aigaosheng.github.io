# AI Governance Brief: US, China and Russia Discuss AI Nuclear Limits

**Today:** Great-power talks on keeping AI out of nuclear decision-making and a fresh US congressional push on AI surveillance cameras show governance pressure building on two very different fronts this week.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **US, China and Russia Weigh Curbs on AI in Nuclear Command**

*Agence France-Presse (via The Manila Times) · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Working-level negotiators from the United States, China and Russia are narrowing in on possible guardrails against letting AI influence nuclear command-and-control systems, with the issue set to feature at next week's APEC summit.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed he will attend the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen on November 18-19 to advance a US-China "superintelligence dialogue" covering how to test advanced models and keep humans in control of them, building on an AI incident-alert channel the two sides agreed to establish in September. Reports cited by AFP indicate Russia has been more cautious than China about accepting formal limits on AI in nuclear systems, and a trilateral meeting on the sidelines of APEC is still being arranged rather than confirmed.

**Why it matters:** Even as Washington and Beijing compete on frontier AI capability, both governments are now negotiating narrow, binding-style commitments on AI's most catastrophic failure modes — a template that could eventually extend to civilian high-risk AI oversight. For Asia-based enterprises and regulators, Shenzhen's role as host puts the region at the centre of a rare moment of major-power AI coordination, something Singapore and ASEAN policymakers will watch closely as they calibrate their own cross-border AI risk frameworks.

### 2. 🏦 **US Lawmakers Press for Federal Rules on AI Surveillance Cameras**

*Bloomberg Law · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Bipartisan momentum is building in Congress for a federal framework governing AI-powered automated license plate readers, such as those made by Flock Safety, after hearings exposed wrongful arrests linked to the technology.

Lawmakers from both parties are weighing further hearings and new legislation to curb misuse of nationwide AI camera networks, including data-sharing practices and the risk of location data being monetised. Proposals already in play include Senator Josh Hawley's Stop Flock Abuse Act, which would ban facial recognition on these networks and require audit logs and data-deletion limits, alongside separate House bills that would restrict federal funding for such systems or require judicial warrants before law enforcement can query them.

**Why it matters:** The push signals that US lawmakers increasingly expect audit trails, data minimisation and human-oversight controls for any AI system processing identity or location data — a standard that compliance teams at fintechs and payment providers using AI for fraud detection or KYC should track, since similar accountability expectations are already emerging in MAS, BSP and OJK guidance across Singapore, the Philippines and Indonesia.

## Key Takeaways

- The most concrete AI governance progress right now is happening on narrow, high-stakes issues (nuclear command-and-control) rather than comprehensive rulebooks, with APEC the next test of whether talk becomes text.
- US AI regulation continues to advance issue-by-issue rather than through a single federal law, reinforcing a compliance patchwork that multinational fintechs must track jurisdiction by jurisdiction.
- Expectations for audit logs, data minimisation and human oversight of AI systems that touch identity or location data are hardening on both the geopolitical and domestic US tracks, a direction regional regulators are already echoing.

## What to Watch

Watch the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen on November 18-19, 2026, for any announced US-China (or trilateral, with Russia) understanding on AI and nuclear command systems, and whether the Stop Flock Abuse Act or competing House proposals advance out of committee in Washington.
