# US AI vs China AI Brief: Trump’s Nvidia Intervention Deepens China Policy Rift

**Today:** Trump’s support for Nvidia’s Jensen Huang sharpens the divide over chip exports, while Southeast Asian partners face growing pressure to enforce US restrictions on China’s access to AI compute.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Trump’s Nvidia Intervention Deepens China Policy Rift**

*The Washington Post · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Trump’s role in restoring Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO has exposed tension between his support for the chipmaker and bipartisan calls to restrict China’s access to advanced US chips.

The Post reports that Trump’s intervention did not change the administration’s tighter chip-export controls. Lawmakers from both parties are pushing for further restrictions and investigations, with Huang expected to face Congress the following week.

**Why it matters:** Export policy now depends on a visible split between the White House’s support for Nvidia and congressional pressure to limit China’s access to advanced chips. The outcome could affect Nvidia’s China business and regional supply chains, including data-centre operations in Asia.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/25/backing-jensen-huang-trump-delivers-setback-china-hawks/)

### 2. 🏦 **Malaysia Discusses AI Chip Curbs With US**

*The Straits Times · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysia says it is tightening oversight of advanced-chip shipments and discussing the new US restrictions with Washington.

Second Finance Minister Amir Hamzah Azizan said Malaysia would comply with applicable rules and expected the two countries to find common ground. He also said Malaysia had not approved the shipments under scrutiny. CIMB estimated that a six-month disruption to data-centre operations could lower Malaysian GDP growth by 0.19 percentage point in 2027 under its worst-case scenario.

**Why it matters:** Malaysia’s response offers an early signal of how regional data-centre hubs may manage US licensing requirements while sustaining investment. Operators with cross-border customers will need to account for tighter scrutiny of chip sourcing and compute access.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/se-asia/malaysia-talks-with-us-on-ai-chip-curbs-tightens-controls)

### 3. 🔒 **BIS Agents Were Reassigned to Lutnick’s Security Detail**

*The Atlantic · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Atlantic reports that some Bureau of Industry and Security agents were temporarily reassigned to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s personal security detail, raising questions about export-control enforcement capacity.

Former officials told the magazine that the agents were pulled from work that included preventing illicit technology transfers to China. A Commerce Department official said the temporary assignments responded to an unusually high level of credible threats and that BIS staffing had since increased.

**Why it matters:** Export controls depend on investigators and enforcement staff as well as rules on paper. The report adds scrutiny of whether the US can effectively police chip transfers as restrictions extend to regional data-centre and cloud access.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.theatlantic.com/national-security/2026/11/lutnick-export-control-personal-detail/689562/)

### 4. 🌐 **China Extends Pause on Critical-Mineral Export Curbs**

*The Straits Times · November 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing extended its pause on export curbs for gallium, germanium and antimony, keeping a key part of the US-China trade truce in place for now.

China’s Commerce Ministry announced the extension on November 24, ahead of the pause’s November 27 expiry. The report says Beijing is holding its response to new US chip restrictions in reserve; it notes no evidence that the minerals decision was connected to Trump’s intervention involving Nvidia.

**Why it matters:** China’s position preserves near-term stability in materials used across semiconductor and electronics supply chains while retaining a potential source of leverage. For companies exposed to critical-mineral inputs, the extension does not remove the risk of future trade-policy shifts.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/chinas-xi-plays-long-game-on-us-chip-retaliation-after-nvidia-saga)

## Key Takeaways

- US chip policy faces a widening political split, even as restrictions on advanced exports and remote access remain in force.
- Southeast Asian data-centre hubs are responding with tighter oversight and direct discussions with Washington.
- China has kept its critical-mineral export pause in place while retaining options for a later response.

## What to Watch

Watch next week’s congressional questioning of Huang and whether export-control bills advance. Malaysia’s discussions with Washington will also indicate how regional data-centre operators may be expected to comply with the new licensing requirements.
