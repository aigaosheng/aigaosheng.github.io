# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Yuanshi Technology Raises Nearly RMB 300 Million

Today: Chinese AI investment is flowing into specialised life-science foundation models, with investors backing companies that combine proprietary biological data, AI modelling and experimental validation.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 Yuanshi Technology Raises Nearly RMB 300 Million in Pre-A Round

投资界 (PEDAILY) · November 19, 2026

Bottom line: Legend Capital led a nearly RMB 300 million Pre-A financing for Shanghai-based Yuanshi Technology, highlighting growing investor interest in AI-powered virtual-cell technology.

The company focuses on AI Virtual Cells (AIVC), combining biological data infrastructure with general-purpose cell models to predict how cells respond to different disease conditions, experimental environments and interventions.

Investors include Huaxia Lingtou, China Merchants China Fund, a JD-affiliated fund, CDH VGC and CMC Capital. Existing investors Monolith, Innovation Works, Daotong Investment and Yanyuan Ventures also participated. The capital will support data accumulation, automated experimental platforms, model development and team expansion. The financing reportedly sets a new single-round funding record for China's AIVC sector.&#x20;

Why it matters: Virtual-cell AI is an emerging intersection of foundation models, biotechnology and automated experimentation. The investment signals that venture capital is increasingly interested in AI companies with proprietary scientific data and measurable experimental feedback, rather than software models alone. Singapore's biotech and healthcare innovation ecosystem could monitor this model of combining AI infrastructure with wet-lab capabilities.

### 2. 📊 Megaro Raises Nearly US$200 Million to Build Real-World AI Infrastructure

China Securities Journal · November 19, 2026

Bottom line: Megaro (镁伽科技) secured nearly US$200 million in new financing, bringing its cumulative funding to approximately US$600 million.

The round attracted Oman's sovereign wealth fund, Oman Investment Authority (OIA), alongside existing investors including Asia Investment Capital, Granite Asia and Innovation Works. CMB International, Suzhou Innovation Investment and Sanjiang Merchants Group also participated.

Megaro is developing integrated AI, software and hardware infrastructure connecting foundation models, agents, scientific instruments, robotics and automated equipment. Its two principal business areas are scientific intelligence and industrial intelligence.

The company plans to use the funding to accelerate technology development, expand its international business and strengthen commercial deployment across global markets.

Why it matters: This financing demonstrates growing institutional interest in AI infrastructure that connects digital intelligence with physical execution. The participation of international sovereign and Asian investment institutions also highlights the cross-border capital opportunities emerging around China's industrial AI ecosystem. For Singapore, Megaro's expansion into Southeast Asia is particularly relevant to laboratory automation, advanced manufacturing and AI-enabled industrial transformation.

## Key Takeaways

- Capital is moving towards specialised AI: Life-science foundation models and virtual-cell platforms are attracting substantial venture investment.
- AI infrastructure is expanding beyond software: Investors are backing companies combining models, proprietary data, robotics, laboratory automation and physical execution.
- International capital remains engaged: Sovereign wealth funds, regional investment groups and established venture investors are participating in China's AI infrastructure development.

## What to Watch

Monitor whether Yuanshi can demonstrate measurable improvements in cell-response prediction and whether Megaro converts its expanded capital base into recurring international business. Commercial validation, proprietary data advantages and deployment economics will be important indicators for the next investment cycle.

## MY TAKE

China's emerging AI investment opportunity is increasingly shifting from general-purpose model development towards domain-specific intelligence with proprietary data and real-world execution capabilities.

Two areas deserve particular attention: AI for Science, where models can accelerate drug discovery and biological research, and Physical AI, where intelligent systems connect software, robotics and industrial workflows.

For Singapore-based investors and technology practitioners, the opportunity is not necessarily to replicate China's scale, but to identify complementary capabilities in regional research institutions, healthcare, advanced manufacturing and enterprise deployment.
