# Enterprise AI Brief: AT&T Scales Agentic AI Across Operations

**Today:** Two fresh data points — one from a telecom giant running agents at scale, one from a top enterprise software vendor — show the conversation shifting from how many agents a company has deployed to what those agents actually deliver.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **AT&T Scales Agentic AI to Nearly 500 Production Workflows**

*SiliconANGLE · November 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** AT&T has moved agentic AI from experimentation to core operations, reporting fivefold returns on production AI investments within a year.

Luke Corbin, AT&T's assistant vice president of digital transformation and AI, said the company has nearly 500 agentic workflows in production, handling more than 1 million transactions a month, with plans to triple that number in 2027. AT&T processes roughly 45 billion AI tokens daily and runs the deployments through its Business Agent Foundry, set up in early 2025 to redesign entire processes rather than automate isolated tasks. Corbin made the remarks at an AWS event in Boston.

**Why it matters:** AT&T's governance model — employees can build personal productivity agents freely, but customer-facing workflows require foundry review for security, privacy and compliance — gives regulated Asian enterprises, including banks and telcos, a concrete reference point for balancing deployment speed against oversight.

### 2. 📊 **Oracle: Measure AI Agents by Results, Not Headcount**

*Business Standard (PTI) · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Oracle's applications chief says the number of AI agents deployed is the wrong success metric — the business improvement they deliver is what counts.

Natalia Rachelson, Oracle's senior vice president of applications development, told PTI that enterprises should pick a measurable goal before deploying agents — such as cutting the time to resolve accounting errors or lowering transport costs — and set a baseline first. She said AI's larger potential lies in reorganising how work gets done, citing finance reconciliation, HR payroll checks, supply-chain shipping and hospital scheduling as early use cases, while cautioning that humans must retain clear control over agent authority and approval triggers.

**Why it matters:** As enterprises across Asia move from pilots toward scaled agentic AI, Oracle's outcome-first framing is a useful checkpoint for compliance and finance teams that need to justify AI spend to boards and regulators with evidence beyond "agents deployed."

## Key Takeaways

- Both stories mark a shift in enterprise agentic AI messaging: from counting pilots and agents deployed to proving measurable business outcomes.
- Governance design — AT&T's foundry review process, Oracle's baseline-and-approval framework — is emerging as the real differentiator between stalled pilots and production-scale rollouts.
- Regulated sectors in Asia, including banking and telecoms, can draw directly on these operating models as boards push for evidence of agentic AI ROI heading into 2027.
