# AI for Society Brief: Gallup Survey Links AI Companions to Youth Loneliness

**Today:** Two verified stories cleared the window, covering how young people use AI for companionship and how AI-fabricated citations are damaging research credibility in Korea.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **Gallup Survey Finds a Third of Young People Use AI as a Companion**

*404 Media · November 18, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A 16-country Gallup survey of young people found that AI companion users reported more loneliness, though the researchers say it cannot show which causes which.

404 Media's report describes a Gallup study with Boston Children's Digital Wellness Lab covering more than 16,000 people aged 15 to 22. About a third said they had used AI as a companion, while larger shares used it to look up information (82%) or ask for advice (68%). Roughly 93% reported a close in-person friendship, and respondents with AI companions reported higher loneliness, with the authors cautioning that the direction of the relationship is unknown. The survey was run online, and the report notes that daily AI use varies by country, at about 20% in the US, Canada and the UK versus nearly 40% in Brazil.

**Why it matters:** Consumer AI products that simulate friendship are drawing scrutiny from product, trust-and-safety and wellbeing teams, and this dataset gives them a cross-country reference point. The correlational design and online sampling mean it should inform design and policy discussions rather than settle them.

### 2. 🔬 **Korean Retractions Show Cost of AI-Fabricated Citations**

*Korea JoongAng Daily · November 18, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Korean universities and research institutes are retracting papers over AI-invented references, and the problem appears to be growing worldwide.

Korea JoongAng Daily reports that Korean institutions have retracted 205 papers since 2023 for AI-generated material and 316 for citation or sourcing problems, citing Retraction Watch data. Cases include a Korea Institute of Science and Technology Information paper in which 11 references were reportedly fabricated by AI and a government-funded study retracted after errors were found in 17 of about 40 references, which led to a three-year publication ban for the researcher. The article also cites a Columbia University study in PNAS, published November 3, that found fake references in 1 in 435 papers in the first three months of this year, versus 1 in 1,600 in 2023.

**Why it matters:** Universities, journals and research funders in Asia now face a verification burden as AI writing tools spread, and citation checking is becoming a compliance task. Organisations that use AI to draft research, policy or client material face the same exposure to fabricated sources.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/false-citing-paper-retractions-come-into-focus-as-ai-hallucinations-blur-academias-vision/12978055)

## Key Takeaways

- Evidence on AI's personal and social effects is arriving as large cross-country surveys, but the associations are correlational and should be read with that limit.
- Fabricated outputs are becoming an institutional integrity problem, with Korea's retractions and the cited Columbia data showing the issue moving from anecdote to measurable rates.
- Both stories point to human oversight, such as friendships offline and reference checks in research, as the practical safeguard against AI's failure modes.

## What to Watch

Watch for Gallup's fuller breakdown of the AI-companion findings and for any peer review or independent replication of the loneliness association. In Korea, watch for further action from the National Research Foundation of Korea and journal editors on AI-assisted misconduct.
