# US AI vs China AI Brief: US Curbs China's Remote Access to AI Chips

**Today:** Washington has extended chip export controls to overseas cloud compute and put Singapore, Malaysia and three other markets under licensing, which changes the rules for Asia's data centre and AI infrastructure investment.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **US Commerce Rule Targets Chinese Remote Access to AI Chips and Adds Five Markets to Licensing**

*TechCrunch · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US controls on AI chips now cover cloud compute sold to Chinese firms from data centres anywhere in the world, not just physical shipments of chips.

The Commerce Department's interim rule requires a licence to export, re-export or transfer AI chips and servers to any data centre worldwide that may supply compute to companies in China or other D:5 embargoed countries. It applies to facilities or networks above 12 million TPP, which TechCrunch puts at roughly the capacity of 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips. It also covers access through brokers and requires operators to monitor who owns their customers. The rule adds new licence requirements for advanced chips sent to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, which Commerce says carry a higher-than-normal risk of diversion. It exempts chips going to US-headquartered companies, gives existing operators 90 days to get licences, and rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule.

**Why it matters:** Southeast Asian GPU clouds that serve Chinese clients now face a direct compliance test. Enterprises in the region that buy compute from neocloud providers should expect more customer due diligence and possibly tighter capacity.

### 2. 🌐 **Singapore Studies US Rule and Seeks Trusted Chip Access for Local Firms**

*Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry, Singapore · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore is talking to Washington about how the licence requirement will work in practice while restating that it will not tolerate firms circumventing export controls.

In a joint statement, Singapore's Ministry of Digital Development and Information (MDDI) and Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry (METI) said they are examining the new rule closely. They are also engaging affected companies and US authorities on its scope and implementation. The ministries noted that the requirement does not apply to chips bound for companies whose ultimate parent is headquartered in the US, provided reporting conditions are met. They also said they do not condone companies using a Singapore connection to break other countries' laws.

**Why it matters:** Whether Singapore can secure a "trusted" status will shape the cost and timing of AI capacity for its banks, fintechs and regional hubs. Non-US multinationals and local operators are the most exposed to case-by-case licensing.

### 3. 📊 **Nvidia Restricts Sales to Data Centres Renting Most GPUs to Chinese Firms**

*CNBC · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nvidia is applying its own China-exposure test to cloud customers ahead of any legal requirement, according to people familiar with the matter.

CNBC reports, citing two sources, that Nvidia told customers it will not for now sell chips to operators that rent more than half of their GPUs to Chinese companies. Over recent months it has also warned buyers that the proposed Remote Access Security Act could expose both Nvidia and its customers to US legal action. The bill passed the House in January but has not cleared the Senate. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for a "dramatic shakeup" at Nvidia following Jensen Huang's departure as chief executive.

**Why it matters:** Vendor-imposed caps on China exposure could reach Asian neoclouds faster than formal licensing. Data centre operators may need to rebalance their customer mix to keep their supply.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-china-cloud-ai-chip-access.html)

### 4. 🔒 **US Agencies Confirm Criminal Investigation of Singapore-Based Megaspeed**

*CNBC · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Commerce and Justice Departments say they have been investigating Nvidia partner Megaspeed for possible export control violations since 2024.

According to a joint statement reported by CNBC, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its no-sell list in June 2025. That same month, US officials and Singapore police carried out an on-site inspection and interviewed former executive Alice Huang. The Justice Department now leads the criminal case and has not concluded whether she was a Chinese spy. Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs said it first learned of US interest in June, and Singapore barred her from re-entering the country in February 2026.

**Why it matters:** The case explains why Washington singled out Singapore. It also raises the reputational and enforcement risk for any regional compute provider with opaque links to Chinese customers.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/megaspeed-investigation-nvidia-jensen-huang.html)

### 5. 🌐 **China Calls US Rule Economic Coercion and Vows Necessary Measures**

*Associated Press via The Manila Times · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing has formally objected to the remote-access rule but has not yet said what countermeasures it will take.

Foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the rule "a typical example of economic coercion and long-arm jurisdiction". He said it goes against the understanding the two sides had reached. He accused Washington of harming Chinese companies' rights and destabilising global supply chains, and said China would take necessary steps to protect its interests. AP reports that the rule requires overseas data centres serving companies in China, Russia, Iran and Cuba to identify their end users.

**Why it matters:** How Beijing retaliates will decide whether this stays a compute dispute or spreads into other supply chains. Asian firms that depend on both US chips and Chinese demand are most exposed.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/17/world/trump-administration-moves-to-block-china-from-indirect-access-to-american-ai-technology/2471683)

### 6. 🌐 **Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil Weigh Their Response to US Chip Licensing**

*W.Media · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Governments named in the rule are mostly treating it as a compliance exercise for now rather than pushing back.

Indonesia's Government Communication Agency said it is reviewing the measure, asking industry for input and building a strategic trade management framework. Thailand's Vice Minister for Commerce, Kirida Bhaopichitr, said the country is prepared and will scrutinise chip importers more closely. Brazil's industry ministry said the rule is an authorisation requirement, not a ban, and that its impact cannot yet be assessed.

**Why it matters:** Licensing regimes that differ across ASEAN could change where hyperscalers and neoclouds choose to build. Markets with credible enforcement are likely to win licences faster.

### 7. 📊 **Malaysia's Data Centre Boom Faces Closer Scrutiny Under New US Rule**

*Tech Wire Asia · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysia has the most investment at stake, because data centres now make up a large share of its approved investment.

Tech Wire Asia cites MIDA figures showing that data centre and cloud projects accounted for RM95.8 billion of RM218.5 billion in approved investment in the first half of 2026, nearly 44%. The report stresses that the US rule is case-by-case licensing, not a blanket ban. It also notes that MITI has said it never cleared Megaspeed or its Malaysian affiliate Speedmatrix, and that it is working with customs to check whether chips were re-exported.

**Why it matters:** Johor's role as a regional AI compute hub now depends on how quickly operators can show US-grade customer screening. That affects pricing and availability for enterprises across the region.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://techwireasia.com/2026/11/us-ai-chip-restrictions-malaysia-singapore/)

## Key Takeaways

- US-China AI competition now extends to cloud compute. Who a data centre serves matters as much as where its chips are shipped.
- Singapore and Malaysia are moving from being seen as neutral AI hubs to being treated as diversion risks. "Trusted access" status and strong local enforcement are becoming competitive assets.
- Private gatekeeping, such as Nvidia's 50% rule, combined with criminal enforcement in the Megaspeed case, means compliance risk for Asian compute buyers is rising faster than the formal rules alone suggest.

## What to Watch

Watch for the rule's formal Federal Register publication, which starts the licensing clock. Also watch for China's promised countermeasures, any chip discussion at the upcoming Trump–Xi talks, and Jensen Huang's requested appearance before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.
