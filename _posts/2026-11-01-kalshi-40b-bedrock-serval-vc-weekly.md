## This Week's Deals

### Kalshi — Fintech / Prediction Markets

- **Region:** US
- **Round:** Series G, $1B
- **Investors:** Wellington Management and Sequoia (co-leads); Baillie Gifford, Stripes, Andreessen Horowitz, Coatue, Layer Global, Morgan Stanley Investment Management, Sequoia Global Equities, CapitalG, Hudson River Trading, Balyasny Asset Management
- **Valuation:** $40B post-money
- **Source:** [Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-series-g-40-billion-valuation)
- **Notes:** Announced October 29, about five months after a $1B round at $22B, an increase of roughly 82%. Kalshi says non-sports annualized revenue now tops $1B and is positioning itself as a broader exchange covering commodities, crypto and perpetual futures.

### Bedrock Robotics — Physical AI / Construction Automation

- **Region:** US
- **Round:** Series C, $600M
- **Investors:** CapitalG and Emergence Capital (co-leads)
- **Valuation:** $4.1B post-money
- **Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/this-startups-self-driving-excavators-help-dig-up-600m-in-new-funding/)
- **Notes:** The two-year-old company, founded by former Waymo leaders, retrofits autonomy kits onto existing excavators. Its machines began paid work on live job sites in August, and total funding is now close to $1B.

### Serval — Enterprise AI / IT Service Management

- **Region:** US
- **Round:** Series C, $150M
- **Investors:** Sequoia (lead); First Round, General Catalyst, Redpoint, Conviction, Headline, Elad Gil
- **Valuation:** $3B
- **Source:** [Serval](https://www.serval.com/blog/we-raised-150m-to-make-everyone-a-builder)
- **Notes:** This is Serval's third round in about 12 months, and the valuation has tripled from $1B in December. Serval is pitching itself as a direct replacement for ServiceNow rather than a layer of AI on top of it.

### Firehawk Defense — Defense Tech / Propulsion

- **Region:** US
- **Round:** Series D, more than $150M
- **Investors:** Advent International and Rochefort Asset Management (co-leads)
- **Valuation:** More than $1.4B
- **Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rochefort-asset-management-co-leads-150-million-series-d-for-firehawk-defense-to-scale-domestic-propulsion-and-munitions-production-302923026.html)
- **Notes:** Firehawk makes thermoplastic solid-rocket propellant for Hydra-70 rockets and artillery. The round funds production sites in Oklahoma and Mississippi as munitions stockpiles are rebuilt.

## Trends & Analysis

**Sequoia was the most active firm of the week.** It co-led Kalshi, led Serval and joined the Hex and Nevis rounds. The common thread is AI agents built to replace an incumbent system of record, not to add features on top of one. Serval targets ServiceNow, Hex rebuilt its product for agents, and Nevis wants to be the operating layer for wealth advisers. Revenue multiples are not disclosed in any of these cases. The implied stake sold is modest: roughly 5% for Serval and about 8% for Hex at their stated valuations. That suggests founders have the leverage. The risk is that "replacement" claims rest on early migrations, such as Serval's Lucid example, rather than evidence across many large customers.

**Physical AI and defense absorbed the large checks.** Bedrock's $600M at $4.1B is the second-largest round in this digest, and Firehawk and Opentrons add hardware-heavy bets in propulsion and lab automation. Bedrock sold roughly 15% of the company at post-money, far more than the software names. That gap reflects how much capital autonomy hardware needs before it scales. The market potential is real, given construction labour shortages and munitions restocking. However, returns depend on fleet utilisation, production yields and procurement cycles, not on model quality alone.

**Fintech infrastructure is being priced like exchanges, and regulatory risk is part of the price.** Kalshi's $40B mark rests on its own reported figures: more than $1T in annualized volume and more than $1B in non-sports revenue. Its sports contracts still face state-level legal challenges, so investors are effectively underwriting a regulatory outcome. Collective and Nevis show the other side of fintech: AI that automates back-office work for solo operators and advisers. Two larger stories were excluded from the deals list because key facts could not be verified against a company announcement. UK chip startup Fractile's Series C appears in Companies House filings in the range of £540M–£577M, with no announced lead investors or valuation. Cohere completed a first close of a Series E targeting up to C$7B, but did not disclose the amount secured. Asia was quiet among tracked funds this week. Peak XV's main move was co-leading Hex, a US company, rather than an Asia-based round.

## Actionable Insights

- **Founders building agentic enterprise software:** prepare migration proof, meaning time-to-cutover, audit trails and permission models. Investors like Sequoia are now paying for "replace the incumbent" stories, and diligence will test whether those claims hold.
- **Investors in physical AI:** compare dilution and capital intensity with software comparables. A 15% sale at Series C points to long payback periods, so look for paid deployments and utilisation data before giving credit for valuation step-ups.
- **Fintech operators and investors:** watch how state-level challenges to Kalshi's sports contracts are resolved. The outcome will shape valuations across prediction markets and any fintech that depends on federal pre-emption.
- **Analysts:** separate headline numbers from committed equity. Collective's $100M includes a $60M facility, Cohere's C$7B is a target, and Fractile's figure comes from filings. All three need different treatment in deal databases.
- **Asia-focused LPs and founders:** the tracked global funds directed little capital to Asia-headquartered companies this week. Expect General Catalyst and Peak XV to keep favouring consumer and AI-native seed and pre-Series A deals in India over large growth rounds in the near term.
