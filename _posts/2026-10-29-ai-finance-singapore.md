# AI Finance in Singapore Brief: MAS Tells Banks to Manage Haze Risk

**Today:** A quiet news window for Singapore AI deployments, but a loud one for risk: the regulator tightened the screws on environmental exposure, while two fraud cases showed where operational losses are actually landing.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **MAS Reminds Banks to Manage Haze-Linked Environmental Risk**

*The Star · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** MAS has reinforced its expectation that banks assess, monitor and where necessary exit relationships with customers linked to haze-causing activity.

The regulator pointed banks to information released by the National Environment Agency under the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act, and said lenders should engage customers to understand their role and whether they have adequate measures to prevent, detect and stop fires. Where heightened risk is identified, MAS expects banks to impose conditions, review financing terms, limit further exposure or reassess the relationship; MAS said clear breaches of environmental law would warrant stronger measures, up to exiting the relationship. The expectations sit under the existing Guidelines on Environmental Risk Management and the Guidelines on Transition Planning.

**Why it matters:** This converts a regional environmental event into a live credit and client-onboarding question for banks with South-East Asian agribusiness and commodities books, and it raises the data burden on screening systems that most institutions are now trying to automate.

### 2. 🔒 **Singapore Police and OCBC Recover US$12.8 Million From Email Scam**

*Singapore Police Force · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Singapore petrochemical firm recovered the full US$12.8 million it lost to a business email compromise scam after police worked with OCBC and the Cambodian National Police.

According to the police, the company received an email on 1 October carrying revised payment instructions purporting to come from its business counterpart. It spotted discrepancies in the documents but still transferred the funds on 13 October after receiving an authorisation letter bearing the counterpart's signature and stamp; the fraud surfaced on 26 October when the counterpart said no payment had arrived. The SPF Cyber Command traced and recovered the money, and repeated its advice to verify payment-detail changes through independently confirmed contacts.

**Why it matters:** Full recovery at this size is unusual and shows cross-border bank-police coordination working, but the case is a reminder that payment-instruction fraud still defeats internal checks at corporates with mature finance functions.

### 3. 📊 **DBS Indonesia and BCA Reject Merger Speculation**

*Fintech News Singapore · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Both banks have denied social media claims of a share-exchange merger between DBS Indonesia and Bank Central Asia.

The rumour, which circulated on Indonesian social media, described DBS Indonesia shareholders receiving newly issued BCA shares with BCA as the surviving entity. BCA shares rose 7.65% to close at Rp5,275 on 28 October as the speculation spread, before weakening the next day following the denials. Mona Monika, head of group marketing and communications at DBS Indonesia, and BCA president commissioner Jahja Setiaatmadja both rejected the claims; DBS Bank held 99% of DBS Indonesia and BCA the remaining 1% per DBS's 2025 annual report.

**Why it matters:** The price swing shows how quickly unverified posts can move a large listed Asian bank, and raises the bar for how fast treasury and investor-relations teams need to respond to synthetic or recycled market rumours.

### 4. 🔒 **Tampered Ledger Wallets Cost Singapore Users S$3.5 Million**

*Fintech News Singapore · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore police have warned that hardware-tampered Ledger devices bought locally have been linked to S$3.5 million in crypto losses across two cases since 10 September.

The advisory says malicious components inside the devices could capture recovery phrases or private keys, giving attackers access to users' wallets. Ledger has suspended sales through a South-East Asian reseller after compromised devices were distributed in the region, and blockchain analytics firm Bitquery estimated roughly US$102 million taken from 324 wallets tied to those devices. Police advised affected users to stop using suspect hardware and move assets to a new wallet with a fresh recovery phrase on a clean device.

**Why it matters:** This is a supply-chain failure rather than a software exploit, and it puts distribution and reseller controls — not just custody technology — on the risk agenda for any firm offering self-custody products to Asian retail customers.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://fintechnews.sg/139942/crypto/tampered-ledger-wallets-singapore-crypto-losses/)

## Key Takeaways

- Singapore's supervisory agenda is widening beyond technology risk: environmental exposure is now being framed as a client due-diligence and credit decision, with the same escalate-or-exit logic banks apply to financial crime.
- Both fraud cases in today's brief turned on trust in an external artefact — a forged authorisation letter and a physical device — rather than on a breached bank system, which is where automated controls remain weakest.
- Market-moving misinformation and distribution-layer tampering are converging on the same problem for Asian financial institutions: verifying that a counterparty, document or device is genuinely what it claims to be.

## What to Watch

Watch whether banks begin disclosing how they are screening haze-linked exposures under the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act, and whether the Singapore Police Force reports further cases tied to the compromised hardware wallets as its advisory circulates.
