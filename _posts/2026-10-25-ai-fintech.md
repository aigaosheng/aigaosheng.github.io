# AI Fintech Brief: US Regulators Question Banks on AI Agent Shutdowns

**Today:** A quiet weekend for AI in banking: only two qualifying items, both on how US banks and their supervisors are handling autonomous AI agents.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **US Bank Regulators Ask How Lenders Would Stop AI Agents**
*ComplexDiscovery · October 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US bank supervisors are gathering evidence on whether lenders can audit and shut down autonomous AI agents, but no rule has been issued.

The Fed, OCC and FDIC are putting written and verbal questions to banks of all sizes on what limits govern AI systems, who approves and monitors their actions, whether decisions can be traced and audited, and how a bank would switch them off, according to Reuters reporting from Oct. 13 that this analysis summarizes. Sources described the effort as information gathering rather than new expectations. New in this piece: the interagency request for information on AI that the agencies promised in April had not appeared in the Federal Register as of Oct. 24, and a Wolters Kluwer survey of 230 US banking professionals found about one in three named kill-switch protocols as the incident-response area where they are least prepared.

**Why it matters:** Supervisors are asking for audit trails, authority limits and a tested off switch before any formal rule exists, which is a practical checklist for Asia-based banks and BaaS providers deploying agents. The survey measures self-reported readiness, not how many banks lack a kill switch.

### 2. 🔒 **Commentary: JPMorgan and Citi Agent Limits Put Human Oversight in Focus**
*Forbes (contributor column) · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** This is an opinion column, not new reporting: it argues that banks restricting outside AI agents shows why human sign-off matters more than raw efficiency.

The column recaps JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon's Oct. 13 earnings-call stance against admitting any outside agent. It notes that agents can still view account information at JPMorgan while safeguards are built to stop them moving money. It also recaps Citi and Bank of America restrictions reported by the Financial Times on Oct. 16, and Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan's comment, reported by CNBC on Oct. 23, that a human should authorize money leaving an account. The author's advice is to separate what AI can see from what it can do, decide in advance which systems go offline in a crisis, and name a human owner for every agent that can act.

**Why it matters:** The "see versus act" line is where large banks are converging. Fintechs building agent-facing products in Asia should expect similar demands for read-only access and named accountability. These recommendations are the author's view, not regulatory requirements.

## Key Takeaways
- Supervisors are asking before prescribing: US regulators want proof of audit and shutdown capability, while formal AI guidance for agents is still pending.
- Large banks are drawing the line at credentials and money movement: viewing data is tolerated, acting on accounts is not.
- Preparedness trails intent: shutdown protocols are a self-reported weak spot, even as banks keep expanding agent use internally.

## What to Watch
Whether the Fed, OCC and FDIC publish the promised request for information on model risk and banks' use of AI, and the Nov. 16 comment deadline on the proposed interagency third-party risk management guidance.
