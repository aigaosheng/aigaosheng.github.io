# **AI for Science Brief: Merck Partners With Isomorphic Labs**

**Today:** Merck's new collaboration with Isomorphic Labs illustrates how pharmaceutical companies are combining proprietary research data with AI-driven molecular design to pursue new drug candidates.

## **Top Stories**

### **1. 🔬 Merck Partners With Isomorphic Labs on AI Drug Discovery**

*Merck · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Merck and Isomorphic Labs have entered a research collaboration to discover small-molecule drug candidates for an oncology target using AI-driven molecular design.

Isomorphic Labs will use its proprietary drug-design engine, IsoDDE, to generate computational predictions and molecular designs, incorporating selected proprietary datasets supplied by Merck. Merck will lead preclinical and clinical development, as well as manufacturing and commercialisation of any resulting products. The agreement includes an upfront payment, milestone-based compensation and tiered royalties; Isomorphic Labs also has an option to co-fund development in exchange for higher royalties.

**Why it matters:** The arrangement illustrates a division of labour that could become important in AI-enabled drug development: specialist AI companies contribute computational design capabilities, while pharmaceutical partners provide proprietary data, experimental expertise and the resources required to advance candidates through development. The commercial value will depend on whether the collaboration produces viable candidates and progresses them through experimental and clinical validation.

## **Key Takeaways**

- **AI drug discovery is increasingly partnership-driven:** Pharmaceutical companies can combine internal datasets and development capabilities with specialist AI platforms rather than building every capability in-house.
- **Commercial terms link AI research to downstream outcomes:** Milestones and royalties connect the economics of computational discovery to research, regulatory and commercial progress.
- **Experimental validation remains decisive:** Computationally designed molecules must still demonstrate biological activity, safety and efficacy through established development processes.
