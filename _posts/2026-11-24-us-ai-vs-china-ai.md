# US AI vs China AI Brief: Megaspeed Added to US Trade Blacklist

**Today:** A new US blacklist action and scrutiny of Nvidia’s China-bound chips put Southeast Asian intermediaries and regional compliance controls in focus.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **US Adds Megaspeed And Six Firms To Entity List**

*The Guardian · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US added seven companies across Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to its trade blacklist, tightening controls around suspected diversion of advanced computing chips.

The Commerce Department’s notice names Megaspeed International and related firms, including Alibaba’s Singapore subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions. The listed entities now need US approval for exports covered by the rules, with licence applications subject to a presumption of denial. The action concerns alleged diversion risks; it is not a public finding that the companies diverted chips.

**Why it matters:** The move raises compliance and supply-chain risks for AI infrastructure providers and customers using regional cloud capacity. For Singapore and neighbouring markets, it puts greater attention on chip end users and cross-border routing.

### 2. 🏦 **Congress Schedules Public Nvidia Testimony**

*Associated Press · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is scheduled to testify publicly to a Senate panel on December 3 as lawmakers press for answers about China-bound chip sales and his reinstatement.

The Senate Banking Committee’s outgoing chairman, Tim Scott, announced the hearing after the Nvidia board restored Huang as CEO. AP also reports that Huang is expected to meet with three House committees next week. Lawmakers are seeking clarity on export approvals, possible diversion and the administration’s role in the company’s leadership decision.

**Why it matters:** Congressional scrutiny could shape future export-control decisions and raise the cost of uncertainty for chipmakers, cloud providers and customers with China exposure. Asian firms in the AI compute chain should track whether the hearings lead to new licensing or enforcement measures.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-jensen-huang-48a005a1415549e69ec69400024aa797)

### 3. 🌐 **Singapore Says Megaspeed Probe Continues**

*Prime Minister’s Office Singapore · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore said it is continuing its own investigation into possible breaches of local law while cooperating with the US on checks related to chip rerouting.

Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said Singapore does not enforce US export controls but does not want companies using the country to circumvent them. He said Singapore is seeking more information from US authorities and that Nvidia chips bought by Megaspeed were routed to other countries rather than entering Singapore. Singapore’s investigation is focused on whether local laws were breached.

**Why it matters:** Singapore’s approach highlights the practical coordination required when chip supply chains and data-centre capacity cross multiple jurisdictions. For regional operators, due diligence on end users, routing and local legal obligations remains central to managing export-control risk.

### 4. 🌐 **Alibaba Disputes US Blacklisting Of Its Singapore Unit**

*The Straits Times · November 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Alibaba called the US listing of its Singapore subsidiary Apex erroneous, denied diversion of restricted chips and said it would pursue legal avenues.

Apex was one of seven companies added to the US Entity List in the action involving Megaspeed-linked firms. The Straits Times reports that corporate records showed an active financial arrangement between Apex and Megaspeed subsidiary Speedmatrix. Alibaba’s statement contests the basis for Apex’s inclusion; it does not change the export restrictions while the listing remains in place.

**Why it matters:** The dispute leaves suppliers and counterparties to manage immediate licensing constraints alongside contested allegations. For companies operating across Asia, screening and contracting processes may need to account for listed entities and related parties.

## Key Takeaways

- US export-control scrutiny is reaching companies and infrastructure links across Southeast Asia.
- Singapore is coordinating with US authorities while investigating possible breaches of its own laws.
- Congressional attention and Alibaba’s challenge add uncertainty around Nvidia’s China-related business and the rules governing chip access.

## What to Watch

Watch Huang’s scheduled December 3 Senate testimony for disclosures or proposals that could affect chip licensing. Singapore’s ongoing investigation and Alibaba’s response to Apex’s listing may clarify how regional authorities and affected companies handle the new restrictions.
