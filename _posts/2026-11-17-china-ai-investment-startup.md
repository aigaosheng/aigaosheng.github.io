# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: CATL Backs AI Industrial Design Startup Ziqian Technology

**Today:** Corporate and state-linked investors are putting money into China AI startups that already have customers, users or factory capacity, not into pure model plays.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **CATL Makes Exclusive Strategic Investment in AI Industrial Design Platform Ziqian Technology**

*PEdaily · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** CATL, the world's largest battery maker, is the sole strategic investor in Ziqian Technology's new round, a bet on AI-native industrial design software.

Founded in 2020, Ziqian Technology builds an AI platform for industrial R&D. It runs on its own CAD kernel and generates 3D assembly models that engineers can edit and simulate. The round size was not disclosed, and the company says the money will go into collecting engineering knowledge for specific industries. According to the company, it had more than 100 enterprise customers in 2025 and more than 200,000 platform users across 80-plus countries and regions in 2026, and roughly a fifth of new sign-ups were referred by ChatGPT.

**Why it matters:** A large manufacturer taking an exclusive position suggests that industrial companies want influence over the AI design tools their engineering teams will rely on. It also adds momentum to China's push to replace foreign CAD software, which matters to manufacturers across Asia that use Western design suites.

### 2. 📊 **Humanoid Robot Sensor Maker Bluepoint Touch Closes D3 Round Worth Several Hundred Million Yuan**

*PEdaily · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing-based Bluepoint Touch has raised more than 1 billion yuan in about six months, so investors are still putting money into suppliers of core components for humanoid robots.

The company makes six-axis force sensors and joint torque sensors. Its D3 round, worth several hundred million yuan, drew 14 investors, among them Hongtai Fund, Hony Capital and Zhongguancun Science City. Earlier backers include HongShan, Hillhouse, CATL, SAIC and GAC, and robot makers AgiBot and Galbot are both shareholders and customers. The company says it will use the funds for next-generation sensor R&D with key customers, overseas expansion, more production capacity, and a move into algorithms and data. It also says its delivery times are 2–3 weeks, against 8–12 weeks for overseas rivals.

**Why it matters:** Robot makers that also hold equity in their suppliers point to a vertically linked domestic supply chain for embodied AI. If Chinese suppliers offer shorter lead times and lower costs, robot makers across Asia may source more components from China.

### 3. 🌐 **Singapore-Founded AutoLife Robotics Raises Pre-A+ Round After Moving Headquarters to Guangzhou**

*PEdaily · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** AutoLife Robotics, which started in Singapore and has moved its headquarters to Guangzhou, has closed a Pre-A+ round of about 100 million yuan to scale up service robots built on world models.

The China-US Green Fund and Nanxi Venture Capital co-led the round, and existing shareholder Haizhu Chengfa added to its stake. This is the company's fourth round, and it has raised several hundred million yuan in equity so far. AutoLife makes service robots for exhibitions, hotels, cultural tourism and retail. The new money will go to a new Guangzhou production line, larger deployments in those settings, and research into differentiable world models. A production base of about 6,000 square metres is under construction, and the team has more than 130 engineers and PhDs.

**Why it matters:** The move shows how Chinese district-level capital and manufacturing ecosystems are drawing robotics teams founded in Singapore. That is a trade-off for Singapore's deep-tech ambitions, even as the move speeds commercial scale-up.

### 4. 💳 **Huling Raises Seed Round of About 10 Million Yuan and Launches AI Voice Health Ring**

*PEdaily · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing startup Huling, founded this year by former Meituan and ByteDance managers, has raised seed money to bring a voice-first AI ring to market as a step towards a personal agent.

Huling did not name the investors in its seed round of about 10 million yuan and says an angel round will start soon. The company says the ring weighs under 5 grams, carries an IPX8 water-resistance rating and lasts seven days on a charge. Pre-sales open in late November and shipping starts in early December, at a price between mass-market wearables and Oura. CEO Liu Yanxiang previously ran a business unit at Meituan.

**Why it matters:** Founders from China's big tech firms are betting that small wearables can become everyday entry points for AI agents. That could create new channels for health, commerce and payment services.

## Key Takeaways

- Strategic and industrial investors such as CATL, carmakers and robot makers are taking stakes in AI startups that fit their own supply chains, which links funding to future procurement.
- Embodied AI money is going beyond robot makers to component suppliers and robots built for specific settings with production capacity, which suggests investors now weigh delivery capability over demos.
- Chinese local-government and state-linked funds keep pulling in robotics teams, including teams founded abroad. This shapes where Asian deep-tech companies choose to scale.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Huling meets its late-November pre-sale and early-December shipping dates and opens its angel round. Also watch for any disclosure of the size of Ziqian Technology's round or of commercial deployments with CATL.
