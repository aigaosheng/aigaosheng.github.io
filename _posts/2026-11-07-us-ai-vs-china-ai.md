# US AI vs China AI Brief: China's Spy Agency Named in Nvidia Huang Probe

**Today:** A New York Times report naming China's top intelligence agency in connection with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang sharpens congressional scrutiny of chip export policy just as lawmakers set a date to question him directly.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **New York Times Names China's MSS in Nvidia-Adjacent Spy Case**

*The Week · November 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A New York Times investigation has identified China's Ministry of State Security as the agency behind a year-long operation targeting Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang through a close contact, adding specific new detail to a congressional spy probe that was already under way.

The report names the contact as Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, an executive at Singapore-based Nvidia cloud customer Megaspeed International, and says US agencies surveilled her for roughly a year, citing a 2024 photograph of the two together in Taipei as supporting evidence. It states her assignment was to gather intelligence on US AI chip technology and to influence Jensen Huang toward pushing Washington to ease export restrictions on China, and that President Trump was briefed on the matter about a year ago but did not act. Three House Republican committee chairs have asked Huang to confirm a November 18 closed-door briefing or propose an alternative date by November 11; Nvidia has not confirmed his attendance and maintains that Megaspeed received only routine business information.

**Why it matters:** The episode keeps chip export policy entangled with espionage politics heading into the Trump-Xi APEC meeting in Shenzhen on November 18-19, raising the odds of tighter congressional conditions on any further easing of AI chip sales to China. Megaspeed's Singapore base also puts regional cloud and data-centre intermediaries back in the spotlight as a channel US regulators are watching for chip-diversion risk, a relevant signal for Singapore-based fintech and cloud infrastructure players navigating US compliance expectations.

## Key Takeaways

- Naming the Ministry of State Security turns a previously vague "foreign asset" allegation into a specific state-intelligence attribution, raising the political stakes of Huang's November 18 briefing.
- Singapore-based Megaspeed's alleged role as a conduit underscores how Southeast Asian cloud intermediaries remain a flashpoint in US chip-diversion enforcement.
- Expect export-control politics and espionage allegations to stay intertwined through the Shenzhen APEC summit, complicating any near-term easing of chip sales to China.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Jensen Huang confirms the November 18 closed-door congressional briefing by the November 11 deadline, and whether the matter surfaces in Trump-Xi discussions at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18-19.
