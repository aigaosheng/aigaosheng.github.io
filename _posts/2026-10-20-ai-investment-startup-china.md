# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief — 2026-10-20

**Today:** Washington's DeepSeek ban draws a sharp Beijing response, while capital keeps flowing: Neolix closes a roughly $400M round, and Infinigence and Micro-Intelligence head to Hong Kong.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **China Defends DeepSeek After US Bars Its Products From Federal Civilian Agencies**

*Business Standard (AP) · 2026-10-20*

**Bottom line:** A US order removing DeepSeek from federal civilian systems has become a new flashpoint in US–China AI relations, weeks before a planned Trump–Xi meeting.

A Department of Homeland Security notice made public Monday tells civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek and affiliated products, plus third-party services built on them, and bars new procurement. The order dates to late July and cites China-based servers and possible Chinese-authority access to sensitive US data. Beijing's Foreign Ministry said it will defend Chinese companies' legitimate rights and opposes stretching the concept of national security.

**Why it matters:** DeepSeek is reportedly in the middle of a very large funding round, so Washington's restrictions add policy risk to one of China's most heavily capitalized AI labs. The dispute also lands before the Trump–Xi meeting expected in mid-November at APEC in Shenzhen, where AI is expected on the agenda.

### 2. 📊 **Neolix Raises Nearly $400M Series E for Global Driverless-Delivery Push**

*TechNode · 2026-10-20*

**Bottom line:** Autonomous-delivery maker Neolix closed a nearly $400M round with state AI-fund and Alibaba-affiliated backing, showing that growth capital for physical AI is still available.

Neolix announced the Series E on October 20, co-led by Boyu Capital and a national-level fund that Jiemian, citing sources, identified as China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund. Other investors include Alibaba's AEF NextGen Hong Kong fund, Vertex Growth and GAC Capital. Proceeds will go toward expanding its global operating and customer-service network and toward data collection and training for end-to-end driving models. Cumulative funding now exceeds RMB 10 billion.

**Why it matters:** The round is smaller than last year's Series D of more than $600M, but state and strategic investors are still concentrating capital in category leaders. It also shows overseas expansion becoming a core use of proceeds for Chinese AI hardware startups.

### 3. 🤖 **Infinigence Reportedly Raises RMB 2.3B and Is Said to Eye a Hong Kong IPO**

*Bamboo Works · 2026-10-20*

**Bottom line:** Infinigence, which sells software that runs AI models across different chips, has reportedly raised RMB 2.3B (about $344M) in a Series C and is reportedly preparing a Hong Kong listing.

Chinese media reported the round last week, taking cumulative fundraising to RMB 4.3B from backers including Tencent, Baidu, Xiaomi, Lenovo Capital, HongShan and Qiming. Bamboo Works also cites a Bloomberg report this month that Infinigence has confidentially filed for a Hong Kong IPO. Rival SiliconFlow posted its own Hong Kong listing application on June 30.

**Why it matters:** Export controls have pushed Chinese companies to test a wider range of domestic chips, which creates demand for software that bridges models and hardware. Infinigence and SiliconFlow would test public-market appetite for AI infrastructure players, which compete with Alibaba Cloud and ByteDance's Volcano Engine. The funding and IPO details are reported rather than company-confirmed.

### 4. 📊 **Robot Maker Micro-Intelligence Opens Hong Kong IPO Books to Raise Up to HK$800M**

*The Standard (Hong Kong) · 2026-10-20*

**Bottom line:** Changzhou Micro-Intelligence, which says it leads China's embodied-intelligence industrial robot market, began taking subscriptions Tuesday for a Hong Kong IPO of up to about HK$800M.

The company is offering 37.28 million shares at an indicative HK$16.63–HK$21.46 each, with the offer period running through Friday and trading expected in late October. It reports a 20% share of China's embodied intelligent industrial robot market by 2025 revenue, and 2025 total revenue of RMB 795.5M, up 32.5% year on year. Orient Securities (Hong Kong) is sole sponsor.

**Why it matters:** The deal is a small but concrete test of investor appetite for embodied-AI hardware makers, at a time when several larger Chinese robotics and AI names are queuing for Hong Kong listings. Pricing and first-day trading will show how public investors value robotics revenue.
