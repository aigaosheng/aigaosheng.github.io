# US AI vs China AI Brief: Congress Summons Huang Over Chip Exports

**Today:** The fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation is accelerating Washington's chip-export crackdown on China, with Congress, Singapore and Beijing all moving simultaneously, raising compliance and supply-chain stakes for every fintech and enterprise AI buyer relying on GPU-backed cloud capacity in Asia.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Senate Banking Committee Summons Huang and Lutnick to Testify**

*NBC News · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US lawmakers have set December hearing dates for former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, turning a personal scandal into a formal inquiry on chip export oversight.

Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott asked Lutnick to testify on December 10 and Huang on December 3, warning of subpoenas if either declines; Lutnick has agreed to appear. Lawmakers from both parties want to know whether export controls on advanced semiconductors to China were relaxed and whether Commerce failed to blacklist firms suspected of diversion. Separately, House committees have requested closed-door interviews with Huang and Nvidia's general counsel on December 1 and 2.

**Why it matters:** A multi-committee probe into chip export enforcement raises the odds of tighter, more litigated licensing rules for GPU sales to China and transit hubs such as Singapore and Malaysia, directly affecting any Asian cloud or AI-infrastructure provider that sources Nvidia capacity.

### 2. 🏦 **Congress Moves to Write Chip Export Curbs Into Defense Bill**

*The Korea Times · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** House Speaker Mike Johnson says there is a "developing consensus" in Congress to tighten China chip export rules, with four bills now attached to the must-pass defense authorization act.

The AI Overwatch Act would give the House Foreign Affairs Committee and Senate Banking Committee a 30-day window to block advanced AI chip export licences to China; the Chip Security Act would mandate location-tracking of advanced processors; the MATCH Act would pressure allied nations on chipmaking equipment; and a proposed Remote Access Security Act targets Chinese firms leasing overseas data centres to reach Nvidia chips remotely.

**Why it matters:** Bundling these measures into a bipartisan, must-pass bill makes near-term passage far more likely than standalone legislation, and a remote-access provision would close a loophole that Southeast Asian data centres have reportedly been used to exploit.

### 3. 🔒 **Singapore Says US Gave 'Limited' Help in AI Chip Smuggling Probe**

*CNBC · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore authorities disclosed that repeated requests to the US Department of Justice and FBI for cooperation from server maker Super Micro yielded only limited information, as a fifth individual was charged with 41 counts in a roughly $390 million server-diversion case.

Singapore has become a major transit and billing hub for Nvidia chips, with the company attributing 22% of its revenue to the country in a recent quarter, up from 8% in 2023. Investigators are examining whether servers legally exported to Singapore or Malaysia were later diverted to China, while Super Micro maintains it has cooperated with authorities in Singapore, the US and Taiwan.

**Why it matters:** A public rift between Singapore and US agencies over cross-border cooperation highlights enforcement gaps that fintech and cloud operators in the transit hub should expect regulators to close quickly, likely through tighter end-user verification requirements.

### 4. 🌐 **China Warns Washington Against 'Abuse' of Export Controls**

*Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing's foreign ministry pushed back directly on the new wave of congressional chip bills, saying further restrictions would disrupt global supply chains and serve no one's interests.

Spokesperson Mao Ning said China "consistently opposed the abuse of export control measures" and called on both governments to honour prior leader-level understandings and maintain stable trade ties. She declined to comment on specific reports of banned Nvidia chips operating inside a Shanghai data centre.

**Why it matters:** Beijing's calibrated, non-escalatory language suggests it wants to preserve space for the Trump-Xi meeting later this month rather than retaliate immediately, but enterprises planning China-adjacent AI infrastructure should treat the rhetoric as a leading indicator of further friction, not resolution.

## Key Takeaways

- Washington's chip-control crackdown is now running on three simultaneous tracks: congressional testimony, defense-bill legislation and enforcement cooperation with transit hubs like Singapore.
- Singapore's public disclosure of limited US cooperation signals that Southeast Asian jurisdictions may tighten their own licensing and audit regimes independent of US timelines.
- Beijing's restrained official response leaves room for diplomacy, but the volume of parallel US actions raises the probability of broader licensing delays for GPU-dependent AI and fintech infrastructure across Asia.

## What to Watch

Senate Banking Committee hearings are set for December 3 (Huang) and December 10 (Lutnick), with House closed-door interviews on December 1 and 2; watch whether the AI Overwatch Act, Chip Security Act and MATCH Act survive defense-bill negotiations intact.
