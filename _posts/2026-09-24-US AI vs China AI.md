## Strategic Takeaways

* **The AI contest is moving beyond model benchmarks.** Compute, semiconductor supply, data centers, electricity, talent and capital are becoming equally important determinants of national AI capacity.
* **China is pursuing vertical integration.** Alibaba and Huawei illustrate a strategy spanning chips, infrastructure, models and applications, reducing dependence on individual foreign technology suppliers.
* **The US retains major infrastructure advantages.** Current comparative data continues to show a substantial US lead in AI computing capacity and hyperscaler investment.
* **Open-weight models are becoming a strategic variable.** Their global availability gives Chinese AI companies a distribution mechanism that is difficult to control through conventional restrictions on proprietary model access.
* **AI diplomacy is becoming institutionalized.** The emergence of formal dialogue and potential incident-notification mechanisms suggests that competition and risk-management channels will increasingly coexist.
* **The central strategic question is shifting.** Rather than simply asking which country has the strongest frontier model, the more consequential question is which ecosystem can sustain large-scale AI deployment across chips, models, infrastructure, energy, talent and global markets.
