# AI for Science Brief: Tencent AI Designs Dual-Target Peptides

**Today:** Tencent's AI-designed dual-target peptide platform reached Nature's main journal, a concrete validation of AI-driven drug design with immediate relevance for Asia's biotech and pharmaceutical ecosystem.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔬 **Tencent Life Science Lab's ORIDTP Platform Reaches Nature with AI-Designed Dual-Target Peptides**

*Tencent / 36Kr · Nov 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Tencent has published a Nature paper demonstrating an AI platform that designs high-activity dual-target therapeutic peptides from scratch, achieving significant efficacy in preclinical models for weight loss/diabetes and tumor immunity without relying on existing molecular templates.

The ORIDTP platform combines diffusion generative models, dual-objective reinforcement learning, flexible peptide affinity modeling, and biological experiments into a closed-loop workflow of computational design, experimental validation, and feedback optimization . Each design round requires testing only about 15 peptides, shifting drug discovery from large-scale blind screening to AI-driven iterative refinement. In obese and diabetic mouse models, the candidate achieved significant weight reduction and glucose control at lower molar doses than comparator drugs; in a humanized target knock-in tumor model, tumor volume decreased by 94.2% versus control . The research remains preclinical, with no human trials initiated.

**Why it matters:** This is Tencent's first Nature main-journal paper as lead institution, following a Cell paper in July, signaling that Chinese technology firms are building credible capabilities in AI-driven drug discovery rather than just investing in it. For Asia's biotech and pharma sector, the ORIDTP platform—already deployed on Tencent Health's BioNexus workbench and open to the research community—offers a potential collaboration or benchmarking reference for peptide therapeutics, a modality gaining commercial traction globally.

### 2. 📊 **China's AI4S Sector Raises Over RMB 20 Billion in 2026 as Major Labs Pivot to Scientific Research**

*The Paper via Sina Finance · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Chinese AI for Science startups have raised more than RMB 20 billion across over 80 funding rounds in the first ten months of 2026, as Tencent, Alibaba, Baidu, Huawei, ByteDance, Moonshot, MiniMax, and Shanghai AI Lab all designate scientific research as a strategic priority.

The capital surge coincides with heightened public attention on AI-assisted mathematical proofs, including Anthropic's Claude and a claimed Riemann Hypothesis proof, which pushed AI4S from a niche research area into mainstream discourse . MiniMax disclosed internal model results in cryptanalysis, claiming it discovered previously unknown algorithmic construction flaws and accelerated certain theoretical attacks from theoretical to seconds or minutes .

**Why it matters:** The funding figures and coordinated strategic pivots indicate that China's AI industry views scientific discovery as both a research frontier and a commercial opportunity, distinct from the consumer chatbot market. For Singapore-based investors and enterprise technology leaders, the emergence of AI4S as a funded sector in China signals potential partnership and competitive dynamics in drug discovery, materials science, and computational biology across the region.

### 3. 🔬 **Google's Gemini 4 Carbon Assists in Proof of 70-Year-Old Hadwiger Covering Conjecture**

*36Kr / New Zhiyuan · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Google Research announced that Gemini 4 Carbon helped derive the proof structure for the three-dimensional Hadwiger covering conjecture, open for roughly 70 years, with the model generating the proof intuition and 13,700 lines of formal verification code autonomously.

The conjecture posits that any d-dimensional convex body can be covered by at most 2^d smaller homothetic copies of itself . The proof uses a "2 + 6 = 8 twilight-line illumination" approach, placing two lights at the poles and six along the shadow boundary, with the model applying modern convex geometry tools including a symmetrization technique and John position analysis . Two human researchers guided the model mid-process, and the formalized Lean code was generated automatically. The paper is available on arXiv.

**Why it matters:** This represents a concrete demonstration of frontier models contributing to original mathematical discovery rather than just computational assistance, with the formal verification code providing a degree of rigor uncommon in AI-generated proofs. For research institutions in Asia evaluating AI's role in fundamental science, the Hadwiger case offers a template for human-AI collaboration in theoretical work where formal verification is feasible.

### 4. 🤖 **Anthropic and OpenAI Simultaneously Publish VP≠VNP Proofs, Advancing Algebraic Complexity Theory**

*Cailian Press via 36Kr · Nov 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Anthropic and OpenAI separately published proofs of the VP≠VNP problem, described as the algebraic version of P≠NP, within roughly four hours of each other, potentially offering new approaches to one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems.

The proofs were posted on the companies' respective GitHub repositories on November 24, with Anthropic publishing first and OpenAI following approximately four hours later . The VP≠VNP problem concerns whether algebraic circuits can efficiently compute polynomials that are hard for arithmetic formulas, a question with implications for computational complexity theory. The results have not yet been peer-reviewed.

**Why it matters:** The simultaneous publication by two leading AI labs of proofs in a foundational complexity-theory problem suggests that AI-assisted mathematical research is accelerating beyond isolated demonstrations into a broader pattern of tool use in theoretical computer science. While these proofs require independent verification, the competitive dynamic between Anthropic and OpenAI in mathematical research may drive faster progress and more public disclosure of AI-assisted discoveries.
