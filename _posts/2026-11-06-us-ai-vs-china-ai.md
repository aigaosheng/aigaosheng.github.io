# US AI vs China AI Brief: Congress Summons Nvidia Chief Over China Spy Ties

**Today:** Nvidia is being squeezed from both sides of the Pacific in the same week, with direct implications for chip supply chains, compliance exposure and the pace of US-China AI diplomacy that enterprise, fintech and payments leaders in Asia depend on for planning.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Congress Summons Nvidia Chief Over China Spy Ties**

*CNBC · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang must brief Congress behind closed doors before the company's next earnings release, intensifying political scrutiny of Nvidia's China relationships just as Washington tightens chip-export enforcement.

Three House Republican committee chairs asked Huang to address reported ties to Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman U.S. intelligence has flagged as relaying information to Beijing, citing "significant national security concerns regarding the PRC's access to sensitive U.S. technology." The briefing is set for November 18, one day after Nvidia's Q3 earnings report. Nvidia says Huang's contact with her involved only routine business engagements and no trade secrets or security-sensitive information, while Beijing has separately dismissed related spying allegations as "groundless speculation."

**Why it matters:** The episode shows Washington is willing to scrutinize even allied executives' China relationships, raising compliance and reputational stakes for any enterprise or fintech in Asia that sources AI compute through Nvidia's regional distribution and cloud partners.

### 2. 🌐 **600,000 Nvidia Chips Stall in Hong Kong Logjam**

*Semafor, citing a Reuters exclusive · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** About 600,000 Nvidia H200 chips sit idle in Hong Kong because Beijing, not Washington, is now the bottleneck blocking their entry into mainland China.

Despite a U.S. licensing framework that permits H200 exports, Chinese authorities have approved only limited volumes for firms including Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent and DeepSeek, each capped at roughly 10,000 chips so far, as Beijing steers domestic firms toward Chinese-made silicon. Unofficial market prices for an eight-card H200 server reportedly tripled to more than 6 million yuan (about US$900,000) by late October.

**Why it matters:** Beijing choosing to leave licensed, approved chips unused signals it will accept near-term compute shortfalls to accelerate chip self-sufficiency — a dynamic that Singapore and regional data-centre and cloud operators should track given parallel U.S. pressure on overseas compute leasing to Chinese tenants.

### 3. 🏦 **SIPRI Flags Gaps in US-China AI Safety Talks**

*Eurasia Review (SIPRI analysis) · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new SIPRI analysis finds the US-China AI dialogue now has more institutional structure than prior efforts, but still lacks shared definitions of what counts as a reportable AI "incident."

Analysts Lora Saalman and Fei Su note the September Trump-Xi summit created a dedicated bilateral format with an AI incident hotline, drawing in trade officials and executives from Nvidia, OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta and Apple rather than relying on normative pledges alone. They flag a core terminology gap: Washington frames the dialogue around future "super intelligence" risks, while Beijing still addresses it in terms of present-day "artificial intelligence" capabilities, complicating incident reporting and escalation protocols.

**Why it matters:** Unresolved definitional gaps could blunt the hotline's usefulness just as it matters most, ahead of the Trump-Xi APEC meeting in Shenzhen on November 18-19, where AI cooperation is expected to be a headline deliverable with knock-on effects for regional AI governance norms, including in Singapore.

## Key Takeaways

- Nvidia is being squeezed from both directions: U.S. lawmakers questioning its China relationships while Beijing throttles chip imports to protect domestic suppliers.
- China's willingness to leave approved, licensed US chips idle in Hong Kong signals growing confidence in its own chip self-sufficiency push, even at a near-term compute cost.
- Bilateral AI dialogue mechanisms are maturing in form but not yet in substance, leaving real execution risk ahead of the November 18-19 Shenzhen summit.
