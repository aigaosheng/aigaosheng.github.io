# AI for Society Brief: Americans Reject AI Shopping Tools This Holiday Season

**Today:** A new consumer survey shows AI reluctance hardening rather than fading, a signal enterprise and fintech leaders building AI-driven commerce and payment experiences cannot ignore heading into peak shopping season.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **Bank of America Survey Finds Sharp Rise in Holiday AI Avoidance**

*Fortune · November 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Consumer resistance to AI shopping tools nearly doubled year-on-year, even as retailers and platforms pour investment into AI-powered commerce features.

Bank of America's latest consumer survey found 72% of Americans do not plan to use AI tools for holiday shopping this year, up from 49% in 2025. Among those avoiding AI, 42% cited personal or ethical reasons and 37% said it strips away the personal touch of gift-giving, while only small minorities plan to use AI for price comparison (17%), gift ideas (14%) or writing messages (7%). A Bank of America representative pointed to "AI fatigue" after last year's novelty wore off, while a marketing professor noted AI use can erode a shopper's sense of ownership over the gift-giving process.

**Why it matters:** For enterprise AI and payments players building agentic shopping and checkout experiences, the data is a reality check: emotionally charged, high-stakes purchase moments may resist automation even as functional, low-stakes tasks get automated. In Asia, where agentic commerce and AI-assisted checkout are being piloted by major platforms, this US sentiment data is an early warning to pair AI features with clear opt-outs and human-touch alternatives rather than assuming adoption will track investment.

## Key Takeaways

- Consumer enthusiasm for AI is not rising in lockstep with vendor investment — trust and emotional fit matter as much as capability.
- Ethical and personal-touch objections, not just accuracy concerns, are now the leading reasons consumers opt out of AI tools in high-stakes, personal transactions.
- Businesses deploying AI in consumer-facing commerce and payment flows should expect resistance to cluster around moments that feel personal or relational, not just complex.

## What to Watch

Watch upcoming holiday-quarter earnings and e-commerce platform data for whether actual AI-assisted shopping behaviour matches this stated reluctance, and whether similar consumer-sentiment surveys emerge from Asian markets where agentic commerce tools are being rolled out.
