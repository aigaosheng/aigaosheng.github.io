# Enterprise AI Brief: DXC and LangChain Expand Governed Agentic AI

**Today:** Enterprise AI is moving towards operational deployment, with partnerships focused on critical IT operations, sovereign AI infrastructure and governed automation. For enterprise leaders in Asia, the key question is how to scale AI agents while retaining control over data, decisions and business processes.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **DXC and LangChain Expand Agentic AI for Critical IT Operations**

*DXC Technology · October 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DXC Technology and LangChain are combining enterprise IT operations expertise with agent orchestration technology to expand governed AI agents across mission-critical environments.

The partnership builds on DXC OASIS, an intelligent operations platform using LangChain's open-source LangGraph runtime. DXC says the platform is scaling towards 125 customer environments across regulated industries and multiple geographies. Across 500 measured use cases, DXC reports an average 95% reduction in resolution time and diagnostic accuracy of at least 92%, measured against senior engineer judgement.

**Why it matters:** This is a practical example of enterprise AI moving beyond content generation into operational workflows. For financial institutions, telecom operators and other businesses with complex IT estates, the relevant evaluation criteria include incident-resolution performance, human oversight, auditability and integration with existing systems.

### 2. 🤖 **Etisalat UAE and DataRobot Expand Sovereign Agentic AI**

*DataRobot · October 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Etisalat UAE and DataRobot have extended their strategic partnership through a multi-year agreement to develop, deploy and govern AI agents within Etisalat's own data-centre environment.

The expanded arrangement uses DataRobot's Agent Workforce Platform as part of Etisalat UAE's sovereign AI ecosystem. The stated objective is to support agentic workloads across business units while strengthening governance and enterprise controls as AI systems move from generating insights to taking action.

**Why it matters:** Sovereign deployment is increasingly relevant to enterprises operating under data-residency, confidentiality and regulatory constraints. Organisations across Singapore and Asia should assess not only model performance but also where agents execute, how data moves between systems, and who controls access and operational policies.

### 7. 🌐 **Enterprise Leaders Highlight Accountability Gaps in Agentic AI**

*Axios · October 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Business leaders are warning that enterprise agent deployments need clearer accountability for agent permissions, decisions, privacy and liability.

An Axios Live discussion featuring Box CEO Aaron Levie and Credo AI CEO Navrina Singh examined the governance challenges surrounding agentic AI. The discussion highlighted the need to define what agents may access and do, who is responsible for their actions, and how organisations should manage safety and compliance in higher-stakes applications.

**Why it matters:** As agents gain the ability to execute actions rather than merely generate recommendations, responsibility cannot stop at model selection. Enterprises should establish named owners, permission boundaries, approval requirements and incident-escalation procedures before granting agents access to sensitive systems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Enterprise AI is becoming an operational systems challenge.** Integration with IT operations, enterprise applications and business workflows is central to moving beyond pilots.
- **Governance is increasingly part of the deployment architecture.** Permissions, audit trails, human oversight and cost controls need to be built into the operating model, not added after launch.
- **Sovereignty and measurable performance matter.** Organisations should evaluate deployment location, data access and independently validated outcomes alongside model capability.

## What to Watch

Watch for evidence that enterprise agent deployments are delivering sustained improvements in production, including independently validated performance, lower exception rates and fewer operational incidents. In Singapore and across Asia, pay particular attention to deployments that demonstrate how data residency, access control and auditability work in real operating environments.
