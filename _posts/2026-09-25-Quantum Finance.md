# Quantum Finance Brief — 2026-09-25

**Today:** Financial institutions should watch quantum readiness less as a distant computing story and more as an emerging cryptographic, identity, and transaction-security requirement.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **WISeKey and OISTE.ORG expand post-quantum Root of Trust for autonomous AI and financial transactions**

*WISeKey · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** WISeKey and OISTE.ORG announced a post-quantum Root of Trust architecture designed to give AI agents, devices, organizations, and humans cryptographically verifiable identities.

The architecture combines post-quantum cryptography with public-key infrastructure and hardware-backed security to establish identity, authorization, provenance, and accountability for autonomous systems. WISeKey specifically identifies financial services among the sectors where autonomous AI actions could have direct financial consequences.

**Why it matters:** As financial institutions move toward agentic payments and autonomous transaction workflows, quantum-safe identity and authorization could become part of the security layer beneath those systems—not merely a future cryptography upgrade.

### 2. 🤖 **Applied Quantum Software brings finance-native FabriQ platform into IBM Quantum Network**

*Applied Quantum Software · September 25, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Applied Quantum Software announced that its AQS FabriQ platform is being brought into the IBM Quantum Network, giving finance teams access to IBM quantum computing resources for testing financial workloads.

FabriQ is designed around finance-specific use cases including portfolio optimization, risk analysis, and asset pricing, with quantum and quantum-inspired algorithms benchmarked against classical methods. AQS says the platform can use IBM Heron and Nighthawk quantum processing units through the cloud alongside Qiskit tooling.

**Why it matters:** The development illustrates the emerging commercialization path for quantum finance: financial institutions can experiment with real quantum hardware while retaining classical benchmarking rather than treating quantum advantage as an assumption.
