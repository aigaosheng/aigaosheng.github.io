# AI Fintech Brief: Multi-Agent AI Scales Across Banks, Insurers

**Today:** New research and a live bank deployment both show the same gap — financial institutions are racing to pilot multi-agent AI, but few have turned it into enterprise-wide, measurable value.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Capgemini: 59% of Banks and Insurers Piloting Multi-Agent AI, Few at Scale**

*Capgemini · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Most banks and insurers are now running multi-agent AI in production somewhere in the business, but only a small fraction have the orchestration to run it enterprise-wide.

Capgemini Research Institute's World Cloud Report for Financial Services 2027, based on a survey of 1,100 financial-services executives and 40 executive interviews, found 59% of institutions are piloting or deploying multi-agent AI, yet only around one in ten have achieved full enterprise orchestration and just 5% have institutionalised the capabilities to run agentic AI at scale. The report found customer service is the leading use case (75% of banks), followed by fraud detection and loan processing, while 52% of banks and insurers say they prefer hyperscalers to govern multi-agent deployments and roughly half cannot yet consistently demonstrate ROI on agentic AI investments.

**Why it matters:** The findings put a number on a pattern executives have sensed anecdotally — agentic AI adoption is outpacing governance and measurement. For banks and insurers across Asia weighing similar investments, the report is a reminder that scaling beyond pilots requires tech-debt reduction and clear value-tracking frameworks before cost, not after.

### 2. 🤖 **NH NongHyup Bank Deploys 10 AI Agents for Loan and Project Finance Work**

*Seoul Economic Daily · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea's NH NongHyup Bank has rolled out a suite of AI agents that cut a five-day project finance report down to about four hours.

The bank, working with AI subsidiary AgileSoDA, unveiled 10 agents that draft project finance reports, cross-check loan documents against collateral and income data, enter fax-received transaction records into internal systems, and recommend credit cards to individual customers. NH NongHyup plans demonstrations and front-line staff feedback sessions through November 6 before finalising the models for deployment, as part of a broader push to become a fully "Agentic AI Bank" by 2027.

**Why it matters:** The deployment is a concrete data point on agentic AI's labour-saving potential in back-office lending work, an area regional and emerging-market banks are watching closely as a lower-risk entry point than customer-facing AI. It also illustrates the "narrow pilot, not enterprise-wide" pattern Capgemini's data describes above.

## Key Takeaways

- Survey data and a live deployment tell the same story: multi-agent AI is moving from experimentation to production in narrow workflows, but enterprise-wide orchestration remains rare.
- Back-office lending and document-heavy processes (project finance, loan screening) are emerging as a practical, lower-risk starting point for bank AI agents, ahead of customer-facing rollouts.
- ROI measurement is lagging deployment — roughly half of financial institutions surveyed by Capgemini cannot yet consistently demonstrate returns on their agentic AI spend, a gap boards and regulators are likely to press on.

## What to Watch

Watch whether NH NongHyup's agent demonstrations and staff feedback sessions, running through November 6, lead to a firm production deployment date, and whether other regional banks disclose similar back-office agent rollouts or ROI figures in the weeks ahead.
