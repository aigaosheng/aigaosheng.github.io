# Payments Brief: DBS PayLah Gains Alipay and Weixin Pay Access

**Today:** Singapore's largest bank deepens its China payments reach just as stablecoin infrastructure consolidates across Asia, giving payments leaders a clear read on where cross-border rails are heading next.

## Top Stories

### 1. 💳 **DBS PayLah Adds Alipay and Weixin Pay for China Travel**

*Fintech Singapore · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DBS is now the first and only Singapore bank to support all three major Chinese QR payment ecosystems, giving PayLah users fee-free access to more than 100 million merchants in mainland China.

DBS struck partnerships with Ant International and TenPay Global so PayLah's more than three million users can scan merchant QR codes, or present their own app-generated code, to pay across China without extra transaction fees. The service adds Alipay and Weixin Pay on top of DBS's existing UnionPay QR support, and the bank is running cashback promotions from November 16, 2026 through January 31, 2027 to drive adoption.

**Why it matters:** As outbound travel from Singapore to China rebuilds, banks that remove friction from cross-border QR payments capture a larger share of travel spend and remittance flows; rivals in the region will face pressure to match DBS's three-network coverage.

### 2. 📊 **Gemini Sells Europe Business to Singapore's KAST**

*CoinDesk · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Gemini offloaded its UK and European operations to Singapore-based stablecoin payments firm KAST for under $10 million, continuing its retreat from international markets.

The deal follows Gemini's earlier decision this year to exit the UK, EU and Australian markets and cut roughly a quarter of its global workforce as it refocuses on the US and Singapore. KAST, founded by a former Circle executive and backed by an $80 million Series A round in March 2026, offers stablecoin accounts, cross-border transfers and payment cards; the acquisition still requires regulatory sign-off from the UK's FCA and Malta's regulator before KAST can take over Gemini's MiCA licence.

**Why it matters:** The transaction shows smaller, licence-driven M&A becoming the preferred route into regulated stablecoin markets, and reinforces Singapore's position as a base for firms building stablecoin payment infrastructure for Western markets.

### 3. 💳 **Visa and Octopus Expand QR Acceptance in Hong Kong**

*PR Newswire · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa and Octopus Cards are letting Asia-Pacific tourists pay at Octopus QR merchants in Hong Kong using their home wallet apps, without cash or extra local apps.

The first phase connects wallets such as Hyundai Card, Woori Card, Samsung Card and VNPT Money, with LINE Pay and Shinhan Card to follow; small merchants using the Octopus App for Business can accept these cross-border payments without new hardware. Visa and Octopus plan a second phase in 2027 that would let Octopus wallet users scan-to-pay across the wider Asia-Pacific region.

**Why it matters:** Interoperable QR acceptance lowers the cost of accepting foreign visitors' home payment methods, a model other Asian tourism hubs will likely look to replicate as regional travel volumes grow.

### 4. 🏦 **South Korea Weighs Bank-Led Won Stablecoin Rules**

*crypto.news · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea's Financial Services Commission is preparing legislation that would require banks to hold a majority stake in consortiums issuing won-denominated stablecoins.

Under the proposed bank-led model, banks would control issuance while fintechs and exchanges handle blockchain operations, distribution and merchant settlement; the FSC is expected to detail the provisions this month. Fintech firms, including VIDEX, are pushing back, arguing that eligibility should rest on financial safeguards and risk management rather than ownership structure, while regulators separately review whether a pending Dunamu-Naver Financial merger could concentrate stablecoin issuance, trading and payments.

**Why it matters:** A bank-controlled issuance model would reshape which firms can bring won stablecoins to market, offering a regulatory template other Asian jurisdictions may watch as they weigh bank versus fintech control of stablecoin rails.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://crypto.news/south-korea-bank-led-won-stablecoin-rules/)

### 5. 🌐 **Asia Cements Role as Stablecoin Payments Hub**

*PR Newswire (Mercuryo) · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Stablecoins made up 56.2% of all crypto purchases across Asia-Pacific, with the region now accounting for roughly 60% of global stablecoin payment volume, according to new Mercuryo data citing McKinsey figures.

Taiwan leads regional adoption at 89.5%, followed by Hong Kong and Malaysia, and about two-thirds of APAC stablecoin transactions use local currencies rather than US dollars. Cross-border activity in Central and Southeast Asia runs more than three times domestic use, and stablecoins are estimated to account for 5-10% of inbound remittances in the Philippines.

**Why it matters:** The data confirms stablecoins are shifting from a trading instrument to core remittance and payments infrastructure across Asia, a trend that should inform how regional banks and PSPs prioritise stablecoin integration.

### 6. 🤖 **Imprint Raises $130M to Bring AI Agents to Card Rewards**

*Business Wire · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Co-branded card platform Imprint raised $130 million at a roughly $1.9 billion valuation, led by Khosla Ventures, to extend its cards and loyalty infrastructure into AI agent-initiated purchases.

Imprint, which powers co-branded cards for brands including Shell, Kroger and Booking.com, has built a Model Context Protocol server letting AI agents access cardholders' rewards, virtual cards and transaction history, with members able to review and authorise agent-initiated purchases. The round also funds Imprint Access, a deposit-backed credit product for customers new to credit.

**Why it matters:** As shopping increasingly starts inside AI assistants, card issuers that fail to make their rewards visible to agents risk being bypassed at the point of purchase, making this a template other issuers will need to follow.

### 7. 📊 **UK's Griffin Raises $20M to Expand Stablecoin Banking**

*CoinDesk · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** UK-based banking platform Griffin raised $20 million in a Series B round led by Octagon Partners, valuing the company at more than $100 million, to fund stablecoin reserve services and blockchain-based cross-border payments.

Griffin, which serves more than 300,000 underlying retail and business customers and processes nearly 10 million payments a year, will use the funding to let fintechs access traditional banking and blockchain payment rails through a single provider.

**Why it matters:** The round is further evidence that regulated banks, not just crypto-native firms, are building the reserve and settlement infrastructure stablecoin payments will depend on, a dynamic Asian banks weighing similar moves should track.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/12/uk-bank-griffin-raises-usd20-million-to-expand-into-stablecoin-payments)

## Key Takeaways

- Singapore remains a focal point for payments infrastructure, hosting both DBS's expanded China QR reach and KAST's acquisition of Gemini's European stablecoin licences.
- Regulators across Asia are converging on different models for stablecoin control: South Korea is leaning bank-led, while Singapore-based firms like KAST continue to acquire licences in Western markets.
- Card and wallet networks (DBS, Visa, Imprint) are racing to make cross-border and agent-initiated payments frictionless, signalling that interoperability is now a competitive necessity rather than a differentiator.

## What to Watch

Watch for the Financial Services Commission's detailed won stablecoin provisions, due later this month, and for regulatory decisions from the UK FCA and Malta on the Gemini-KAST licence transfer.
