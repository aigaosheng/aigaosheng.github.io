# AI Governance Brief: Philippines DOJ Seeks AI Evidence Rules

**Today:** A Philippine push for clearer AI-evidence standards is a reminder that courts and prosecutors across Southeast Asia are still catching up to AI's role in fraud, cybercrime and financial-crime cases — a gap that matters directly to fintechs relying on AI-flagged signals for KYC and fraud decisions.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Philippine DOJ Calls for Clearer Rules on AI-Generated Evidence**

*Daily Tribune · November 1, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Philippines' Department of Justice says the law needs clearer standards for assessing the authenticity and admissibility of AI-generated and AI-assisted evidence in criminal cases.

DOJ Office of Cybercrime Director Aris Manguera raised the concerns at the 3rd INTERPOL Asia and South Pacific Working Group Meeting on Cybercrime, held in Hanoi, Vietnam from October 27–30 and attended by roughly 70 participants from 22 countries. Manguera said doubts over the reliability of AI-related evidence could undermine prosecutions, and called for closer coordination between law enforcement, prosecutors and the private sector so digital evidence can withstand legal scrutiny. The meeting's broader agenda also covered ransomware, phishing and cryptocurrency-related crime.

**Why it matters:** For fintechs and banks operating in the Philippines and wider Southeast Asia, this signals that AI-assisted fraud detection and transaction-monitoring outputs may face tighter evidentiary scrutiny if cases reach prosecution. Firms should expect regulators and prosecutors across the region to start asking harder questions about how AI-flagged evidence is documented, validated and preserved.

## Key Takeaways

- Legal and evidentiary standards for AI-generated material are lagging behind AI adoption in fraud and cybercrime investigations across Southeast Asia.
- Regional law enforcement coordination (via INTERPOL working groups) is becoming a venue where AI-evidence standards are first raised, ahead of formal legislation.
- Fintechs and payment providers should treat AI-assisted fraud and KYC decisioning as a future evidentiary exposure point, not just an operational tool.

## What to Watch

Watch for whether the Philippine DOJ follows up with formal guidance or a department circular on AI-evidence admissibility, and whether other ASEAN prosecutorial or law-enforcement bodies raise similar standards following the Hanoi INTERPOL meeting.
