# Quantum Brief: IBM, NVIDIA and Fermilab Plan Quantum Co-Design Platform

**Today:** Quantum-classical integration is maturing at SC26, and Asian payments players are being told to act on quantum-safe migration now rather than wait for complete cryptographic inventories.

## Top Stories

### 1. ⚛️ **IBM, NVIDIA and Fermilab Plan Modular Quantum-Classical Co-Design Platform**

*PR Newswire · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Three heavyweights are building a shared testbed to work out how multiple quantum processors and GPU supercomputers should operate together, a step towards modular, error-corrected machines.

Announced at SC26 in Chicago, the platform will put NVIDIA DGX B200 systems alongside IBM quantum computers, including the Nighthawk processor, at Fermilab. Fermilab's SQMS Center will use it to study error-correction codes, decoding and physics workloads. NVIDIA also released a decoding algorithm in CUDA-Q QEC 0.9.0 that it says delivers up to a 13,000x speedup. The partners also reported a joint IBM, RIKEN and NVIDIA chemistry result and new Slurm support so that quantum processors can be scheduled in standard HPC jobs.

**Why it matters:** Vendors increasingly expect quantum value to arrive through hybrid HPC stacks rather than standalone machines. Banks and data-centre operators planning for quantum workloads should track scheduler and decoder standards as closely as qubit counts. RIKEN's role also gives Japan an early seat in this ecosystem.

### 2. 🔒 **EPAA Report Urges Asian Payments Firms to Start Quantum-Safe Migration Now**

*The Paypers · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Emerging Payments Association Asia argues that payments firms should begin quantum-safe work with one accountable executive and one payment flow, rather than wait for a full cryptographic inventory.

The report comes from EPAA's Quantum Safe Cryptography Working Group. It recommends assessing a single payment flow end to end, from initiation through clearing and settlement, and sets out five actions for the next 90 days. These include asking critical vendors for their post-quantum plans and reviewing contracts that are close to renewal. It cites the Monetary Authority of Singapore's July 2026 commitment to issue formal supervisory expectations, and a Hong Kong Monetary Authority readiness index that scored local banks 2.3 out of 10.

**Why it matters:** Coming just before the Singapore FinTech Festival, the report turns regulatory signals from MAS and the HKMA into a practical first-quarter plan. For payments firms, the most urgent exposure sits in vendor contracts now up for renewal.

### 3. 🔒 **NEC and IBM Japan Demonstrate Combined QKD and Post-Quantum TLS, Eyeing Banks**

*The Quantum Insider · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** NEC and IBM Japan say they have completed Japan's first demonstration combining quantum key distribution with post-quantum cryptography, and they name banking as a target deployment.

NEC's QKD equipment fed keys to a custom IBM Quantum Safe Proxy over an ETSI-standard interface. TLS session keys combined the QKD material with ML-KEM-768, and ML-DSA-44 handled certificate authentication. Validation ran from July to September 2026. The companies say existing applications did not need to change because protection was applied at the proxy layer.

**Why it matters:** A hybrid design means a weakness in one method does not expose the session. The proxy approach also addresses a major obstacle for banks, the cost of re-engineering legacy applications. The partners plan to explore links between financial institutions and data centres next.

### 4. 🔒 **IDEMIA and IonQ Validate QKD Interoperability With Enterprise Security Platform**

*The Quantum Insider · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IonQ's quantum key distribution systems now work with IDEMIA Secure Transactions' IDEMIA Sphere platform, so quantum-generated keys can be managed within existing enterprise security controls.

The validated architecture combines QKD, post-quantum cryptography and hardware security modules. QKD is positioned as an added layer for selected high-value communications, not a replacement for existing protections. The companies say compliance processes and governance controls remain intact, but they disclosed no performance figures or commercial terms.

**Why it matters:** IDEMIA is a major supplier of payment cards and secure transaction technology, so this points to quantum key management entering the card and payments security supply chain. Buyers should still ask for performance data before treating QKD as production-ready.

### 5. 🏦 **US Department of Energy Seeks Input on Cryogenic and Quantum-HPC Testbeds**

*The Quantum Insider · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** DOE's Advanced Scientific Computing Research programme has issued a request for information on two testbeds, one for cryogenic technologies and one for integrating quantum computers with high-performance computing.

Responses are due December 17, 2026. This is a request for information, not a procurement, so DOE has not committed to funding or a contract competition. The cryogenic scope covers helium-4 cooling and dilution refrigeration, scaled to support a few larger experiments and dozens of smaller ones.

**Why it matters:** Cooling infrastructure and integration with classical systems are emerging as bottlenecks for scaling. The RFI signals where US public money may go next, which matters for Asian cryogenics and control-electronics suppliers chasing national-lab demand.

### 6. 🔬 **Saltgrain and NTT Research Unveil Large-Universe Post-Quantum Attribute-Based Encryption**

*The Quantum Insider · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Researchers from Saltgrain, NTT Research and Carnegie Mellon University have built lattice-based schemes that apply post-quantum protection to fine-grained, policy-driven data access.

The work covers key-policy and ciphertext-policy schemes plus a multi-authority version, built on LWE and Ring-LWE. On an Apple M4 Pro laptop, key-policy decryption took roughly 5.6 to 22.5 milliseconds for one to four attributes. These are research benchmarks, not product figures. The paper is slated for ACM CCS 2026, and Saltgrain has started integration testing without giving an availability date.

**Why it matters:** Attribute-based encryption suits regulated data sharing between banks, insurers and their partners. A practical post-quantum version would extend migration beyond key exchange and signatures into access control.

### 7. 💳 **Falcon Systems Launches Falcon 5000 Series MDI-QKD Hardware**

*The Quantum Insider · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Dutch firm Falcon Systems has launched the next generation of its measurement-device-independent QKD portfolio, adding resilience features aimed at operational networks.

The series includes a connectivity hub, a QKD node and a quantum optical switch. New features include dual-homing, links between hubs, tamper detection, and redundant hot-swappable power and cooling. Forschungszentrum Jülich is the launch customer and will study QKD in power-system communication networks. Pricing and availability were not disclosed.

**Why it matters:** Redundancy and tamper detection are the features critical-infrastructure operators require before deployment. They show QKD vendors moving from lab pilots towards carrier-grade equipment.

### 8. 📊 **Paris Photonic Startup Qluster Raises €4.7 Million Pre-Seed Round**

*Tech.eu · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Qluster, a spin-off from Laboratoire Kastler Brossel, has raised €4.7 million led by Quantonation, with Quantum Launchpad and Bpifrance also supporting the round.

The startup is pursuing continuous-variable photonic quantum computing. It targets an intermediate-scale machine by 2032 and a large-scale fault-tolerant universal computer by 2036. Qluster also won the 2026 i-Lab Grand Prize, supported by France 2030, and plans to build its own R&D lab.

**Why it matters:** Early-stage capital is still flowing to alternative qubit approaches with long timelines, even as listed quantum stocks remain volatile. French state backing continues to anchor Europe's photonics pipeline.

### 9. 🤖 **BlueQubit Names Eight Teams, Including SUTD, for AI-for-Quantum Grants**

*The Quantum Insider · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** BlueQubit has picked eight research teams from more than 160 proposals in 29 countries for its $150,000 Quantum Flywheel programme, which is backed by IBM, AWS and NVIDIA.

Awards of $10,000 to $40,000 per proposal are paid in AI and compute credits. Projects cover AI for quantum advantage, AI for quantum error correction and adversarial classical simulation. Recipients include teams from the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics, ETH Zurich and Caltech, and Davit Aghamalyan of the Singapore University of Technology and Design.

**Why it matters:** Machine learning is becoming a standard tool for error correction and benchmarking. The SUTD selection shows Singapore researchers competing for global grant funding in this area.

## Key Takeaways

- Hybrid quantum-HPC integration is where vendors are competing now, through shared schedulers, GPU decoders and co-design testbeds.
- Quantum-safe security is moving from guidance to deployment patterns. Hybrid QKD-plus-PQC designs from NEC, IBM Japan, IDEMIA and IonQ explicitly target banking and transaction security.
- In Asian payments, the practical first step is governance and vendor contracts, backed by expected MAS supervisory expectations and HKMA readiness scoring.

## What to Watch

Watch for further MAS quantum-safe supervisory detail and payments-sector reactions around the Singapore FinTech Festival, and for responses to the DOE testbed RFI, which close on December 17, 2026.
