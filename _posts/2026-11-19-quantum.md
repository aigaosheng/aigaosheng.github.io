# **Quantum Brief: BMO Demonstrates Quantum Utility in Banking ML**

**Today:** BMO's reported quantum machine-learning result brings the technology closer to a concrete banking use case, while Singapore's financial sector is beginning structured quantum-readiness work.

## **Top Stories**

### **1. ⚛️ BMO Reports Quantum Utility in Banking Machine Learning**

*BMO · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** BMO says it has demonstrated a quantum-computing approach to machine-learning feature selection that achieved comparable result quality with substantially fewer samples.

The work was developed with Democritus University of Thrace using IBM quantum computers, including the Nighthawk r2 processor, and was reviewed by IBM's quantum research team. The method adapts the Proxy Quantum Games framework, treating dataset features as participants in a strategic game to rank their usefulness. BMO reports that it required one to two orders of magnitude fewer samples than conventional feature-selection techniques.

**Why it matters:** Feature selection affects model development costs, data requirements and predictive performance. If independently replicated and shown to scale on commercially relevant datasets, this approach could become relevant to banking applications such as client modelling, risk assessment and fraud detection. The announcement is a reported technical milestone, not evidence of a production banking deployment or a general quantum advantage across financial workloads.

### **2. ⚛️ OCBC Joins Singapore Quantum Finance Testbed**

*Global Finance & Technology Network · November 19, 2026*

**Bottom line:** OCBC is joining a Singapore-led initiative to test quantum computing applications in banking while preparing for the future transition to quantum-safe cryptography.

The Q-FINEX testbed brings together the Global Finance & Technology Network (GFTN), ST Engineering and researchers from the University of Western Australia. OCBC will explore use cases in credit and counterparty risk and optimisation, with researchers developing quantum and hybrid algorithms for comparison against conventional methods. ST Engineering will use its Quantizant platform to assess the bank's exposure to quantum-related cryptographic risks and develop a prioritised transition roadmap.

**Why it matters:** This connects quantum research to two practical banking priorities: finding workloads where quantum methods may eventually add value, and reducing exposure to future cryptographic threats. For Singapore's financial institutions, the testbed offers a route to evaluate both opportunities and migration requirements before the technology matures.

## **Key Takeaways**

- **Financial services is becoming a practical proving ground:** BMO's feature-selection experiment and OCBC's Q-FINEX participation show banks moving beyond general interest towards defined quantum applications.
- **Evidence standards remain important:** BMO's result is a company-reported demonstration. Independent replication, broader benchmarks and operational economics will determine its commercial significance.
- **Quantum readiness has two tracks:** Institutions need to assess potential quantum-computing applications while planning migration to post-quantum cryptography.

## **What to Watch**

Watch for independent technical validation and more detailed benchmarks of BMO's feature-selection method, particularly on larger datasets and practical banking workloads. In Singapore, the next meaningful Q-FINEX milestone will be evidence from OCBC's risk and optimisation experiments and progress on its post-quantum cryptography roadmap.

## **MY TAKE**

Quantum computing is not yet a general-purpose replacement for classical computing. The near-term enterprise opportunity is disciplined experimentation: identify narrowly defined workloads, compare quantum and classical approaches under reproducible conditions, and build cryptographic migration plans early. Banks that separate measurable technical progress from long-term promises will be better positioned to invest at the right time.
