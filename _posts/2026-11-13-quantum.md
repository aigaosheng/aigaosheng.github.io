# Quantum Brief: Q-CTRL Raises $290M, Hits Unicorn Status

**Today:** Two deals show where quantum money is actually flowing right now — into control software that makes today's noisy machines usable, and into the classical silicon that will run alongside them, both signals for enterprises timing their own quantum pilots.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Q-CTRL Raises $290M Series C, Hits Unicorn Status**

*The Quantum Insider · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Q-CTRL's $290 million round, the largest quantum-software raise to date, shows investors betting on control software — not just hardware — as the nearer-term commercial bottleneck in quantum computing.

Salesforce Ventures led the oversubscribed Series C, which had to be expanded twice to meet demand, pushing Q-CTRL's valuation above $1 billion. New backers include AMD Ventures, UAE's EDGE Group, Australia's National Reconstruction Fund Corporation and Japan's SPARX Asset Management, joining returning investors Lockheed Martin Ventures and In-Q-Tel. The Sydney-based firm builds AI-powered software that stabilises quantum hardware and a GPS-free quantum navigation product already used by customers including Lockheed Martin, Airbus and IBM.

**Why it matters:** The investor list shows quantum capital broadening well beyond the US — Gulf, Australian and Japanese institutional money are now backing quantum-adjacent software — and signals that finance, aerospace and defence buyers are paying for quantum tools years before fault-tolerant hardware arrives, a trend worth watching for Asian sovereign and strategic funds weighing deep-tech allocations.

### 2. ⚛️ **AMD Takes Equity Stake In PsiQuantum To Build Quantum Infrastructure**

*The Quantum Insider · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** AMD's planned equity investment in PsiQuantum links a major classical-chip maker's roadmap directly to photonic quantum computing, pushing hybrid quantum-AI infrastructure further into the commercial mainstream.

The expanded partnership pairs PsiQuantum's silicon-photonics quantum platform with AMD's Versal adaptive SoCs, Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs and ROCm software stack, aimed at utility-scale, fault-tolerant quantum systems. Initial applications target pharmaceutical research, building on GPU-based simulations of cancer-relevant molecules and a machine-learning decoder for real-time error correction; further details are due at the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago (November 15–20).

**Why it matters:** Pairing a mainstream chipmaker with a photonic quantum pure-play lowers the integration risk enterprises cite when evaluating quantum pilots, and it reinforces drug discovery and materials science as the leading commercial wedge for hybrid quantum-classical computing — relevant to Asia's growing biotech and advanced-manufacturing sectors.

## Key Takeaways

- Quantum capital is globalising: Gulf, Australian and Japanese investors are now funding quantum control software and navigation technology alongside US venture and defence-linked money.
- Classical chipmakers are moving from quantum-agnostic to quantum-invested, with AMD following the industry pattern of betting equity on specific quantum hardware architectures.
- Drug discovery and materials science remain the clearest near-term commercial wedge for hybrid quantum-classical systems, ahead of broader fault-tolerant applications.

## What to Watch

AMD and PsiQuantum are expected to disclose further partnership and investment details at SC26 in Chicago (November 15–20). Singapore's MAS consultation on quantum-safe migration timelines for financial institutions closes for feedback on November 23, which will shape how regional banks plan their post-quantum roadmaps.
