# AI Governance Brief: Texas Sues OpenAI and Altman Over ChatGPT Safety

**Today:** Texas opens a new state-level front against OpenAI, while the UK, India and the Pentagon each add their own levers for overseeing AI.

## Top Stories
### 1. 🏦 **Texas Sues OpenAI and Sam Altman Over ChatGPT Deception and Child Safety**

*Bloomberg Law · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Texas has taken OpenAI and its CEO to court over alleged deceptive marketing and failures to protect children who use ChatGPT.
Attorney General Ken Paxton filed the suit on Friday against OpenAI Global and Sam Altman. It alleges the company misled users about ChatGPT and also violated a state law that requires digital service providers to build in certain child-safety features. Paxton's statement said OpenAI has a responsibility to protect users and warn them of the platform's dangers.

**Why it matters:** With no comprehensive federal AI statute, US state enforcers are using consumer-protection and child-safety laws as de facto AI rules. This is an analyst's reading, but any company marketing AI safety claims to US consumers, including Asia-based firms, should expect those claims to be tested against the same theories. ### 2. 🔒 **UK Sets Out About £600 Million for AI Security, Including a Biosecurity Lab**

*Reuters via The Star · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The UK government is putting most of a roughly £600 million package into defending against health and security threats from AI.
The largest share goes to a new biosecurity initiative, including £100 million for an AI Biosecurity Lab and £65 million for pathogen countermeasures. OpenAI and Anthropic will offer up to £200 million of model credits to that effort. The existing AI Security Institute receives an extra £120 million, and £130 million is earmarked for cyber-defense projects covering critical infrastructure and the wider economy.

**Why it matters:** The UK is building state capacity to test and defend against advanced AI, and tying frontier labs into public programs through compute credits. Regulators in Asia that are weighing their own testing bodies now have a funded benchmark to compare against.

### 3. 🏦 **India Plans an AI Consultation Paper Stressing Human Control**

*The420.in · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's IT minister says a public consultation paper on AI governance is coming in the next few weeks, but no draft AI law has been announced.
Speaking at the NDTV World Summit on Friday, Ashwini Vaishnaw said the government will seek views from citizens, businesses, industry and experts. He said people must keep control over AI models, agents and their outcomes, and called for ways to stop AI systems that operate beyond human control. He named banking, insurance and financial services among the fast-adopting sectors, and did not say whether the exercise will lead to legislation.

**Why it matters:** Fintechs and banks with Indian operations get an early chance to shape the framework. The human-control and stop-mechanism language also points toward expectations for agentic AI in regulated services.

### 4. 🤖 **Pentagon Issues Templates for Buying LLMs Under "Unbiased AI" Rules**

*DefenseScoop · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Pentagon has given acquisition teams standard language to require AI vendors to document compliance with the administration's "truth-seeking" and "ideological neutrality" principles.
An October 20 memo from John Tenaglia, principal director for Defense Pricing, Contracting, and Acquisition Policy, says compliance with the White House directives is mandatory. It attaches three templates: a transparency-requirements selection memo, solicitation language telling vendors what documentation to provide, and contract language for statements of work. When buying a large language model, the Pentagon must obtain enough information from the contractor to judge compliance. The memo notes that how much is available depends on the contractor's place in the software supply chain.

**Why it matters:** Procurement is becoming the main US lever for AI requirements. LLM vendors and integrators serving US government customers should expect documentation duties to flow down the supply chain, which also affects partners outside the US.

### 5. 🏦 **House Members Introduce Bills on AI Model Distillation and AI-Generated Election Content**

*Nextgov/FCW · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Two new House proposals target the copying of US AI models and the use of AI in elections, though both are early-stage.
Reps. Pat Harrigan and George Whitesides would direct NIST to develop voluntary industry standards against distillation attacks, which copy a model by flooding it with prompts. Their bill would also create an AI Cybersecurity Roundtable and names Russia, China, Iran and North Korea as actors of concern. Separately, Rep. Shontel Brown's bill would bar using AI to defraud people of the right to vote and require a disclaimer on political ads that use AI content. Rep. Yvette Clarke reintroduced a similar ad-disclaimer bill last week.

**Why it matters:** Protecting model weights and outputs is emerging as a policy topic in its own right. Because the proposed standards are voluntary, the near-term effect is more likely to show up in vendor and API terms than in binding law. That is a view, not a reported outcome.

### 6. 🏦 **SEBI Chair Says Regulator Is Exploring an AI Sandbox**

*ANI via Asianet Newsable · October 24, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's securities regulator is exploring an AI sandbox for testing new tools, with no details or timeline announced.
At a Mumbai convention on Saturday, SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said innovators would gain regulatory certainty while the regulator gains safeguards. He listed responsible technology as one of four roadmap pillars, alongside simpler rules, deeper markets and wider access. He also said SEBI is exploring graded compliance for stockbrokers based on scale, client exposure and reliance on technology, and urged firms to treat cybersecurity as a business priority.

**Why it matters:** For AI vendors and fintechs serving Indian capital markets, a sandbox could become an entry route, but it is still only under exploration. The remarks fit a regional pattern of testing AI under supervision before writing binding rules.

## Key Takeaways

- Enforcement is arriving through existing law first: Texas relies on deceptive-marketing and child-safety statutes rather than a new AI statute.
- Governments are building oversight capacity and procurement levers, as seen in the UK's funding package and the Pentagon's vendor templates.
- In Asia, India and SEBI are consulting and sandboxing before drafting binding rules, with human control a recurring theme.
## What to Watch
India's AI consultation paper, which the minister says is due in the coming weeks, and whether other US state attorneys general follow Texas against OpenAI.
