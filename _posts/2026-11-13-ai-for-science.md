# AI for Science Brief: Orakl Oncology Raises $34M For AI Cancer Trials

**Today:** Fresh capital and deal flow for AI-driven drug discovery landed alongside sober new research on where agentic AI still needs human guardrails in labs and genomics, a timely read for enterprises weighing how fast to hand science over to autonomous systems.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Orakl Oncology Raises $34M To Scale AI Cancer Drug Prediction**

*EuropaWire · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Gustave Roussy spin-out just raised fresh capital to prove that AI-modelled "virtual patients" can predict cancer drug trial outcomes before human trials begin.

Orakl Oncology, a TechBio company founded in 2023, closed a $34 million Series A co-led by Norrsken VC and Singular, with InfraVia Growth, Bpifrance's Patient Autonome Fund and others participating, bringing total funding to $50 million. The company links AI models to laboratory-grown patient tumours and clinical histories, and says its platform reaches 90% accuracy forecasting cancer clinical trial outcomes — a figure reported by the company and not yet independently verified. Funds will expand coverage to new cancer types and pharma partnerships.

**Why it matters:** As drug development costs climb, pharma and investors are betting that AI-driven trial-outcome prediction can cut late-stage failure rates; Asian biotech hubs eyeing similar TechBio models should watch whether Orakl's accuracy claims hold up under independent scrutiny before treating them as benchmarks.

### 2. 🔬 **Hansoh Pharmaceutical Taps AilsynBio's AI To Hunt Oral Peptide Drugs**

*TipRanks (company filing) · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's Hansoh Pharmaceutical signed a collaboration giving it rights to AI-discovered oral cyclic peptide drug candidates from Hong Kong-incubated AilsynBio.

Under the deal, a wholly owned Hansoh subsidiary can select drug targets and acquire the intellectual property for resulting programmes, while AilsynBio — whose platform applies AI to cyclic peptide design — is eligible for payments of up to RMB317 million plus single-digit royalties on any approved products. Hansoh will lead subsequent development of selected candidates.

**Why it matters:** The deal adds to a wave of 2026 pharma-AI licensing agreements and signals Chinese biotechs are increasingly packaging AI drug-design platforms for large domestic pharma partners rather than only Western biotechs; it's a data point for Singapore and regional players assessing how AI drug-discovery deal structures are evolving in Asia.

### 3. 🤖 **Study Finds Chemists Want AI Lab Robots — But Not Full Autonomy**

*Frontiers in Robotics and AI · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A peer-reviewed study of practising chemists finds they welcome robotic and AI assistance to reclaim lab time, but reject fully autonomous labs over fears of skill loss and missed serendipitous discoveries.

Researchers interviewed nine experimental chemists with 2–30 years' experience across institutions in the UK, US and Germany, finding robots could free up 20–25 hours a week currently spent on repetitive sample prep and screening. Chemists said they want modular, reconfigurable systems, visual dashboards for transparency, tiered safety responses, and clear human-in-the-loop checkpoints rather than hands-off automation.

**Why it matters:** For enterprises building "self-driving lab" or agentic R&D platforms, the findings are a concrete design brief: adoption depends on preserving scientist oversight and interpretability, not maximising autonomy — a lesson that extends to any regulated environment deploying agentic AI.

### 4. 🔬 **Researchers Set Out Guardrails For Agentic AI In Genomics**

*Frontiers in Genetics · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new perspective paper warns that autonomous, multi-agent AI systems in genomics research create governance gaps that today's model-oversight practices don't cover.

Authors Qu H-Q and Hakonarson H argue agentic systems can alter their own analytical workflows mid-run, autonomously combine datasets with incompatible consent restrictions, and obscure accountability when multiple AI agents and models interact. They recommend bounding agent permissions in advance, enforcing machine-readable consent standards, logging every tool call and agent exchange, and requiring human sign-off at consequential decision points such as external data transmission.

**Why it matters:** The recommendations mirror concerns enterprise AI governance teams are already raising about agentic systems in regulated industries generally; research institutions and biotech firms deploying agentic AI on sensitive genomic or health data should treat this as an early checklist, pending any formal regulatory guidance.

## Key Takeaways

- Capital is still flowing into AI-native drug discovery (Orakl, Hansoh-AilsynBio), but both deals rest on company-reported accuracy or valuation figures that haven't been independently verified.
- Two independent research teams, working in different domains (wet-lab chemistry and genomics), reached the same conclusion this week: agentic AI needs bounded autonomy and human checkpoints, not full hands-off control.
- Asia's role in AI drug discovery is shifting from licensing Western platforms to domestic AI biotechs (AilsynBio) striking deals directly with large domestic pharma (Hansoh).

## What to Watch

Watch for independent replication or scrutiny of Orakl Oncology's 90%-accuracy trial-prediction claim as it seeks further pharma partnerships, and for whether Hansoh or AilsynBio disclose specific target programmes under their new agreement.
