# Payments Brief: Visa Launches Live AI Agent Invoice Payments Pilot

**Today:** Agent-initiated B2B payments went live at Visa while PayPal told the market to wait until 2028, and Asian card issuers and exchanges kept signing stablecoin pilots with no launch dates.

## Top Stories

### 1. 💳 **Visa Puts AI Agents Into Live B2B Invoice Payments**
*Visa via Business Wire · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa paired a live agent-payments pilot with new fraud controls for virtual cards, aiming to make automated business payments safe enough to scale.

Visa announced three commercial-payments capabilities. The first is SESA 2.0, an AI-driven tool that helps issuers find suppliers likely to accept card payments; Visa says the earlier version has surfaced more than $600 billion in potential cardable spend. The second is Visa Commercial Fraud Solutions, which covers supplier validation, 3-D Secure authentication for virtual cards, and transaction-level controls. Supplier Validation and Virtual Authentication are in pilot and due for general availability in 2027. The third is a live pilot with BILL and Billtrust in which approved AI agents pay invoices through supplier portals. The agents use tokenized credentials instead of shared portal logins, and the pilot is already processing end-to-end transactions.

**Why it matters:** For issuers and fintechs in Asia, the useful signal is the pairing of agent identity (Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol) with supplier validation and spend controls, which is the package B2B buyers will probably ask for before delegating payments to software. General availability for the fraud tools is still a year away.

### 2. 📊 **PayPal CEO Says Meaningful Agentic Commerce Is a 2028 Story**
*Payments Dive · October 20, 2026*

**Bottom line:** PayPal's chief executive told Money20/20 that agent-driven commerce will stay small through 2027, with meaningful volume not expected until 2028.

Enrique Lores said PayPal is investing in infrastructure and partnerships for agentic commerce but does not expect significant sales volume from it for roughly two years. He argued that trust, which PayPal built in early e-commerce, will matter more when agents shop for consumers. His turnaround plan also includes flattening management layers, refocusing on consumer financial services such as buy now, pay later and Venmo, and ongoing job cuts. Payments Dive adds that a Stripe and Advent bid for PayPal was reportedly dropped in August; Lores did not address it.

**Why it matters:** The timeline is a useful counterweight to launch-day announcements, since a large incumbent is budgeting for a slow consumer ramp. Planners should treat consumer-side agent volumes as a medium-term bet and B2B pilots like Visa's as the nearer-term test.

### 3. 🌐 **Shinhan Card and SBI Sign Yen Stablecoin Payments MOU**
*crypto.news · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A major Korean card issuer will test yen-denominated stablecoins for traveler card payments and trade settlement with SBI units in Japan, with no launch date set.

Shinhan Card signed the memorandum in Tokyo on October 20 with SBI VC Trade and SBI XDC Network APAC. The proof-of-concept work covers card payments, wallet-to-wallet transfers, cross-border trade settlement, and anti-money-laundering and fraud controls. SBI has not said which yen stablecoin will be used, and merchants, trial dates and limits are unspecified. The report puts Japan–Korea visitor arrivals at more than 10.1 million in the first eight months of 2026.

**Why it matters:** An established issuer is testing stablecoins at the card layer, where acceptance already exists, which is the same route Visa and Mastercard partners are taking elsewhere. The open questions for Asian issuers are currency conversion, merchant settlement and the compliance model, none of which the partners have disclosed.

### 4. 🌐 **Coinbase Joins Thunes' Network to Reach Wallets in 140 Countries**
*TechTimes · October 20, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Coinbase will connect its stablecoin infrastructure to Thunes' payout network, which includes wallets such as GCash in the Philippines.

The partnership was unveiled at Money20/20 USA on Monday. Coinbase will plug into Thunes' network, which Thunes says covers 140-plus countries and 90-plus currencies. Thunes will draw on Coinbase's stablecoin tooling for settlement. Target uses include remittances, supplier payments and gig-worker payouts. The report describes a model in which USDC settles on Coinbase's Base network and Thunes handles local-currency delivery, reducing the need for pre-funded accounts in each destination market. Financial terms and a country-by-country rollout were not disclosed.

**Why it matters:** Thunes traces its roots to Singapore-based TransferTo, so the deal links a regional payout network with a US stablecoin platform. For payers sending to Southeast Asian wallets, it adds another route to compare against bank and card-based corridors.

### 6. 📊 **Stablecoin Payments Rise 45% While Supply Stalls, Reports Show**
*CoinGeek · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Usage of stablecoins for payments is growing quickly even as total supply barely moves, according to three reports published earlier this month.

This is a roundup of earlier reports. Allium's Q3 report, published October 15, shows payments up 45% to $482 billion through September, while supply grew only 0.4% to $305 billion. The average payment fell from $544 to $462, and stablecoin-linked card spending reached $3.9 billion. Borderless.xyz puts median stablecoin remittance cost near 1%, against a World Bank global average of 6.36% for traditional transfers. These come from different periods, so the comparison is not exact. A BIS working paper from October 13 links higher bank deposit rates to slower stablecoin growth, by roughly three percentage points over ten weeks against an average of about 15%.

**Why it matters:** More frequent, smaller payments are what a payment method looks like once it is in everyday use. The BIS finding suggests banks' deposit pricing will shape adoption pace, a point regulators in Singapore and elsewhere weigh when they decide whether stablecoins may pay yield.

### 7. 💳 **Paymentology CTO: Stablecoins Should Fund Cards, Not Replace Them**
*PYMNTS · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A card-issuing vendor argues that stablecoins will gain ground by sitting behind existing card credentials rather than building new checkout experiences. This is an interview-based opinion from a vendor with a stake in cards.

Paymentology's chief technology officer, Tim Joslyn, says cards already solve merchant acceptance, so stablecoins' stronger role is as an always-on funding source, especially for cross-border use. He cites research that 42% of stablecoin holders want to use them for major purchases while 28% do. He expects a single card to eventually draw on checking, credit and stablecoin balances, chosen by software.

**Why it matters:** The view matches how Visa and Mastercard partners are shipping stablecoin products. For issuers and processors, it frames the competition as who controls the balance closest to the transaction.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/stablecoins-must-get-behind-cards-not-replace-them/)

### 8. 💳 **Mangopay Adds Local Payouts in SGD, HKD and Three Other Currencies**
*The Paypers · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Mangopay now lets platforms pay beneficiaries in Singapore and Hong Kong dollars over domestic rails instead of cross-border transfers.

The new coverage adds Australian, Hong Kong, Singapore and Mexican dollars-equivalents in name only: the five currencies are AUD, HKD, SGD, MXN and TRY. When a platform holds funds in the same currency as the payout, it can pay locally without a foreign exchange conversion. Mangopay says this lowers costs and shortens payout times for marketplaces and similar platforms.

**Why it matters:** Local payouts are a practical alternative to stablecoin routes for marketplace and gig platforms paying sellers in Singapore and Hong Kong. They require holding balances in each currency.

## Key Takeaways

- Agent payments are moving from announcements to live B2B pilots, but PayPal's own guidance suggests consumer volumes will take years.
- Stablecoin activity in Asia is channeling through incumbents, via card issuers, payout networks and exchanges, and most arrangements are still exploratory.
- Usage data shows more, smaller stablecoin payments while supply stalls, so competition is shifting to distribution and bank deposit pricing.

## What to Watch

Visa reports results on October 27, per market coverage, where agentic and stablecoin commentary may follow today's announcements. In Singapore, MAS's stablecoin consultation closed on October 16, so its response is the next regulatory signal.
