# AI Governance Brief: India Readies AI Regulation Consultation Paper

**Today:** If you deploy AI in India, China or the US consumer market, oversight is moving earlier: before deployment, before model release and before a minor logs in.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **India Says AI Regulation Consultation Paper Is Weeks Away**
*MediaNama · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's IT ministry is close to publishing draft AI rules for public consultation, possibly as early as November.

Speaking at the NDTV World Summit 2026, IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said, "We will be issuing a consultation paper in the coming weeks." He had said on October 8 that the paper would come within a month. The ministry has already consulted tech companies and now wants input from civil society, citizens and industry users of AI. It is still unclear whether the rules will come as a new standalone AI law or under the existing IT Act, 2000. Vaishnaw argued that AI risks have now crossed a threshold that justifies regulating harms, while keeping India's pro-innovation stance.

**Why it matters:** Vaishnaw named banks, insurers and financial services as heavy AI adopters whose views the ministry wants. That suggests fintechs operating in India should prepare to comment, not just react, once the draft appears.

### 2. 🏦 **China Requires Employment-Risk Reviews Before Some AI Deployments**
*Tech Times · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing has made labor-market impact a formal checkpoint in AI project approvals, a pre-deployment control with no direct equivalent in the EU or US.

A State Council directive, Guobanfa [2026] No. 29, published October 23, sets out 18 tasks across six areas of AI employment governance. Its key provision requires AI projects in regulated market-access sectors to complete an employment-risk assessment before approval if they could cause significant job disruption. The directive also encourages companies to give workers a transition "buffer period" before large AI rollouts, and it calls for rules against discriminatory or privacy-abusing AI in HR management. Analysts quoted in the piece note that it is a policy document, not a statute: it has no penalties or budget, and ministries and provinces still have to define "significant" disruption.

**Why it matters:** Multinationals automating operations, back-office or customer service work in China should expect workforce impact to become part of project approval. Implementation details at provincial level will decide how heavy the burden is.

### 3. 🏦 **Texas Sues OpenAI, Seeks Court Vetting of New Models**
*TNW · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Texas wants a court-approved independent evaluator to approve any new OpenAI model before it can be released in the state.

Attorney General Ken Paxton filed a 67-page petition in Collin County District Court on October 23. The suit relies on the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act and the SCOPE Act, and it names CEO Sam Altman personally. Texas asks the court to bar ChatGPT for minors in the state, require age verification and parental consent, and impose civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation. Earlier this month, eight other states filed separate suits against OpenAI over harms to children.

**Why it matters:** State consumer-protection law is being used to seek pre-release model gating, a power no US statute currently grants. Enterprises that build on frontier models should track whether courts accept this approach, because it could affect release timing in some markets.

### 4. 🏦 **Florida AG Proposes Age Checks for App Stores, Phones and Chatbots**
*Tech Times · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Florida's attorney general wants age verification built into three layers at once: app stores, operating systems and AI chatbot platforms.

The Safe Screens Act, unveiled by AG James Uthmeier on October 22, would require chatbot operators to determine every account holder's age before giving access. It would also bar features for minors that simulate companionship, offer unsupervised therapy or suggest the bot is a real person. Violations could bring fines of up to $50,000 each. The proposal has no legislative sponsor yet, and Florida's 2027 session does not start until March 2.

**Why it matters:** This is a proposal, not law. Still, it shows age assurance moving from social media to conversational AI. Any consumer-facing AI assistant, including in banking and wallet apps, may eventually need age-gating logic for US users.

## Key Takeaways
- Oversight is moving upstream: China's job-impact review and Texas's demand for court-vetted model releases both put checkpoints before deployment rather than after harm.
- In the US, child safety has become the main route for state AG enforcement against AI providers, and age assurance is spreading to chatbots.
- In Asia, India is shifting from an innovation-only stance toward harm-based rules, and it explicitly wants input from financial services.

## What to Watch
MeitY's AI consultation paper, expected as early as November, is the next signal. Separately, Colorado's attorney general holds its rulemaking hearing on the automated decision-making and chatbot safety rules on October 26 (Mountain Time), ahead of their January 1, 2027 effective date.
