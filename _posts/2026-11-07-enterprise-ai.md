# Enterprise AI Brief: Enterprise AI Spending Growth Stalls Sharply

**Today:** Fresh spending data shows enterprise AI's hypergrowth phase cooling just as Anthropic's IPO valuation gets repriced, a signal worth watching for any fintech weighing how fast to scale its own AI infrastructure bets.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Enterprise AI Token Spend Growth Collapses to 1.5%**

*TechCrunch · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Corporate card and spend-management firm Ramp found enterprise AI token spending grew just 1.5% across August and September combined, down sharply from 42% growth in July, even as usage volumes kept rising.

Ramp's data showed the heaviest-spending firms — the top 1% of corporate AI buyers, averaging roughly $10,000 per employee per month at their summer peak — dipped slightly below that level in August and September. Ramp's lead economist said assumptions about AI spending's trajectory are "starting to break down," pointing to aggressive price cuts from OpenAI and Anthropic and more disciplined buyer behaviour as firms route workloads to cheaper models. Token spend did partially rebound, up 8.9% between September 23 and October 23, but Ramp said the slowdown pressure remains strong. The report landed alongside news that Anthropic's indicated IPO valuation has slid from an earlier $2 trillion estimate to $1.2–1.3 trillion, and that OpenAI closed an initial $11 billion tranche of new funding on November 6 at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below its original $1.4 trillion target.

**Why it matters:** For enterprise buyers across Asia, including fintechs evaluating AI vendor contracts, the data complicates the narrative of unconstrained AI budget growth and strengthens the case for tying spend to measurable ROI rather than token volume alone — a point regulators such as MAS have also pressed banks on when assessing AI investment governance.

### 2. 🤖 **Kong and Hitachi Partner on Japan Enterprise AI Rollout**

*IT Business Today · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** API management vendor Kong signed a strategic partnership with Hitachi to help Japanese manufacturers and financial institutions move AI agent projects from testing into production.

Hitachi will contribute its open-source systems integration and build/management expertise, while Kong supplies API and AI connectivity tools including Kong Gateway, Kong Konnect and Kong AI Gateway. The companies plan joint customer proposals targeting manufacturing, where AI agents would use machinery and sensor data for maintenance and quality control, and financial services, where the focus is API security, access monitoring and audit compliance for core banking systems integrating new AI applications.

**Why it matters:** The deal reflects a broader pattern of global infrastructure vendors pairing with regional systems integrators to move Asian enterprises past pilot-stage AI, a dynamic relevant to fintechs in Singapore, the Philippines and Indonesia weighing similar integration partnerships for agentic AI in regulated environments.

### 3. 📊 **Australia's Future Secure AI Hits $1.2B Valuation**

*Startup Daily · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Sydney-founded Future Secure AI raised US$80 million in convertible notes at a US$1.2 billion valuation, becoming Australia's newest AI unicorn ahead of a planned ASX listing.

The round was backed by Square Peg, Yarra Capital Management, Macquarie Asset Management and Firetrail. Future Secure AI, founded in 2023 and now headquartered in Austin with offices including Melbourne, builds teams of named AI "co-workers" aimed at complex tasks in investment banking, consulting, healthcare and law. The company expects annual recurring revenue to approach US$100 million by the end of 2026 and plans to appoint banks for an ASX float in late 2027.

**Why it matters:** The raise shows continued investor appetite for enterprise AI "agent workforce" vendors targeting regulated, white-collar sectors, even as broader enterprise AI spending growth cools — a combination worth tracking for Asia-Pacific fintechs assessing vendor consolidation risk in this category.

## Key Takeaways

- Enterprise AI spending growth is decelerating sharply even as usage keeps climbing, suggesting buyers are getting more price-sensitive and ROI-focused rather than pulling back from AI altogether.
- Vendor valuations for frontier AI labs are being repriced downward in tandem with the spending slowdown, a signal that public-market scrutiny of AI economics is intensifying ahead of expected IPOs.
- Regional infrastructure partnerships, such as Kong-Hitachi in Japan, show enterprise AI adoption in Asia still advancing at the integration layer even as headline spending growth cools globally.

## What to Watch

Watch for Anthropic's and OpenAI's final IPO/fundraising terms to confirm whether the valuation resets seen this week stick, and for Ramp's next monthly spend data release to show whether the October rebound in AI token spend continues into November.
