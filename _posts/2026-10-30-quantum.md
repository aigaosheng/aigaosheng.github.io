# Quantum Brief: Alice & Bob Cuts RSA-2048 Qubit Estimate

**Today:** A sharply lower resource estimate for breaking RSA-2048, a blunt warning from Coinbase's CEO, and fresh moves into quantum hardware and post-quantum security all point the same way: the migration clock enterprise and fintech leaders face is running faster than many roadmaps assume.

## Top Stories

### 1. ⚛️ **Alice & Bob Nearly Halves RSA-2048 Qubit Estimate**

*The Quantum Insider · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** French quantum computing firm Alice & Bob now estimates far fewer qubits are needed to break RSA-2048 encryption than it previously calculated, narrowing the runway organizations have to complete post-quantum migration.

Using cat qubits paired with low-density parity-check error-correcting codes and a redesigned arithmetic circuit, Alice & Bob cut its estimate for factoring RSA-2048 via Shor's algorithm from 49,649 to 22,593 physical qubits, with a projected runtime of about 3.14 days on fault-tolerant hardware. CEO Théau Peronnin attributed the drop to engineering refinements rather than raw qubit scaling. The company projects hardware progress and shrinking resource requirements could converge in the early 2030s.

**Why it matters:** For banks, card schemes and payment networks still reliant on RSA-based encryption, a shrinking estimated "cost" to break RSA-2048 adds urgency to post-quantum migration timelines that regulators including MAS and NIST are already pushing financial institutions to complete before decade's end.

### 2. ⚛️ **Foxconn Pursues Quantum Computer Manufacturing Role**

*The Quantum Insider · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Foxconn wants to become the world's largest contract manufacturer of quantum computers while leaving the underlying technology to its partners.

Chairman Young Liu said Foxconn aims to build quantum systems for companies developing the technology, mirroring its existing electronics contract-manufacturing model. Liu said commercial viability remains at least two years away industry-wide, pointing to cost barriers such as specialized cryogenic cabling that can exceed $1,000 per cable. He described quantum machines as accelerators that will complement, rather than replace, conventional CPUs and GPUs within hybrid systems.

**Why it matters:** A move by one of the world's largest electronics manufacturers into quantum hardware supply chains signals growing confidence the sector will scale commercially this decade, a trend enterprise buyers across Asia's manufacturing-heavy economies should track as a sign of falling hardware costs ahead.

### 3. 🔒 **Coinbase CEO Calls Quantum Bigger Risk Than AI**

*Benzinga · October 29, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong told investors quantum computing poses a more serious long-term threat to crypto security than artificial intelligence, and said custody upgrades are already underway.

Speaking on Coinbase's Q3 2026 earnings call, Armstrong said he is not worried about AI breaking current cryptographic standards but called the quantum threat "a matter of when, not if." He said Coinbase has begun upgrading its custody infrastructure to be post-quantum and is backing industry efforts such as Bitcoin improvement proposal BIP 360 to make blockchain networks quantum-resistant. Chief Financial Officer Alesia Haas added that Coinbase has retained cryptography experts to track the issue.

**Why it matters:** As a major digital-asset custodian, Coinbase's public timeline signals to exchanges, wallets and payment providers across Asia that quantum-resistant infrastructure is shifting from research agendas into production roadmaps.

### 4. 🔒 **pQCee, Carahsoft Expand Quantum-Safe Access for US Agencies**

*The Quantum Insider · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore-founded post-quantum cryptography provider pQCee partnered with Carahsoft to distribute quantum-safe security tools across US federal, state, local government, education and healthcare buyers.

Carahsoft will act as pQCee's Master Government Aggregator, selling through reseller partners and existing public purchasing contracts. pQCee's offerings include quantum-safe encryption for data exchanges, communications and legacy IT systems with crypto-agility features. CEO Teik Guan Tan linked the deal to US Executive Order 14412, which designates post-quantum migration a national security priority.

**Why it matters:** The deal shows a Singapore-founded post-quantum vendor winning distribution into a major government market, a useful reference point for regional fintechs evaluating local post-quantum suppliers ahead of MAS's upcoming supervisory expectations for financial institutions.

### 5. 🔒 **Manila Forum Urges Early Post-Quantum Preparation**

*BusinessWorld Online · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Experts at a Manila forum warned Philippine businesses that a cryptographically relevant quantum computer, dubbed "Q-Day," could arrive as early as 2029 or 2030.

Speaking at the Asian Institute of Management's "Y2Q Horizon" forum, Eigensystems quantum education lead Ramesh Nagarajan said organizations should begin adopting quantum-safe algorithms now rather than waiting for the threat to materialize. He warned that migrating legacy systems to quantum-safe cryptography is a lengthy process that only gets harder to execute the longer it is delayed.

**Why it matters:** The forum reflects widening regulatory and industry attention to post-quantum migration across Southeast Asia, echoing moves already underway at MAS in Singapore, and should push regional banks and payment providers to start cryptographic asset inventories now.

## Key Takeaways

- Technical resource estimates for breaking RSA-2048 keep falling, compressing the timeline enterprises and regulators have assumed for post-quantum migration.
- Crypto-native firms like Coinbase are now treating quantum risk as a nearer-term threat than AI, a reframing payments and digital-asset players should note.
- Post-quantum security is becoming a commercial and public-sector growth market in its own right, with Singapore-founded vendors like pQCee winning distribution abroad even as Southeast Asian forums push local businesses to start preparing.

## What to Watch

Watch for MAS's promised formal supervisory expectations on quantum-resilient cryptography for Singapore financial institutions, and for further resource-estimate updates from quantum hardware and software vendors that could keep shifting industry assumptions about when RSA and ECC encryption become practically vulnerable.
