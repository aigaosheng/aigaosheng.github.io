# Payments Brief: Zelle Picks Solana for ZelleUSD Cross-Border Push

**Today:** A US bank-owned payment network chose a public blockchain for its stablecoin, and card networks pushed acceptance and QR interoperability deeper into Asia. Together these show bank rails, card rails and token rails converging ahead of the Singapore FinTech Festival.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Zelle Picks Solana to Run ZelleUSD Stablecoin for International Payments**

*Cointelegraph · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Zelle's bank-owned operator will put its ZelleUSD stablecoin on Solana, a public blockchain, to carry international peer-to-peer payments.

Early Warning Services runs Zelle and is owned by a bank consortium that includes JPMorgan and Wells Fargo. It announced at Solana's Breakpoint 2026 conference in London that ZelleUSD (ZLUSD) will run on Solana for its cross-border push. Cointelegraph says Zelle processed more than $1.2 trillion in US payments in 2025. Anchorage Digital Bank issues ZelleUSD, and India was named in June as the first remittance corridor. Zelle and Solana are also testing confidential transfers, which hide amounts on the public chain but let member institutions decrypt them for compliance checks. Zelle gave no launch date on stage.

**Why it matters:** This is a strong signal that large US banks are willing to send regulated dollar tokens over a public chain rather than a private ledger. Remittance providers serving India and the wider Asia corridors could face a new low-cost competitor with bank distribution behind it.

### 2. 💳 **Mastercard Launches Mastercard Accept, Turning Debit Cardholders' Phones Into Card Terminals**

*Mastercard via PR Newswire · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Mastercard is letting eligible debit and prepaid cardholders take card payments through their existing banking or payments apps, with no separate terminal.

Under Mastercard Accept, buyers pay by tapping a contactless card or wallet on the seller's phone, or by scanning a QR code. Funds arrive in near real time in a linked bank or prepaid account. Mastercard will demonstrate it with Maya Philippines at the Singapore FinTech Festival on November 18. Bank of Ceylon and LOLC in Sri Lanka plan to roll it out in the coming months, and Kardal and Oriental Bank plan to do so in Cambodia, subject to regulatory approval. The first markets are Asia Pacific, Latin America, the Middle East and Africa.

**Why it matters:** Micro-merchants who would otherwise use account-to-account QR schemes can now accept cards, which helps card networks defend share in emerging Asian markets. It also lets issuers earn acceptance revenue from their existing cardholders.

### 3. 🏦 **US Supreme Court Declines Custodia Appeal, Leaving Fed Free to Refuse Master Accounts**

*Bitcoin Magazine · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Supreme Court declined to hear Custodia Bank's case, so Federal Reserve Banks keep their discretion to deny master accounts even to legally eligible institutions.

The Court's denial of certiorari leaves in place a 2025 Tenth Circuit ruling that the Kansas City Fed could reject the application from the Wyoming special purpose depository institution. Custodia first applied in October 2020 and was refused in January 2023. It then lost in district court in 2024, on appeal in October 2025, and on its request for rehearing in March 2026. The denial does not rule on the merits.

**Why it matters:** Crypto-native banks and stablecoin issuers still have no guaranteed direct access to Fed payment rails. They will keep depending on sponsor banks, which helps incumbent banks that issue their own tokens, such as Zelle's owners.

### 4. 💳 **Visa and Samsung Bring QR Payments to Samsung Wallet in Singapore and Australia**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Galaxy users in Singapore and Australia can now pay by scanning QR codes with Visa cards stored in Samsung Wallet.

Visa Scan to Pay on Samsung Wallet works at participating merchants that accept QR codes through networks such as Alipay+ (in Malaysia and Hong Kong), FOMO Pay, Lakala, VNPay, OpenRice and Octopus. Visa and Samsung say they expect to add more markets over the next few quarters.

**Why it matters:** Card credentials can now reach Asia's fragmented QR acceptance networks. That weakens one of the main advantages local QR schemes and super-app wallets have held over card networks across the region.

### 5. 🌐 **Wise Adds BNI and Expands Bank Partnerships Across Asia and the Middle East**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Wise is putting its cross-border transfers inside more banks' apps, adding Indonesia's BNI and broadening ties with Standard Chartered, GoTyme, MBSB and Aspire.

BNI has built Wise Platform into its Wondr app, which has more than 13 million customers. Standard Chartered has extended Wise-powered transfers to the UAE after Singapore. Malaysia's MBSB has raised its supported currencies from 12 to 20, and Aspire has taken the partnership to Hong Kong. Wise says a new direct link in Malaysia gives it access to nine domestic payment systems worldwide, and it reports that 78% of its payments arrive in under 20 seconds.

**Why it matters:** Wise is increasingly the infrastructure supplier behind other banks' remittance products rather than only a rival to them. That changes how Asian banks weigh building cross-border capability against buying it.

### 6. 🌐 **Finmo Taps J.P. Morgan Payments for Payouts in More Than 120 Currencies**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Singapore-based treasury platform Finmo is using J.P. Morgan Payments' network to expand business payouts and collections worldwide.

Businesses on Finmo will be able to send payments in more than 120 currencies to more than 160 countries and collect in more than 40 currencies. Payment activity feeds Finmo Treasury, which tracks cash positions across accounts, entities and currencies in real time.

**Why it matters:** Global banks are competing to be the rails underneath Asian fintechs. Smaller firms get correspondent reach without building their own banking networks.

### 7. 💳 **Sokin Goes Fully Live in Singapore Under MAS Major Payment Institution Licence**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Cross-border payments firm Sokin now offers its full range of regulated services in Singapore, building on the Major Payment Institution licence MAS granted in April.

Overseas businesses can use a local Singapore dollar account for domestic payments without setting up a Singapore company. Fintechs, marketplaces and software providers can embed Sokin's transfers, accounts and payment acceptance into their own products. Sokin plans to add more currencies in 2027 and to double its Singapore headcount by the end of March 2027.

**Why it matters:** The MPI licence remains the entry ticket for international payment firms using Singapore as a regional hub, and competition for embedded cross-border business services keeps rising.

### 8. 💳 **Paysafe Launches PaysafeGo White-Label Wallet for Merchants**

*FX News Group · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Paysafe is offering merchants their own branded digital wallets, built on its regulated infrastructure.

PaysafeGo comes in two models. The Embedded Wallet connects through Paysafe APIs and SDKs, with Paysafe handling KYC, AML, issuing, settlement and cross-border payouts. The Turnkey Wallet is co-branded and hosted by Paysafe. Target sectors include travel, retail, gaming, marketplaces, the gig economy and creator platforms.

**Why it matters:** Wallets-as-a-service let platforms hold customer balances and cut acceptance costs, which adds pressure on card-centric checkout economics.

### 9. 💳 **NETS to Retire CashCard From January 2027 as Singapore Moves to ERP 2**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** NETS will end CashCard use for road charges, parking and retail on 1 January 2027, following Singapore's switch to the satellite-based ERP 2 road-charging system.

Motorists are advised to move to the NETS Motoring Card or NETS FlashPay Card by 31 December 2026. Refunds are available at DBS/POSB, OCBC and UOB branches until 31 March 2027. After that, the NETS Customer Service Centre will handle refunds.

**Why it matters:** One of Singapore's oldest stored-value products is ending, which shows how infrastructure upgrades are consolidating legacy payment instruments into account-based and contactless options.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://fintechnews.sg/141360/payments/nets-cashcard/)

## Key Takeaways

- Bank-issued stablecoins are moving onto public chains, with privacy features added to meet compliance needs, while access to central bank rails for crypto-native banks remains restricted.
- Card networks are defending against account-to-account and QR schemes in Asia by bringing card credentials into those systems through phone-based acceptance and Samsung Wallet QR.
- Cross-border payment capability in Asia is increasingly bought rather than built, through Wise Platform, J.P. Morgan Payments and licensed hubs in Singapore.

## What to Watch

Watch the Singapore FinTech Festival (November 18–20) for live Mastercard Accept demonstrations and announcements on stablecoins and tokenised settlement. Also watch whether Zelle confirms a live date for its US–India ZelleUSD corridor, which it earlier targeted for the end of 2026.
