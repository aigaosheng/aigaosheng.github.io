# Quantum Brief: MAS Proposes Quantum-Safe Deadlines For Finance

**Today:** Singapore's financial regulator has put concrete dates on the quantum threat to encryption, forcing banks and payment providers to start budgeting for migration now rather than treating it as a distant research problem.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **MAS Proposes Quantum-Safe Migration Timeline for Financial Sector**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Monetary Authority of Singapore wants financial institutions to have quantum-safe migration plans by end-2027 and all systems upgraded by end-2034, with tighter deadlines for systemically important players.

MAS's consultation paper asks domestic systemically important banks, critical infrastructure operators and NETS to submit migration plans by 30 June 2027 and migrate critical infrastructure by 31 December 2029, ahead of the general 2034 deadline for all other institutions. The regulator warned that sufficiently powerful quantum computers could eventually break widely used public-key cryptography, putting financial data and communications at risk, and is taking industry feedback until 23 November 2026.

**Why it matters:** This is the first concrete, dated compliance obligation tying quantum risk to Singapore's banking and payments infrastructure, meaning BaaS providers, card issuers and payment rails operating under MAS oversight should start cryptographic inventories and vendor assessments well before the 2027 planning deadline. Regional regulators in Hong Kong and Australia are moving in parallel, suggesting Asia-wide supervisory convergence on post-quantum timelines. **[Compliance/Risk] review recommended** before any internal planning commitments are made against these dates, as the guidance remains a non-binding proposal.

### 2. ⚛️ **IBM Patents Scheduling System to Share Quantum Processors**

*The Quantum Insider · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IBM has secured a patent for software that lets multiple users' jobs share a single quantum processor, aiming to cut the idle time and repeated error-correction overhead that make quantum compute scarce and expensive.

The application, filed in May 2025 and approved this week, describes a scheduler that groups compatible programs onto different sections of a chip (illustrated with 52- and 30-qubit jobs sharing a 127-qubit processor) and reuses previously collected noise-calibration data rather than re-measuring it for every job. The filing also proposes billing rebates to encourage customers to opt into shared runs, and notes no measured throughput or cost improvements have yet been reported.

**Why it matters:** Quantum access remains a bottleneck for enterprise experimentation; a working multi-tenancy scheduler could lower the cost of cloud quantum access for banks and fintechs exploring optimisation or risk-modelling pilots, though this is a patent filing, not a shipped product.

### 3. ⚛️ **IQM and AMD Demonstrate Hybrid Quantum-GPU Chemistry Workflow**

*Quantum Zeitgeist · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IQM and AMD ran an end-to-end workflow splitting a molecular chemistry calculation between a quantum processor and GPUs, establishing a reproducible template for combining the two rather than claiming a performance advantage.

The teams used Quantum-Selected Configuration Interaction to let the quantum machine narrow down relevant electron configurations for a hydrogen molecule, with AMD GPUs completing the remaining calculation across 17 bond lengths. The companies published the method with open-source tutorials and said they are pursuing tighter, lower-latency hardware links between quantum processors and GPU clusters.

**Why it matters:** This adds to a growing pattern of quantum-HPC-AI convergence architectures that enterprise R&D teams in pharma, materials and energy are starting to pilot; it signals infrastructure maturity rather than near-term commercial advantage.

### 4. ⚛️ **IBM Names Deloitte, AbbVie, Fermilab as Illinois Quantum Partners**

*The Quantum Insider · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Follow-up reporting on IBM's newly deployed Chicago quantum computer names its first application partners and discloses a 750-job workforce commitment, details not in IBM's original deployment announcement.

Deloitte and AbbVie will work with IBM and Northwestern University on quantum molecular simulation for drug discovery under the National Quantum Algorithm Center's Grand Challenge, while EPRI and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign explore quantum optimisation for power grids and Fermilab advances high-energy physics research. IBM is also establishing a "FutureNow Chicago" workforce centre with City Colleges of Chicago, targeting 750 regional jobs.

**Why it matters:** Named enterprise partners and a specific jobs figure move the Illinois deployment from infrastructure announcement to applied pipeline, a template Asian governments co-investing in quantum hubs, including Singapore's National Quantum Office, may look to replicate for anchor-tenant partnerships.

### 5. 🔒 **Australia Funds Hub to Test Quantum-Safe Security Claims**

*Quantum Zeitgeist · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Australia's Research Council is funding a AUD 4.9 million hub led by Monash University, with the University of Queensland and UNSW, to independently verify whether quantum-safe security products actually work as claimed.

The hub will test and validate post-quantum security technologies in real-world conditions across finance, telecoms and critical infrastructure, aiming to give organisations verified migration tools rather than self-reported vendor assurances. Researcher Muhammed Esgin said the gap is less about building new cryptography and more about proving it performs as intended.

**Why it matters:** Independent verification capacity is a missing piece as banks across Asia-Pacific, including those facing MAS's new timeline, begin selecting post-quantum vendors; certification gaps could slow migration or create false confidence if left unaddressed.

### 6. 🔬 **HKUST Triples Spin Lifetime in Room-Temperature Material**

*Quantum Zeitgeist · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology extended spin polarisation lifetime roughly 300-fold at room temperature using a layered chiral perovskite structure, addressing a key barrier to practical spin-based quantum devices.

The team used a two-step crystallisation method to build quantum wells with graded chirality, creating an energy gradient that lets spin states cascade and persist far longer than in conventional materials, with over 80% spin-dependent charge transport polarisation measured. The work targets spintronic devices and advanced optical sensing rather than a specific quantum computer architecture.

**Why it matters:** Room-temperature spin stability is a long-standing materials constraint on scalable quantum and spintronic hardware; progress from Asian research institutions adds to the region's growing footprint in quantum materials science alongside hardware and software plays.

## Key Takeaways

- Regulatory timelines for post-quantum migration are now concrete and dated in Singapore, following similar moves flagged elsewhere in the region, shifting quantum risk from a research topic to a compliance deadline for financial institutions.
- Commercial quantum computing progress this cycle is incremental and infrastructure-focused — better scheduling, hybrid quantum-GPU workflows, named industry partners — rather than new quantum advantage claims.
- Independent verification of post-quantum security claims is emerging as its own discipline, separate from building the cryptography itself, which matters for any institution selecting vendors against MAS-style deadlines.

## What to Watch

MAS's consultation on quantum-safe migration closes 23 November 2026, after which a finalised circular would carry firmer supervisory weight; separately, the Quantinuum-National Quantum Office SG Grand Challenge concludes with a Singapore grand finale on 19 November 2026.
