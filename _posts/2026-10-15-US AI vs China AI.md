# US AI vs China AI Brief: US and China Pursue AI Framework Before Summit

**Today:** Washington and Beijing are seeking a more structured approach to AI-related incidents, while export controls and local model approvals continue to reshape Asia's semiconductor supply chains and AI deployment strategies.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **US and China Pursue AI Framework Before Summit**

*South China Morning Post · October 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The United States and China are working towards an operational framework for AI-related crises ahead of the November APEC summit in Shenzhen.

Technical teams from both countries are discussing how to implement a bilateral channel for AI-related incidents, building on arrangements announced following their September meetings. The discussions could also lead to a continuing institutional mechanism involving government officials, research institutions and industry representatives, although key operational details remain unresolved.

**Why it matters:** For enterprises deploying frontier models across international markets, a functioning bilateral communication channel could help manage risks arising from AI incidents. However, progress on cooperation does not resolve the wider competition over advanced models, technology controls and global AI governance.

### 2. 🏦 **SK Hynix Links US Factory Plan to China Tool Licences**

*Reuters · October 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** SK Hynix is seeking US approval to install additional American chipmaking equipment at its Dalian facility in China, linking the request to plans for new US manufacturing capacity.

The chipmaker's US subsidiary, Solidigm, wants to equip unused space at the Dalian plant to produce NAND flash memory used in solid-state drives for AI and cloud data centres. The company is pursuing additional export authorisation while considering a new NAND factory in the United States, with expanded Chinese production potentially helping to finance the American investment.

**Why it matters:** Semiconductor export controls are increasingly intertwined with industrial policy and investment incentives. For Asia's AI infrastructure ecosystem, licensing uncertainty can complicate capacity planning for memory chips even as governments seek to expand domestic manufacturing.

### 3. 📊 **ASML China Sales Rose 109% in Q3**

*South China Morning Post · October 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** ASML's sales to China increased 109% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter, highlighting continued demand for semiconductor manufacturing equipment despite pressure for tighter US export restrictions.

China accounted for 23% of ASML's machine sales in the quarter, compared with 14% in the second quarter. The Dutch equipment maker expects China to represent approximately 20% of its total sales for the full year as Washington presses its allies to tighten restrictions on sales of less advanced lithography equipment.

**Why it matters:** The figures illustrate the tension between strong Chinese demand for chipmaking technology and efforts to constrain China's access to strategically important equipment. Further restrictions could affect equipment suppliers, Chinese semiconductor investment and the pace at which alternative domestic technologies develop.

### 4. 🏦 **China Approves Alibaba AI Models for Apple Handsets**

*South China Morning Post · October 14, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China has approved the listing of Alibaba's AI models for integration into Apple handsets sold in the mainland market, clearing another hurdle for Apple's local AI strategy.

Updated network-access permits for the iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro and several earlier Apple models include Apple's Intelligence system alongside Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen, known as Qwen, and Tongyi Wanxiang. Apple has relied on Google and OpenAI for AI capabilities in other major markets, while China's regulatory requirements have pushed the company towards local technology partners.

**Why it matters:** AI competition increasingly extends beyond model benchmarks to distribution, regulatory access and integration into established consumer platforms. For multinational enterprises, local model partnerships and jurisdiction-specific approval processes can determine which AI features are commercially viable in each market.

## Key Takeaways

- **Strategic competition and cooperation are advancing simultaneously.** Washington and Beijing are exploring mechanisms to manage AI incidents even as they compete over advanced models and technological leadership.
- **Export controls are reshaping semiconductor investment decisions.** SK Hynix's proposed US factory and ASML's continued China sales illustrate how licensing, manufacturing location and customer demand interact across borders.
- **AI distribution is becoming more geographically differentiated.** Apple's use of Alibaba models in China illustrates how local partnerships and regulatory approvals influence enterprise and consumer AI deployment.

## What to Watch

Watch for concrete deliverables from the November APEC summit in Shenzhen, a US decision on SK Hynix's additional equipment licences for Dalian, and confirmation of Apple's mainland China AI rollout timeline.
