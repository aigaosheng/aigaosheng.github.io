## 2. In-Depth Highlight

[UK banks pilot tokenised deposit transactions](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48467/uk-banks-pilot-tokenised-deposit-transactions) marks a significant step in moving tokenised money beyond demonstrations into real customer activity. The Great British Tokenised Deposit initiative involves seven major UK banks and uses infrastructure developed by Quant to enable programmable commercial-bank money. Early transactions included remortgage completions and consumer shopping, with funds effectively locked until predefined conditions were satisfied. The model could eventually support digital-asset settlement, automated corporate payments and delivery-versus-payment transactions. The broader significance is that tokenisation is increasingly being tested inside regulated banking infrastructure, rather than existing solely within crypto markets.

## 3. Market & Industry Insight

The week's strongest infrastructure theme is the convergence of **real-time payments, tokenised deposits and programmable money**. The UK pilot, The Clearing House's US initiative and the Federal Reserve's work on cross-border FedNow payments point toward payment systems becoming more interoperable and increasingly capable of supporting conditional or automated settlement. The Clearing House expects its tokenised-deposit network to connect with established systems such as RTP and CHIPS, demonstrating that tokenisation is being designed as an extension of existing financial infrastructure rather than an isolated replacement.

At the same time, **agentic AI is moving deeper into regulated banking workflows**. BNP Paribas plans to deploy specialised agents beginning with corporate credit-memo preparation, while Deutsche Bank is applying agentic AI to Source of Wealth processes in Singapore and Hong Kong. These deployments suggest that the next phase of financial AI will focus less on generic chat interfaces and more on controlled workflows, data access, auditability and human accountability.
