# US AI vs China AI Brief: Beijing Denies Spying On Nvidia's Huang

**Today:** Fallout from the Megaspeed spying report and a fresh chip-smuggling bust in Malaysia underscore how export-control enforcement is now entangling Singapore and Southeast Asian intermediaries directly in US-China AI tensions.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **China Calls US Spying Report On Nvidia's Huang "Groundless"**

*France 24 · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing and Nvidia both pushed back on a US intelligence report alleging a Chinese spy targeted CEO Jensen Huang through Singapore-based cloud firm Megaspeed.

Chinese foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun dismissed the claims as "groundless speculation" intended to smear China, following a *New York Times* report (covered previously) that US officials believe a Megaspeed executive, Alice Huang, was funnelling information to Beijing. Nvidia said its dealings with Megaspeed were those of a normal regional customer and that it shares only routine business data, adding that earlier allegations the Singapore firm smuggled chips to China were "thoroughly investigated and debunked last year."

**Why it matters:** The episode shows how Singapore-domiciled cloud and logistics firms have become flashpoints in US-China tech rivalry, raising due-diligence stakes for any Asia-based company transacting with major AI chip suppliers.

### 2. 🔒 **Malaysia Seizes $12.1 Million In Smuggled AI Chips**

*Digital Today (Korea) · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysian customs intercepted 30 servers loaded with advanced AI chips at Kuala Lumpur International Airport, the fifth such seizure this year tied to a suspected smuggling syndicate.

The shipment, found on October 15 and worth 49.5 million ringgit, was falsely declared as "general electronic parts" and lacked required trade permits; authorities suspect it was routed through Malaysia to dodge US export controls en route to another Asian destination. Total seizures linked to the network now exceed 100 million ringgit in 2026, following a June bust of 72 servers at the same airport.

**Why it matters:** Southeast Asian transshipment hubs, including Singapore's neighbours, face mounting scrutiny as enforcement chokepoints for US chip controls, meaning compliance and trade-documentation checks will tighten across the region's logistics and cloud sectors.

### 3. 📊 **China's AI Stock Boom Loses $2.4 Trillion Since May**

*Taipei Times (Bloomberg) · October 30, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Chinese equities have shed roughly $2.4 trillion in value since May as investors question whether heavy state-backed AI investment can translate into profits amid US competition.

Shanghai's STAR 50 tech index has plunged more than 40% since its June peak, and AI-related stocks now make up over 40% of China-Hong Kong market value, according to Goldman Sachs, concentrating risk across the broader market. Analysts cited in the report point to weak domestic consumption, thin margins at Chinese tech firms, and anxiety over US restrictions on humanoid robots and data-centre components as compounding pressures.

**Why it matters:** A prolonged AI-stock correction could slow Beijing's ability to fund its chip self-sufficiency push, a dynamic enterprise and fintech investors with China exposure should watch alongside currency and capital-flow signals.

## Key Takeaways

- US-China AI tension is increasingly playing out through Southeast Asian intermediaries, with Singapore-linked firms now at the centre of both the spying dispute and chip-smuggling enforcement.
- Enforcement pressure on transshipment routes is escalating, with Malaysia logging five seizures in 2026 alone, a trend regional logistics and cloud providers cannot ignore.
- China's AI investment boom is colliding with financial-market reality, as a $2.4 trillion equity drawdown signals that state-driven compute buildout has yet to produce matching returns.

## What to Watch

Watch for any formal US or Malaysian findings from the Megaspeed and Kuala Lumpur investigations, and whether further STAR 50 declines prompt Beijing to adjust state support for AI and chip firms ahead of the scheduled US-China AI dialogue in November.
