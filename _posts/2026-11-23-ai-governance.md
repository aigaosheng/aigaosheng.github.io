# AI Governance Brief: Governors Urge Shared Federal and State AI Rules

**Today:** New proposals in the United States and South Korea show how AI policy is taking shape across both governance structures and practical rules for data use.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Governors Urge Shared Federal and State AI Rules**

*Bloomberg Law · November 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The National Governors Association called for a federal AI framework that sets national rules while preserving states’ authority over deployment.

The bipartisan governors’ recommendations assign national and international risks to the federal government and leave states room to address consumer protection, discrimination and workplace effects. The plan also calls for independent safety reviews of advanced models and reporting of serious security and safety incidents.

**Why it matters:** The recommendations signal an effort to shape the federal-state balance before the incoming Congress develops an AI framework. For companies operating across jurisdictions, that balance could affect whether compliance requirements converge or continue to vary by state.

### 2. 🏦 **South Korea Proposes Safeguards for AI Training Data**

*Personal Information Protection Commission of Korea · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea’s privacy regulator proposed detailed safeguards and review procedures for a new legal pathway to use personal data in AI development.

The draft enforcement-decree amendments would require enhanced access controls, internal management plans and deletion when data are no longer needed. Higher-risk uses, including sensitive data, children’s data and systems expected to influence decisions affecting people’s rights, would require advance risk assessments. The public comment period runs through January 4, 2027; the underlying law is scheduled to take effect on March 9, 2027.

**Why it matters:** The proposal would give developers a defined process for qualifying data use while adding review and safeguards. It offers a concrete policy development for companies tracking privacy rules across Asian markets.

### 3. 🏦 **South Korea Drafts Standard AI Training Contracts**

*Financial News · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** South Korea’s culture ministry released two draft contract templates to clarify licensing and rights transfers for copyrighted works used to train AI.

The proposed templates cover licenses for specified works and models, as well as assignments of reproduction and transmission rights. They set out options for fees, allow rights holders to request usage records, and require training works and copies to be destroyed within 30 days after a contract ends. The ministry plans a public hearing on November 26 and aims to finalize the templates following feedback.

**Why it matters:** The drafts give rights holders and AI developers a more specific starting point for negotiating data access and permitted model use. The terms could influence how training-data deals are structured in South Korea.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202611230439327053)

## Key Takeaways

- US governors are advocating a federal framework that preserves a role for state-level AI protections.
- South Korea is developing procedural safeguards for AI training with personal data.
- Draft contract templates in South Korea address licensing, usage records and post-contract handling of training works.

## What to Watch

Watch for feedback at South Korea’s November 26 hearing on the draft training-data contracts and for revisions to the privacy decree before its January 4 comment deadline.
