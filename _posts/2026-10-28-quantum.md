# Quantum Brief: NIST Deprecates RSA and ECC Keys After 2030

**Today:** If you own a cryptography roadmap or a quantum vendor shortlist, NIST's final transition plan and Intel's quantum strategy both change your planning assumptions.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **NIST Final IR 8547 Deprecates RSA and ECC at Every Approved Key Size After 2030**
*PostQuantum.com · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** No classical public-key algorithm now stays "acceptable" past 2030 under NIST's tables. P-256 and RSA-3072 lose the extra five years the draft gave them.

NIST published the final version of its post-quantum transition report on October 27 and withdrew the November 2024 draft. The draft deprecated only 112-bit parameter sets, such as RSA-2048, after 2030. The final version deprecates RSA, ECDSA, EdDSA and classical key establishment at all approved strengths after 2030, and disallows them after 2035. NIST also confirmed several other points: a hybrid key exchange can still be FIPS 140-validated after 2035 if one input comes from an approved scheme such as ML-KEM, ML-KEM-768 is the recommended default, HQC is the selected backup KEM, and TLS is an early migration priority.

**Why it matters:** Banks, card issuers and payment processors whose quantum-safe plans assumed P-256 or RSA-3072 were safe until 2035 now face a deprecated status from 2031. That status requires a documented risk decision for each system. The author's opinion is that auditors and regulators outside the US are likely to copy NIST's tables, so regulated firms should take updated roadmaps through compliance and risk review.

### 2. 📊 **Intel Details Classiq Plan: Hardware-Agnostic Software Plus Quantum Foundry Services**
*The Quantum Insider · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Intel plans to earn from quantum in three ways: its own machines, manufacturing for rival quantum companies, and software that runs on competitors' hardware.

This is a follow-up to the October 26 deal announcement. In a Tuesday briefing, Intel said it expects the deal to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approval. It will keep Classiq's software working across rival quantum hardware and offer manufacturing, photonics and advanced packaging to other quantum developers. Intel's own processors will stay on silicon spin qubits made in Hillsboro, Oregon, on a process derived from its 18A technology. The CTO warned the business will not be high-margin at first and gave no profitability timeline. Intel did not disclose a price; Calcalist reported $2.3 billion in cash and shares.

**Why it matters:** A major chipmaker offering quantum manufacturing to other companies could lower hardware costs across the sector. Classiq's named users include Wells Fargo, so financial institutions running pilots on Classiq should track whether the promise to support all hardware platforms holds after closing.

### 3. ⚛️ **IonQ Secures $7.5M AFRL Phase 2 to Extend Rome Quantum Network Into Free Space**
*Quantum Computing Report · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IonQ's network for the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) moves beyond fibre, adding open-air links that could later connect mobile or satellite nodes.

The $7.5 million Phase 2 award draws on options already planned in IonQ's broader $21.1 million AFRL collaboration, so it is not extra money beyond that total. The upgrades include:
- open-air optical links in the C-band with automatic pointing and tracking
- frequency converters that shift photons from IonQ's trapped-ion qubits onto standard telecom wavelengths
- upgraded network interface cards
- on-site software that monitors links and fails over automatically

**Why it matters:** Quantum networking is still mostly funded by government and defence budgets. Enterprises and telecom operators in Asia should treat it as a long-horizon capability to monitor, not something to buy now.

### 4. ⚛️ **D-Wave's Qubits Asia in Seoul Showcases NTT Docomo Production Use Cases**
*The Financial News (Korea) · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** In Asia, quantum adoption is being pitched through optimisation problems already running in production, not through future breakthroughs.

At its Qubits Asia conference in Seoul, D-Wave and its customers presented applications in network optimisation, manufacturing inspection, generative AI, semiconductor manufacturing and port operations. NTT Docomo, the Japanese telecom operator, showed two applications built on D-Wave technology that run in live environments and improve network efficiency. D-Wave CEO Alan Baratz named South Korea, Japan, Singapore and Taiwan as countries moving fast to lead in quantum.

**Why it matters:** For Asian telecom, logistics and manufacturing operators, these cases are the reference points vendors will use in sales. Buyers should ask for measured comparisons against classical solvers before scaling up.

### 5. ⚛️ **Toppan, SoftBank and Four Japanese Firms Form NEDO-Backed Quantum-AI Materials Platform**
*IBTimes Japan · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Japan is paying for industrial firms to combine quantum computing and AI on real materials problems, through a project running until fiscal 2028.

Toppan, Aisin, Asahi Kasei, SoftBank, AGC and DIC signed a joint agreement after NEDO, Japan's government research funding agency, selected their proposal. The partners will build a materials research platform that combines quantum calculations with AI to reduce computing cost and improve accuracy. Each partner will test the approach in a different materials area from fiscal 2026 to fiscal 2028.

**Why it matters:** Groups of companies sharing one platform, with government funding, are becoming Asia's common way to explore quantum use cases. The model spreads both the cost and the technical risk of early experiments.

### 6. ⚛️ **Quantinuum and GE Vernova Partner on High-Temperature Superconductivity Research**
*The Quantum Insider · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Quantinuum is pairing its Helios quantum computer with an energy-equipment maker to study physics that could eventually lead to lighter, more efficient power hardware.

The project will simulate a simplified model of interacting electrons (the Fermi–Hubbard model) on Helios through Quantinuum's Nexus developer platform. GE Vernova's Advanced Research Center will contribute materials and engineering expertise. Quantinuum says it has already measured superconducting pairing signals in such simulations on Helios. Both firms acknowledge that cooling, manufacturing and cost still limit the use of superconductors in industry.

**Why it matters:** Simulating materials is still the most credible near-term use for quantum computers. Partnerships with industrial companies like this one signal where early commercial value is most likely to appear.

### 7. 📊 **QuantrolOx Raises €12M Series A2 for Quantum Hardware Automation and Training**
*The Quantum Insider · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Investors are funding the software tools and skilled workforce needed to build and run quantum hardware, not just the qubits.

QuantrolOx is an Oxford spinout headquartered in Finland. IW Capital led the round, with co-investment from the European Innovation Council (EIC) Fund. Voima Ventures, Singapore-based Serendipity Capital, Hoxton Ventures and Transpose Capital also took part, bringing total funding to about €28 million. The money will expand its calibration platform, grow its training academy and build its US team, following a new Berkeley office and a five-year agreement with UC Berkeley.

**Why it matters:** Automated calibration and a shortage of skilled engineers are real constraints on scaling quantum hardware. Singapore capital in the round shows regional investors are backing this supply-chain layer.

## Key Takeaways
- Post-quantum migration now has firm dates, not just guidance: every classical key size gets a 2031 deprecation, and hybrid ML-KEM has a confirmed path to FIPS validation.
- Large incumbents are positioning as platforms and suppliers, as Intel's foundry offer shows. Pure-play quantum firms still rely heavily on government contracts, as IonQ's AFRL award shows.
- In Asia, demand comes from industry: telecom, materials and manufacturing, as the D-Wave Seoul event and Japan's NEDO consortium show.

## What to Watch
Two US deadlines fall around December 19, 2026: NIST's revised cryptographic module validation (CMVP) processes, and a proposed federal procurement rule requiring contractors to comply with NIST's cryptography standards, including post-quantum ones. Both will show how quickly NIST's timelines turn into requirements in vendor contracts.
