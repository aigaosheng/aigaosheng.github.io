# AI Finance in Singapore Brief: Banks Grow Wary Of AI Lending Risk

**Today:** Singapore banks are showing early signs of AI-fuelled market jitters feeding back into risk management, even as fresh survey data shows local financial institutions racing ahead on AI adoption faster than governance can keep pace.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Singapore Banks Tighten Scrutiny of AI-Related Lending Amid Market Selloff**

*Reuters (via Investing.com) · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Banks and investors, including OCBC, are pulling back from financing AI infrastructure deals as a sharp equity selloff and fresh political uncertainty over data-centre oversight spook credit markets.

Reuters reported that lenders who were racing to fund AI data-centre deals weeks earlier have turned cautious within days, even for projects backed by large tech names. OCBC Group CEO Tan Teck Long said the bank is "very focused on the risk aspect" of such deals and structures financing to ensure cash flow backs the debt. The piece also cites Goldman Sachs Research projecting global hyperscaler debt issuance will reach US$420 billion in 2027, and JPMorgan data showing data-centre-linked loans now make up 12% of its commercial real estate book, up from 8% three years ago.

**Why it matters:** For a Singapore banking sector where AI infrastructure financing has been a growth narrative, the pullback signals that credit risk teams are now pricing in AI capex sustainability as a distinct risk factor, not just a growth opportunity — a shift regional lenders and their BaaS and fintech partners should watch closely.

### 2. 🤖 **Global Banks Outpace Governance on AI, Singapore Adoption Still Climbing**

*Fintech News Singapore · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A global banking survey finds AI use in credit and risk decisions is nearly universal but governance is lagging, while separate data shows Singapore institutions continuing to scale AI deployment under MAS's new Future of Finance Institute.

Fintech News Singapore reported on joint research by FT Longitude, SAS and Microsoft polling 300 credit, treasury and risk leaders across 31 countries, finding 82% of banks now use generative AI and 41% use agentic AI, with 299 of 300 banks using some form of AI in credit and risk decisions. Only 18% of banks have fully operational, embedded AI governance, and 42% lack AI "kill switches." The article separately notes that, per the Financial Services State of the Nation Survey 2026, 64% of Singapore financial institutions were already actively deploying AI, with MAS's Future of Finance Institute now working to move the sector from experimentation to broader, better-governed deployment.

**Why it matters:** The governance gap identified globally is directly relevant to Singapore, where MAS's AI Risk Management Guidelines (effective from October 2026) put the onus on banks and insurers to close exactly these gaps — kill switches, drift monitoring and model risk policies — well before the 2027–2028 compliance deadlines.

## Key Takeaways

- Credit risk teams at Singapore banks are starting to treat AI infrastructure exposure as a lending risk category in its own right, not just a technology trend to finance.
- Survey data confirms Singapore's financial institutions are ahead on AI deployment, but global and local evidence both point to governance — kill switches, drift monitoring, model risk policies — lagging adoption.
- MAS's regulatory architecture (AI Risk Management Guidelines, Future of Finance Institute) is the mechanism meant to close that governance gap, putting pressure on banks and insurers to formalise AI oversight ahead of the 2027–2028 deadlines.

## What to Watch

Watch for how Singapore banks' Q4 and full-year 2026 results (due from mid-January 2027) characterise AI-related lending exposure and provisioning, and for early output from MAS's Future of Finance Institute's BuildFin.ai and SafeFin.ai workstreams as the sector moves toward the October 2027 guideline compliance milestone.
