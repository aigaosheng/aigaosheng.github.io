# AI Fintech Brief: US Banks Push Back on Customer AI Agents

**Today:** A quiet weekend produced one verifiable in-scope story: major US banks are redefining who bears the risk when customers let AI agents act on their accounts, which matters for anyone building or buying agentic financial products.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Major US Banks Restrict Third-Party AI Agents and Shift Risk to Customers**

*WIRED · November 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Large US banks are blocking or warning against third-party AI agents in customer accounts while continuing to build their own first-party AI tools.

Wired reports that Wells Fargo's updated agreement with its customers takes effect on Tuesday, November 24, and tells users that any AI agent they employ is used at their own risk. It says JPMorgan Chase changed its terms in late October to bar third-party agents that log in with customer credentials, and that Bank of America issued a warning against sharing login details with agents the same day. Citi and PNC have also added agent-related warnings to their terms.

Wired adds that American Express has said some agent errors, such as ordering too many items, may become eligible for reimbursement in future. Analysts quoted in the piece say banks are acting early because they are risk-averse, even though few consumers use agents today. Wired also notes that the same banks are investing in their own tools, citing Wells Fargo's generative AI assistant, which the article says has handled over a billion interactions, and an internal agent platform at Citigroup.

**Why it matters:** The banks are drawing a line between agents they control and agents they do not, which sets early terms for liability and account access in agentic banking. Fintechs and AI vendors that depend on customer-credential access should expect pressure toward sanctioned integrations, and banks and regulators in Asia may face the same question as consumer agents spread.

## Key Takeaways

- Banks are separating first-party AI, which they are expanding, from third-party agents, which they are restricting or disclaiming.
- Liability for agent mistakes is being allocated through customer terms before any law or regulation settles the question.
- Card networks and issuers, such as American Express, may become the first to offer limited protection for agent errors, which could shape consumer adoption.

## What to Watch

Whether other large banks adopt Wells Fargo-style terms when its agreement takes effect on November 24, and whether card issuers formalise agent-error reimbursement policies. Also watch for any regulatory guidance on authorisation and liability for agent-initiated transactions.
