## Strategic Takeaway

**The US still leads the frontier, but the competitive equation is changing.**

The most important development today is the widening definition of "AI leadership." The US retains major advantages in frontier-model research, semiconductor design, hyperscale cloud infrastructure and venture capital. China, however, is increasingly competing through **cost, domestic AI chips, infrastructure construction, manufacturing scale, open models and international deployment**.

The emerging battlefield therefore has at least five layers:

1. **Frontier intelligence** — currently an important US advantage.
2. **Compute and chips** — US/Nvidia leadership versus China's accelerating domestic substitution.
3. **Cost and deployment** — China's aggressive price competition could drive faster adoption.
4. **Physical scale** — data centers, electricity, robotics and manufacturing increasingly favor China's industrial ecosystem.
5. **Global ecosystem influence** — the contest is expanding into Saudi Arabia, Southeast Asia and other markets where countries want AI sovereignty rather than exclusive alignment.

**Bottom line:** The US-China AI race is becoming less about who releases the single best model and more about **who can build the cheapest, largest and most globally deployable AI ecosystem**.
