# Quantum Brief: IBM Links Two 120-Qubit Processors With Cables

**Today:** Modular hardware took a measurable step forward, post-quantum security moved onto SIM cards in Korea, and two government reports show where public quantum money is going. Read this if you track quantum timelines or plan cryptographic migration.

## Top Stories

### 1. ⚛️ **IBM Connects Two 120-Qubit Processors With 99% State-Transfer Fidelity**
*The Quantum Insider · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IBM has shown that separate large quantum chips can share quantum information over cables, a building block for scaling past the limits of a single chip.

IBM researchers joined two 120-qubit Nighthawk R1 processors using four superconducting aluminium cables, each about 80 cm long. Average state transfer across the links reached 99%, with the best link at 99.29%. Full logic operations between the chips were weaker, averaging 94.75%, and only selected qubits near the links were wired. The links survived three warm-up and cool-down cycles, but the magnetic shielding is described as temporary. The results are in an arXiv preprint and have not been peer reviewed.

**Why it matters:** Most fault-tolerant roadmaps depend on connecting modules, so this is evidence for the scaling path behind the vendor timelines that drive enterprise planning. The gap between transfer fidelity and full-gate fidelity shows that useful modular machines still need more engineering.

### 2. 🔒 **IDEMIA and KT Pilot Post-Quantum SIMs in South Korea's Defence Network**
*The Quantum Insider · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Post-quantum cryptography is being built into secure-element hardware, the same kind of chip used in SIMs and payment cards.

IDEMIA Secure Transactions is working with KT on South Korea's 2026 Post-Quantum Cryptography Pilot Conversion Project, which MSIT and KISA organise. IDEMIA is loading both NIST-standardised and Korean post-quantum algorithms onto its Quantum-Resistant SIM platform. Subject to final validation, the companies expect this to be Korea's first deployment of Korean PQC algorithms on a smart card. KT's wider pilot for the defence ministry covers PCs, CCTV, drones and 5G networks. The source is a company press release.

**Why it matters:** Secure elements are the root of trust for cards and mobile wallets. Running both international and national algorithms on one chip is a practical example of crypto-agility, which matters for card issuers working across Asian markets with different national standards.

### 3. 🤖 **Outside Researchers to Review OpenAI's AI-Generated Complexity Proof**
*The Quantum Insider · October 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** OpenAI says an AI-generated proof disproves the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a core assumption in computational complexity, but the claim is unverified and has no proven effect on practical quantum advantage yet.

OpenAI has asked Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi of UC San Diego and Ryan Williams of MIT to verify and explain the proof. Impagliazzo and Paturi helped formulate the hypothesis. OpenAI says the proof was checked in the Lean verification system and released publicly. Some arguments that quantum circuits are hard to simulate on classical computers rely on a related assumption. The article notes that losing a hardness argument does not in itself produce a faster classical algorithm. A separate group led by David Gross will examine OpenAI's claimed existence proofs for quantum field theories.

**Why it matters:** If the proof holds, some theoretical claims of quantum advantage would need a new basis. Executives should keep watching measured performance rather than headlines while the review runs.

### 4. 🏦 **UK's £30 Million Quantum Testbed Programme Reports Phase 1 Results**
*The Quantum Insider · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The UK's government-as-first-customer model delivered seven working prototype quantum computers, but performance testing is only now under way.

The National Quantum Computing Centre's Phase 1 report covers seven suppliers on the Harwell Campus: Aegiq, Infleqtion, ORCA, Oxford Ionics, Quantum Motion, QuEra and Rigetti. Together they span five hardware approaches. Six systems have entered independent technical evaluation, and collaborative projects have won £4.6 million in follow-on funding. The programme only partly met its delivery target. Phase 2, run with the University of Edinburgh's Quantum Software Lab, is due to finish in Q2 2027.

**Why it matters:** This is a reusable public-procurement model for building a domestic quantum supply base, relevant to Asian governments running their own national programmes. The independent benchmarks from Phase 2 will be more useful to buyers than vendor claims.

### 5. 📊 **Israel Ranks Fourth Globally in Private Quantum Investment**
*The Quantum Insider · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Israeli quantum companies raised nearly US$500 million in 2025, about 9% of global private quantum investment, according to a new government-backed report.

The report from the Israel Innovation Authority and C4IR Israel says the sector grew from about 5 companies and 50 employees in 2019 to more than 20 companies and roughly 1,000 employees in 2025. Only the US, UK and Canada attracted more private quantum capital. The report finds adoption is still limited in sectors including finance. It proposes a second national quantum initiative for 2027–2031 focused on infrastructure, partnerships and software.

**Why it matters:** Israel's strength is in software, control and orchestration that work across hardware types, so Israeli firms are likely partners for banks and fintechs exploring quantum without betting on one hardware platform.

## Key Takeaways
- Scaling is becoming an interconnect and engineering problem. IBM's cable links and the UK testbeds both show progress, but measured performance lags the headline figures.
- Post-quantum security is moving into hardware roots of trust such as SIMs and secure elements, where support for both NIST and national algorithms is turning into a practical requirement.
- Governments are moving from building ecosystems to driving adoption, and both reports say real use in industries such as finance is still early.
