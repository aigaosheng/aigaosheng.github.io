# AI for Society Brief: Fed Weighs AI’s Workforce Disruption

Today: Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh highlights the tension between AI-driven productivity gains and potential employment disruption, reinforcing the need to prepare workers and institutions for structural changes in the economy.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 Federal Reserve Warns of AI-Driven Employment Disruption

Reuters · November 17, 2026

Bottom line: Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh says artificial intelligence could accelerate economic growth and innovation, but warns that its widespread adoption could also trigger higher unemployment and significant economic disruption.

Speaking at a Federal Reserve conference on AI and productivity in San Francisco on November 16, Warsh described AI as a technology capable of accelerating idea generation and raising long-term productivity. In an optimistic scenario, productivity gains could benefit both workers and capital owners, supporting sustained economic growth while keeping the US close to full employment.

However, he also highlighted a downside scenario in which disappointing returns on substantial AI infrastructure investments could trigger falling equity valuations, an economic slowdown and rising unemployment. Warsh added that AI's potential effects on education deserve attention, with conference participants raising concerns that excessive reliance on AI could undermine student learning. The Federal Reserve has established a group to examine the technology's economic implications and prepare for different adoption scenarios.

Why it matters: AI's economic impact will depend not only on productivity improvements but also on how quickly workers, businesses and education systems adapt. For enterprises, workforce transformation needs to extend beyond technology deployment into reskilling, job redesign and the development of complementary human capabilities. Across Asia, where digital transformation and AI adoption are accelerating, employers and policymakers face the challenge of ensuring productivity gains translate into broader employment opportunities rather than widening skills and income disparities.

## Key Takeaways

- Productivity gains do not guarantee inclusive growth. AI could strengthen economic output, but the distribution of benefits between workers and capital owners will shape its broader social impact.
- Workforce adaptation is becoming an economic priority. Reskilling, continuous learning and job redesign will be essential as AI changes occupational requirements and workplace structures.
- Education must evolve alongside AI adoption. Institutions need to develop AI literacy while preserving critical thinking, independent learning and problem-solving capabilities.
