# US AI vs China AI Brief: EU Quietly Sidesteps China's WAICO AI Bloc Invitation

**Today:** It was a light news window, but two stories show the US-China AI split moving from chips into alliance politics and into memory supply chains that Asian buyers depend on.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **EU Keeps China's WAICO Invitation at Arm's Length Under US Pressure**
*Tech Times · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Brussels is treating Beijing's invitation to join its AI organisation as a political tripwire, which in practice lines it up with the US-led Pax Silica bloc.

According to diplomats cited in the analysis, China formally invited the European Commission to join the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO). The Commission briefed national experts on the offer around October 21, but its spokesperson then publicly denied receiving any official invitation. The invitation was first reported by Rapporteur on October 23; this piece adds analysis rather than new facts. The EU signed the US-led Pax Silica declaration on June 25, 2026, and agreed under a wider EU-US trade framework to buy at least $40 billion of American AI chips. A US State Department draft letter in August warned 35 partner countries that joining a rival AI initiative would put their Pax Silica standing at risk. Kazakhstan remains the only known member of both blocs, and no G7 country had joined WAICO as of October.

**Why it matters:** The author's view is that silence has become a de facto alignment choice, and that view is opinion. Still, the US exclusivity logic now reaches the EU, which suggests that hedging between the blocs is getting harder even for large economies. Singapore is an early Pax Silica signatory. For Singapore-based firms that operate across both ecosystems, it is worth tracking whether non-binding declarations turn into conditions on access to chips and investment.

### 2. 📊 **PwC Warns Korean Chipmakers as China's CXMT and YMTC Gain Share**
*South China Morning Post · October 26, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Chinese memory makers are scaling fast enough that South Korea's advisers are telling Samsung and SK Hynix to compete on cost in commodity chips and stay ahead in AI memory.

Samil PwC, PwC's South Korean member firm, released a report on Monday. It says Chinese competition will likely hit conventional memory, mature-node manufacturing, and packaging and testing first. The report urges Korean chipmakers to sharpen cost competitiveness in commodity memory and to invest more in advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the stacked memory used alongside AI processors. SCMP also notes earlier Reuters reporting that Samsung and SK Hynix are upgrading their China plants to produce more advanced NAND flash, both to counter YMTC and to meet AI-driven storage demand.

**Why it matters:** Even under US export controls, China is gaining ground in the less restricted layers of the chip stack, and that is putting pressure on margins for allied suppliers. For Asian enterprises buying AI infrastructure, competition in commodity memory may ease costs. HBM, however, remains concentrated among a few non-Chinese suppliers.

## Key Takeaways

- The US-China AI contest is turning into membership politics. Pax Silica and WAICO increasingly ask partners to choose, and silence is being read as a choice.
- China's chip progress is showing up first in mature and commodity segments rather than at the frontier, and that is squeezing allied suppliers' profitability.
- For Asia-based firms, the practical exposure is moving from "which chips can we buy" to "which bloc's standards and supply chains do we sit in."

## What to Watch

The next concrete signal is whether the US State Department turns its August draft warning into a binding Pax Silica membership condition. That step would make any EU or ASEAN engagement with WAICO incompatible with US chip access arrangements.
