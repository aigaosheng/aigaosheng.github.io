# Quantum Brief: Classiq Expands Enterprise Quantum Hub in Romania

Today: Enterprise quantum adoption is advancing through software, hardware access and training infrastructure, although practical commercial value still needs to be demonstrated.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 Classiq Expands Enterprise Quantum Hub in Romania

Romania Insider · October 16, 2026

Bottom line: Classiq and FreeYa Mind Campus are combining quantum software, hardware access and training to help companies and public institutions develop quantum applications.

Their commercial agreement will bring Classiq's quantum programming and circuit-synthesis platform to the Quantum AI centre in Iași, which provides access to IBM Quantum System Two hardware. The initiative includes application development, education and specialist training, while Classiq plans to establish its Romanian regional headquarters at the campus. [Read the full story](https://www.romania-insider.com/business/romanias-quantum-computing-hub-iasi-adds-classiq-software)

Why it matters: For enterprise technology leaders, the partnership illustrates an emerging adoption model: combine quantum hardware access with higher-level software and practical training rather than building an entire quantum capability in-house. Financial services and other computationally intensive industries should assess such environments through measurable use cases, including optimisation and simulation, while comparing results against established classical computing methods.

## Key Takeaways

- Access is becoming more practical. Shared quantum infrastructure and software platforms can reduce the initial barriers to experimentation.
- Skills remain essential. Training and application-development capabilities are important complements to access to quantum hardware.
- Commercial proof matters more than access alone. Enterprise pilots should establish measurable performance, economic value and advantages over classical alternatives before progressing to production.

## What to Watch

Watch for concrete enterprise applications emerging from the Romanian hub, including evidence of customer adoption, repeatable workloads and measurable business outcomes. For Singapore and Southeast Asia, the key question is whether regional quantum initiatives can connect research infrastructure with industry-specific problems and commercially useful results.
