# Payments Brief: Cambodia's Bakong Goes Global With Asia-Africa Links

**Today:** Cambodia used the inaugural Black Swan Summit in Phnom Penh to turn its national payment system, Bakong, into a cross-border platform spanning Japan and Africa, a notable test of how smaller Asian markets can export their domestic rails.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Cambodia Launches Bakong Global, Linking Stablecoins, Cards and Africa Remittances**

*Manila Times / PR Newswire · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) unveiled "Bakong Global," a package of initiatives that extends its domestic payment and wallet system into international card payments, stablecoin remittances and cross-border links with Rwanda and Francophone Africa.

Bakong users will be able to generate Visa or Mastercard virtual cards inside the app for overseas contactless spending, while NBC and Japan's SBI Group will pilot stablecoin-based remittances between the two countries, with funds landing directly in Bakong wallets. A separate SBI Bank (Cambodia) pilot will test tokenising Cambodian government bonds on the Canton Network, and NBC is also linking Bakong with Rwanda's RSwitch and exploring corridors into Francophone Africa, starting with remittances and small-value trade. NBC reported Bakong now has 38 million registered accounts against Cambodia's population of roughly 17 million, having processed 1.3 billion transactions worth USD 241.84 billion in 2025, with the riel's share of domestic transactions rising from 17% in 2020 to 58% in 2025.

**Why it matters:** For BaaS and payments providers across Southeast Asia, Bakong Global is a concrete example of a single domestic rail being repackaged for card issuing, stablecoin remittances and bond tokenisation simultaneously, a model that Singapore, the Philippines and Indonesia's own instant-payment schemes may look to replicate as they pursue regional connectivity beyond existing QR linkages.

## Key Takeaways

- Domestic instant-payment and wallet systems in Southeast Asia are increasingly being repurposed as the base layer for stablecoin remittances and card issuing, not just local QR payments.
- Bond tokenisation and stablecoin remittance pilots are being bundled into a single national initiative, suggesting central banks see these as complementary infrastructure rather than competing bets.
- Bilateral corridors (Cambodia-Japan, Cambodia-Rwanda) are emerging alongside multilateral efforts like ASEAN QR linkages, giving smaller markets more direct routes to liquidity and remittance flows outside larger regional blocs.

## What to Watch

The Black Swan Summit runs through November 16 in Phnom Penh and may surface further details on the SBI-Cambodia bond tokenisation pilot and the Rwanda-RSwitch corridor; the Singapore FinTech Festival follows on November 18-20 and is expected to feature related discussion on agentic commerce and tokenised infrastructure across the region.
