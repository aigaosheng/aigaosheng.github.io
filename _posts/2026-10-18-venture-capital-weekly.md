# Modal Reaches $15.75B as Enterprise Agent Infrastructure and Vertical AI Accelerate

---

This week's funding activity points to three priorities in enterprise AI: infrastructure for running autonomous agents, integration with existing business systems, and specialised AI products for regulated industries.

Modal Labs' $750 million Series D at a $15.75 billion valuation was the standout infrastructure deal. Elsewhere, Duvo, RapidCanvas and Parallel Loop attracted funding for enterprise workflow execution, while Scarlet, Dougs, Stride and The Phage focused on specialised regulatory, accounting and health applications.

## Executive Summary

- **Agent infrastructure attracts late-stage capital.** Modal's round reflects demand for compute infrastructure and isolated execution environments for AI workloads.
- **Enterprise integration is a key investment thesis.** Investors are backing platforms that connect AI agents to existing ERP, supplier and operational systems.
- **Vertical AI targets high-friction sectors.** Healthtech and accounting startups are applying AI to workflows shaped by regulation, specialised data and local requirements.
- **Deployment economics still matter.** Companies using forward-deployed engineers must turn custom implementations into reusable products to protect software margins.

## Trends & Analysis

### 1. Agent execution infrastructure moves into focus

Modal's $750 million Series D at a $15.75 billion valuation highlights investor interest in infrastructure that supports production AI workloads. As multi-agent frameworks, coding assistants and autonomous reasoning loops become more common, developers need secure, disposable execution environments and predictable performance.

Infrastructure providers can simplify GPU orchestration and reduce the operational burden on engineering teams. However, their economics remain exposed to GPU pricing, customer concentration and competition from hyperscalers that may bundle execution capabilities into broader cloud agreements.

**Investor lens:** Assess utilisation, gross margins, customer concentration and the durability of demand—not valuation growth alone.

### 2. Enterprise AI funding shifts toward integration and execution

Duvo, RapidCanvas and Parallel Loop represent a broader push to connect AI capabilities with fragmented enterprise systems. After early experimentation with standalone LLM interfaces, buyers increasingly need solutions that work with existing ERP platforms, supplier systems, ticketing tools and internal data.

Forward-deployed engineers can help overcome integration barriers and move projects into production. The challenge is maintaining software-like economics: custom work must translate into reusable components rather than recurring, low-margin consulting engagements.

**Investor lens:** Look for repeatable deployments, measurable business outcomes and a declining need for bespoke engineering over time.

### 3. Vertical AI benefits from domain expertise and regulation

Scarlet and Dougs illustrate how regulatory requirements and specialised workflows create opportunities for focused AI businesses. Stride and The Phage similarly target health applications that rely on biological, clinical and longitudinal data.

These markets can offer differentiation through domain expertise, trusted processes and proprietary data. They also face longer sales cycles, demanding validation requirements and jurisdiction-specific regulation.

**Investor lens:** Evaluate regulatory readiness, evidence of product efficacy, data rights, liability exposure and the time required to reach commercial scale.

## Actionable Insights

### For investors

1. **Prioritise secure agent execution infrastructure.** Evaluate sandbox isolation, latency, orchestration, utilisation and exposure to GPU supply costs.
2. **Back enterprise interoperability over demos.** Prefer products that improve measurable workflows inside existing ERP, CRM and operational systems.
3. **Scrutinise deployment economics.** For forward-deployed engineering models, track gross-margin progression and how much implementation work becomes reusable product capability.
4. **Assess regulatory capability as a competitive advantage.** In healthtech, accounting and other controlled sectors, look for compliance, auditability and data governance embedded in the product.
5. **Stress-test high private-market valuations.** Test assumptions around customer durability, gross margins, competitive responses from hyperscalers and potential inference-cost compression.

### For enterprise leaders

- Define measurable outcomes before deploying AI agents.
- Require permission boundaries, logging, monitoring and human escalation for consequential actions.
- Test integration with legacy systems and data-quality constraints early.
- Establish clear accountability for AI-generated actions and exceptions.
- Track total cost of ownership, including inference, integration, monitoring and ongoing remediation.

## The Bottom Line

This week's deals point to a maturing enterprise AI market. Capital is flowing not only into models, but also into the infrastructure that runs agents, the integration layers that connect them to business systems, and specialised products designed for regulated workflows.

The next phase will reward companies that can turn technical capability into **reliable execution, measurable return on investment and durable operating economics**.

For investors and enterprise leaders, the central question is no longer simply whether AI can perform a task. It is whether the solution can perform it safely, repeatedly and economically at scale.
