# AI for Science Brief: Isomorphic Labs Nears $5B AI Drug Funding

**Today:** AI-native drug discovery just drew its largest funding signal yet, while frontier AI labs and hyperscalers pushed further into running science itself, from cell biology to climate modeling.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Isomorphic Labs in Talks for $5 Billion Round at $50 Billion Valuation**

*Tech Funding News, citing Bloomberg · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Alphabet-backed Isomorphic Labs is reportedly in early talks to raise roughly $5 billion at a $50 billion valuation, a sharp bet on AI-designed drugs before any have reached human trials.

A new round would take the DeepMind spinout's external funding to about $7.7 billion in under two years, following a $2.1 billion Series B in May 2026 backed by Thrive Capital, Alphabet, GV, MGX, Temasek and the UK Sovereign AI Fund. Founder Demis Hassabis has said Isomorphic's first AI-designed candidate could enter clinical trials by the end of December 2026.

**Why it matters:** Temasek's continued participation ties Singapore state capital directly to the AI drug-discovery boom, giving regional investors a concrete benchmark for how much capital is chasing the sector ahead of any validated clinical results.

### 2. 🔬 **Anthropic's Claude Helps Resolve a 40-Year Cell Biology Mystery**

*Anthropic · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Anthropic's biology team used Claude to determine the first 3D structure of a bacterial "vault" particle, advancing a question about these hollow cell structures that has stood unanswered since their 1986 discovery.

Claude searched roughly 15 billion predicted proteins to find vault-like candidates in bacteria and viruses; of 15 prokaryotic proteins tested, seven assembled into vault-like particles and four were confirmed by cryo-EM. The AI-assisted workflow cut vault purification from three days to about 90 minutes and compressed weeks of structural analysis into 34 hours, and the team used Claude to engineer a vault roughly 20% larger to probe how size could evolve.

**Why it matters:** this moves frontier models beyond structure prediction into running full experimental workflows, a template pharma and biotech R&D leaders across Asia-Pacific are starting to evaluate for their own labs.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.anthropic.com/research/investigating-vault-particles-with-claude)

### 3. 🔬 **Google Funds Ai2 to Extend Its Weather AI Into Climate Projection**

*Allen Institute for AI · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Google.org is backing a three-year Allen Institute for AI project to turn its NeuralGCM weather model into NeuralGCM+, targeting decades-ahead projections of rainfall and water availability.

The philanthropic grant comes with Google Research technical support; Ai2 will expand its climate modeling team and work toward stable multi-decade atmosphere-ocean simulations, stronger performance under unfamiliar warming scenarios, and open-source release of models and GPU-optimised tools. Google's existing NeuralGCM already simulates a year of global atmospheric conditions in eight minutes on a single TPU.

**Why it matters:** faster, open climate models are directly relevant to Southeast Asia's flood and drought planning; enterprise and public-sector teams in Singapore and ASEAN should watch for open-source releases that could feed regional climate-risk tools.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://allenai.org/blog/google-climate-partnership)

### 4. 🔬 **Nature Publishes AI-Native Autonomous Biology Lab**

*Nature · November 4, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Researchers detailed AutoDNA, an AI-native laboratory in which AI agents independently design and run multi-step biomolecular engineering experiments across more than 20 instrument types from a single human prompt.

The system coordinates tasks such as DNA synthesis and sequencing end-to-end, with the paper accepted in September 2026 and published November 4. It adds to a run of autonomous-lab disclosures in recent months from organisations including Anthropic and Danaher.

**Why it matters:** autonomous labs could compress the design-build-test cycle for biologics and materials, but running physical experiments with minimal human oversight raises fresh data-governance and reproducibility questions enterprise AI buyers will need to work through.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11228-6)
