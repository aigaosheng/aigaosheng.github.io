# US AI vs China AI Brief: China Proposes Export Controls on AI Models

**Today:** China has drafted its own export controls on AI models, agents and chip designs, which matters if you track US-China rivalry or depend on Chinese open-weight models.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **China Proposes Export Controls on AI Models and Chip Designs**
*The Next Web · October 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing has drafted a licensing regime for advanced AI models, agents and chip design know-how, turning the logic of US export controls back on China's own technology.

China's commerce and science ministries published a draft on Friday covering 40 technologies, 23 of them in AI, with public comments open until November 15. For large models, the controls would reach weights, source code and other core components of models with at least 2 trillion parameters, or models ranking in the world's top 10 on tests such as performance and safety. Bloomberg and the Associated Press reported that Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 and Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max are above the size threshold, though the draft names no model. The draft also covers design know-how for AI chips at 14 nanometers or below, or built with 2.5D or 3D stacking. Open-source code and published research are exempt, Chinese firms can keep serving overseas users through apps, websites and APIs, and running a covered model on servers abroad counts as an export. A temporary registration requirement for exporters took effect the same day, and the ministry says the plan does not target any country or firm.

**Why it matters:** For Asia-based fintech and enterprise teams that build on Chinese open-weight models, the draft leaves hosted API access open, but future releases of the largest models may need a license or a filing step. Vendor-risk and model-sourcing reviews should track the final text after the comment period.

## Key Takeaways
- Export controls in the AI race now run in both directions: Washington restricts what China can buy, and Beijing is drafting limits on what it can sell.
- The draft separates serving a model (APIs, apps, websites stay open) from handing it over (weights, source code and overseas deployment are covered), with open-source releases offered a faster path.
- The text is still a draft, so scope, thresholds and enforcement could change before it is final.

## What to Watch
The November 15 close of public comment, after which Beijing says it will publish a final version of the catalog. Watch whether open-source and overseas-deployment carve-outs survive, and whether Washington responds publicly.
