## Strategic Takeaways

**1. The AI race is becoming institutionalized.**
The immediate US-China contest is no longer limited to model benchmarks. Diplomatic channels, sanctions, export controls, national-security reviews, and AI-safety discussions are becoming part of the competitive architecture.

**2. Model access is emerging as the next battleground.**
The dispute over distillation demonstrates that controlling GPUs alone may not be sufficient. Access to frontier-model APIs, inference outputs, routing platforms, and training data can also transfer capabilities across borders.

**3. China has stronger incentives to accelerate self-sufficiency.**
Every additional US restriction increases the economic value of domestic Chinese models, accelerators, cloud infrastructure, and AI software ecosystems. Conversely, restrictions may slow China's access to frontier compute while accelerating its efforts to eliminate dependence on US technology.

**4. The US retains the stronger frontier ecosystem, but the competitive metric is changing.**
The question is increasingly whether US advantages in frontier models, chips, cloud platforms, and capital can translate into durable strategic superiority before China closes gaps through domestic scale, engineering efficiency, and ecosystem integration.

**5. AI governance itself is becoming geopolitical infrastructure.**
The emerging US-China dialogue will matter not only for safety. Standards around model access, provenance, misuse, evaluation, cybersecurity, and responsible deployment could become another mechanism through which the two technology ecosystems compete for global influence.
