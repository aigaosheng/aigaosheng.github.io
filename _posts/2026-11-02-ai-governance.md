# AI Governance Brief: HKMA Launches Project SAGE For Agentic Finance

**Today:** Asia's financial regulators are moving first to set rules for agentic AI, while antitrust enforcers and courts elsewhere confront the technology's unintended side effects.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **HKMA Launches Project SAGE to Govern Agentic AI in Banking**

*Hong Kong Monetary Authority · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Hong Kong's central bank has opened a formal push to define accountability and identity rules for AI agents handling financial transactions before the technology scales across banking.

HKMA unveiled Project SAGE ("Securing Agentic Finance with Governance and Enablement") at Hong Kong FinTech Week 2026, forming a cross-sector Agentic Finance Expert Group and publishing a discussion paper covering agent identity verification, a "Know Your Agent" concept, interoperability standards, and legal and liability questions. The initiative sits within HKMA's wider DART framework and seeks industry feedback on named use cases including consumer and business payments, treasury operations and machine-to-machine transactions, with HKMA Chief Executive Eddie Yue stressing that institutions remain accountable for their AI agents' conduct.

**Why it matters:** Project SAGE signals that Asian regulators expect clear accountability chains before agentic payments go live in production, a template MAS, BSP and OJK are likely to draw on as similar use cases emerge across Singapore, the Philippines and Indonesia.

### 2. 🏦 **Turkey Opens Antitrust Probe Into Google's AI Overviews**

*Hürriyet Daily News · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Turkey's competition authority has launched a formal investigation into whether Google's AI-generated search summaries unfairly harm news publishers.

The Turkish Competition Board opened an abuse-of-dominance probe into Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode features, coupled with interim measures intended to protect publisher visibility while the case proceeds. Investigators will examine fair value distribution for news content, source-link prominence, licensing terms and opt-out options, with potential fines if violations are confirmed.

**Why it matters:** The case joins a growing list of competition actions targeting how generative AI search features redirect traffic and revenue from content creators, a precedent enterprise AI deployers and publishers in Asia should watch as similar AI search products expand regionally.

### 3. 🏦 **Korean Judges Report Widespread AI-Fabricated Legal Precedents**

*The Korea Herald · November 2, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Supreme Court survey found most South Korean judges have encountered AI-fabricated case citations, intensifying calls for formal courtroom safeguards.

The survey of 360 judges handling civil, family and administrative cases, conducted October 6–16, found about 80% had seen nonexistent or false precedents generated by AI, and 164 judges said they had personally cited such fabrications before catching the error. Nearly 92% of respondents called for institutional measures, including possible fines, mandatory verification steps and disciplinary action against lawyers who repeatedly cite false material.

**Why it matters:** As courts across Asia confront AI-generated fabrications in legal filings, the findings strengthen the case for mandatory verification protocols in legal and compliance workflows, a concern regulators in Singapore and the Philippines have separately raised about AI-generated evidence.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10922963)

## Key Takeaways

- Financial regulators in Asia are acting pre-emptively on agentic AI governance rather than waiting for a market incident to force their hand.
- Competition enforcement is expanding to cover AI-driven products like search summaries, a model Asian regulators may replicate as generative AI reshapes content distribution.
- AI reliability failures are surfacing well beyond finance and tech, pushing judicial systems toward mandatory verification and disclosure rules for AI-generated material.

## What to Watch

Industry feedback on HKMA's Project SAGE discussion paper and the outcome of Turkey's Google AI Overviews probe will test how fast "know your agent" and antitrust concepts move from proposal to enforceable rule. Separately, watch the 11 November deadline for frontier AI labs to report safety-incident accounting to US House committees and the UK ICO's 20 November deadline for evidence on agentic AI accountability.
