# AI Governance Brief: Trump Signals Openness to AI Guardrails

**Today:** A rare public shift from President Trump toward accepting federal AI guardrails is the clearest signal yet that Washington's hands-off posture could move toward binding rules, a shift enterprise AI vendors and fintechs will need to track alongside tightening EU and Asian frameworks.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Trump Says AI Risks "Can Be Managed," Open to Guardrails**

*Reuters · November 9, 2026*

**Bottom line:** President Trump's first public openness to federal AI guardrails raises the odds of eventual US rules, though he announced no specific policy.

Speaking to CBS's "60 Minutes" in an interview reported by Reuters, Trump said AI dangers "can be managed" and that he "doesn't mind guardrails." He added he did not want regulation to leave the US in "a deep second position" behind China, which he said faces no comparable restrictions, while signalling willingness to work with Democrats on safeguards. He did not specify which guardrails he would support.

**Why it matters:** Any move toward US federal AI rules would reshape compliance planning for global AI vendors and financial institutions operating across borders, including in Singapore, the Philippines and Indonesia, where local regulators already watch US and EU positions when calibrating their own AI risk frameworks. The comments land as Democrats, anticipating a Congressional majority from January 2027, prepare hearings and bills on AI testing, liability and data-centre energy costs, meaning the US debate is shifting from "whether" to "how" to regulate AI.

## Key Takeaways

- Washington's AI policy stance is softening from pure self-regulation toward conditional openness to guardrails, though no legislative text has moved.
- The shift creates a contrast with the EU, where AI Act enforcement procedures are already being finalised, widening the gap between US ambiguity and EU specificity that multinational fintechs must bridge.
- Congressional Democrats are positioning AI testing and liability legislation for 2027, meaning the current calm in US federal AI policy is likely temporary regardless of who holds the White House.

## What to Watch

The European Commission's public feedback window on draft AI Act enforcement procedures (Articles 75c-75d) closed today, November 9, 2026, with formal adoption targeted for Q4 2026. Separately, watch the 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila on November 15-17, 2026, where the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement, which embeds AI governance and cross-border data provisions, is expected to be signed.
