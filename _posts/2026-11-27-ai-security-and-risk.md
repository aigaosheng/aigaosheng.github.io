# AI Security & Risk Brief: Swiss Critical Infrastructure Hit by AI Agents

**Today:** Switzerland confirmed five AI-agent-driven cyberattacks against critical infrastructure in days, SAP disclosed a suspected AI-agent intrusion, and Singapore's NCS convened global AI labs to arm local CISOs against the escalating threat.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Swiss Critical Infrastructure Breached in Four of Five AI-Agent Attacks**

*The Hindu / Reuters · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Switzerland's Federal Office for Cybersecurity (BACS) reported five cyberattacks in recent days using AI agents against companies and critical infrastructure operators, with four successful compromises.

Three of the targeted operators were in electricity, water, and telecommunications, according to Swiss public broadcaster SRF . BACS did not attribute the attacks but advised companies to establish protective measures including regular cyber tests . Italy's CSIRT also issued a critical alert about AI-agent-driven intrusions capable of exploring multiple attack paths simultaneously and writing malware to bypass defenses within hours .

**Why it matters:** This is among the first confirmed cases of autonomous AI agents successfully breaching critical infrastructure at scale, validating warnings that agentic attack frameworks lower the technical bar for adversaries. For Asia-based operators of utilities, telcos, and financial market infrastructure, the Swiss incidents underscore that AI-agent threats have moved from theoretical to operational.

### 2. 🔒 **SAP Investigates Suspected AI-Agent Intrusion Affecting GitHub Source Code**

*Yonhap / Der Spiegel · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** SAP has launched an internal security operation dubbed "Project Granite" after attackers suspected of using AI agents stole employee authentication tokens and manipulated SAP-related source code on GitHub.

Der Spiegel reported that SAP told employees it found suspicious activity and was acting swiftly to protect systems and customers, with no evidence of customer data exfiltration . Attackers reportedly stole authentication tokens and copied or altered source code hosted on GitHub. Microsoft, which operates GitHub, warned earlier this month that attacks exploiting the AI-based penetration testing tool Artex were increasing .

**Why it matters:** An intrusion at Europe's largest software company affecting source code repositories raises supply-chain risk concerns for SAP's enterprise customers across Asia. The suspected involvement of AI agents in credential theft and code manipulation signals that attackers are targeting the development pipeline, not just production systems—a vector that many enterprise security programs under-monitor.

### 3. 🏦 **NCS Mobilises Global AI Labs to Equip Singapore CISOs Against AI-Powered Threats**

*Taiwan News / PR Newswire · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** NCS brought together experts from OpenAI, Mistral AI, Google Cloud Security, Mandiant, and Palo Alto Networks at its Frontier AI Cyber Day in Singapore, and signed an MOU with Palo Alto Networks to establish a joint Frontier AI Cyber Defence programme.

The event addressed offensive frameworks such as ARTEX that enable attackers with minimal technical expertise to autonomously discover vulnerabilities and launch sophisticated attacks at scale . NCS outlined five capability areas: continuous threat validation, frontier AI red-teaming, adaptive virtual patching, autonomous rapid response, and autonomous breach detection. The proposed Palo Alto collaboration will explore AI-assisted vulnerability discovery, AI-driven security operations, and protection for generative and agentic AI systems.

**Why it matters:** Singapore is building institutional infrastructure to help enterprises defend against the same AI-agent attack techniques that hit Swiss infrastructure and SAP. For financial institutions and critical infrastructure operators in the city-state, NCS's partnership model offers a way to access frontier-lab expertise without building those capabilities in-house.

### 4. 🔒 **Florida Man Sentenced to 47 Months for AI Voice-Cloning Crypto Scam**

*The Times of India · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** David Anthony Sanchez, 36, was sentenced to 47 months in federal prison for using AI to clone streamer N3on's voice to defraud crypto investors of approximately $260,000.

Sanchez posed as the streamer and his representative, signed agreements in N3on's name, and sent an AI-generated voice recording to confirm a memecoin endorsement deal . Victims transferred roughly $260,000 in cryptocurrency to accounts he controlled. He pleaded guilty to money laundering and aggravated identity theft and was ordered to pay restitution.

**Why it matters:** This case illustrates how accessible voice-cloning tools have become for financial fraud, with a single AI-generated audio clip sufficient to close a six-figure crypto deal. For fintech platforms and payment processors in Asia, it reinforces the need for voice authentication systems to incorporate liveness detection and out-of-band verification, particularly for high-value transactions initiated through messaging channels.

### 5. 🔒 **Malaysian Police Bust AI Face-Swap Gang Impersonating NYPD**

*8world · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Malaysian police arrested 11 foreign nationals in Kuala Lumpur for operating a cross-border extortion syndicate that used AI face-swapping and forged NYPD documents to threaten victims in the United States.

The gang, active for about six months, rented an apartment for RM15,000 monthly and used AI to impersonate New York police officers, complete with uniforms and fabricated police documents, to extort money from Americans under false criminal accusations . Suspects came from South Africa, Taiwan, Mexico, Zimbabwe, Kenya, and India.

**Why it matters:** The operation demonstrates that AI-enabled fraud is now a transnational, multi-jurisdictional business, with Southeast Asia serving as a base for attacks targeting Western victims. For Singapore and regional financial intelligence units, it underscores the need for cross-border law enforcement coordination and for banks to monitor for extortion-related payment patterns, not just traditional account takeover.

## Key Takeaways

- **Agentic attacks are confirmed in production:** Switzerland's critical infrastructure breaches and SAP's token theft represent a shift from AI-assisted to AI-executed intrusions, where agents autonomously discover and exploit vulnerabilities.
- **Singapore is building a defense ecosystem:** NCS's convening of frontier labs and its Palo Alto partnership signal that the city-state is treating AI-powered cyber threats as a national resilience priority for its financial and infrastructure sectors.
- **Fraud is the most accessible AI weapon:** Voice cloning and face-swap scams in the US and Malaysia show that low-cost, widely available AI tools are already enabling six-figure financial crimes, with Southeast Asia as an operational hub.

## What to Watch

BACS's investigation into the Swiss attacks and whether the agentic frameworks used—reportedly including ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI—are formally attributed to a state or criminal group, which would trigger coordinated international response measures.
