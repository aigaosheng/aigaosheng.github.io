# US AI vs China AI Brief: China's AI Export Curbs Reshape Global Tech Access

**Today:** Beijing's draft rules to control outbound AI technology are taking clearer shape, showing enterprise and fintech leaders how China intends to keep its most valuable models and chip designs at home even as it sells access abroad.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **China's Draft AI Export Curbs Would Split Models Into Domestic and Overseas Versions**

*Reuters (via Taipei Times) · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's proposed export-control catalogue would let foreign users keep accessing Chinese AI through apps and cloud platforms, while requiring government approval for transfers of the underlying models, weights and chip-design know-how.

Reuters reports that the draft rules, registered on an interim basis since mid-October with a public comment period open until November 15, set three thresholds for controlled technology: models trained with more than 10^26 computing operations, models exceeding two trillion parameters, or systems ranking in the global top ten on performance benchmarks. Chinese developers such as DeepSeek, Moonshot and Z.ai may need to maintain separate domestic and overseas versions of the same model, and regulators are also weighing curbs that could stop overseas foundries including TSMC and Qualcomm from manufacturing advanced chips based on designs from Huawei, Alibaba or ByteDance.

**Why it matters:** The rules mirror, in reverse, Washington's approach of controlling outbound hardware and inbound capital — Beijing is now moving to control outbound software, models and chip-design know-how. For enterprise and fintech buyers in Singapore and the region using Chinese AI tools or cloud infrastructure, the API-access layer looks set to stay open, but deeper technology partnerships or self-hosting arrangements with Chinese model providers will face new compliance friction once the rules are finalised by year-end.

### 2. 🤖 **Cambricon Co-Founder Urges Shared US-China AI Safety Standards**

*South China Morning Post · October 31, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A leading Chinese chip executive broke from the national rivalry narrative to call for a common "ethics checker" that both Chinese and American frontier models would have to satisfy.

Speaking at the World Laureates Forum in Shanghai, Cambricon Technologies co-founder Chen Yunji said rising AI capabilities and cyber risks make it necessary for "all human beings and nations" to agree on shared safeguards covering leading models from DeepSeek to OpenAI, according to SCMP. Chen, a professor at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, helped build Cambricon into one of China's largest domestic AI chip designers, with a market value cited at roughly $90 billion.

**Why it matters:** The appeal signals that even figures embedded in China's chip self-sufficiency push see value in cross-border safety coordination, a notable data point as Washington and Beijing prepare to resume their bilateral AI dialogue. For regulated industries like payments and banking, any eventual shared technical safeguards would matter more than diplomatic statements, but the gap between this kind of industry appeal and actual government-to-government standards remains wide.

## Key Takeaways

- Beijing's draft export catalogue is the clearest sign yet that China intends to compete globally on AI access while tightly guarding the underlying technology, forcing a two-tier model strategy on its own AI champions.
- Global foundries and chip-design houses with China exposure, including TSMC and Qualcomm, face a new layer of regulatory uncertainty on top of existing US controls.
- Calls for shared US-China AI safety standards are starting to come from industry figures rather than governments, a gap worth watching as formal dialogue channels resume.

## What to Watch

China's public comment window on its AI export-control catalogue closes November 15, 2026, with Beijing expected to adopt final rules by year-end — enterprise and fintech teams with Chinese AI vendor relationships should watch for implementation guidance in that window.
