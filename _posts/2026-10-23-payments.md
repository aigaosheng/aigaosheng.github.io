# Payments Brief: MAS Expands BLOOM Cross-Border Digital Money Initiative

Today: Southeast Asia is strengthening the foundations for cross-border digital money, with Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand coordinating policy and industry efforts on interoperability, settlement and transaction controls.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 MAS, Malaysia and Thailand Expand BLOOM for Cross-Border Digital Money

Monetary Authority of Singapore · October 23, 2026

Bottom line: Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand are coordinating approaches to make cross-border transactions using different forms of digital money more interoperable and practical.

The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) announced that Bank Negara Malaysia and the Bank of Thailand have joined its BLOOM initiative as policymaker members. The expanded collaboration covers tokenised deposits, regulated stablecoins and settlement across financial institutions, networks and jurisdictions. New industry participants include Airwallex, Mastercard, Maybank, Monee, Nium, Ripple, ShopeePay, Visa and the World Bank Group. ([Source: MAS announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-bnm-and-bot-partner-with-industry-to-advance-cross-border-settlement-in-digital-money))

Why it matters: Common approaches to acceptance, settlement and interoperability could reduce the cost and complexity of connecting financial institutions across Asian markets. The initiative also highlights the need for programmable safeguards, including identity verification, sanctions screening and transaction controls, alongside technical integration.

### 2. 🌐 Sweden Reopens Debate on a Digital Krona

Reuters · October 22, 2026

Bottom line: Sweden's central bank is urging renewed consideration of a digital krona as stablecoins and Europe's digital euro reshape the competitive and resilience questions surrounding national payment systems.

The Riksbank has renewed calls for parliament to consider the conditions needed to introduce an e-krona. The debate reflects concerns about the future role of central-bank money as cash usage declines and alternative digital payment instruments expand. Any introduction would require legislative and practical preparation rather than an immediate launch. ([Source: Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/swedish-central-bank-revives-e-krona-debate-digital-euro-stablecoin-threat-grows-2026-10-22/))

Why it matters: Payment-system resilience and access to public money are becoming strategic considerations alongside speed and cost. For Asian payment providers, the broader lesson is that private digital money and public payment infrastructure may develop in parallel, with implications for interoperability, contingency planning and regulatory design.

## Key Takeaways

- Regional interoperability is becoming a priority: BLOOM's expanded membership signals a coordinated effort to address the practical barriers to cross-border digital money, rather than treating each national market as a standalone system.
- Digital money requires more than faster settlement: Identity checks, sanctions screening, transaction controls and clear operating arrangements remain essential to commercial deployment.
- Public and private payment infrastructure will coexist: Central banks' digital-currency discussions reinforce the importance of designing payment systems that balance innovation, resilience and trust.
