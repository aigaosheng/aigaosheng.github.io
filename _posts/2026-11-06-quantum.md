# Quantum Brief: IonQ Revenue Jumps 336%, Raises Full-Year Outlook

**Today:** Two of the best-funded quantum computing vendors posted blowout quarterly results and a bank-scale cloud contract within hours of each other, signalling that quantum compute spending is moving from pilots to committed, multi-year revenue well ahead of fault tolerance.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **IonQ Posts Record Revenue, Raises Full-Year Guidance**

*IonQ · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** IonQ's record quarter and raised outlook show commercial demand for trapped-ion quantum computing is scaling faster than its own earlier forecasts.

IonQ reported record third-quarter revenue of $173.8 million, up 336% year-on-year and above analyst expectations, helped by its SkyWater acquisition and demand for its Superion 256 system. The company raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $505–515 million, up from $450–460 million, while remaining performance obligations more than doubled to $1.2 billion.

**Why it matters:** Backlog growth of this size suggests quantum compute demand, and the parallel quantum-security risk it creates, is arriving faster than many enterprise and banking roadmaps assumed, adding urgency to post-quantum migration planning across Asian financial institutions.

### 2. 💳 **Quantinuum Signs Largest-Ever Deal With a Global Bank**

*Quantinuum · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Quantinuum's biggest cloud contract to date, with an unnamed global financial institution, shows a major bank is locking in multi-year quantum compute access well before fault-tolerant systems arrive.

Quantinuum reported Q3 revenue of $9.1 million, up 233% year-on-year, and raised its full-year outlook to $31–34 million from $28–32 million. The quarter included the largest cloud agreement in company history, a multiyear deal giving the financial institution committed access to its Nexus software platform and its planned Apollo fault-tolerant system, targeted for 2029.

**Why it matters:** A major bank pre-committing to years of quantum compute access is an early signal of how seriously global financial institutions are treating quantum as a near-term capability, a trend Singapore and regional banks tracking MAS-linked quantum initiatives should watch closely.

### 3. 🔬 **IBM, AMD and Oak Ridge Target Quantum Error Correction**

*The Quantum Insider · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A new co-design platform pairing IBM's quantum hardware with AMD-powered supercomputing aims to close the error-correction gap that still separates today's quantum machines from practical, fault-tolerant use.

Oak Ridge National Laboratory, IBM and AMD launched a joint platform integrating IBM's 120-qubit Nighthawk processor with ORNL's Umbra high-performance computing system, built on AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and EPYC CPUs. The partners are targeting 100 logical qubits running 100,000 error-corrected operations by late 2028, roughly 230 times beyond IBM's current benchmark of 70 logical qubits and 438 operations.

**Why it matters:** Error correction remains the central bottleneck determining when quantum computers could meaningfully threaten or benefit financial-grade cryptography, making this a key indicator for how urgently payment networks and banks need to execute post-quantum migration.

### 4. ⚛️ **UK Opens Enterprise Access to Quantum Software Tools**

*The Quantum Insider · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The UK's National Quantum Computing Centre is opening enterprise access to quantum software through a new licensing deal with Phasecraft, lowering the barrier for businesses to trial quantum tools.

Under the agreement, UK businesses, researchers and academic institutions can apply from late November 2026 for access to three Phasecraft tools covering materials and molecular modelling, density functional theory, and optimisation for finance, logistics and infrastructure problems, with a first user cohort starting Spring 2027.

**Why it matters:** Government-backed enterprise access schemes reduce the cost of quantum experimentation and could become a template other hubs, including Singapore's national quantum programmes, adapt as they compete for pilot projects and talent.

### 5. ⚛️ **Toshiba Wins UK Contract for Quantum Networking**

*The Quantum Insider · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Toshiba Europe secured an Innovate UK contract to build prototype quantum networking subsystems as part of a wider £20 million push toward commercial quantum networks.

The award, to be validated with telecom partners BT and Colt Technology Services, funds prototype subsystems for long-distance quantum links and multi-user networks designed to run over existing fibre infrastructure, supporting the UK's target of commercial quantum network deployment by 2035.

**Why it matters:** Quantum networking is the connective layer for future quantum-safe communications between data centres and distributed quantum processors, work that will eventually shape how payment and banking infrastructure secures data links against quantum-era threats.

### 6. 🔒 **QNu Labs Launches Quantum-Safe Security Training**

*The Quantum Insider · November 6, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's QNu Labs introduced a corporate training programme, backed by the National Quantum Mission, to prepare enterprise security teams for the shift to quantum-safe cryptography.

The 12-hour, three-day programme covers quantum key distribution, quantum random number generation, post-quantum cryptography and cryptographic inventory management, taught by QNu engineers alongside academics from IITs, NITs, IIITs and IISERs, aimed at CISOs, risk officers and IT architects through workshops or executive briefings.

**Why it matters:** As regulators push banks toward crypto-agility ahead of post-quantum migration deadlines, structured training like this gives Asia-based security teams a concrete, near-term way to close the "harvest now, decrypt later" skills gap.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/06/qnu-labs-corporate-training-quantum-safe-security/)

## Key Takeaways

- Quantum vendors IonQ and Quantinuum are converting pilots into multi-year, bank-backed cloud contracts well before fault-tolerant machines exist, ahead of many enterprise timelines.
- Error correction remains the industry's shared bottleneck, with IBM, AMD and a US national lab now co-designing hardware and HPC stacks specifically to close that gap.
- Enterprise-access and training programmes from the UK's NQCC and India's QNu Labs point to a parallel race to build the tooling and workforce needed both to use quantum systems and to defend against quantum-era threats.
