# AI Security & Risk Brief: FTC Scrutinises OpenAI and Anthropic

**Today:** Regulatory scrutiny of AI safety is intensifying, cybersecurity companies are expanding AI-driven defence capabilities, and Singapore is highlighting the safeguards needed for autonomous AI agents.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 FTC Scrutinises OpenAI and Anthropic Over AI Safety Risks

**Reuters · October 15, 2026**

**Bottom line:** The US Federal Trade Commission is reportedly seeking extensive documentation from OpenAI and Anthropic concerning AI safety, security, privacy and potential consumer harm.

According to Reuters, citing an Axios report, the FTC issued lengthy subpoenas as part of its examination of AI developers' practices. The reported inquiry covers safety incidents, security measures, privacy policies and marketing claims, against a backdrop of concerns about harmful or deceptive behaviour by AI systems.

The investigation underscores growing scrutiny of how AI companies assess risks, communicate product capabilities and demonstrate that safeguards work in practice.

**Why it matters:** Enterprise buyers should treat vendor security evidence, incident disclosure and safety evaluations as core procurement requirements. For financial institutions and other regulated businesses in Singapore and Asia, developments in the US may also influence international vendor-risk expectations.

### 2. 🤖 Cohere Moves to Acquire AI Cybersecurity Startup AISLE

**Reuters · October 15, 2026**

**Bottom line:** Cohere has signed a letter of intent to acquire AISLE, an AI cybersecurity startup focused on software vulnerability detection.

The proposed acquisition would strengthen Cohere's cybersecurity capabilities for enterprise and public-sector customers while expanding its European presence. AISLE, founded in 2025, has reportedly identified more than 500 software bugs, including vulnerabilities in OpenSSL. Financial terms were not disclosed.

The move reflects growing interest in using AI to accelerate vulnerability discovery and improve defensive security operations.

**Why it matters:** AI-assisted vulnerability detection could help development and security teams identify flaws earlier, but organisations still need reliable validation, prioritisation and remediation. Buyers should assess whether these tools integrate into existing security workflows and produce reproducible findings rather than relying on automated detection alone.

🔗 [Read the original Reuters report](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/cohere-moves-buy-ai-cybersecurity-startup-aisle-2026-10-15/)

### 3. 🔒 Bank of England Highlights Recovery Capabilities Against AI-Enabled Cyber Risks

**Reuters · October 15, 2026**

**Bottom line:** Bank of England Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden has urged banks to strengthen their ability to recover from cyberattacks, including attacks enabled by AI.

Speaking at the Institute of International Finance's annual meeting in Bangkok, Breeden highlighted the importance of independent recovery infrastructure capable of restoring core services if primary systems are compromised.

The warning reflects a broader shift in cyber resilience: preventive controls cannot guarantee that every attack will be stopped. Organisations must also prepare to restore essential operations when attackers bypass existing defences.

**Why it matters:** Banks and payment providers across Asia should test whether they can restore critical services independently of compromised production environments. Recovery exercises should cover identity systems, privileged access, payment processing, backups and dependencies on external technology providers.

🔗 [Read the original Reuters report](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/banks-need-bare-metal-recovery-face-ai-risks-says-boes-breeden-2026-10-15/)

### 4. 🔒 Singapore Highlights Security Controls for Agentic AI

**Cyber Security Agency of Singapore · October 15, 2026**

**Bottom line:** Singapore's Cyber Security Agency and Google have examined security and operational risks associated with increasingly autonomous AI agents through a scenario-based exercise.

The exercise, held during Singapore International Cyber Week 2026, brought together cybersecurity policymakers, critical-infrastructure leaders and AI safety experts. Discussions highlighted the importance of runtime monitoring, hardened execution environments, access controls and independent verification of consequential agent actions.

Participants also identified opportunities for greater collaboration in threat intelligence, pre-deployment testing, agent identity verification and incident response.

The central challenge is ensuring that autonomous systems remain within defined permissions even when they interact with multiple services or execute actions at machine speed.

**Why it matters:** Singapore-based financial institutions and enterprises adopting AI agents should build controls around executable permissions rather than relying solely on model instructions. High-impact actions should be constrained by enforceable safeguards, with audit trails, continuous monitoring and human escalation for sensitive decisions.

🔗 [Read the original CSA announcement](https://www.csa.gov.sg/news-events/press-releases/outcome-statement-tabletop-exercise-on-agentic-ai-security-and-control-at-the-singapore-international-cyber-week-2026/)

## Key Takeaways

- **AI safety is increasingly an accountability issue.** Regulatory scrutiny reinforces the need for documented safeguards, transparent incident handling and evidence supporting claims about AI capabilities.
- **Defensive AI requires operational validation.** Automated vulnerability discovery can improve security workflows, but effective outcomes depend on verification, prioritisation and remediation.
- **Resilience must extend beyond prevention.** Financial institutions and enterprises need independently recoverable infrastructure and enforceable controls over autonomous agents.

## What to Watch

Watch for further details on the FTC's reported investigation, formal announcements on Cohere's proposed AISLE acquisition, and concrete security measures emerging from Singapore's agentic AI discussions. For financial institutions, a key signal will be whether these developments lead to stronger recovery testing, agent permission controls and incident-response practices.
