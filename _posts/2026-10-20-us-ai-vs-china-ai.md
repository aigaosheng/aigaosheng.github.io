# US AI vs China AI Brief: Chinese Tech Giants Order Nvidia Chips

**Today:** Chinese demand for AI computing hardware and Washington's restrictions on DeepSeek highlight how export controls are reshaping competition over chips, models and access to AI technology.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent Reportedly Place Major Nvidia Chip Orders**

*Reuters · October 20, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Chinese technology giants are reportedly seeking large quantities of Nvidia hardware, highlighting the gap between AI computing demand and available supply.

ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have reportedly placed orders worth more than $35 billion in total for over 3 million Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 graphics cards. These workstation-class chips are among the Nvidia products reportedly available for sale under US export restrictions. Production constraints could limit fulfilment, while Chinese buyers seek additional computing capacity for AI inference workloads.

**Why it matters:** Export controls can redirect rather than eliminate demand for US-designed AI hardware. For Asian enterprises, chip availability, procurement lead times and inference costs are increasingly important considerations when planning AI infrastructure.

### 2. 🏦 **Beijing Condemns US Restrictions on DeepSeek**

*Associated Press · October 20, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's objections to US restrictions on DeepSeek add another point of friction to the technology and national-security rivalry between the two countries.

Washington has moved to restrict DeepSeek software and services in federal civilian agency systems, citing concerns about the handling of sensitive information and potential access by Chinese authorities. Beijing has criticised the US position and said it would defend Chinese companies' rights.

The dispute reflects broader tensions involving AI services, semiconductor exports and the international use of Chinese-developed technology.

**Why it matters:** Government restrictions can influence enterprise procurement and technology risk assessments beyond the public sector. Singapore-based organisations evaluating US and Chinese AI services should consider data protection, contractual safeguards, regulatory obligations and the possibility of changing cross-border restrictions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Compute access remains strategic:** Demand for eligible Nvidia hardware highlights the importance of chip supply, infrastructure planning and inference economics in China's AI ecosystem.
- **AI procurement is increasingly geopolitical:** Restrictions affecting AI providers can create compliance, vendor-concentration and business-continuity risks for multinational enterprises.
- **Technology competition extends beyond chips:** US-China rivalry increasingly encompasses AI models, software distribution, national security and access to international markets.

## What to Watch

Monitor Nvidia's reported shipments to Chinese customers and any changes in US export licensing. Further restrictions on Chinese AI providers, or new measures from Beijing affecting foreign technology companies, could reshape enterprise AI procurement decisions across Asia.
