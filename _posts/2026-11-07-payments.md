# Payments Brief: Bank Lobby Presses Treasury On Crypto AML Gaps

**Today:** Regulators and industry bodies are racing to close anti-money-laundering gaps around stablecoins just as emerging markets tighten their own crypto-FX controls, a pairing that matters for any payments business moving money across borders.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Bank Policy Institute Presses Treasury On Crypto AML Gaps**

*Bank Policy Institute · November 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US bank lobby is urging Treasury to extend anti-money-laundering obligations to all digital asset providers, warning that unregulated stablecoin and crypto channels are outpacing bank oversight.

The Bank Policy Institute sent Treasury a letter calling for FinCEN to designate all digital asset service providers, including custodians, exchanges and DeFi operators, as "financial institutions" under the Bank Secrecy Act. The letter cites Chainalysis data showing illicit crypto addresses received $154 billion in 2025, a 162% year-on-year increase, and references the roughly $6.3 billion Binance hack as evidence banks lack visibility into transaction chains once funds move to unhosted wallets. Separately, BPI's weekly analysis found only two of the ten largest dollar stablecoins currently meet the reserve requirements the GENIUS Act will impose from January 2027.

**Why it matters:** If Treasury adopts BPI's recommendations, stablecoin issuers and intermediaries serving Asian corridors could face tighter Bank Secrecy Act-style compliance and reporting obligations, raising the bar for any BaaS or payments player routing settlement through US dollar stablecoins.

### 2. 🏦 **Nigeria's Central Bank Folds Stablecoin P2P Trades Into FX Controls**

*Techpoint Africa · November 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beyond the capital floors already announced, Nigeria's new crypto rules now treat naira-to-stablecoin peer-to-peer trades as regulated foreign exchange transactions requiring explicit central bank approval.

Newly disclosed details of the Central Bank of Nigeria's Regulations for the Operations of Non-Security Virtual Assets, effective November 5, require P2P platforms to match customer names and Bank Verification Numbers to the naira account used, and bar any platform from facilitating naira-to-dollar-stablecoin trades without express CBN approval, with settlement routed through designated FX dealers. Platforms also cannot split trades to evade FX limits, and operators have 30 days to register and 180 days to seek a full licence.

**Why it matters:** The move signals that emerging-market regulators increasingly see retail stablecoin trading as an FX-control issue rather than a licensing one, a precedent Asian regulators weighing their own stablecoin frameworks are likely to watch.

### 3. 🌐 **Visa Research Finds Scam Fears Shadow Saudi Remittance Growth**

*Arab News (Visa) · November 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Visa's Money Travels 2026 research found 45% of surveyed Saudi Arabia consumers send money internationally, but one in four remitters reported being targeted by or falling victim to a scam.

The study, based on a Morning Consult survey of 1,117 Saudi adults conducted earlier in 2026, found 54% of remitters now use digital channels such as online banking or mobile apps. Visa framed trust, not just speed or cost, as the deciding factor in remittance channel choice, noting World Bank data putting the average global remittance cost at 6.36% of the amount sent against a 2030 target of 3%.

**Why it matters:** The same trust-versus-fraud tension is playing out across Asian remittance corridors, where Visa's own regional research has flagged comparably high scam exposure in India and the Philippines, underscoring the need for stronger authentication as digital remittances scale.

### 4. 📊 **USDC Supply Shrinks $800 Million As Redemptions Outpace Issuance**

*KuCoin · November 7, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Circle's USDC circulation fell by roughly $800 million over seven days to $69.2 billion as redemptions briefly outran new issuance.

Circle issued about $12.7 billion and redeemed about $13.5 billion of USDC in the period, leaving reserves at roughly $70.5 billion against the reduced supply. The dip follows Circle's Q3 2026 results, in which USDC accounted for 79% of all stablecoin transaction volume even as reserve income fell on lower interest rates.

**Why it matters:** Short-term supply swings in the largest regulated dollar stablecoin are a leading indicator for corporate treasury and cross-border payment flows that increasingly rely on USDC as settlement rails, including in Asia-Pacific corridors.

## Key Takeaways

- US and Nigerian regulators are converging on the same conclusion from different angles: stablecoin flows need the same AML and FX oversight as traditional money movement, not lighter-touch treatment.
- Remittance growth across the Middle East and Asia keeps running ahead of consumer trust, with scam exposure now a bigger adoption barrier than cost or speed in Visa's research.
- Stablecoin market metrics remain volatile week to week even as institutional and corporate use cases mature, a reminder that reserve and liquidity monitoring still matters for any payments business settling in USDC or similar tokens.

## What to Watch

Watch for the US Treasury's response to BPI's AML recommendations and any movement on the GENIUS Act's January 2027 reserve compliance deadline, alongside how Nigerian P2P platforms adapt to the 30-day registration window under the CBN's new FX-linked stablecoin rules.
