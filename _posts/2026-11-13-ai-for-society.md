# AI for Society Brief: UK Retailers Pivot Hiring Toward AI Roles

**Today:** Three new signals show how unevenly AI's social effects are landing — on who gets hired, whose voice can be faked, and which teenagers get a head start with the technology.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **UK Retailers Pivot Hiring Toward AI and Tech Roles**

*Bloomberg · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** UK retailers are redirecting recruitment toward AI and technology positions even as they pull back hiring elsewhere, reshaping retail workforces around AI capability.

Bloomberg reporting found retail accounted for almost half of all technology job vacancies posted by consumer-facing companies so far this year, outpacing sectors such as luxury, travel and leisure. Retailers are concentrating recruitment on AI and tech skills while cutting back in other areas, including front-line, in-store roles, as cost pressures and automation reshape headcount priorities.

**Why it matters:** For fintech and payments firms competing for the same scarce AI talent, this intensifies wage pressure on AI-skilled hires and raises the question of how displaced operations and support staff get redeployed — a dynamic already playing out as Singapore and regional banks reskill workforces for AI-enabled roles.

### 2. 👥 **Malaysia Warns Election Deepfakes Are 'Dangerously Accessible'**

*The Star (Malaysia) · November 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Malaysian consumer watchdog says generative AI has made convincing political deepfakes achievable with nothing more than a smartphone and a short audio clip, outpacing platforms' ability to moderate them during election periods.

The Malaysia Cyber Consumer Association said a 15-to-30-second audio sample and freely available AI tools now suffice to fabricate a deepfake video of a politician, pointing to a fake TikTok clip of a Barisan Nasional leader that circulated during the Melaka state election. Malaysia's communications regulator logged 7,967 deepfake-related complaints as of mid-2026, up from 917 in 2024, against only 713 political deepfake posts removed between January 2025 and September 2026.

**Why it matters:** The gap between complaint volume and takedowns shows moderation lagging generation speed — a warning for Singapore and other Southeast Asian markets approaching election cycles, and for any business relying on social platforms to protect executive or brand likeness.

### 3. 👥 **RAND Finds Income Gap Widening in Teen AI Use for School**

*RAND Corporation · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Higher-income US parents are substantially more likely than lower-income parents to use and encourage AI for their children's schoolwork, and RAND says the gap is widening rather than closing.

A RAND survey of roughly 1,000 parents and 1,800 teenagers found 67% of higher-income parents encouraged their children to use AI for schoolwork, versus 45% of lower-income parents — a disparity RAND reported had grown from 15 to 21 percentage points over 15 months. Parental encouragement was more strongly linked to teen AI use than almost any other factor studied besides age.

**Why it matters:** If AI literacy compounds existing advantage rather than levelling it, employers and policymakers building AI-skills pipelines — including Singapore's workforce-reskilling push — may need to target support toward lower-income households and schools rather than assume uptake is broad-based.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.rand.org/news/press/2026/11/higher-income-parents-more-likely-to-use-ai-encourage.html)

## Key Takeaways

- AI's labour-market effects are bifurcating: demand surges for AI-skilled hires even as adjacent roles are cut, intensifying competition for scarce talent.
- Generative AI is outpacing institutional response in both information integrity (election deepfakes) and education (teen AI access), widening gaps faster than moderation or policy can close them.
- Singapore and Southeast Asian regulators and employers face a parallel challenge: protect information integrity ahead of elections while ensuring AI-skills training reaches underserved groups, not just well-resourced ones.

## What to Watch

Watch whether Southeast Asian election bodies follow India's Election Commission in mandating deepfake labelling, following Malaysia's complaint data, and whether UK-style AI-driven hiring shifts in retail show up in upcoming Singapore and regional labour-market releases.
