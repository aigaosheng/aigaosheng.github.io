# AI Governance Brief: EU AI Summit Ends With No Financing Pledges

**Today:** Two governance stories show the gap between AI ambition and implementation: Brussels' gigafactory rhetoric met a stalled budget process, while Manila published the granular machinery behind its new AI oversight body.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **EU Leaders Leave Dublin AI Summit Without Binding Financing Commitments**

*Xinhua · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The EU's first leaders-level AI summit produced statements of intent but no new money, leaving its flagship gigafactory plan stuck pending budget talks.

European Council President António Costa described the Dublin discussion as the first of several, with investment, adoption and governance questions deferred to a follow-up meeting in February 2027. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen's plan for seven AI gigafactories mobilising roughly €30 billion in computing capacity remains tied to negotiations over the bloc's next long-term budget, and analysts cited by Xinhua put Europe's broader tech-investment shortfall at around €500 billion. The Centre for European Policy Studies also flagged that many proposed sites sit outside established research hubs and that reliance on foreign chip suppliers risks new dependencies.

**Why it matters:** For enterprises planning EU AI infrastructure or compliance investment, the summit signals that sovereign compute capacity and funding clarity are further away than the original pledge suggested; Asian cloud and chip suppliers should expect the financing timeline, not the ambition, to be the real constraint on EU demand.

### 2. 🏦 **Philippines Discloses Full Structure of Its New National AI Council**

*Manila Shaker · November 15, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Newly published detail on Executive Order 131 shows the Philippines' AI Council will run on six technical working groups and a quarterly reporting cycle straight to the President.

Beyond the council's chair and ten-member agency roster already announced, the fuller account describes working groups covering institutions, data, research and development, capabilities, infrastructure and demand generation, each tasked with drafting action plans. The Department of Information and Communications Technology will operate a digital tracking platform, with agencies filing quarterly updates and the council reporting to President Marcos within 30 days of each quarter's end; the governance framework itself is due for review at least every two years.

**Why it matters:** The reporting cadence and working-group split give regulated fintechs and BaaS providers operating in the Philippines a concrete oversight rhythm to plan around, rather than the broad principles disclosed when the executive order was first signed.

## Key Takeaways

- Governments are increasingly willing to announce AI ambition publicly while leaving financing and enforcement detail for later, as seen in both the EU's deferred budget talks and the Philippines' phased working-group rollout.
- Implementation detail, not headline announcements, is where compliance and infrastructure planning teams will find the information they actually need to act on.
- Asia-Pacific jurisdictions are moving faster on concrete institutional mechanics (reporting lines, working groups) than some larger blocs are moving on financing.

## What to Watch

Watch for the EU's next AI-focused leaders' discussion, slated for February 2027, for any firmer financing commitment on the gigafactory programme, and for the Philippines' National AI Council's first quarterly report to President Marcos under Executive Order 131.
