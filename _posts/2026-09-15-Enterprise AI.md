# Enterprise AI Brief — 2026-09-15

## Top Stories 

### 1. **Air India Expands Salesforce Agentforce Into Complex Customer-Service Workflows**

* **Source**: Salesforce · September 15, 2026
* **Summary**: Air India is expanding its use of Salesforce Agentforce beyond refund processing into automated email resolution, passenger-name changes, and a knowledge agent for service teams. The airline says Agentforce reduced refund turnaround from roughly 14 days to four hours and name-change processing from three days to 30 minutes. Its email-resolution workflow can identify multiple intents, invoke specialized sub-agents, validate information across enterprise systems, and execute actions, with a 95% confidence threshold and human oversight for eligible cases.
* **Why It Matters**: This is a strong example of enterprise AI moving from conversational assistance to **multi-step process execution**. The important benchmark is no longer model quality alone, but measurable cycle-time reduction inside complex operational workflows.

### 2. **LM-Kit One Launches as a Private Enterprise AI Application Server**

* **Source**: LM-Kit · September 15, 2026
* **Summary**: LM-Kit One launches as a private AI application server designed to run models, agents, RAG, search, document intelligence, and administration on infrastructure controlled by the customer. It supports OpenAI-, Anthropic-, Ollama- and MCP-compatible interfaces, with local deployment across Windows, Linux, and macOS and support for CUDA, Vulkan, Metal, and CPU execution. The architecture emphasizes local operation, policy controls, auditability, explicit network access, and air-gapped deployment.
* **Why It Matters**: The product reflects growing enterprise demand for **AI sovereignty and private inference**, particularly where sensitive data, regulatory requirements, latency, or cloud dependency make fully managed public AI unattractive. The emerging competition is increasingly around the complete AI application stack rather than the model endpoint alone.

## Executive Takeaway

**Enterprise AI is entering an execution phase.** The strongest developments today are not about another marginal improvement in model benchmarks; they are about connecting AI to enterprise systems, allowing agents to execute multi-step workflows, and putting governance around the resulting authority.

Three architectural layers are becoming increasingly important:

1. **Agent execution** — agents that can reason, call tools, and complete workflows.
2. **Enterprise context** — governed data, semantic context, knowledge, and application connectivity.
3. **Agent control** — identity, permissions, approvals, observability, audit, security, and cost controls.

The competitive advantage is therefore shifting from **"having access to a frontier model"** toward **building a reliable operating system for AI-powered business processes**.
