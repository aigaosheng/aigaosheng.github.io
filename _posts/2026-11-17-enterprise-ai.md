# Enterprise AI Brief: Upstage and Cohere Partner on Sovereign Enterprise AI

**Today:** Enterprise AI vendors are competing on where agents run and how they are governed, and today's announcements show that clearly in Asia's regulated industries.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Upstage and Cohere Partner to Build Sovereign Agentic AI for Enterprises**

*The Korea Times · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Korea's Upstage and Canada's Cohere are pairing Korean-language models with an enterprise agent platform for customers who need to keep their data under national control.

The two companies signed a strategic partnership at Upstage's Seoul office. Under it, Upstage's Solar Pro and Solar Mini models and its Document Parse technology will be integrated into North, Cohere's enterprise agentic AI platform. The joint offering targets finance, government and manufacturing customers and can be deployed on-premises or in a virtual private cloud. The companies also plan to develop models together and pursue business across Asia-Pacific and other markets.

**Why it matters:** Across Asia, regulated buyers increasingly want local-language models running on infrastructure they control. Partnerships between local model builders and global agent platforms are becoming a common way to meet that demand. Banks and public agencies in the region should expect more of these jointly built "sovereign stack" offerings in procurement shortlists.

### 2. 🤖 **Genpact Launches Onyx Platform to Build, Run and Govern Enterprise AI Agents**

*PR Newswire · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Genpact has made Onyx generally available, turning its business-process outsourcing expertise into a product for running governed AI agents in back-office operations.

The company says Onyx is built on its Process Intelligence Core. It offers prebuilt workflows and agents for finance, risk, supply chain, banking, insurance and procurement, and lets clients choose models from Genpact's partners. Its "earned autonomy" approach starts agents off proposing actions for human approval, and the platform includes auditable actions, guardrails and data isolation. Onyx will first power Genpact's Accounts Payable Suite and was unveiled at the company's Agentic Operations Day in New York on November 12.

**Why it matters:** Outsourcing providers are trying to protect their headcount-based revenue by selling governed agent platforms in its place. That puts pressure on Asian delivery centres and shared-service hubs that handle finance and banking operations. Buyers should press vendors on how autonomy is earned and audited, since these are vendor claims and haven't been independently tested.

### 3. 🤖 **SnapLogic Launches MCP Discovery Hub to Curb Agent Tool Sprawl**

*GlobeNewswire · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** SnapLogic is adding a central registry of Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, betting that tracking and reusing agent tools will become a core enterprise integration problem.

SnapLogic announced the update at its INTEGRATEAI 2026 event in London. Discovery Hub lets developers find and reuse MCP servers and copy client configurations for tools such as Claude Code, Cursor and VS Code. The company also added agent-building skills to SnapCode and checks in SnapGPT that test pipelines against governance standards set by administrators. Support for its own MCP servers is available now. Third-party MCP support, self-healing integrations and agent observability are promised in the coming months. SnapLogic also announced a separate partnership with observability firm Honeycomb the same day.

**Why it matters:** As companies deploy more agents, the hard part shifts from building connectors to keeping an inventory of the tools agents can call and controlling who uses them. Integration vendors want to own that layer, and CIOs consolidating agent estates should see MCP registries as an emerging governance category.

### 4. 🌐 **Korea Deep Learning Completes AI Agent Credit-Screening Pilot with Shinhan Vietnam Finance**

*PR Newswire · November 17, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Korean document-AI startup has tested its agent platform on consumer-lending workflows at a Vietnamese finance company, as Korean vendors expand into Southeast Asian financial services.

Korea Deep Learning says it has completed a proof of concept with Shinhan Vietnam Finance Company. The pilot applied its DEEP Agent platform to credit screening workflows. The Seoul-based company says it plans to expand into more Vietnamese industries through local partners.

**Why it matters:** Credit screening is one of the first lending tasks where agents are being tried, and a Korean bank group's Vietnamese subsidiary makes a natural pilot customer for Korean AI vendors moving into the region. This is still a pilot, not a production rollout, so lenders should watch whether it leads to a commercial contract and measurable results.

## Key Takeaways

- Sovereignty is becoming something vendors sell as a product: agent platforms are being bundled with local models and on-premises or private-cloud deployment for finance and government buyers in Asia.
- Governance is now a headline feature. Genpact's earned autonomy and SnapLogic's MCP registry both focus on controlling what agents do, not just making them more capable.
- Services firms and integration vendors are turning their domain knowledge into agent platforms, which puts per-seat and per-headcount pricing models under pressure.

## What to Watch

Microsoft Ignite runs from November 17 to 20 in San Francisco, with agent management and security as the main themes. Watch for any announcements that compete with the governance layers launched today. Also watch whether Upstage and Cohere name their first regulated customers in Korea or elsewhere in Asia-Pacific, and when SnapLogic ships its promised third-party MCP support.
