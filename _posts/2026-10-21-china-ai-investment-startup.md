# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: YITA Raises RMB 100 Million From Galbot, Fourier

**Today:** A light news day: two early-stage embodied-AI rounds, both showing robot makers and specialist funds backing new component and application startups.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **YITA raises more than RMB 100 million in seed rounds from Galbot, Fourier and Matrix Partners China**

*TechNode · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A robot-hardware startup founded only in May 2026 has raised over RMB 100 million in seed and seed-plus funding, with two robot companies among its backers.

YITA announced on October 20 that it closed the money across its seed and seed-plus rounds. Investors include Galbot, Fourier, Matrix Partners China, CCB International, SenseTime Guoxiang Capital and the L2F founder fund, and Lighthouse Capital acted as incubator and exclusive financial adviser. YITA builds force-controlled robotic arms and high-performance joint modules, which let robots regulate contact force when handling objects, and positions them as standardized components for humanoid and other embodied-AI developers.

**Why it matters:** Robot developers investing in a supplier of arms and joints suggests they want secure access to key components, not just financial returns (our reading, not stated in the source). For Asia-based enterprise buyers and investors, component-layer startups are an early indicator of where embodied-AI supply chains are forming.

### 2. 📊 **Shanghai cleaning-robot startup Jingji Intelligent raises seed funding a month after founding**

*TechNode · October 21, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Shanghai startup applying medical-robotics techniques to cleaning has raised tens of millions of RMB in seed funding and is already targeting overseas customers, including in Singapore.

The round came from Haowen Tianshu Robotics and Shanghai Lanyun Industrial Development, and was disclosed on October 20, about a month after the company was established in September. Jingji focuses on cleaning vertical surfaces, countertops and sanitary fixtures using force control, machine vision and precision actuation. It plans to keep R&D and its supply chain in China, has partnerships in Australia, Spain, Singapore and Vietnam, and is moving into product validation.

**Why it matters:** The company's export-first stance shows how quickly new Chinese robotics startups are looking to Southeast Asia and other markets. For Singapore enterprises in facilities management and hospitality, such vendors may soon appear as pilot candidates, though the source reports no commercial deployments yet.

## Key Takeaways

- Early-stage capital in China's embodied AI is flowing to narrow, specialized layers (joints, force control, cleaning tasks) rather than general-purpose humanoids.
- Strategic investors, such as robot makers backing a component supplier, are joining financial funds even at seed stage.
- New companies are raising money within weeks to months of founding, and some are pitching overseas customers from the start.

## What to Watch

Changzhou Micro-Intelligence, an industrial-robot maker, has an offer period in Hong Kong running to Friday and is expected to begin trading on October 28, an early test of public-market appetite for embodied-AI hardware.
