# AI Security & Risk Brief: Deepfake CEO Fraud Nets $129 Million

**Today:** A US indictment unsealed this week shows how AI voice and video cloning, combined with layered social engineering, can extract nine figures from well-run finance and healthcare organisations, a direct warning for payment and banking teams on wire-transfer authorisation controls.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Three Charged Over $129 Million AI Deepfake CEO Fraud Scheme**

*Greek City Times · November 8, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US federal prosecutors charged three men with using AI-generated voice and video to impersonate chief executives and law firm attorneys, defrauding companies worldwide of roughly $129 million between July 2024 and February 2026.

Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit allegedly posed as company CEOs on calls and video conferences, then directed accounting staff to coordinate "confidential transactions" with AI-impersonated lawyers at major international law firms to make the wire requests appear legitimate. Identified victims include a UK fintech (about $11.6 million), hospital systems in Missouri and California, a Rhode Island bank, a Massachusetts mutual fund manager and a Florida pet food company. Soall was arrested in Spain in June, Hazan in Spain in August and Sheetrit in the UK in September; extradition requests for all three are pending.

**Why it matters:** Stolen funds were wired to accounts in the US, Spain, Portugal, Greece, the UAE, Singapore, Austria, Slovakia and China before conversion to cryptocurrency, underscoring that AI-enabled fraud proceeds are now routinely laundered through Asian financial hubs. For banks, fintechs and payment firms in Singapore and the wider region, the case is a reminder to tighten callback verification and dual-approval controls on high-value transfers, since voice and video are no longer reliable proof of identity.

## Key Takeaways

- Layering a second impersonation (the "lawyer") on top of the CEO deepfake added false corroboration that helped the scheme evade normal red flags for nearly two years.
- Laundering routes spanning nine countries, including Singapore, show that AI-driven fraud proceeds increasingly pass through APAC banking and crypto on-ramps, not just US and EU accounts.
- A nearly two-year operating window before indictment suggests existing wire-transfer authorisation and callback-verification controls at victim firms did not catch AI-cloned voice and video in time.

## What to Watch

Extradition proceedings for Soall, Hazan and Sheetrit from Spain and the UK, and whether Singapore or other transit-country authorities move to freeze or trace funds linked to the case.
