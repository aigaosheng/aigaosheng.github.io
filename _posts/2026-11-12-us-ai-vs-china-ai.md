# US AI vs China AI Brief: Nvidia Board Weighs Ousting Huang Over Spy Scandal

**Today:** A corporate governance crisis at Nvidia has collided with the US-China chip war, as a spy scandal involving CEO Jensen Huang coincides with a new Commerce Department rule that would pull Singapore directly into America's export-control regime.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Nvidia Board Weighs Removing Jensen Huang as Scandal Deepens**

*CNBC · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nvidia's board met to decide whether to remove CEO Jensen Huang after a second reported affair surfaced and a former partner was alleged to have helped divert chips to China, sending the stock down more than 8% in premarket trading.

CNBC reported that the New York Times disclosed a second relationship between Huang and Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, Kimberly Powell, days after Huang admitted to an earlier affair with Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based cloud firm Megaspeed whom US intelligence officials believe was working for Chinese intelligence. Analysts cited by CNBC warned Nvidia has no chief operating officer or board chair and lacks a clear succession plan, and some lawmakers have called for Huang's resignation.

**Why it matters:** Huang has been the chip industry's most effective opponent of tougher export-control legislation in Washington; his removal would weaken that lobbying position just as Congress pushes China-focused chip bills ahead of the Trump-Xi summit at the November 18-19 APEC meeting in Shenzhen.

### 2. 🏦 **US Set to Expand Chip Export Curbs to Singapore, Southeast Asia**

*Reuters (via TimesLive) · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US Commerce Department is finalizing a rule extending export licensing requirements to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway and South Africa to close a loophole letting Chinese firms rent Nvidia computing power through offshore data centres.

Reuters reported that a major Chinese cloud provider and smaller AI labs are rushing to lock in overseas Nvidia capacity before the rule, which could be announced this month, takes effect; Tencent, Alibaba and ByteDance already hold offshore data-centre agreements for advanced Nvidia chips. The move would pre-empt the stalled Remote Access Security Act, passed by the House 369-22 in January but not yet taken up by the Senate.

**Why it matters:** Singapore- and Malaysia-based cloud operators would face new end-user disclosure and ongoing oversight obligations, raising compliance costs for regional data-centre operators even though the chips never physically enter China.

### 3. 🌐 **Singapore's Megaspeed Says It's Cooperating With US Chip Probe**

*Reuters (via The Star) · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the centre of the Nvidia chip-diversion allegations, said it takes US export-control obligations "extremely seriously" and has engaged a US law firm to audit its GPU compliance.

CEO James Tan Yong Pong said Megaspeed has cooperated with Bureau of Industry and Security inspections and is committed to working transparently with US authorities. Singapore police confirmed last month that Megaspeed remains under domestic investigation after a US senator asked Commerce to probe suspected chip diversion.

**Why it matters:** As a Singapore-incorporated company at the centre of both the Huang scandal and the proposed Commerce rule, Megaspeed's compliance response will shape how US regulators and Singapore authorities treat other regional cloud operators serving Chinese AI customers.

### 4. 🌐 **Beijing Pushes Back on US Chip-Smuggling Claims**

*Global Times · November 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's foreign ministry said Washington is "overstretching" national security concepts to justify chip restrictions, responding to reports that a blacklisted Chinese university obtained Nvidia H200 chips through third parties.

Spokesperson Mao Ning declined to confirm specifics of the Northwestern Polytechnical University purchase but reiterated China's opposition to US export controls and objected to what she called discriminatory entity lists targeting Chinese institutions.

**Why it matters:** Beijing's public rebuttal, issued the same week as the Huang scandal and the proposed Southeast Asia chip rule, signals China intends to resist new restrictions rather than negotiate, raising the stakes for the Trump-Xi APEC meeting.

## Key Takeaways

- The Jensen Huang governance crisis is now entangled with chip policy: Nvidia's weakened lobbying position could accelerate pending China export-control legislation.
- Singapore has moved from bystander to direct subject of US export enforcement, via both the proposed Commerce rule and the ongoing Megaspeed investigation.
- Beijing is signaling it will contest tighter curbs publicly rather than quietly absorb them, hardening the backdrop for the Trump-Xi summit.

## What to Watch

Nvidia's board decision on Huang's status, the Commerce Department's formal announcement of the Southeast Asia export rule, and the outcome of the Trump-Xi APEC meeting on November 18-19 in Shenzhen are the near-term signals to track.
