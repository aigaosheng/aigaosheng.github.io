**AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: China Captures 70 Percent of Asia Q3 Funding**

Today: Chinese AI developers are absorbing the vast majority of Asian venture capital, but mounting US regulatory enforcement threatens to severely complicate cross-border backing.

### Top Stories

### 1. 📊 China Dominates Asia's Q3 Startup Funding

Value Add VC · October 17, 2026

**Bottom line:** Chinese startups accounted for roughly $27 billion—nearly 70 percent—of the $38.5 billion raised across Asia in the third quarter, heavily driven by artificial intelligence.

Venture funding data for Q3 2026 reveals that AI companies secured 55 percent of all Asian startup capital, with Chinese firms absorbing the lion's share despite geopolitical headwinds. Multi-billion dollar valuations for entities like Moonshot AI and Kling AI demonstrate that Chinese AI developers are still commanding Western-scale cheques. However, market analysts note that the current environment is highly polarised: investors are distributing smaller bets among unproven teams while concentrating massive capital into a select few mature startups.

**Why it matters:** The heavy concentration of capital into Chinese AI implies that non-AI founders across Asia are competing for a shrinking slice of liquidity. For enterprise AI buyers, the deep capitalisation of China's top tier ensures a robust pipeline of heavily funded, highly capable domestic foundation models.

### 2. 🏦 US Treasury Fines VC for Chinese AI Investment

Davis Polk · October 17, 2026

**Bottom line:** The US Department of the Treasury has issued its first civil penalty under the Outbound Investment Security Program against an American venture capital firm.

Treasury fined Amidi LLC, the US parent of a China-based investment fund, $200,000 for failing to report its investment into a Chinese artificial intelligence company. The violation was not voluntarily disclosed but was instead discovered independently by Treasury officials monitoring market activity. This marks the first enforcement action under the program, which restricts and monitors US capital flowing into Chinese high-tech sectors.

**Why it matters:** This enforcement signals that Washington is actively policing and penalising unreported venture flows into Chinese AI, regardless of the transaction's size. Cross-border investors operating in Asia must implement rigorous screening protocols, as ignorance of indirect portfolio exposure to Chinese AI is no longer a viable legal defence.

### 3. 🤖 InfiMaker Secures Pre-A+ Funding for Desktop Manufacturing

TechNode · October 16, 2026

**Bottom line:** Chinese desktop manufacturing startup InfiMaker has raised tens of millions of yuan in a Pre-A+ funding round to scale its production capacity.

Following a highly successful Kickstarter campaign that generated over RMB 30 million, InfiMaker will deploy its new capital to expand its Shenzhen manufacturing facility and enhance its software stack. The company focuses on desktop-level manufacturing solutions, riding a wave of interest in hardware that bridges the gap between digital AI designs and physical production.

**Why it matters:** The successful raise highlights ongoing domestic investor appetite for hardware and robotics startups positioned at the intersection of AI and manufacturing, an area where China's supply chain advantages remain formidable.

### 4. 🤖 Robot AI Developer Ruoyu Completes Pre-A Round

TechNode · October 16, 2026

**Bottom line:** Ruoyu, an AI robotics developer specialising in industrial applications, has secured Pre-A funding led by Sunway Communication.

The startup has been commercialising its AI-driven robotics through practical industrial deployments, notably testing an explosion-protected robotic refuelling system at a station in Foshan. Ruoyu intends to channel the fresh funding into refining its core robotic AI, gathering operational data from real-world environments, and accelerating its broader commercial rollout.

**Why it matters:** Investors are increasingly directing capital away from purely conceptual AI towards startups solving dangerous, heavy-industry problems through embodied robotics.
