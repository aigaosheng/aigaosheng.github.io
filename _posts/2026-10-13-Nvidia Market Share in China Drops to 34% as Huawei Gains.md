# US AI vs China AI Brief: Nvidia Market Share in China Drops to 34% as Huawei Gains

**Today:** US export controls are accelerating structural shifts in the global AI hardware landscape, squeezing American chipmakers in China while driving European equipment suppliers and US enforcement into heightened geopolitical scrutiny.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🤖 **Nvidia Market Share in China Drops to 34% as Huawei Expands**

*The Standard · October 13, 2026*

**Bottom line:** US export restrictions sent Nvidia’s share of China’s data center AI semiconductor market down to 34% in the first half of 2026, while domestic rival Huawei expanded its market share to 28%.

IDC data indicates Nvidia’s China shipments fell 44% year-over-year to 678,000 units under tight US trade controls. Over the same period, Huawei shipped 572,000 units as domestic buyers pivoted to local hardware alternatives. The shift reflects a broader transition across Chinese data centers from pure GPU processing power to application-specific systems and localized custom silicon.

**Why it matters:** For Asian tech hubs and enterprise infrastructure providers, Huawei's rapid market capture in China highlights the shrinking footprint of US semiconductor vendors in Asia's largest market, forcing regional supply chains to adapt to distinct US and Chinese AI hardware ecosystems.

### 2. 🏦 **Bipartisan US MATCH Act Targets ASML Tool Sales and Servicing in China**

*IO+ · October 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Proposed US legislation aims to block ASML from selling or servicing deep ultraviolet (DUV) semiconductor equipment in China, placing up to $9 billion in revenue at risk.

The bipartisan bill, known as the MATCH Act, seeks to close gaps in existing export control frameworks by penalizing foreign toolmakers that maintain advanced lithography equipment inside Chinese semiconductor foundries. Dutch officials and ASML leadership have expressed strong opposition, arguing the measure bypasses multilateral export agreements and undermines European sovereignty over dual-use tech policy.

**Why it matters:** Extra-territorial US legislative moves create significant regulatory friction for European and Asian semiconductor equipment suppliers, heightening compliance uncertainty for foundry operations throughout Asia.

### 3. 🔒 **Contractor Pleads Guilty in Super Micro Scheme Diverting AI Servers to China**

*Taipei Times · October 12, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A former outside contractor involved with Super Micro Computer co-founder Yih-Shyan Liaw pleaded guilty in US federal court to violating export controls by diverting restricted AI servers to China.

Court filings detail a smuggling scheme that routed Nvidia-powered high-performance AI servers to an entity in Southeast Asia before illegally redirecting them to Chinese buyers. Defendant Ting-Wei Sun entered a guilty plea in New York as federal prosecutors intensify enforcement against illicit transshipment channels used to bypass US AI technology restrictions.

**Why it matters:** Southeast Asian logistics hubs face escalating scrutiny from US authorities monitoring third-party transshipments, requiring stricter end-user auditing for AI infrastructure vendors operating across the ASEAN region.

* Federal enforcement against transshipment networks in Southeast Asia demonstrates elevated risk for regional distributors handling restricted AI hardware.
