# US AI vs China AI Brief: US Expands Scrutiny of China Tech Investments

**Today:** Washington is tightening scrutiny of capital and research links involving China, reinforcing the importance of investment compliance and cross-border technology controls for companies operating across Asia.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **US Treasury Proposes Tighter Controls on China Tech Investments**

*Associated Press · October 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The US Treasury Department has proposed expanding restrictions on American investments in sensitive technologies in China and other countries of concern, including artificial intelligence.

The proposal implements legislation passed in December 2025 and extends scrutiny to areas including AI, supercomputing, biotechnology and hypersonic systems. The proposed rules would cover investments exceeding $50,000, while exempting many financial investments, including stock purchases. The Treasury Department will accept public comments before finalising the rule.

**Why it matters:** Investors, venture capital firms and multinational companies with exposure to Chinese AI development should assess whether their investment structures and reporting processes could fall within the expanded restrictions. For Singapore-based funds and regional technology businesses, US investor participation and cross-border corporate structures may warrant additional legal review.

### 2. 🌐 **China Challenges US Restrictions on Research Institutions**

*Reuters · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Beijing has called on Washington to end what it describes as discriminatory restrictions affecting Chinese research institutions, highlighting growing friction over access to strategic technology research.

China's Commerce Ministry criticised US Department of Defense restrictions on research funding involving dozens of Chinese universities. The dispute concerns measures that prevent US defence research funding from going to entities collaborating with designated Chinese universities, including institutions working in strategically important fields such as semiconductors, AI and quantum computing. China argues that these measures undermine scientific cooperation and impede technological progress.

**Why it matters:** Research partnerships, university collaborations and technology development programmes may face greater compliance and funding uncertainty as US–China competition extends beyond chips and equipment to research relationships. Asian institutions working with US-funded programmes or Chinese research partners should review applicable restrictions and assess the implications for joint projects, personnel and funding.

## Key Takeaways

- **Capital is becoming a strategic control point.** US scrutiny of investment in sensitive technologies could increase due diligence and reporting requirements for investors and companies with China exposure.
- **Research collaboration faces geopolitical constraints.** Universities, technology companies and research institutions must consider funding eligibility and restrictions alongside scientific and commercial objectives.
- **Asia-based businesses need cross-border visibility.** Singapore's role in regional investment, technology partnerships and corporate structures makes jurisdiction-specific legal review important when US and Chinese requirements intersect.

## What to Watch

Watch for the US Treasury's final investment-rule provisions and public-comment process, including how AI-related investments and exemptions are defined. Also monitor further US measures affecting Chinese research institutions and any Chinese response that changes the operating conditions for international technology partnerships.
