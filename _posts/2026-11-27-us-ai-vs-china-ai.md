# US AI vs China AI Brief: Nvidia Halts Southeast Asia Server Shipments

**Today:** Nvidia has instructed manufacturing partners to stop shipping AI servers to four Southeast Asian countries as new US export rules take effect, directly disrupting the region's data centre buildout and delaying Chinese frontier model releases.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🌐 **Nvidia Halts AI Server Shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia**

*Financial Times via Daily Mirror · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Nvidia has told Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia, as Washington's new export controls targeting Chinese access to AI compute take effect.

The halt follows US Commerce Department rules introduced last week requiring licences for advanced AI chip exports to these countries, with cloud providers given 90 days to obtain licences proving their end users exclude restricted entities . Nvidia booked $104 billion in revenue through its Singapore subsidiary in 2025, a 72% year-on-year increase, though this figure includes some Taiwan and China revenue recorded via the unit . A cloud provider executive described the situation as chaotic, with no clear guidance on how to apply for the licence while the 90-day clock ticks .

**Why it matters:** Singapore is Nvidia's largest revenue booking location in the region, and the shipment halt puts billions in planned Southeast Asian data centre investment at risk—including a $500 million financing facility for a Singapore cloud company planning to lease compute to ByteDance that has reportedly stalled . For enterprises in Singapore and across the region relying on Nvidia-powered cloud capacity, the disruption signals that US-China tech competition is now directly constraining AI infrastructure availability in Southeast Asia.

### 2. 🤖 **Zhipu and Moonshot Delay Flagship Models as US Compute and Distillation Curbs Bite**

*Reuters via Ming Pao · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Zhipu AI has pushed its GLM-5.5 flagship model from November to Q1 2027 or later, while Moonshot AI expects its Kimi K4 to slip to mid-2027 and its model release cycle to double from six months to over a year.

Zhipu's delay stems from stronger anti-distillation protections in US models and the new US rules limiting Chinese access to overseas data centre compute . Moonshot, which relies on leased overseas compute, has reportedly shifted to distilling from Google and Meta models after finding it harder to extract training data from OpenAI and Anthropic systems, and is accelerating efforts to rent compute in Europe and engage Chinese chipmakers . DeepSeek researchers assessed internally last month that if distillation from US models is fully blocked, the US-China frontier gap could widen from 3-6 months to as much as 9 months .

**Why it matters:** The delays validate concerns that US export controls and anti-distillation measures are eroding the cost-efficiency advantage Chinese labs have relied on to compete with better-resourced American counterparts. For Asian enterprises that have adopted Chinese open-weight models as cheaper alternatives, the slowing release cadence may force reassessment of roadmaps tied to Zhipu and Moonshot model updates.

### 3. 🏦 **China's Data Administration Proposes New Rules to Govern AI Training Data**

*Xinhua · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's National Data Administration has issued draft rules to clarify data property rights, compensation, and risk controls for AI training and inference, as Beijing seeks to shore up domestic data resources amid tightening US restrictions.

The draft proposes twelve measures across three areas, including using data property rights systems for AI, supporting paid data access, establishing risk prevention mechanisms for AI outputs, and improving accountability for data circulation . The rules are open for public comment.

**Why it matters:** As access to foreign compute and model distillation narrows, China is moving to formalize and monetize its domestic data advantages—the country's largest remaining lever in the AI competition. For multinational enterprises operating in China, clearer data property rules could reduce compliance uncertainty but may also increase costs for accessing training data.

### 4. 📊 **Zhipu Executive Predicts China Will Close AI Gap with US Within 24 Months**

*MoneyDJ via TTV · Nov 27, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A Zhipu board secretary told a Hong Kong capital summit that China's AI models have a strong chance to surpass overseas models within 24 months, arguing that US and Chinese labs are pursuing divergent technical routes.

Zhipu's Xiao Lei said Chinese models have focused on mixture-of-experts architectures for engineering efficiency and lower cost at equivalent intelligence, while US labs concentrate on raw compute and base model scale . He argued that both sides face physical limits—US data centre power costs and Chinese data shortages—but China's large engineering workforce gives it an edge in closed-loop training with environmental data. Separately, Chinese GPU maker Biren Technology said its BR20X flagship chip for training and inference has entered customer testing, with plans for volume deployment in 2027 .

**Why it matters:** Zhipu's optimistic forecast contrasts sharply with the delays and compute constraints reported elsewhere today, and should be read as a company view rather than an industry consensus. The Biren chip timeline is a more concrete signal: domestic silicon remains at least a year from volume deployment, meaning Chinese labs will operate under compute constraints well into 2027 regardless of architectural efficiency gains.

## Key Takeaways

- **Southeast Asia is now a frontline, not a backwater:** The Nvidia shipment halt to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia shows that US export controls are no longer just a US-China bilateral issue—they are reshaping AI infrastructure economics across the region where Chinese firms have sought compute workarounds.
- **Distillation and compute controls are compounding:** Zhipu and Moonshot's delays reflect a dual squeeze—restricted access to Nvidia chips and tighter anti-distillation measures from OpenAI and Anthropic—that attacks the cost-efficiency model Chinese labs have relied on.
- **China is responding on data and domestic silicon, but slowly:** The data rules draft and Biren's 2027 timeline signal a strategic pivot toward self-reliance, but neither addresses the near-term compute gap that is already delaying flagship model releases.

## What to Watch

Whether the 90-day licence window for cloud providers in Singapore and other affected Southeast Asian markets produces a workable compliance path, or whether Nvidia server shipments remain frozen into 2027—and whether Zhipu's GLM-5.5 slips beyond Q1 2027.
