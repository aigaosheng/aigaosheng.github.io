# AI for society Brief: UN Climate Chief Calls AI Double-Edged Sword

**Today:** Only one qualifying story cleared verification and the date window today, but it shows AI's energy and environmental footprint becoming a climate-diplomacy issue that enterprise and infrastructure planners will need to track.

## Top Stories

### 1. 👥 **UN Climate Chief Calls AI a Double-Edged Sword as COP31 Pledge Launches Without Major Backers**

*France 24 (AFP) · November 16, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A voluntary AI pledge launched at COP31 in Antalya has drawn broad endorsements but few national signatories, and none of the largest AI powers or labs.

The Antalya Artificial Intelligence Pledge was launched by COP31 host Turkey alongside UN climate chief Simon Stiell, who described AI as holding both great promise and great peril. According to the AFP report, it had 80 endorsements at launch but only 10 signatory countries. The United States, China and the EU were absent, as were AI companies Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic, while the World Business Council for Sustainable Development did sign. Stiell argued that people should not be asked to accept higher bills, more pollution and deeper inequality in return for promised future benefits. The report also cites a UN study finding that data centres used more electricity in 2025 than all but ten countries, and IEA figures putting roughly 30 percent of data-centre power as coal-fired. One campaigner called the pledge a distraction.

**Why it matters:** Pressure for AI energy and emissions disclosure is moving from advocacy into formal climate diplomacy, even if this pledge is voluntary and lacks the biggest players. For Asian enterprises and operators building or buying data-centre capacity, power sourcing and reporting expectations are likely to become part of procurement and stakeholder scrutiny.

## Key Takeaways

- AI's resource footprint is now framed as a question of public cost, including bills, pollution and inequality, rather than only a technical efficiency issue.
- Voluntary commitments without the largest governments and AI developers carry limited weight so far, which critics already highlight.
- The reported UN and IEA figures on data-centre electricity use and coal share give executives concrete benchmarks for sustainability questions from regulators and customers.

## What to Watch

Watch whether the US, China, the EU or leading AI developers sign the Antalya pledge, and whether further COP31 outcomes set concrete disclosure or renewable-power expectations for data centres.
