# AI Investment & Startup in China Brief: Sand.ai Hits Unicorn Status With $200M Round

**Today:** A video-AI unicorn and a regulator-driven IPO freeze show China's AI capital market splitting into fast-moving private rounds and a slowing public listing pipeline, a split enterprise and fintech investors eyeing China exposure need to track closely.

## Top Stories

### 1. 📊 **Sand.ai Becomes a Unicorn With $200 Million Series A+ Round**

*36Kr · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Video-generation startup Sand.ai crossed the $1 billion valuation threshold after closing a $200 million Series A+ round, its third raise in ten months.

The round was co-led by Jiyuan Capital and Nasdaq-listed CDN operator Wangsu Science & Technology, with participation from state-backed funds in Shanghai and Shandong alongside existing investors Matrix Partners and CAS Star. Sand.ai, founded by former Microsoft Research Asia scientist Cao Yue, has now raised roughly $300 million in 2026; its open-source MAGI-2 video model and consumer app VidMuse reportedly passed $10 million in annualised revenue within two months of launch.

**Why it matters:** The deal shows Chinese state-linked and corporate capital still chasing frontier video-generation models even as broader tech financing cools, a pattern Singapore-based funds and fintechs should watch for cross-border co-investment opportunities and intensifying price competition in AI video tools.

### 2. 🏦 **China Quietly Slows AI and Chip IPOs as YMTC Pauses Listing**

*Electronics Weekly · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** China's securities regulator has informally told investment banks to slow down AI and chip company IPOs after a market rout, prompting NAND chipmaker YMTC's parent, CCSH, to pause its planned $4.9 billion Shanghai listing.

CCSH filed in August for a 33 billion yuan STAR Market IPO, with a strategic share placement planned for late October to early November now shelved "due to various factors," per an internal staff memo. The pause follows an 8.5% October slide in the CSI 300, its worst month in over a decade, and a roughly 40% drop in the STAR50 chip-and-AI index from its July peak; YMTC is now expected to list no earlier than the first half of 2027.

**Why it matters:** Regulators appear to be prioritising market stability over the pace of AI-adjacent capital raising, which could delay chip capacity expansion and cool near-term valuation momentum for China's broader AI IPO pipeline, including unlisted hopefuls such as Moonshot AI and Kling AI.

### 3. 🌐 **Global Investors Bet on China Leading AI Application Boom**

*Xinhua via China Economic Net · November 5, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Investment bankers and financiers at Hong Kong's Global Financial Leaders' Investment Summit said China is positioned to lead the next phase of AI commercialisation, especially in physical AI and manufacturing.

Hillhouse Investment founder Zhang Lei said the industry has entered a "show me the money" phase in which investors now expect measurable AI productivity gains across manufacturing, healthcare, services and consumption, with Asia offering stronger use cases than other regions. A New York-based equities chief investment officer, James Lydotes, told Xinhua that China's manufacturing base positions it to lead in physical AI, pointing to China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology drafting new smart-manufacturing policy in late October.

**Why it matters:** The commentary signals global capital's AI thesis is shifting from model-scaling bets toward revenue-generating industrial applications, a cue for Singapore-based funds and fintechs assessing where the next wave of China-linked AI capital and partnership opportunities will concentrate.

## Key Takeaways

- Private funding for frontier Chinese AI applications, like Sand.ai's video models, remains strong even as public markets cool.
- Beijing is actively managing the pace of AI and chip IPOs to protect market stability, creating a bifurcated path between easy private capital and a tighter public listing window.
- Global investors are increasingly framing China's AI edge around physical AI and manufacturing applications rather than frontier model scaling alone.

## What to Watch

Watch whether CCSH/YMTC's delayed listing becomes a template other AI-adjacent IPO candidates follow, and whether Moonshot AI and Kling AI adjust their own Hong Kong listing timetables in response to the CSRC's informal guidance.
