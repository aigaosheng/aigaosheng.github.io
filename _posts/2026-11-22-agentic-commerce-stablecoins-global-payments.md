## 2. In-Depth Highlight

### Agentic Payments Move Beyond Domestic Commerce

One of the week's most strategically important developments is the emergence of AI agents as participants in cross-border commercial payments.

At the 2026 Singapore FinTech Festival (18–20 November), China's Lianlian DigiTech introduced three interconnected products: XWorker, an AI workforce platform; XPay, which connects agent-initiated payment requests with payment execution; and XCard, a business payment card operating on the Visa network.

The architecture addresses a fundamental challenge in agentic commerce: allowing autonomous software to initiate transactions without compromising financial controls. Its Agent Wallet establishes spending permissions, transaction limits and authorised business identities. The company also incorporates Know Your Agent (KYA) controls to establish which business an AI agent represents and what it is permitted to do.

Lianlian and Visa have already completed B2B agentic payment transactions, including an October 2026 transaction using an agent-specific payment token. However, interoperability remains a challenge because competing payment networks are developing different trusted-agent protocols.

The broader opportunity is substantial. The article cites an S&P Global study indicating that more than two-thirds of merchants expect AI agents to initiate at least 10% of their e-commerce transactions within three years.

For payment providers, this signals a potential shift from processing human-initiated transactions towards managing delegated financial authority, identity verification and machine-driven payment execution.

**Source:** [SFF 2026: AI agents enter the cross-border payments market](https://www.fintechfutures.com/ai-in-fintech/sff-2026-ai-agents-enter-the-cross-border-payments-market) — FinTech Futures, 19 November 2026.

## 3. Market & Industry Insight

### Three Forces Reshaping Payment Infrastructure

#### 1. Agentic Commerce Is Creating a New Payment Architecture

The development of AI agents is changing the relationship between commerce platforms and payment providers. Traditional payment infrastructure assumes that a human selects a product, confirms the transaction and authorises payment. Agentic commerce introduces a different model in which software can perform these activities under delegated permissions.

Visa's expansion of its Agentic Ready programme and the developments showcased at Singapore FinTech Festival demonstrate that the industry is moving towards infrastructure designed for autonomous transactions.

The critical requirements will include:

- Agent identity and authentication.
- Delegated authorisation and spending limits.
- Merchant acceptance and payment tokenisation.
- Real-time transaction monitoring.
- Auditable execution and accountability records.

#### 2. Cross-Border Payments Are Becoming Embedded Banking Services

The partnership between South Korea's Woori Bank and Singapore-based Nium illustrates the growing importance of embedded international payment infrastructure.

Woori Bank will integrate Nium's white-label payment infrastructure into its mobile banking application, enabling customers to send real-time remittances across 20 international corridors covering the United States, Europe and Asia Pacific.

Meanwhile, Wise is expanding its APAC distribution through Bank Negara Indonesia. Its Wise Platform will be integrated into BNI's wondr application, which already serves more than 13 million customers.

Wise reports that its infrastructure supports more than 200 countries and territories, over 110 currencies, and delivers 78% of cross-border payments in under 20 seconds.

These developments demonstrate how banks can expand international payment capabilities without independently building and maintaining global payment networks.

**Sources:**

- [Woori Bank and Nium partnership](https://www.fintechfutures.com/digital-payments/woori-bank-partners-nium)
- [Wise Platform expands APAC footprint with BNI](https://www.fintechfutures.com/cross-border-payments/wise-platform-grows-apac-footprint-with-bni-partnership-in-indonesia)

#### 3. Stablecoins Are Moving Towards Institutional Payment Applications

The World Bank's collaboration with Citi and Circle to test stablecoin-based disbursements represents another important development.

The initiative explores whether stablecoins can improve the existing correspondent banking infrastructure used for international development payments. The World Bank Group reportedly disbursed approximately $80.7 billion in FY2026, providing considerable scale for evaluating alternative settlement approaches.

The important distinction is that institutional stablecoin adoption is increasingly being evaluated through practical payment applications rather than cryptocurrency speculation.

For banks and payment providers, the opportunity lies in faster settlement, improved transparency and more efficient international fund distribution. Regulatory compliance, liquidity management and interoperability will remain essential.

**Source:** [World Bank enlists Citi and Circle to test stablecoin-based disbursements](https://www.fintechfutures.com/blockchain-crypto-digital-assets/world-bank-enlists-citi-and-circle-to-test-stablecoin-based-disbursements).
