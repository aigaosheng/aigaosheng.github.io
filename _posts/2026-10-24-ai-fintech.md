# AI Fintech Brief: US Banks Block Unauthorized AI Agents in Accounts

**Today:** US banks are policing customers' own AI agents, while lenders start putting hard numbers on AI-driven loan speed and headcount cuts.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **US Banks Detect Unauthorized AI Agents Inside Customer Accounts**
*CBS News · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Customers are handing credentials to personal AI agents that banks have not vetted, and lenders are treating this as an access-control and liability problem rather than a hacking one.

According to an Axios report relayed by CBS, several of the largest US banks have detected third-party AI agent activity inside customers' online accounts. Axios's Madison Mills said the agents were largely following user instructions without the bank's permission to transact, not breaking in or draining funds. Chase said it supports customer-authorized data sharing through open banking providers, Morgan Stanley said it had seen no unauthorized agent activity, and Schwab's CEO said earlier this month that it was blocking such agents (per Reuters). White House AI czar David Sacks pushed back, arguing that personal agents helping users manage accounts are distinct from hackers using AI.

**Why it matters:** The friction point is credential sharing, not the AI itself. In our view, any agent product aimed at financial accounts, including in Asia, is safer built on scoped, bank-sanctioned access than on customers' logins.

### 2. 🤖 **Customers Bank Scales AI Loan Closing After Seven-Day Pilot**
*PYMNTS · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A US regional lender says an AI-driven closing process took select commercial loans from a typical 30 to 60 days down to seven, and it is now scaling the process.

Customers Bancorp said on October 22, with its Q3 results, that it is expanding the process and doubled its deployed AI agents in the quarter, a count that includes custom GPTs. It estimates more than $15 million in annualized capacity, which it counts as salary and benefits savings from roles not backfilled or left open. An employee survey put cumulative time saved at about 76,000 hours. PYMNTS adds that its own survey found only 5% to 10% of large US firms in financial services, healthcare and media report a full return on AI tools, and that these figures are self-assessed.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the few disclosed cycle-time figures for AI in commercial lending, which makes it a useful benchmark for lenders in Asia. It is also self-reported, and the "capacity" figure is largely a headcount measure, so treat it as indicative.

### 3. 📊 **Barclays Ties 5,000 Job Cuts to AI Efficiency Push**
*Newsweek · October 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Barclays is turning AI efficiency claims into a concrete staffing plan, though its CEO says AI is only part of the story.

Barclays announced on October 22 that it will cut about 5,000 roles, roughly 5% of its workforce, with 1,000 already gone. About 60% of the cuts fall in support functions, including fewer developers, and the bank expects £1.1 billion in gross efficiency savings next year. Its CEO framed AI as a supplement to years of systems modernization rather than the main driver, while the Unite union called it an excuse to discard staff. Newsweek places the move alongside Standard Chartered's planned cuts of about 7,000 roles and, citing Bloomberg, HSBC's reductions in its UK wealth business.

**Why it matters:** Headcount is becoming the most visible output of bank AI programs. In our view, that raises the bar for how banks substantiate AI value claims and manage labor relations.

## Key Takeaways

- Who is allowed to act inside an account is now the central design question for agentic finance, and banks are answering it with restrictions on credential-based access.
- Banks are reporting AI results as operating metrics (cycle times, hours saved, capacity, headcount) rather than pilot counts.
- The savings are increasingly tied to staffing, whether through unfilled roles at Customers Bank or support-function cuts at Barclays.

## What to Watch

Whether Bank of America and Wells Fargo, which had not responded to CBS, set out public positions on third-party agents. Also whether other lenders follow Customers Bank in disclosing hard AI figures for lending cycle times in their next results.
