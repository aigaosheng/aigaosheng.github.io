# AI Governance Brief: New York Curbs AI Companion Chatbots for Minors

**Today:** If you run customer-facing chatbots or plan to deploy AI agents in India, two rulebooks moved in the last 24 hours.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **New York Signs Law Barring AI Chatbots From Posing as Humans to Minors**
*Fortune · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** New York now requires age verification for AI chatbots and bans a list of human-like and engagement-driven behaviours toward anyone under 18, backed by per-violation penalties and the possible destruction of models.

Governor Kathy Hochul signed the bill on Tuesday. Under the law, chatbots offered to minors, or to users whose age has not been confirmed, may not pretend to be a real person, claim to be alive or to have emotions, claim a romantic or authority-figure relationship with a child, or use flattery designed to keep children engaged. They are also barred from promoting self-harm, eating disorders or substance use, from encouraging secrecy from parents, and from producing sexual content. Companies must offer more than one way to verify age, including at least one option that does not rely only on a government ID, and must delete the age data after use. The attorney general can seek up to $25,000 per violation. The state can also order unlawfully collected data destroyed, along with any AI models trained on it. A companion law requiring security features on internet-connected baby monitors takes effect 90 days after signing.

**Why it matters:** The model-destruction remedy and the requirement to delete age data after verification go further than disclosure-only chatbot rules. Any firm whose conversational AI could reach minors appears to need age assurance and data minimisation built into the product, not added afterwards.

### 2. 🏦 **India Weighs Pre-Release Safety Checks for Frontier AI Models**
*Elets CIO · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** India's forthcoming AI law may place the most capable models under a separate safety regime, with testing required before launch. This is a reported proposal, not yet policy.

Citing an NDTV report based on officials familiar with the talks, Elets says the government is weighing several options for frontier models: pre-release evaluations, mandatory sandbox testing, and safeguards scaled to each model's capabilities. IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw has said a consultation paper will be released in the coming weeks. Officials see defining a "frontier" model as a hard question. A compute threshold is one option, possibly combined with capability assessments. The consultation is also expected to cover deepfakes, financial fraud and impersonation. On agentic AI, it may address keeping humans in control, through measures such as approval for sensitive actions, incident reporting and the ability to halt an unsafe agent. It may also cover how liability is shared among developers, deployers, platforms and users.

**Why it matters:** This would move India from a guidance-led approach toward statutory obligations. Payments and fintech firms running AI agents in India should watch for human-approval and liability rules aimed at deployers, not only at model developers.

### 3. 🏦 **Opinion: Make Harm the Organising Principle of AI Regulation**
*Asia Times · October 28, 2026*

**Bottom line:** *(Opinion)* Legal scholar Sylvia Lu argues that AI laws should start from the harms they prevent, not from the technologies they target, so that the rules stay current as systems change.

Lu argues that today's patchwork of technology-specific rules does not connect protected interests, institutional obligations and individual rights. In her view, legal duties should flow from interests the law already protects, such as privacy and property, and should apply across systems, organisations and people. She suggests that companies and agencies deploying AI could have a duty to monitor harms after deployment. Serious or recurring harms could then trigger reports to regulators, affected individuals or the public. She sets this against the 2025 US AI Action Plan's push to cut regulation and limit conflicting state laws.

**Why it matters:** Post-deployment monitoring and harm-triggered reporting are already visible in financial-sector AI guidance. A harm-based model would put more of the compliance burden on deployers, such as banks and payment firms, rather than only on model builders.

## Key Takeaways
- Child safety is still where AI rules become binding fastest, and the remedies now reach the models themselves, not just fines.
- Asian governments are weighing statutory, pre-release controls for the most capable models instead of relying on voluntary guidelines.
- Deployer duties keep coming up: human approval for sensitive agent actions, incident reporting, and monitoring after launch.

## What to Watch
The next signal is India's AI regulation consultation paper, which Minister Vaishnaw said will be published in the coming weeks. Its definition of a "frontier" model and how it splits liability among developers, deployers and platforms will determine who has to comply.
