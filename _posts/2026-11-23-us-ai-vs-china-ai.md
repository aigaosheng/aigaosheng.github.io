# US AI vs China AI Brief: Bessent Says Nvidia Still Owes China Chip Answers

**Today:** Fresh scrutiny of Nvidia’s China sales puts export compliance and the reach of US chip controls back in focus for companies operating across Asia.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🏦 **Bessent Says Nvidia Still Owes China Chip Answers**

*CBS News · November 22, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the administration is still waiting for Nvidia to explain whether failures in its compliance processes enabled restricted chips to reach Chinese entities.

Bessent said officials had been in contact with Nvidia’s interim management, directors and lawyers, and that the company should move quickly to establish what happened and where responsibility lay. He described the question of Nvidia’s compliance culture as separate from the allegations involving a former executive and the Singapore-based Megaspeed.

**Why it matters:** The inquiry raises the stakes for chipmakers’ customer screening and compliance controls as US authorities scrutinise routes into China. For Singapore and other Asian hubs, enforcement attention could extend to intermediaries and remote access arrangements.

### 2. 🤖 **Washington Post Details Nvidia’s China Chip Lobbying**

*The Washington Post · November 23, 2026*

**Bottom line:** The Washington Post reported that Nvidia pressed US officials and lawmakers for years to preserve access to China’s market for advanced AI chips.

The report, drawing on interviews with congressional aides and former officials, describes Huang’s lobbying and the company’s opposition to proposals to prioritise US buyers or track chips to deter smuggling. It also notes that licensing records released by the House Foreign Affairs Committee showed almost 580,000 Nvidia chips approved for sale by May, while Nvidia said it recorded $2.4 billion in China sales under the licences in its most recent quarter.

**Why it matters:** The reporting highlights the tension between chipmakers’ commercial access to China and Washington’s national security controls. For companies across Asia’s AI supply chain, policy shifts and compliance scrutiny can affect where chips are sold, hosted and accessed.

🔗 [Read the full story](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/23/how-nvidia-fought-years-washington-sell-its-prized-chips-china/)

## Key Takeaways

- US scrutiny now spans both the rules governing chip access and questions about how companies handled compliance.
- Nvidia’s China market strategy remains commercially significant even as the company faces new limits and investigations.
- Asian intermediaries and data centre operators should track how enforcement affects chip sales and remote compute access.

## What to Watch

Watch for Nvidia’s response to the administration’s questions and for any new US enforcement or legislative steps affecting advanced-chip access to China. Further signals from Asian data centre operators will show how the restrictions shape regional compute availability.
