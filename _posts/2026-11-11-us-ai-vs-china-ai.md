# US AI vs China AI Brief: Blacklisted Chinese University Buys Nvidia H200 Chips

**Today:** Fresh evidence of export-control evasion and a classified warning to the US Commerce Secretary are colliding with a congressional push to tighten chip curbs, raising compliance stakes for any enterprise touching the Nvidia-China supply chain.

## Top Stories

### 1. 🔒 **Reuters Finds Blacklisted Chinese University Bought Nvidia H200 Chips**

*Al Jazeera (Reuters) · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A defence-linked Chinese university on the US Entity List obtained restricted Nvidia H200 chips through a domestic reseller, exposing continued gaps in export-control enforcement.

A Reuters review of procurement records found Northwestern Polytechnical University, one of China's "Seven Sons of National Defense" institutions, bought two H200 chips in October for roughly 875,000 yuan (about $123,000) via Shaanxi Bosuan Network Technology rather than directly from Nvidia. The same university separately rented H200, A100 and A800 computing capacity through five contracts with Chinese firms this year, despite a US "policy of denial" on exports to blacklisted entities.

**Why it matters:** The findings show that licensing rules and entity-list restrictions are being routinely sidestepped through intermediaries, a pattern compliance and sanctions-screening teams at banks and BaaS providers financing chip-linked trade should treat as a live red flag, including across Asia-based resellers and leasing arrangements.

### 2. 🔒 **Axios: Classified Memo Warned Commerce Chief on Nvidia CEO**

*Axios · November 10, 2026*

**Bottom line:** A highly classified memo overseen by Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick about Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's ties to a suspected Chinese intelligence asset before Commerce cleared advanced chip sales to China-linked buyers.

Axios reported the memo drew on surveillance of Alice Huang and a chip-smuggling probe into her company, Megaspeed, and circulated ahead of President Trump's May trip to China. House Foreign Affairs Democrats have since demanded Lutnick explain how the department weighed the warning against its chip-export approvals.

**Why it matters:** The disclosure puts the credibility of the US licensing process itself in question, which could accelerate tighter scrutiny of any China-bound chip or cloud-compute deal that enterprises and financial institutions help finance or process.

### 3. 🏦 **Congress Races to Lock Three China Chip Bills Into Defense Act**

*The Korea Times · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Bipartisan lawmakers are pushing to keep the Chip Security Act, AI Overwatch Act and MATCH Act in the final FY2027 National Defense Authorization Act, using the Huang scandal to build momentum.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, Senator Pete Ricketts and a bipartisan group including Elizabeth Warren and Jim Banks confirmed the Senate's NDAA draft already contains all three measures, which target chip smuggling, expand congressional oversight of advanced exports, and would require US allies to halt chipmaking-equipment sales to China. The Trump administration has engaged on the text but not formally endorsed it.

**Why it matters:** If enacted, the bills would add new allied-country equipment restrictions and smuggling-tracking requirements that could reach chipmaking supply chains running through Southeast Asia, adding compliance steps for any Asia-based enterprise sourcing advanced compute.

### 4. 🌐 **Bessent Unveils US-China "Superintelligence" Dialogue Ahead of Xi Summit**

*CNBC · November 11, 2026*

**Bottom line:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a new US-China "superintelligence" dialogue to exchange best practices on testing advanced AI models, alongside the previously announced incident-alert channel, ahead of next week's Trump-Xi meeting in Shenzhen.

Bessent said the two sides agreed to keep "human judgement firmly in command" of AI systems but stopped short of any commitment to limit frontier model development. He added that Washington wants to "de-risk, not decouple" from China even as chip-export restrictions and competition over AI leadership continue.

**Why it matters:** The dialogue signals both governments want guardrails without slowing the race, a balance financial institutions in Singapore and the region should watch given how model-testing norms could eventually shape cross-border AI governance expectations.

## Key Takeaways

- Export-control enforcement gaps and a classified internal warning are surfacing in the same week, undercutting confidence that current US licensing safeguards are working as intended.
- Congress is using the Huang affair as leverage to lock in three chip-export bills before the NDAA closes, which would add allied-country and smuggling-tracking obligations relevant to Asia-based supply chains.
- Washington and Beijing are pursuing parallel tracks: tightening chip enforcement domestically while also expanding diplomatic AI-safety dialogue ahead of the Trump-Xi summit.

## What to Watch

Watch whether the Chip Security Act, AI Overwatch Act and MATCH Act survive final NDAA negotiations, and whether Commerce Secretary Lutnick responds to Democrats' questions about the classified Huang memo, both likely before the November 18-19 APEC summit where Trump and Xi are due to meet.
